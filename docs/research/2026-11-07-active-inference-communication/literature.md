# Literature map: communication policies and collective boundaries

Prepared November 7, 2026, for the independent model in [the research report](report.md). This is a targeted primary-source comparison, not a systematic review or a priority claim. Search covered active-inference communication, strategic partner models, learned message channels, epistemic communities, and hierarchical blankets. Searches included recent 2025–2026 work. A scholarly aggregator returned incomplete and poorly matched results; the usable comparisons came from primary publisher, proceedings, author, and arXiv sources, with existing Research notes used as leads.

The broad ideas of choosing what to communicate, choosing whom to attend to, modeling a listener, and forming group boundaries all have precedents. Any distinct contribution would need to be stated at the level of a particular probabilistic construction, geometric constraint, policy derivation, or demonstrated formation mechanism.

## Communication as inference and action

| Primary paper | What it contributes | Scope relative to this investigation |
|---|---|---|
| Friston and Frith (2015), [A Duet for One](https://www.fil.ion.ucl.ac.uk/~karl/A%20Duet%20for%20one.pdf), abstract and introduction | Coupled inference and alternating speaking/listening produce generalized synchrony in a shared generative-model example. | The shared-model premise differs from agents with independent preferences and potentially incompatible interpretations. It is a communication precedent, not a general N-agent formation result. |
| Friston and Frith (2015), [Active inference, communication and hermeneutics](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4502445/), simulation and learning sections | Communication involves inference and perceptual learning of generative-model parameters over different timescales. | Useful for separating rapid interpretation from slower learning. It does not supply the proposed low-rank GL channel with EFE-controlled recipient and modality choices. |
| Frank and Goodman (2012), [Predicting pragmatic reasoning in language games](https://web.stanford.edu/~ngoodman/papers/FrankGoodman-Science2012.pdf), equations (1)–(2) | A speaker selects utterances using a model of a listener; a listener reasons about the speaker's selection. | A direct precedent for strategic message likelihoods. It supplies shared semantic and contextual structure rather than deriving arbitrary cognitive alignment. |
| Albarracin, Demekas, Ramstead, and Heins (2022), [Epistemic Communities under Active Inference](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9027706/), sections 2.5, 2.12, 3.2.4, and 4 | Each agent chooses an expression and which neighbor to observe. Simulations study how epistemic confirmation bias and habits affect consensus and polarization. | Particularly close to the requested action structure. The admissible neighbor network is a supplied fixed random graph. Epistemic communities in this model should not be identified with emergent conditional-independence blankets. |
| Maisto, Donnarumma, and Pezzulo (2024), [Interactive Inference: A Multi-Agent Model of Cooperative Joint Actions](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.13113), cooperative-action construction and simulations | Agents predict partners in joint tasks; action can communicate knowledge through its legibility to a partner. | A useful receiver-model comparator, with cooperative tasks rather than unrestricted individual preferences. |
| Ruiz-Serra, Sweeney, and Harré (2025), [Factorised Active Inference for Strategic Multi-Agent Interactions](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2025/pdfs/p1793.pdf), sections 3–4 | Agents maintain separate beliefs about others and plan in general-sum games. Their simulations examine individual and aggregate VFE/EFE. | Directly relevant to different preferences. Aggregate EFE need not decrease in their examples; this is model-specific evidence, not a theorem against every possible collective potential. |
| Gwon, Murphy, and Chung (2026 preprint), [Evidence and Intervention: A Coupled Active-Inference Extension of Rational Speech Act Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04347), sections 3.6–3.7 and 3.10–3.12 of [the PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.04347) | The listener's utterance likelihood uses an attributed speaker policy. The model distinguishes interpretation, partner learning, and EFE policy selection; speaking can affect and probe the listener. | The closest recent pragmatic-policy overlap located. It retains a state-dependent policy normalizer. It is a preprint and does not establish the GL geometry or collective blanket claim. |
| Millidge, Tschantz, and Buckley (2021), [Whence the Expected Free Energy?](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_01354) | Examines the relation between EFE, future variational objectives, and epistemic exploration. | Supports declaring the policy functional and its assumptions explicitly. Present-time VFE minimization alone should not be used as a derivation of the desired exploratory policy. |

The 2022 epistemic-community model already combines expression actions with selective attention. The additional question here concerns what those expressions mean across different internal spaces, how their lossy channels are modeled, and when anticipated receiver responses alter the sender's policy. The 2026 pragmatic preprint overlaps strongly with the last of those questions.

## Partial expression, attention, and learned communication

| Primary paper | What is learned or supplied | Relation and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Das et al. (ICML 2019), [TarMAC: Targeted Multi-Agent Communication](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/das19a.html), section 4, equations (1)–(4), and section 5.4 of [the paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/das19a/das19a.pdf) | Sender signatures and value messages, receiver queries, and multi-round attention are learned. Main training uses task reward and a centralized critic; extensions include mixed incentives. | The closest attention architecture. It already learns message content and targeting. Belief-valued states, Bayesian reconstruction, and a specified EFE derivation would be additional structure. |
| Kim et al. (ICLR 2019), [Learning to Schedule Communication in Multi-agent Reinforcement Learning](https://yung-web.github.io/home/Publication/Conference/learning%20to%20schedule.pdf), sections 2.2 and 3.1–3.3 | SchedNet learns encoders, transmission priorities, and action policies; a prescribed scheduler allocates scarce broadcast access. | Separates channel access from message content. Its selected senders broadcast, so it is not pair-specific recipient choice. The main task uses shared reward. |
| Wang et al. (ICML 2020), [Learning Efficient Multi-agent Communication: An Information Bottleneck Approach](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/wang20i.html), sections 5.1–5.3, equations (2)–(6) | IMAC learns stochastic messages and weighted recipient aggregates with information-bottleneck penalties and task-return objectives. | A direct comparator for compressed, scheduled messages under resource constraints. Its return-minus-information objective is not automatically EFE. |
| Lin et al. (NeurIPS 2021), [Learning to Ground Multi-Agent Communication with Autoencoders](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/80fee67c8a4c4989bf8a580b4bbb0cd2-Paper.pdf), sections 3–5 | Independent agents learn symbols using an auxiliary reconstruction of their own observations; listeners learn how to use received symbols. | An alternative to propagating gradients across agents. The auxiliary autoencoder's decoder is not the receiving agent's interpretation model. |
| Hansen and Ghrist (2021), [Opinion Dynamics on Discourse Sheaves](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/20M1341088), sections 2–4 and 8–9 of [the author manuscript](https://jakobhansen.org/publications/opiniondynamics.pdf) | Agents have private vector spaces and edge-specific discourse spaces; restriction maps express partial opinions. Some constructions adapt opinions and expression maps. | A close geometric precedent for differing private spaces and partial expression. Its discrepancy dynamics do not supply independent EFE objectives or a blanket-formation theorem. |
| Taniguchi (2024), [Collective predictive coding hypothesis: symbol emergence as decentralized Bayesian inference](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2024.1353870/full) | Relates symbol emergence and naming-game constructions to decentralized inference over shared representations. | A relevant hypothesis-and-synthesis paper for learned semantics. Shared latent symbols and the assumptions of its constructive examples should not be replaced by an unrestricted society-wide free-energy claim. |

These works distinguish three learning problems: learning an economical expression of one's own state, learning which exchanges are useful, and learning how another agent interprets those expressions. A proposed model should state which problem each parameter update solves. Low message dimension alone supplies neither a calibrated physical capacity nor identifiable common semantics.

## Interior agents, boundary agents, and collective formation

| Primary paper | Evidence or construction | What it leaves open here |
|---|---|---|
| Palacios, Razi, Parr, Kirchhoff, and Friston (2020), [On Markov blankets and hierarchical self-organisation](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2019.110089), sections 1, 6, and 7; [author manuscript](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10089189/1/Friston_1-s2.0-S0022519319304588-main.pdf) | Simulates higher-level blankets from elements endowed with priors about possible roles and short-range signaling. | Direct precedent for the requested role-informed case. Membership is not fixed, but possible blanket roles are supplied. It is not a result that arbitrary private objectives and communication produce a blanket. |
| Heins et al. (PNAS 2024), [Collective behavior from surprise minimization](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2320239121); [published PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/210480/8/heins-et-al-2024-collective-behavior-from-surprise-minimization.pdf) | An active-inference model generates collective motion and studies effects of beliefs about uncertainty and model adaptation. | A relevant collective-dynamics mechanism. Cohesion, coordinated motion, and sensitivity to perturbations are separate from proving a collective blanket in the proposed communication model. |
| Thestrup Waade et al. (Entropy 2025), [As One and Many: Relating Individual and Emergent Group-Level Generative Models in Active Inference](https://www.mdpi.com/1099-4300/27/2/143), section 2.2 and “Applications and Extensions”; [institutional record](https://pure.au.dk/portal/en/publications/as-one-and-many-relating-individual-and-emergent-group-level-gene/) | Fits a group-level generative model to a bandit collective with a specified sensory/internal/active arrangement. | Useful after a boundary is supplied or found. Its blanket arrangement is predefined; the paper is not evidence of autonomous role formation. Publisher access was intermittent; the primary institutional record and publisher-indexed method passages were also consulted. |
| Maisto, Nuzzi, and Pezzulo (2025 preprint; v2 January 29, 2026), [What the flock knows that the birds do not](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10835v2), section 2 and Appendix A.4 | Simulates flocking, assigns internal/sensory/active/external birds, and analyzes collective response and information. Appendix A.4 constructs a graph from shared heading over a time window and identifies a boundary using a Fiedler-vector threshold. | Very close to the requested picture. Our reading of that stated method is that spectral assignment supplies candidate boundary labels; it does not by itself demonstrate conditional independence under the simulation's joint law. The title's collective-knowledge claim and the blanket claim are separate questions. |
| Beck and Ramstead (2025 preprint), [Dynamic Markov Blanket Detection for Macroscopic Physics Discovery](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21217v1), sections 2.1 and 3 | Infers time-varying internal, boundary, and environment labels using a structured generative model; explicitly distinguishes path blankets from steady-state ones. | A candidate detection/comparison method. Its latent dynamics and label transitions impose blanket structure, so a successful fit must be evaluated against alternatives. It does not explain why agents' EFE policies create the structure. |
| Biehl, Pollock, and Kanai (Entropy 2021), [A Technical Critique of Some Parts of the Free Energy Principle](https://doi.org/10.3390/e23030293); [published PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/entropy/entropy-23-00293/article_deploy/entropy-23-00293-v2.pdf) | Gives technical counterexamples to steps in particular FEP arguments and distinguishes inequivalent blanket conditions. | Relevant to the step from interaction sparsity to statistical screening-off. It does not prohibit explicitly constructing agents that optimize a declared EFE objective. |

A group found by clustering, a group with an interaction boundary, and a group satisfying a conditional-independence statement need separate evidence. The distinction is especially consequential when a boundary agent combines signals from both sides, when shared causes remain hidden, or when a partition is selected after observing the full population.

The most useful initial reading sequence is Albarracin et al. for expression and attention actions; TarMAC and the live GL manuscript for communication architecture; Ruiz-Serra et al. for distinct preferences; Gwon et al. for receiver-aware pragmatic policy; then Palacios et al., Maisto et al., and Beck–Ramstead for three different approaches to collective boundaries. The critical comparison is between supplied role structure, dynamically selected graph communities, and a blanket verified under a specified law.
