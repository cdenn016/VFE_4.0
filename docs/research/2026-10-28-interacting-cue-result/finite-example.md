# An exact two-agent exchange with correlated beliefs

This example instantiates the general process in [exact-interaction.md](exact-interaction.md). Agent 1 speaks to agent 0, then agent 0 replies. Each sends a fresh sample from its conditional result law. Neither agent observes the other's private preparation record. The delivered message and the speaker identity are observed by both agents. All variables are binary, all preparation probabilities are strictly positive, and every calculation below is exact.

## Preparation and the local cue/result banks

Let $X_0$ be uniform on $\{0,1\}$ and choose

\[
P(X_1=X_0\mid X_0)=\frac34.
\]

For each agent $i$, conditionally independently given the cues, prepare a presentation $A_i$, a result $Y_i$, and a private record $D_i$ with

\[
P(A_i=X_i\mid X_i)=\frac34,\qquad
P(Y_i=1-X_i\mid X_i)=\frac34,\qquad
P(D_i=X_i\mid X_i)=\frac34.
\tag{1}
\]

The joint preparation is the product of the specified normalized conditional probabilities. The order used to specify it does not alter its local bank interpretation: $A_i$ is uniform and $P(X_i=A_i\mid A_i)=3/4$. Hence the local marginal has exactly the form $r_i(a_i)\mu_{i,a_i}(x_i)K_i(x_i,y_i)$, with the cue law depending on the presentation. The result kernel is the same for both presentations in this example. It reverses the cue response and is not a same-state retrieval.

The coupling between $X_0$ and $X_1$ is supplied model data. Without a relation between the two agents' represented contents, evidence about the peer need not inform the receiver's own content. This preparation supplies that relation explicitly rather than obtaining it by multiplying incompatible local posteriors.

The represented state is $Z=(A_0,X_0,Y_0,A_1,X_1,Y_1)$. The sampled private records $D_0,D_1$ enter the agents' registers. Their accessible information is respectively $(D_0,H_{1:t})$ and $(D_1,H_{1:t})$. A transmitted bit $H_t$ is a fresh draw from the speaker's current posterior distribution of $Y_j$. It is not an observation of the already represented $Y_j$ in $Z$.

## Correlation is retained locally

For either agent, if its current cue probability is $p_i=P(X_i=1\mid\mathcal I_i)$ under this protocol, then

\[
P(A_i=1\mid\mathcal I_i)=\frac14+\frac12p_i,\qquad
P(Y_i=1\mid\mathcal I_i)=\frac34-\frac12p_i.
\tag{2}
\]

The message likelihoods depend on $Z$ only through the private records. Therefore the conditional laws of $A_i$ and $Y_i$ at fixed cues remain those in (1). It follows that

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(A_i,X_i\mid\mathcal I_i)
=\frac12p_i(1-p_i)>0,\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(Y_i,X_i\mid\mathcal I_i)
=-\frac12p_i(1-p_i)<0.
\tag{3}
\]

These identities exhibit model/content dependence and a negative cue/result response simultaneously. Replacing the local joint by its presentation and content marginals as independent factors would remove the first covariance.

## First message

Initially $P(X_i=1\mid D_i=0)=1/4$ and $P(X_i=1\mid D_i=1)=3/4$. Equation (2) gives the peer's sampling kernel

\[
\kappa_1(1\mid D_1=0)=\frac58,\qquad
\kappa_1(1\mid D_1=1)=\frac38.
\tag{4}
\]

Integrating the unknown private record gives

\[
P(H_1=1\mid X_1=0)=\frac9{16},\qquad
P(H_1=1\mid X_1=1)=\frac7{16}.
\tag{5}
\]

For example, the first value is $(3/4)(5/8)+(1/4)(3/8)$. The corresponding likelihood conditional on the receiver's cue is

\[
P(H_1=1\mid X_0=0)=\frac{17}{32},\qquad
P(H_1=1\mid X_0=1)=\frac{15}{32}.
\tag{6}
\]

Take the actual receiver record $D_0=1$ and the delivered message $H_1=1$. Then

\[
P(X_0=1\mid D_0=1,H_1=1)
=\frac{(3/4)(15/32)}{(3/4)(15/32)+(1/4)(17/32)}
=\frac{45}{62}.
\tag{7}
\]

Its presentation and result probabilities become $19/31$ and $12/31$. The message provides evidence that partly opposes its private record.

The peer's own sampled bit leaves its posterior unchanged. Given $D_1=d_1$, the factor in (4) is constant in $Z$, so it cancels between the numerator and denominator of Bayes' rule. For the actual private record $D_1=0$, its cue probability remains $1/4$. This is an exact conditional-independence statement about the sampling protocol, not a decision to suppress a legitimate observation.

## A reply that genuinely depends on reception

After $H_1=1$, the receiver's cue probability at its other possible private record is

\[
P(X_0=1\mid D_0=0,H_1=1)=\frac5{22}.
\]

Therefore the second transmission is generated by

\[
\kappa_0(1\mid D_0=0,H_1=1)=\frac7{11},\qquad
\kappa_0(1\mid D_0=1,H_1=1)=\frac{12}{31}.
\tag{8}
\]

These differ from the initial $5/8$ and $3/8$: the incoming message has changed the outgoing law. This is a two-way interaction between posterior-predictive agents, not a pair of independent emissions.

Condition on the actual values $D_0=1,D_1=0$ and the public transcript $(H_1,H_2)=(1,1)$. Each local posterior conditions only on its own private value and the transcript; it does not gain access to the other's private value. The resulting marginals are

| Agent and available information | $P(X_i=1)$ | $P(A_i=1)$ | $P(Y_i=1)$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Receiver 0, $D_0=1$ | $3/4$ | $5/8$ | $3/8$ |
| Receiver 0, $D_0=1,H_1=1$ | $45/62$ | $19/31$ | $12/31$ |
| Receiver 0, $D_0=1,H_1=H_2=1$ | $45/62$ | $19/31$ | $12/31$ |
| Peer 1, $D_1=0$ | $1/4$ | $3/8$ | $5/8$ |
| Peer 1, $D_1=0,H_1=1$ | $1/4$ | $3/8$ | $5/8$ |
| Peer 1, $D_1=0,H_1=H_2=1$ | $437/1918$ | $349/959$ | $610/959$ |

The receiver changes upon reception and the peer changes upon the reply. Neither changes its own represented-state posterior when it samples an outgoing message.

## The four-weight retained state

The common dynamic state can be $\lambda_t(d_0,d_1)=P(D_0=d_0,D_1=d_1\mid H_{1:t})$, together with the fixed reconstruction kernel $W(d_0,d_1;dz)=P_0(dz\mid d_0,d_1)$ and the known schedule and encoders. In the order $(00,01,10,11)$,

\[
\lambda_0=\frac1{32}(9,7,7,9),\qquad
\lambda_1\bigm|_{H_1=1}=\frac1{128}(45,21,35,27),
\tag{9}
\]

and

\[
\lambda_2\bigm|_{H_1=H_2=1}
=\left(\frac{105}{242},\frac{49}{242},\frac{70}{341},\frac{54}{341}\right).
\tag{10}
\]

The fixed cue reconstruction table for agent 0 is

\[
P_0(X_0=1\mid D_0,D_1)
=\left(\frac16,\frac5{14},\frac9{14},\frac56\right),
\tag{11}
\]

in the same order. For agent 1 exchange $D_0,D_1$. Equations (1) then reconstruct the model and result conditionals and their dependence. The fixed result-probability tables are

\[
V_0(D;1)=\left(\frac23,\frac47,\frac37,\frac13\right),\qquad
V_1(D;1)=\left(\frac23,\frac37,\frac47,\frac13\right).
\tag{12}
\]

To generate a message, the speaker averages its table over the conditional distribution of the other private record given its own. To update the common state, multiply all four weights by the corresponding speaker-private likelihood and normalize. For example, averaging (11) with $\lambda_1(D_1\mid D_0=1)$ gives $45/62$. After the reply, $P(D_0=1\mid D_1=0,H_1=H_2=1)=308/959$. Hence the peer's cue probability is

\[
\frac{651}{959}\frac16+\frac{308}{959}\frac5{14}
=\frac{437}{1918}.
\tag{13}
\]

The expanded common posterior has 256 joint atoms when the private records are included. Its relation to the four weights is exactly $\Gamma_t(dz,dD)=W(D,dz)\lambda_t(dD)$. The fixed kernel is part of the retained model data. This removes redundant changing coordinates, not information from the admitted family. Actual private records and the public transcript remain registered if past records are part of the operational interface; four weights alone do not reconstruct the literal transcript.

## Two distinct errors in message interpretation

Replacing the posterior-sampled message by a direct observation of the represented $Y_1$ gives $P(X_0=1\mid D_0=1,Y_1=1)=9/14$, rather than $45/62$. The two message models have the same unconditional binary output marginal here, but different correlations with the represented state.

A different error arises from repeated fresh samples. Suppose the same peer speaks twice before a reply. Its own information is unchanged by its first sample, so both bits are independent conditional on $D_1$, with the probabilities in (4). They are not independent conditional only on $X_1$. In fact,

\[
P(H_1=H_2=1\mid X_1=0)=\frac{21}{64},\qquad
P(H_1=H_2=1\mid X_1=1)=\frac{13}{64}.
\tag{14}
\]

The exact receiver posterior is then $P(X_0=1\mid D_0=1,H_1=H_2=1)=45/64$. Squaring the once-marginalized likelihoods in (5) instead gives $171/244$, a different answer. The second fresh sample can carry additional information about the same unknown private record; its correct conditional likelihood accounts for that shared cause.

A literal echo is another protocol. If $H_2=H_1$ deterministically, the second conditional likelihood given the registered first message is one. Its assimilation changes neither posterior. Treating that echo as a fresh sample would be an error even under the full retained state.

## Executed evidence

[check_interaction.py](check_interaction.py) enumerates the 256-atom preparation with exact rational arithmetic. It checks all four two-message public histories, normalization, sender invariance, the model/content correlations, the independently tabulated joint-history likelihood, the four-weight reconstruction and recursion, and the direct-sensor, repeated-sample, and echo distinctions. [interaction-checks.json](interaction-checks.json) records the resulting fractions. The final run took 0.0314 seconds on CPU, with library thread counts capped at four and no process pools. No Monte Carlo samples, trajectory integration, parameter scan, GPU query, or training was used. The general finite-history theorem is analytical; these checks establish only the displayed finite instance.
