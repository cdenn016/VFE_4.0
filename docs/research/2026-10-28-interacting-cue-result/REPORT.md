# Interacting cue/result agents: exact records, inference, and retention

An explicit two-agent cue/result system closes under exact Bayesian communication before any moment projection. Each agent retains a joint belief over its model presentation, cue, and result. It sends a fresh sample from its current result prediction through a known encoder, and its peer conditions on the actual sampling mechanism. The common evolving state can be the joint posterior of the two private preparation records, together with a fixed reconstruction kernel. A finite example exhibits a reply whose distribution changes because of the first reception. It also distinguishes posterior-sampled communication from a direct measurement and from a repeated independent observation.

This is one supplied common-model protocol. Its cross-agent coupling, private-record mechanisms, encoders, and shared transcript are assumptions. The result establishes an interacting realization of the local cue/result construction. It does not establish that a trained transformer implements that joint law, that different agents discover a common model, or that reciprocal Fisher dynamics optimize its free energy.

## General normalized model

For each agent, write its represented variables as $Z_i=(A_i,X_i,Y_i)$ and its local bank as

\[
\pi_i(da_i,dx_i,dy_i)
=r_i(da_i)\mu_{i,a_i}(dx_i)K_{i,a_i}(x_i,dy_i).
\]

Choose a normalized coupling $P_0(dz_0,dz_1)$ with these marginals and normalized private-record kernels $L_i(x_i,dd_i)$. The preparation law is

\[
\Lambda_0(dz,dd)
=P_0(dz_0,dz_1)L_0(x_0,dd_0)L_1(x_1,dd_1),
\qquad D=(D_0,D_1).
\]

All spaces are standard Borel and all kernels measurable. In density formulas, denominators are required to be positive and finite at observed records; conditional laws and null-history extensions are fixed as described in [the exact derivation](exact-interaction.md). Gaussianity is unnecessary.

Agent $i$ observes $D_i$ and the transcript $H_{1:t}$. Let

\[
\Gamma_t=P(Z,D\mid H_{1:t}),\qquad
\Pi_{i,t}=P(Z,D\mid D_i,H_{1:t}).
\]

Its local recognition is the $Z_i$ marginal of $\Pi_{i,t}$, retaining the disintegration $s_i(da_i)q_i(dx_i,dy_i\mid a_i)$. When agent $j$ speaks, it samples a fresh $\widetilde Y$ from its current $Y_j$ posterior and encodes it using $C_j(y,dh)$. Thus the actual message kernel is

\[
\kappa_j(\Gamma_t,d_j;dh)
=\int C_j(y,dh)P(Y_j\in dy\mid D_j=d_j,H_{1:t}).
\]

The random draw uses fresh randomization independent of the represented variables conditional on the speaker's information. Its equality in conditional distribution with $Y_j$ does not make it an observation of that same variable. Both agents register the delivered message; the fixed schedule alternates the speaker.

Successive multiplication of these normalized kernels defines a consistent probability law at every finite history. Bayes' rule under that law gives, in the dominated case,

\[
\Gamma_{t+1}(dz,dd)
\propto k_j(h_{t+1}\mid d_j,\Gamma_t)\Gamma_t(dz,dd).
\]

The speaker's private posterior is unchanged by its own transmission: conditional on $D_j=d_j$, this likelihood factor is constant in every remaining variable and cancels. For the receiver $i$, the effective local likelihood is instead

\[
\ell_{i\leftarrow j,t}(h\mid z_i)
=\int k_j(h\mid d_j,\Gamma_t)
P(dd_j\mid Z_i=z_i,D_i,H_{1:t}).
\]

This conditional integration is the interaction term. A receiver's local marginal alone does not generally determine it. Each later message uses the newly conditioned speaker prediction, so the protocol includes feedback through inference.

## Exact retained state

Disintegrate the preparation into

\[
\Lambda_0(dz,dd)=W(d,dz)\lambda_0(dd),\qquad
W(d,dz)=P(Z\in dz\mid D=d).
\]

At any fixed transcript, every message likelihood depends on the prepared variables only through $D$. Therefore the reconstruction kernel is unchanged:

\[
\boxed{\Gamma_t(dz,dd)=W(d,dz)\lambda_t(dd),\qquad
\lambda_t=P(D\in\cdot\mid H_{1:t}).}
\]

Define the fixed encoded-result kernel $V_j(d,dh)=\int C_j(y,dh)W^{Y_j}(d,dy)$. The message law is the conditional average

\[
\kappa_j^{\lambda_t}(d_j,dh)
=\int V_j(d,dh)\lambda_t(dd\mid D_j=d_j).
\]

Its density gives the closed update

\[
\boxed{\lambda_{t+1}(dd)
=\frac{k_j^{\lambda_t}(h_{t+1}\mid d_j)\lambda_t(dd)}
{\int k_j^{\lambda_t}(h_{t+1}\mid d_j')\lambda_t(dd')}.}
\]

Each agent reconstructs its complete represented-state posterior by integrating $W$ against $\lambda_t(dd\mid D_i=d_i)$. The retained state includes the fixed model and encoders, $W$, the joint $\lambda_t$, its own private value, and the next speaker. It also includes the persistent transcript when queries of actual past records are part of the operational interface. The statistical state need not reconstruct the literal transcript.

This is an exact sufficient representation. On the admitted family $\{W\lambda\}$, retaining $\lambda$ with $W$ is invertible by reconstruction. It reduces the changing coordinates without asserting a lossy parent compression or a minimal representation. The static kernel can be large, and continuous private-record laws need not have finite descriptions.

The same reduction applies to variational free energy. With historical policy values fixed by the declared protocol and observed prefix, let $\mathcal L_t(D)$ be the history likelihood and $Z_t$ its evidence. Then

\[
\mathcal F_t^D(\nu)
=\operatorname{KL}(\nu\Vert\lambda_0)
-\mathbb E_\nu\log\mathcal L_t(D)
=\operatorname{KL}(\nu\Vert\lambda_t)-\log Z_t.
\]

For a candidate full law with private-record marginal $\nu$, the remaining term is the $\nu$-average of its conditional KL to $W$. Optimizing that conditional gives $W$ exactly. The derivation also gives each agent's private and local correlated free-energy identities. These are fixed-history inference objectives; recomputing historical policies from a candidate variational law defines a different optimization problem.

## A checked two-way exchange

The [finite example](finite-example.md) uses binary cues with $P(X_1=X_0)=3/4$, correlated presentations with $P(A_i=X_i)=3/4$, a negative result response $P(Y_i=1-X_i)=3/4$, and private records with $P(D_i=X_i)=3/4$. Conditional preparation noises are independent. Each local presentation/content covariance remains positive and its cue/result covariance negative.

Take private values $D_0=1,D_1=0$ and public messages $H_1=H_2=1$, with agent 1 speaking first. The accessible local probabilities are

| Stage | Agent 0: $P(X_0=1\mid D_0,H)$ | Agent 1: $P(X_1=1\mid D_1,H)$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before messages | $3/4$ | $1/4$ |
| After agent 1 sends | $45/62$ | $1/4$ |
| After agent 0 replies | $45/62$ | $437/1918$ |

Agent 0's probability of replying with a one changes from $3/8$ to $12/31$ after reception. Thus the reply depends on the incoming message and informs agent 1. The four private-record weights begin at $(9,7,7,9)/32$, in the order $(00,01,10,11)$. Their update and fixed reconstruction reproduce all 256 atoms of the expanded posterior.

The interpretation of the message matters quantitatively. A direct observation of represented $Y_1=1$ would give agent 0 the cue probability $9/14$, instead of $45/62$. If agent 1 sends two fresh samples before any reply, the correct receiver probability is $45/64$; falsely treating the two samples as independent conditional only on $X_1$ gives $171/244$. The samples share the hidden private record $D_1$. A deterministic echo carries no additional evidence after the original bit has been registered.

The unchanged [checking script](check_interaction.py) was executed with exact rational arithmetic. All four two-message histories passed normalization, sender-invariance, correlated-marginal, direct-product, and four-weight reconstruction checks. The [output](interaction-checks.json) records the fractions and a 0.0314-second CPU runtime. This enumerates the specified finite experiment; it does not numerically prove the general theorem.

## Gaussian closure and the limits of local retention

The [Gaussian and retention analysis](gaussian-and-retention.md) derives the actual message likelihood for jointly Gaussian cue components, private noisy cue records, and Gaussian encoders. The general mixture case requires integration over the unknown peer record, including its posterior-dependent component weights. It is a normalized exact formula before any projection.

One component per agent with affine Gaussian kernels gives an exact finite-dimensional repeated-message filter. The common law of $D$ stays jointly Gaussian: reconstructing with fixed Gaussian $W$, conditioning on the speaker's coordinate, sampling its result posterior, and encoding it produces an affine Gaussian likelihood in that coordinate. Gaussian conditioning closes the induction. The joint private-record covariance is retained.

Multiple components do not generally remain a finite Gaussian mixture. The note supplies a two-component counterexample whose posterior divided by its pre-message Gaussian has two different positive finite tail limits. A finite positive Gaussian mixture cannot have that property: wider components diverge relative to the baseline, shifted equal-variance components diverge in one tail, narrower components vanish in both tails, and identical components contribute equal limits. This is an analytical obstruction to that particular closure family, not to general-law inference.

A separate finite preparation has a public parity record relating the two cues. Its two possible registered histories produce identical local joint recognition laws, including model/content dependence, but different next receiver updates: $7/16$ versus $9/16$ for the same received bit. Deleting the parity and the corresponding cross-agent dependence therefore violates the retained-state descent criterion. Keeping that record, or its effect in the common reconstruction, resolves this collision. The example does not prove minimality of the full retained state.

Moment projection can now be introduced as an explicit approximation to this declared experiment. Matching a local result mean and covariance does not specify the conditional law of a peer's private evidence. Even retaining every local label/content correlation leaves that separate obligation. A Gaussian carrier accompanied by a complete reconstructible transcript is a different retention from a carrier that deletes the distinguishing information.

## Scope, evidence, and continuation

Communication here redistributes evidence from a fixed preparation. Since $H_{1:t}$ is conditionally independent of $Z$ given the private-record pair, its information about $Z$ cannot exceed what the pair supplies. New observations, bank adaptation, unknown policies, differing agent models, unreliable sharing, and endogenous formation require additional declared mechanisms. A shared bank alone does not supply the cross-agent coupling used here. No convergence or common reciprocal-potential claim follows from normalized Bayesian communication.

The bounded question is resolved for this protocol: an explicit interacting law, correlated local inference, registered messages, and sufficient retention are available before projection. The next practical question is a controlled approximation to the joint private-record posterior in the multi-component case, assessed against future message laws and receiver updates. A different next question would add new private measurements over time; it changes the preparation and filtering model and should be specified separately.
