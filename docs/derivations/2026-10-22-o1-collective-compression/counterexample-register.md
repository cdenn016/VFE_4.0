# Failure tests and scope limits

These are analytical counterexamples or failed implications. They are not simulated outcomes and do not establish general nonexistence of O1.

## Independent primitive or proper-block phase erasure

The cross moment is \(c_{ij}=\zeta_i\overline{\zeta_j}\). Rotating a proper block with its exterior fixed changes a crossing moment. More strongly, that moment and a fixed nonzero exterior first moment identify each interior B. An independently compressive parent for that block cannot preserve the fixed boundary. The global common rotation preserves all joint differences. Evidence: constructive route C19–C22 and observability route A16–A17.

## Absolute-angle probes and evidence

A future absolute psi sample has first moment \(\zeta_i\), which varies along the proposed global fiber. A likelihood depending on an absolute psi direction, with that direction retained, can produce phase-dependent velocities. Such actions are excluded from inception. Adding them invalidates the quotient; deleting them at handoff would not repair it within the frozen target. Internal copying channels remain native data and are not exposed as absolute external readouts.

## Pointwise contextual canonicalization

Take \(B_i(c)=b_i e_{\chi(c)}\), all \(b_i>0\), and a nonflat marked sector. Erasing the common phase separately at every context removes \(\sum_i b_i I_1(b_i)/I_0(b_i)\) from the minimized chi information. Relative histories do not restore it. The whole-section anchor retains \(\chi(c)-\chi(c_0)\) and its evaluation derivative. Evidence: geometry route G24–G25. No preservation is claimed for an outer parameter rotating the entire prepared section.

## History-dependent rotation and the marked action

A context-independent sample map may change orbit scores if its rotation depends on a history value transformed by the marked action. The explicit conditional circular example in the geometry route loses positive orbit inertia under this procedure. The witness instead fixes every stored evidence and query lab label while transforming local direction embeddings. This makes the entire history fixed under the marked frame and proves commutation. Arbitrary history canonicalization is not covered.

## Exact threshold timestamps

A deterministic event time \(\tau(c)\) has a Dirac law that may fail quadratic-mean differentiability as c varies. Omitting an accessible time while calling the history complete is invalid. The witness declares fixed census times initially and retains their state-based partition outputs, constant on its strict-margin patch. The census changes neither field nor probes. Continuously reported crossing times remain outside this geometry theorem.

## Separate marginals and independent edge outputs

Response and next-state marginals do not determine their joint kernel. Three differences drawn from the same three angles also obey a cycle constraint and cannot be replaced by independent pair samples. The witness descends the actual joint kernel and uses fresh samples only between transactions. Evidence: observability route A13–A20.

## Finite noisy records

The fixed-coarsening example in the geometry route has conditional variance \(\sigma^2/(\sigma^2+n)>0\) after n finite-variance observations. Dropping that variable loses this horizontal information even while a separate marked connection stays nonflat. This is a sample-coarsening counterexample, not a Gaussian assumption in the constructive witness. Identity G21 states exactly what the retained records must determine.

## Analytic no-new-equivalence requires its hypotheses

The obstruction uses one common regular fixed-history flow segment and an analytic, wait-closed experiment family. Integer-only observations of the sinc example A6 evade wait closure. C2 functions can vanish on a future interval without vanishing earlier. An injective append can change the objective and need not reflect operational equivalence backward. These limits prevent a universal negative claim. The lemma also does not factor a collective equivalence into primitive factors.

## Singular and long-time limits

The canonical chart requires a nonzero anchor. Positive cone projections prove this at every finite admitted time, while the angular sector approaches zero asymptotically. Nonflatness uses a compact finite window and fixed positive marked amplitudes. There is no infinite-time curvature bound or canonical parent at the singular zero anchor. The zero-parameter limit evaluates only smooth fine costs; parent equality is applied at positive parameters.
