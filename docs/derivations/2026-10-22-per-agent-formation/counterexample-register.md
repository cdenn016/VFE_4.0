## A future phase probe destroys the proposed quotient

For the hidden von Mises factor,

\[
\mathbb E[e^{i\Psi}]=\frac{I_1(b)}{I_0(b)}e^{i\theta}.
\]

For \(b>0\), different hidden phases have different physical diagnostics. If this diagnostic is an admitted future boundary response, phase deletion fails response equality. A recorded positive-probability choice of such a probe still requires equality of its conditional response law; a rare probe is enough to obstruct exact compression.

A persistent hidden-angle record is stronger still. A likelihood proportional to \(\exp(\lambda\cos(v-\psi))\) gives a term \(\lambda\cos(v-\theta)\) in the future amplitude velocity and a term \(\lambda\sin(v-\theta)/b\) in the phase velocity. Retaining the record \(v\) while deleting \(\theta\) therefore fails same-time projectability. This failure does not require a finite record to statistically identify the phase.
