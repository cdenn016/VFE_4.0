## 2. Findings

### L3-01: Frame independence of the exact-lift configuration metric is asserted without an equivariance hypothesis
**Location:** c07_fiber_geometry.tex:358-362 (def:hist-configuration-manifold, the sentence "These metrics are frame independent by prop:pb-statistical-tensor-descent"), together with hyp:hist-exact-vfe-lift at c07:421-452.
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** prop:pb-statistical-tensor-descent proves that g^F_x and T^F_x on one law fiber B_x are invariant under the represented action, which is pushforward by the bimeasurable sample map ρ_x(g) (ca_proofs_c07:4-12; c02:67-71). The sentence also covers the second metric listed on Q_V, the pullback G_i = ι_i^* G_{R_B} of a joint Fisher metric along an exact lift ι_i : Q_i → R_B into conditional recognition kernels r_B(dy_B | y_{B^c}) on the latent sample space. A pullback is frame independent only if ι_i ∘ ρ̂(g) = ρ̂_R(g) ∘ ι_i for an action of G on R_B, and only if the outside marginal Q_{B^c}, which is written in the frames of the other agents, transforms compatibly. hyp:hist-exact-vfe-lift gives R_B no G-action and imposes no equivariance on ext_i or ι_i.
The chapter itself lists as open "the existence of correlated lifts equivariant under a connected structure group acting on continuous law fibers" (open:hist-parity-lift-scope, c07:745-748). The parity lift shows that equivariance does not hold by default: eq:hist-parity-odd-pseudoscalar sends the lift with amplitude κ to the lift with amplitude −κ under a single complement. prop:hist-general-score-residual shows that the pulled-back metric depends on the choice of right inverse (dλ_1² against 2dλ_1²), so a frame change that changes the lift changes the metric.
**Fix:** Restrict the sentence to the channel-weighted and block-diagonal metrics, and add to hyp:hist-exact-vfe-lift that R_B carries a stated represented action (per agent or diagonal) under which ext_i and ι_i are equivariant. Otherwise declare G_i = ι_i^* G_{R_B} frame-relative.
**Falsifies:** A derivation from hyp:hist-exact-vfe-lift as written that ι_i^* G_{R_B} is invariant under every ρ̂(g), or a passage that defines R_B and ι_i on associated-bundle fibers rather than in frame coordinates. I found neither in c07:342-460.
**Status:** unlabeled prose inside a definition, not a tagged result. The joint-metric reading of thm:hist-fisher-clock-invariance and of the clocks on Q_V inherits the unproved invariance. Inflated as worded.

### L3-02: Bauer–Bruveris–Michor is cited for uniqueness on smooth positive densities, where it fails
**Location:** c07_fiber_geometry.tex:130-132 (prop:ig-fisher-characterizations).
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** The manuscript says uniqueness up to a constant holds "for diffeomorphism-invariant smooth weak Riemannian metrics on smooth positive densities on a closed manifold of dimension at least two [BauerBruverisMichor2016, Theorem in the introduction]". The theorem in the introduction of arXiv:1411.5577v2 is stated on Prob(M), "the space Prob(M) of smooth positive probability densities". The Main Theorem of the same paper classifies Diff(M)-invariant bilinear forms on Dens+(M) as C_1 ∫(α/μ)(β/μ)μ + C_2 ∫α ∫β. That is a two-coefficient family, the smooth-density analogue of the Campbell cone result in the preceding sentence. A concrete counterexample on Dens+(M): G = G^FR + (∫α)(∫β) is Diff(M)-invariant, because ∫α is invariant under pushforward, and positive definite. By Cauchy–Schwarz, (∫α)² ≤ μ(M) ∫α²/μ, so any C_1 > 0 with C_1 + C_2 μ(M) > 0 is positive definite. This G is not a constant multiple of Fisher–Rao.
**Fix:** Replace "smooth positive densities" with "smooth positive probability densities", or state the Dens+(M) classification with its two coefficients.
**Falsifies:** A version of the BBM introduction theorem stated on Dens+(M). The arXiv v2 text reads Prob(M).
**Status:** STANDARD. The citation does not say what this clause attributes to it, so the clause is inflated. It does not propagate, because no uniqueness is used anywhere in the scope (c07:24-26; grep for "uniqu" in c07 and g01 to g06 finds no downstream use).

### L3-03: The Cencov locator "Lemma 11.3" is unverified, and the finite-simplex statement omits that uniqueness is a property of the family over all finite sets
**Location:** c07_fiber_geometry.tex:113-115.
**Severity:** low
**Evidence:** Two secondary sources that read the volume pin the Fisher-uniqueness statement to Theorem 11.1, p. 159. AJLS Proposition 3.19 (arXiv v4) maps the Cencov argument to Lemma 11.1, p. 157 (invariant functions are constant), Lemma 11.2, p. 158 (invariant 1-forms vanish) and Theorem 11.1, p. 159 (invariant quadratic forms are multiples of Fisher). Lê 2017, Appendix 6, cites "Theorem 11.1, p. 159". Neither cites a Lemma 11.3. The vault record multiagentelbo-2026-09-24-theory-restructure.md:152 says that a locator agent inserted "Lemma 11.3, p. 161" and that "the report does not say whether this page was read in full or through a snippet". The current text cites "Theorem 11.1; Lemma 11.3". Separately, the uniqueness is a statement about a sequence of metrics g_n on the open simplices of every finite set, invariant under Markov morphisms between simplices (Amari–Nagaoka Theorem 2.6, p. 38, as restated in Lê 2017, Proposition 6.1). On one simplex alone the conclusion fails. For n = 2 the only Markov automorphism is the swap, and every metric f(p)dp² with f(p) = f(1−p) is invariant under it.
**Fix:** Cite "Theorem 11.1, p. 159", and drop Lemma 11.3 or verify it on the page. Phrase the result as uniqueness of the family of metrics on the open simplices of all finite sets.
**Falsifies:** Page 161 of the AMS volume showing that Lemma 11.3 is the Fisher-uniqueness statement or a step its proof cannot do without.
**Status:** STANDARD. The claim is right; the locator is unverified.

## 4. Implementation notes for VFE_4.0

On Gaussian law fibers every object in these chapters has a closed form, so an implementation should compute it exactly rather than estimate it. The Fisher metric in the moment chart is μ̇ᵀΛμ̇ + ½Tr(ΛΣ̇ΛΣ̇). Its covariance block is one half of the affine-invariant metric, and code that calls an AIRM routine must carry that factor. The natural gradient is Σ∇_μ for the mean and 2Σ(sym ∇_Σ)Σ for the covariance. If the covariance is stored as vech, the Euclidean covector must double the off-diagonal entries before the Fisher solve; my check reproduces 2Σ∇_ΣΣ only with that convention. The expectation chart (μ, M_2) has a different mean block, (1 + μᵀΛμ)Λ + ΛμμᵀΛ, so a mean-only update must state which coordinate is held fixed (g05, prop:ig-fisher-expectation-chart). The Amari–Chentsov tensor, the KL jets and the orbit Fisher form are polynomial in (μ, Σ, ξ). For the GL(K) action, k(ξ,ξ) = ‖Zη‖² + 2‖sym Z‖² with Z = L⁻¹ξL, η = L⁻¹μ and LLᵀ = Σ. Its radical is the stabilizer algebra, of dimension K(K−1)/2 at μ = 0 and (K−1)(K−2)/2 at μ ≠ 0, so a link or frame natural gradient built on it is singular. prop:hist-semidefinite-gradient-obstruction forbids inverting it silently; a quotient (thm:hist-retained-fisher-quotient) or a declared completion must be chosen and recorded. For non-Gaussian fibers the Fisher and cubic tensors are expectations of score products and need quadrature or Monte Carlo. Attention rows on the simplex have the softmax Fisher diag(p) − ppᵀ with radical R1, and the Moore–Penrose solution is the representative orthogonal to 1.

The theory leaves several algorithmic choices open, and an implementer must declare each one. The configuration metric is chosen data (c07:24-26, def:hist-configuration-manifold). The options are the channel weights w_b and w_m, a block-diagonal sum over agents, or the exact-lift joint Fisher metric. The last needs a constructed right inverse ι_i, which these chapters do not supply beyond the finite parity witness, and which is frame-relative unless it is made equivariant (L3-01). The positive rate a(t) and any belief/model rate ratio are also choices. A scalar rate only reparameterizes the orbit, but a block rate ratio is an anisotropic mobility that changes the orbit itself (prop:hist-scalar-mobility-orbit). A two-timescale implementation therefore follows a different history from the joint natural-gradient flow, and its duration must be read with a declared metric, using the bound in eq:hist-clock-metric-dissipation-bound. The theory defines duration only for C¹ curves, so the clock of a discrete history needs an estimator. Three candidates are Σ_n √G(ΔQ_n,ΔQ_n), Σ_n √(2 KL(Q_n ‖ Q_{n+1})) (equal to the first to second order by the orbit and jet expansions), and, for the natural-gradient Euler scheme, Σ_n Δt·a‖grad‖_G, which comes free from eq:hist-vfe-clock-rate. All three carry an O(Δt) error relative to the length of the underlying curve. The exact Fisher–Rao step length has a closed form only on the fixed-mean submanifold (√(½Σ log² λ_i) over the eigenvalues λ_i of Σ_n⁻¹Σ_{n+1}) where the fixed-mean submanifold is totally geodesic. At fixed covariance the straight mean segment has the Mahalanobis length, which is a curve length and exceeds the Fisher–Rao distance of the full family. For general (μ, Σ) with K > 1 there is no closed form. The clock origin must be stored with the clock.

The numerical hazards follow from the same results. An explicit natural-gradient step decreases F only for Δt < 2/c_G, where c_G is the largest generalized eigenvalue of the Hessian relative to G (eq:hist-discrete-descent-bound). Either estimate it, for instance with a few Lanczos steps on G⁻¹H, or use backtracking, and note that Euler iterates do not lie on the selected orbit. An additive covariance step can leave the SPD cone, and an algebraic map on natural parameters can leave the natural domain, where no law, Fisher metric or evidence bound exists (g01, prop:exp-domain-boundary-no-law, prop:exp-fixed-point-no-law). Updates in natural parameters, or exponential-map retractions on Σ, keep iterates in the domain, and every evaluation should assert Λ ≻ 0. A clock built from √(2 KL) of tiny steps loses its relative accuracy in fp32 because KL is a difference of nearly equal log-determinants. Accumulate step lengths from the quadratic form G(ΔQ,ΔQ), or in float64. Inside the domain, KL is the Bregman divergence of A in natural parameters (prop:exp-kl-bregman), which gives an exact, cheap KL when natural parameters are maintained. Affine mean-constrained information projections are unique and solve a KKT system in natural parameters; a Gaussian mean constraint leaves the precision unchanged.

Record clocks and meta-agent contraction need the conditional score E[s | Ȳ] and E Var(s | Ȳ). These are closed form for linear-Gaussian channels and otherwise need sampling. The theorem applies only when the meta-agent law is the image of the same fine history under one parameter-independent kernel. A meta-agent recomputed from its own free energy falls under the semiconjugacy route, whose factor a_ℓ has the closed form eq:hist-semiconjugacy-factor-formula once both fields are evaluated. Whether an orthogonal clock exists depends on dα = n⁻² dn ∧ dF, which needs Hessian-vector products and can be tested pointwise. The Hermite spectrum gives an exact test oracle for a score-pushforward implementation: at N(0,1), the normalized-sum channel must return the eigenvalue b^{1−k/2} on He_k. A Gauss–Hermite quadrature reproduces this to 4e-16, so a unit test of any conditional-expectation estimator can be checked against it.
