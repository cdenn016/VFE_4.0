## 2. Findings

### L2-1: "Held links are the zero-width limit of the latent model" holds only for held links in the exponential chart
**Location:** `c08b_lie_links.tex:21-23` (chapter introduction), `:1150-1156` and `:1207-1210` (prose around `thm:link-held-limit`); `c01_program_scope.tex:37-40` and `:117`; `c14_interpretation.tex:120`. The theorem itself is `thm:link-held-limit` (`c08b:1158-1203`), and the chart gap is `prop:link-chart-image` (`c08b:220-238`).
**Severity:** medium
**Evidence:** The theorem fixes an anchor alpha in g and takes the limit at the anchor links Theta_e(alpha) = U_i exp(alpha_e) U_j^{-1} (`eq:link-held-limit`), so it reaches only held link families whose twists lie in exp(g). For G = GL(K,R) or GL+(K) and K ≥ 2, `prop:link-chart-image`(b) shows that exp(gl(K)) is a proper subset of GL+(K), and for GL(K,R) it also misses the whole negative-determinant component. The gap is open, so it is not reachable as a limit of anchors either. A real 2x2 matrix with two distinct negative eigenvalues keeps two simple negative eigenvalues under small perturbation, so it has no real logarithm (Culver 1966, Theorem 1) and lies outside the closure of exp(gl(2)). `check_chart_gap.py` finds that 0.207 of Gaussian 2x2 matrices with det > 0 fall in this open set, and `check_c02_c08b.py` reports imag(logm(diag(-1,-2))) = pi. The gap cannot be removed by a choice of frames or reference point. Twists are unchanged by passive re-expression and conjugated by reference and active changes (`prop:geo-edge-twist-invariance`), and exp(g) is conjugation invariant (`prop:link-chart-image`(a)), so a twist outside the chart stays outside under every gauge change. Chapter 1 nevertheless states without restriction that "a result stated at held link values is a statement about the conditional laws given the generators ... and held links are the zero-width limit of the latent model". The held-link theory of the other chapters (for example Supplement G with Gamma_b = GL+(K)) admits arbitrary GL+(K) links. The conditional-transfer statement `prop:link-conditional-transfer` is itself correct, because it is stated at Theta(omega), but read as a claim about every held link it inherits the same restriction.
**Fix:** Restrict the prose in all four places to "held links whose twists lie in exp(g), which is every link family when exp is onto, for example SO(K) or a compact connected G". The alternative is to widen the chart with a held base twist, Theta_e = U_i A_e exp(omega_e) U_j^{-1} with A_e in G in the structural data and A_e -> g0^{-1} A_e g0 under a reference change. Every held link is then the anchor alpha = 0, and the theorem and its proof carry over unchanged because the proof uses only the Gaussian base law around alpha.
**Falsifies:** The finding is wrong if the theorem's statement or another result in the manuscript already reaches every held link family of GL+(K), for example through a multi-chart or base-twist construction I did not find, or if every cited sentence is already qualified to chart links somewhere in its context.
**Status:** `thm:link-held-limit` is ESTABLISHED and is correct as stated, at anchor links. The inflation is in the untagged summary prose of `c01`, `c08b`, and `c14`.

### L2-2: The Hodge-decomposition gloss of `cor:link-cycle-splitting` does not match the decomposition proved
**Location:** `c08b_lie_links.tex:1964-1969` (remark after `cor:link-cycle-splitting`), with the definition of T_har at `:1899-1906` and the decomposition at `:1923-1931`.
**Severity:** low
**Evidence:** The corollary proves T = T_ex (+) T_har (+) Sigma im C_+^T, where T_ex is orthogonal to T_har in the product (Euclidean times <.,.>_G) inner product and the third summand is orthogonal to the first two in <., Sigma^{-1} .>. All of this I verified (`check_c08b_history_hodge.py`: rank 6 of 6, Sigma^{-1}-orthogonality residuals 2.8e-16 and 3.9e-16, dimensions 3 and 2 as stated). The remark then calls this "the Hodge decomposition of its one-cochains ... [HansenGhrist2019, Theorem 3.1] ... Sigma im C_+^T its circulating part in the metric of the prior". A Hodge decomposition is orthogonal in one inner product. In the prior metric <., Sigma^{-1} .>, the harmonic space is ker C_+ intersected with ker(d^T Sigma^{-1}), which is not T_har = (Z intersected with {z_p}^perp) (x) g unless Sigma preserves Z (x) g (for example, Sigma is a common scalar on every edge). The check uses K_4 with one declared triangle and per-edge prior variances drawn from [0.3, 3]. The largest entry of T_ex^T Sigma^{-1} T_har is 0.518, which should be 0 for a Hodge decomposition, and the principal-angle cosines between T_har and the Sigma^{-1}-Hodge harmonic space are 0.979 and 0.913, so the two subspaces differ. In the product metric the coexact part would be im C_+^T, not Sigma im C_+^T. So the triple is orthogonal in neither metric. I could not open the Hansen and Ghrist PDF to quote Theorem 3.1 (it is password protected), so the locator is unchecked. The mismatch does not depend on that check.
**Fix:** Either define T_har^Sigma = ker C_+ intersected with Sigma(Z (x) g), the Sigma^{-1}-orthogonal complement of T_ex in ker C_+ (same dimension, and (b) still holds because Sigma im C_+^T is Sigma^{-1}-orthogonal to all of ker C_+), so that the triple is the Sigma^{-1}-Hodge decomposition, or replace "is the Hodge decomposition" with "is a direct-sum decomposition that coincides with the Hodge decomposition of <., Sigma^{-1} .> when Sigma is a scalar on every edge".
**Falsifies:** The finding is retracted if T_har is meant with Sigma-weighted orthogonality elsewhere in the text, or if Sigma is restricted to a common scalar in this corollary. In either case the printed check residual 0.518 would be 0.
**Status:** The corollary is ESTABLISHED and correct. The remark is untagged and the inflation is interpretive.

### L2-3: The "temporal gauge" label names a gauge-invariant restriction
**Location:** `c08b_lie_links.tex:1664-1669` (`prop:link-history-prior`(c)) and `:1871-1879` (lattice reading of the linearized operator).
**Severity:** low
**Evidence:** In this framework, the twist U_i^{-1} Theta U_i of the identity time-like link is invariant under every passive re-expression (`prop:geo-edge-twist-invariance`; numerically 2.0e-14 under random non-orthogonal g_i). Declaring it e_G ("frames are static ... joined by the identity link") is therefore a restriction on the physics, a frozen time-like link, and not a choice of gauge. In lattice gauge theory the temporal gauge U_0 = 1 is reached from arbitrary time-like links by a gauge transformation. Its residual content, Gauss's law as a constraint on physical states, comes from the time-like link variables that are gauged away. Here no time-like variable exists, so no Gauss constraint arises, and the linearized operator acts on spatial components only, as the text concedes at `:1878-1879`. The algebraic statement, that a time-like plaquette with identity time-like links couples two space-like links at consecutive times, is correct. My check of the gradients (`eq:link-timelike-gradients`) gives FD residuals 1.4e-9 and 3.0e-11.
**Fix:** Write "has the configuration of the temporal gauge. Because twists are gauge invariant, the identity time-like twist is a declared restriction (frozen time-like links), not a gauge choice, and no Gauss-law constraint arises".
**Falsifies:** The finding is retracted if the framework lets time-like links carry a twist that a passive re-expression can remove, which would contradict `prop:geo-edge-twist-invariance`, or if a Gauss-type constraint is derived elsewhere for the history prior.
**Status:** `prop:link-history-prior` is ESTABLISHED and its mathematics is correct. The analogy sentence is interpretive.

### L2-4: The Chapter 2 summary contrasts with pure lattice gauge theory, while the frames make this a gauge-matter system; "rotation" is used for non-isometric GL actions
**Location:** `c02_geometry.tex:562-567` (Physicist summary: "the only active symmetry is one global rotation", "whereas in lattice gauge theory only closed-loop products are invariant"); `c08b_lie_links.tex:1979-1981` ("a change of reference point rotates all of them by one adjoint map").
**Severity:** low
**Evidence:** The per-edge invariant class [U_i^{-1} Theta_e U_j] is a link dressed by a site field U_i that transforms as U_i -> g_i U_i (`eq:geo-regime-one-gauge-law`). This is the structure of the composite link phi_i^{-1} U_ij phi_j in a lattice gauge-Higgs model with a full-rank site field, which is gauge invariant. The exponential chart at sigma is then the unitary gauge of that system: the frame field is used up to fix the vertex gauge completely, so generators carry no vertex gauge (`prop:link-generator-gauge`(a)), and the linearized link operator acquires a bare mass and a Proca form (`c08b:1876-1879`), as a vector field does in unitary gauge. The contrast "in lattice gauge theory only closed-loop products are invariant" holds for pure gauge theory and omits this. Separately, for noncompact G, Ad_{g0} is not an isometry of any inner product on gl(K) for all g0 (Milnor 1976, Lemma 7.5, applied to GL+(K)), and the text itself shows that the loop defect is invariant only under multiples of isometries. "Rotation" therefore misdescribes a general GL+(K) reference change. The check gives a defect deviation of 18.8 under a non-isometric reference change with the metric held, and 1.1e-15 for 2.7 times a G-isometry.
**Fix:** In `c02`, write "whereas in pure lattice gauge theory only closed-loop products are invariant; the frames act as a site field, and dressed links are invariant as in gauge-Higgs models". Suggested citations are Osterwalder and Seiler 1978 (Ann. Phys. 110, 440) and Fradkin and Shenker 1979 (Phys. Rev. D 19, 3682); their locators are unchecked. Replace "rotation" or "rotates" with "transformation" or "acts by Ad" in both summaries.
**Falsifies:** The finding is retracted if the summaries are scoped to compact G, or to pure gauge theory, by an explicit qualifier I missed.
**Status:** Untagged summaries; interpretive wording only.

### L2-5: The ledger paraphrase of the Culver criterion drops the per-block-size quantifier
**Location:** `MultiAgentELBO/docs/reviews/verified-ledger.md:399` (row "diag(-1,-2) has no real logarithm (Culver)"). This row is in the ledger, not in the vault manuscript.
**Severity:** low
**Evidence:** The ledger glosses the criterion as "odd number of Jordan blocks per negative eigenvalue". The Culver condition is that, for each negative eigenvalue and each block size, the number of blocks of that size is even. An even total count per eigenvalue is not sufficient: J_1(-1) (+) J_2(-1) has two blocks and no real logarithm. The statements in the manuscript (`prop:link-real-logarithm`, `c08b:215-218`; `ca_proofs_c08b:463`; `g11:350`) use the Culver wording, "each Jordan block belonging to a negative eigenvalue occurs an even number of times", which carries the per-block quantifier and is correct. The ledger verdict (no logarithm for diag(-1,-2)) is also correct.
**Fix:** Amend the ledger gloss to "for every negative eigenvalue and every block size, the number of blocks of that size is even".
**Falsifies:** The finding is retracted if the ledger row elsewhere states the per-size condition.
**Status:** Ledger record; the manuscript is unaffected.

## 4. Implementation notes for VFE_4.0

Every computation can be done in the reference gauge. By `prop:link-generator-gauge`(a), generators do not see the frames. An implementation can push all matter data into reference coordinates once, with g_i = U_i^{-1}. Each Gaussian log-determinant then shifts by the link-independent constant 2 sum_i log|det U_i| (`g11:63-72`). From that point it never needs U_i again, except to report links in agent frames as Theta_e = U_i exp(omega_e) U_j^{-1}. The per-edge state is one generator in g (K^2 real numbers for gl(K)), stored only for e in E_+, with omega of the reversal equal to -omega_e. A change of reference point is the linear map Ad_{g0^{-1}} on all generators together. The anchors, prior covariances, G^fib, and the inner product must be transported with it (`prop:link-generator-prior`(c)), and they cannot be held fixed unless g0 acts by a multiple of a G^fib-isometry. For GL+(K) the prior therefore fixes the reference gauge up to that compact residual group, and a reference change is a model change unless all of these data move together.

The following objects have closed forms. The Gaussian base law and KL(R || nu) (`eq:link-gaussian-kl`). The link sector up to log Z_S, which is constant in the recognition parameters and can be dropped in the E-step. The law-level Gaussian divergence and its gradient (`eq:glink-law-level-energy`, `eq:glink-law-level-gradient`). Record currents (`eq:link-delta-current`). The Frechet derivative L(B,T), which is best computed exactly as the upper-right block of exp([[B, T], [0, B]]), as in `check_c08b_history_hodge.py` and `check_g11.py`, rather than by quadrature. The time-like defect gradients. The linearized operator K, and on a ring its spectrum (`prop:glink-linear-spectrum`). The induced action and its gradient: log det Lambda(omega) and Lambda^{-1}, with dLambda computed through L. The following objects need sampling or approximation. The expected loop action E_R Q_p: even with product recognition and walks without repeated pairs, `eq:glink-expected-defect` needs E[e^omega] and E[e^omega (x) e^omega] for Gaussian omega in gl(K), which have no closed form for noncommuting matrices, so Monte Carlo or the small-variance expansion `eq:glink-defect-first-order` is required. The normalizer Z_S and the prior moments E_P[omega] that the exact anchor M-step needs (`eq:link-anchor-gradient`). These are not closed form once loop terms are present, except for the quadratic, abelian action of `eq:link-anchor-quadratic`. Self-normalized importance sampling with nu as the proposal and weights e^{-S} is a valid choice, since Z_S >= exp(-E_nu S) bounds the weight degeneracy, but the theory does not specify an estimator.

The theory leaves several choices to the implementer. First, each walk in the declared loop family needs a base vertex. The defect depends on it (0.159 between cyclic rotations of one random K = 3 triangle in `check_c02_c08b.py`), and symmetrizing over rotations is not part of the theory. Second, the inner product on g must be declared when both channels share one generator family, because each channel has its own pullback. Third, the BLR precision update (`eq:link-blr`) uses E_R of the Hessian of the generator integrand, which can be indefinite through N_0, chart folds, and record terms. The theory guarantees a positive-definite update only when the projected expected Hessian is positive semidefinite, so damping or a Gauss-Newton surrogate is a choice the implementer must make and record. Fourth, the theory supplies no estimator for that expected Hessian; the Price identity in Stein form (E Hess h = Sigma^{-1} E[(omega - mu) grad h^T]) avoids the second derivative of the matrix exponential.

Numerical hazards. The generator chart has folds where two eigenvalues of omega_e differ by a nonzero multiple of 2 pi i. There L(omega, .) is singular, gradient flow in generator coordinates can stop at non-stationary links (`cor:link-prior-necessary`(a)(iii)), and the stopping point can be a saddle (`prop:glink-sample-record`(iv)). Monitor the imaginary spectral gaps of each omega_e. Do not re-chart silently to the principal logarithm: the prior lives on generators, so moving to another branch changes the prior mass, and with it the model. Because exp is not injective, the exact generator posterior can be multimodal across branches. A single Gaussian R then captures one branch, and the generator-space bound is looser than a link-space bound by the conditional divergence on the fibers of exp. For undirected matter with latent links, enforce A_i positive definite. Without self terms, an unanchored cycle has no joint law (`cor:glink-unanchored-cycle`), and the K >= 2 multi-cycle case is open. For the history prior, sample or smooth with the Gibbs-conditioned kernels of `eq:link-history-kernels`. The per-step locally normalized chain is a different law (`prop:link-history-prior`(b)). The backward functions are not closed form unless the action is quadratic and the kinetic term is the Gaussian increment, in which case the history is linear Gaussian and a Kalman smoother computes it exactly. Finally, if VFE_4.0 must reproduce held-link results for GL+(K) links outside exp(gl(K)), the base-twist extension in L2-1 is required, and the plain chart cannot represent those links at any width.
