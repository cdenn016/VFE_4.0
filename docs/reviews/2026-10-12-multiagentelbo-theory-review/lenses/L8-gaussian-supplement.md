## Findings

### L8-1: Factor-of-two convention for cut weights differs between G7 and the cited main-manuscript cut lemma
**Location:** `g07_coarse_maps.tex:39-44` (declares that every quadratic energy carries the factor one half and that the no-new-anchor family is `def:cg-no-new-anchor-pair`); `c10_network_state.tex:836-855` (cut energy written without the factor one half, with `Lambda_II = sum_e W_e`, `Lambda_IJ = sum_e W_e Theta_e`); `c10_network_state.tex:981` (weights `W_e = (1/2) w_e Sigma_e^{-1}`); `g10_reciprocal_pairs.tex:488-498` (restates the cut energy without the factor one half inside Supplement G).
**Severity:** low
**Evidence:** In G7 a pair term is `(1/2)(z_i - Theta z_j)^T W (z_i - Theta z_j)`, so `W` is the precision block. In C10 the cut energy is `sum_e (z_I - Theta_e z_J)^T W_e (z_I - Theta_e z_J)` with no one half, and C10:981 sets `W_e = (1/2) w_e Sigma_e^{-1}` so that this energy equals a weighted sum of mean KL terms. The symbols C10 calls `Lambda_II`, `Lambda_IJ`, `Lambda_JJ` are therefore one half of the corresponding precision blocks of a law proportional to `exp(-E_cut)`, while G7 and G9 use `Lambda` for the precision itself. No stated result is false: the separating criterion `Delta = 0` (`eq:cg-cut-excess`), the Loewner interval `eq:cg-cut-admissible-weights`, and the balanced weight `eq:cg-cut-balanced-weight` are all homogeneous of degree one in the weights, and I verified the lemma's identities with random nonorthogonal twists (excess identity 3.5e-12 at scale 8.0e2; completed square 2.9e-11 at scale 7.6e4; balanced equations 2.1e-13 and 1.1e-11; `check_cut_evidence.py`). An implementer who reads `Lambda_II` from C10 as the precision block that G7's Schur and Galerkin formulas consume will be off by a factor of two in every weight and self term.
**Fix:** Where G0, G7, and G10 cite `def:cg-no-new-anchor-pair` and `lem:cg-cut-completed-square`, add one sentence stating that C10 writes the cut energy without the factor one half, so its `W_e` and `Lambda` blocks are one half of the G7 weights and precision blocks, and write `g10:492` with the factor one half to match G7's declared convention.
**Falsifies:** A passage in C10 declaring the cut law to be proportional to `exp(-E_cut/2)`, or a G7/G10 statement that adopts the C10 scaling explicitly, would retract this.
**Status:** not a tagged statement; notation and convention only. Nothing is inflated.

### L8-2: The introduction attributes a measurability hypothesis to a proposition that uses positivity instead
**Location:** `g00_introduction.tex:51-53`; `g09_operator_coarsegraining.tex:201-225` (`prop:grg-positive-additive-linear`)
**Severity:** low
**Evidence:** G0 says "under a measurability hypothesis positive additive rules are forced to be linear". The proposition has no measurability hypothesis. Its proof excludes the non-measurable Hamel-basis solutions of Cauchy's equation with positivity: `f(y) - f(x) = f(y - x) >= 0` makes `f` monotone, and monotone additive functions on the half-line are linear. Positivity replaces measurability; the two hypotheses differ.
**Fix:** Replace "under a measurability hypothesis" in G0 by "under positivity (positive-semidefinite values), with no measurability assumption".
**Falsifies:** A version of `prop:grg-positive-additive-linear` that adds a measurability premise, or a G0 revision, would retract this.
**Status:** G0 is tagged NOT-CLAIMED, and the proposition's ESTABLISHED tag is correct (proof reconstructed). Only the summary sentence is wrong.

### L8-3: The Kron-regime table says the nonflat series class generates no self terms, but a retained loop carries a new PSD self term
**Location:** `g07_coarse_maps.tex:923` (row "Nonflat links, hidden vertices of degree two with zero anchor ... Generated self terms: none"); `thm:cg-nonflat-series-kron`, `g07:1722-1731`; `ga_decision_metatheory.tex:233-235`
**Severity:** low
**Evidence:** When a cycle meets the retained set in exactly one vertex, elimination leaves the marked self-loop `(Hol, W_path)`. Its quadratic `Q_loop = (I - Hol)^T W_path (I - Hol)` adds a PSD term to that vertex's diagonal block. I checked the retained-loop formula on a three-edge cycle with random nonorthogonal links: residual 7.1e-14 at scale 6.2e1 (`check_g07d.py`). In the exact example of `prop:cg-nonflat-series-example`, the two loops contribute `diag(0, 7/11)` and `diag(2/5, 0)` to the retained precision, beyond the anchor `I_2`. GA's own proof of `prop:cg-finite-pair-presentation-bound` says "A loop is the PSD anchor `pr_i^T (I - Theta_e)^T W_e (I - Theta_e) pr_i`. Absorb unmarked loops into anchors". So "none" holds only in the marked presentation. Read as unmarked, the table contradicts the G0 sentence that coarse families admit newly generated PSD self terms.
**Fix:** Change the cell to "no new anchors; a retained loop carries the marked PSD term `(I - Hol)^T W_path (I - Hol)`".
**Falsifies:** A convention, stated in G7, that marked loops are never counted as self terms in the table would make the cell correct as written.
**Status:** table entry; the underlying theorem (ESTABLISHED) is correct.

## Implementation notes for VFE_4.0

What is closed form. Every object in these chapters that VFE_4.0 is likely to implement first is closed form and costs O(n^3) dense linear algebra, with no sampling. Here n = N K, or less after blocking. The list: the information-form density and log normalizer `eq:gauss-lognorm`; the reframing action on information parameters, `Lambda' = g^{-T} Lambda g^{-1}`, `h' = g^{-T} h`, `mu' = g mu`, `Sigma' = g Sigma g^T` (G2 states the moment and precision laws; the information-vector law appears only in `eq:cg-nonflat-series-gauge-law` and `eq:cg-quotient-gauge-data`, and I verified it at cond(g) = 1e3); the assembled directed precision `eq:gauss-directed-blocks`; the transported Gaussian KL `eq:glink-law-level-energy` with its gradient `eq:glink-law-level-gradient`; the block-restricted recognition optimum `Sigma_b = Lambda_bb^{-1}` with mean `Lambda^{-1} h`, and its cost, the determinant gap; the mean-restriction cost; Galerkin aggregation `S^T Lambda S` with source `S^T h` and projector `P_Lambda`; Kron elimination with source and scalar offset `eq:cg-kron-complete-schur`; the induced sensor `eq:cg-kron-induced-sensor`; nonflat series fusion `(Theta, C) * (Theta', C') = (Theta Theta', C + Theta C' Theta^T)`; the exterior interface, a symmetric-definite generalized eigenproblem; the forward-KL barycenter, which is moment matching; and quotient Gaussians through the weighted pseudoinverse `eq:cg-quotient-metric-inverse`. The Haar-projected barycenter is closed form for a finite holonomy group, as an average over the group. For a continuous compact holonomy group, Supplement G gives no algorithm. The implementer must either integrate over Haar measure (quadrature or sampling) or project the raw moments onto the commutant of the group's representation. That second route is my suggestion, not the supplement's. The decision metatheory (GA) is not implementable at any useful scale: real quantifier elimination is doubly exponential, and GA disclaims any complexity bound. Its practical content is the exact closure tests it encodes, for example symmetry of a manufactured block, and these an implementation evaluates numerically.

What the theory leaves to the implementer. The supplement never displays the Gaussian evidence `log p(o | X)`. The determinant gap `eq:restrict-determinant-gap` is stated relative to it. In information form the identity is `log p(o) = h_o^T o - (1/2) o^T Lambda_oo o + A_n(h_Y - Lambda_Yo o, Lambda_YY) - A_{n+m}(h, Lambda)`. I derived it and checked it against the moment form to 3.6e-15 (`check_cut_evidence.py`). The ELBO of any Gaussian recognition law is then `log p(o) - KL(Q || p*)`, with the KL of `eq:restrict-gaussian-kl`. Several algorithmic choices are left open by declaration rather than by error, and each needs an explicit configuration key. First, the coarse information-vector rule under a rescaling `zeta`: `prop:grg-interior-law-step` gives `S^T h` and `zeta^{-1/2} S^T h`, and only the second is a pushforward for every `zeta`. Second, the partition: `open:cg-natural-selector` is open, and `prop:restrict-gap-no-selection` shows the determinant gap cannot select one. Third, the interface rank `r` for the exterior interface, and a tie rule when `d_ref,r = d_ref,r+1`: the range is determined by the pencil only when that gap is strict. Fourth, link learning inside GL+(K): `prop:gen-lg-m-step` shows the covariance-optimized link term can approach its supremum without attaining it, so a link prior or regularizer is required, consistent with `cor:link-prior-necessary`. Record-factor covariances likewise have no maximizer. Fifth, whether retained loops are stored as marked loops or folded into anchors (L8-3). Sixth, the factor-one-half convention when C10 cut formulas are reused (L8-1).

Numerical hazards, measured in float64 against 50- or 60-digit mpmath. (a) Evaluate the transported KL in the pullback form of `eq:glink-law-level-energy`: take a Cholesky factor of `Sigma_j`, apply triangular solves to `e^{-omega} L_i` and to `e^{-omega} mu_i - mu_j`, and use the exact log-volume term `Tr omega`. Never form `(Omega Sigma_j Omega^T)^{-1}`, and never take `det(expm(omega))`. With cond(Sigma) = 1e3 and generator entries of scale 3 (cond(Omega) = 7.5e3, cond(Omega Sigma_j Omega^T) = 2.1e10), the naive form has relative error 9.6e-7 and the pullback form 5.7e-13. At scale 2 the errors are 8.4e-12 and 2.4e-14 (`check_transport_numerics.py`). (b) The trace-minus-K form loses near-zero KL values to cancellation. At a true KL of about 2.4e-14 its relative error is 5.0e-3. The eigenvalue form `(1/2)[sum (lam - 1 - log lam) + |d|^2]`, with `lam` the eigenvalues of the whitened source covariance, gives 5.4e-9 at the same point. Use the eigenvalue form, or sums built on `log1p`, wherever attention weights depend on small KL differences. (c) Schur and Kron eliminations must factor the hidden block by Cholesky, not invert it explicitly. On an exactly closed congruence-diagonal star with cond(M) = 1.5e12, the inverse form has relative error 2.0e-9 and the Cholesky form 8.0e-11. The closure test of `thm:cg-congruence-diagonal-kron` and `thm:cg-independent-kron-classification` asks whether the manufactured block is symmetric; on blocks of size 1.4e12 it reads an antisymmetry of 3.0 in the inverse form and 9.0e-4 in the Cholesky form (`check_schur_numerics.py`). Every closure or no-new-anchor test, including `Delta = 0` in `lem:cg-cut-completed-square`, therefore needs a tolerance proportional to the block scale times cond(M) times machine epsilon. The theory supplies no tolerance, and GA says so. (d) Explicit-inverse formulas lose about log10(cond) digits. `lem:gauss-schur` (i) reproduces only to 4.7e-10 relative at cond 1e8, and at cond 1e10 the reverse KL in covariance form differs from the precision form by 3.7e-8 relative. Work in precision (information) form throughout, with Cholesky log-determinants `2 sum log diag(L)`. (e) Quotient Gaussians, pseudo-determinants, and `prop:cg-epsilon-identification-cost` all need a rank decision. The supplement treats rank as exact, and `prop:cg-rank-change-statistical-obstruction` shows that a rank change is discontinuous in total variation. A numerical rank threshold therefore changes the law, not just its accuracy, and it should be a declared parameter. (f) Compute the exterior interface by whitening with the Cholesky factor `L` of `Lambda` and taking a symmetric eigendecomposition of `L^{-1} Lambda_rec L^{-T}` (equivalently `scipy.linalg.eigh(Lambda_rec, Lambda)`). Then renormalize the generalized eigenvectors to be `Lambda`-orthonormal. The surrogate log-evidence ratio is unbounded below (`eq:cg-exterior-evidence`), so evaluate it in log space. (g) The matrix exponential of a link generator is well conditioned only at moderate norms: cond 2.5 at scale 0.3, 1.3e2 at scale 2, 7.5e3 at scale 3. Use scaling-and-squaring `expm`, keep `Tr omega` analytic, and expect gradients through `L(-omega^T, A_e)` to inherit the conditioning of `e^{-omega}`.
