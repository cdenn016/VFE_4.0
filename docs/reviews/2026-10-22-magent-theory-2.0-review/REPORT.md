Method. Each lens read its scope in full, reconstructed the load-bearing proof steps, recomputed identities with single-threaded sympy or numpy scripts (kept under `scripts/<lens id>/`), searched for counterexamples at small size, and classified each finding by the categories and severities of the brief. This report merges findings that describe the same defect and lists every source ID, ranks the critical and high findings, records every place where two lenses disagree, and collects the results the lenses checked without finding a defect. It does not adjudicate: no finding was dropped, none was judged correct or incorrect, and the synthesis adds no finding of its own. Where this report states a mathematical fact, it reports what the cited lens found and how.

Counts. By severity: 1 critical, 31 high, 120 medium, 89 low. By category: 52 extension, 51 weakness, 45 improvement, 28 gap, 24 citation, 22 consistency, 9 inflation, 8 exposition, 2 error. ## 2. Overall verdict

The panel's combined judgment is that MAgent Theory 2.0 is correct statement by statement but does not yet test its own thesis that interactions produce further agents. No lens found a false main result or a failed load-bearing proof step. The lenses reconstructed or recomputed several hundred labeled results (Section 8), and the only two findings classified as errors are a wording slip and a misnamed root. The weight of the findings is structural, and five lines of criticism recur across independent lenses. The dynamics minimizes a declared law-level potential with no normalized generative model, no evidence and no persistent record term, and the manuscript does not say at program level that it has set aside PIFB2's collective variational free energy (H1); X3 proposes a configuration-level model that would restore an exact identification (C1). The formation definition admits trivial, non-interacting and supplied-scale instances (H2), and by the lenses' controls both formation constructions read their membership from prepared initial data: the chapter 11 schedule is exactly independent of the coupling (H3), and the chapter 12 membership is reproduced numerically with every peer force deleted in the proof's own regime (H4). The exact inheritance results hold by construction for every partition, and the compressions discard information that no agent, channel or energy term reads, namely gauge phases in chapter 10 (H7), an orientation resolved only by an external querier in chapter 12 (H5), an inert allocation in chapter 11 and a decoupled factor in chapter 8; the inherited relations are transient and the contextual geometry is prepared and decays (theme T5). The principal bundle, the connection and the per-agent gauge group do no dynamical work after chapter 2 (H6), and several regularity hypotheses (the measurable structure, general-space well-posedness of the Fisher flow, uniqueness of the coarse flows) are used without being stated. Much of the general apparatus re-derives named classical mathematics without attribution, and VFE_4.0 realizes a different objective from the one 2.0 studies (H9), on a Gaussian family that the 2.0 flow does not preserve (H8). Against this, the lenses report a substantial set of low-cost repairs and extensions that would make the general chapters do inferential work: a strengthened, falsifiable formation standard with null controls; classification theorems for exact inheritance (canonical parents, intertwining handoffs, symmetry and convolution schemes); one Banach-chart well-posedness proposition; routes to interaction-necessary and deeper formation; and ways to give the connection and the link loops an observable role (Section 6). None of these findings has been adjudicated.

### C1. Registered-agreement theorem: the 2.0 potential is T times an exact collective variational free energy, with T = 1/n

**Sources:** X3-PIFB-02 (critical, extension); L03-DYN-06 (medium, extension: the frozen-source case of the same type-class identity). Related: L03-DYN-07, X4-PRIOR-07, X3-PIFB-10 (alternative restorations, see R1).

**Location:** chapters/03_dynamics.tex:35-89 (def:dyn-potential, prop:dyn-gibbs, eq:dyn-reduced), :151-184 (sec:dyn-likelihood, row costs "need additional hypotheses" at :162); supplements/s03_variation.tex:33-64; PIFB2 36_section.tex (thm:belief_configuration_gibbs_lift), 07_section.tex:68-92.

**Finding.** X3 constructs, on finite alphabets with T_q = T_s = T = 1/n, a normalized population model: a latent configuration X uniform on a finite lattice of n-type configurations; source labels J ~ π; batches of n messages drawn from each reference R_i and from the compared laws ν_ij(X) (law-dependent channels allowed); and binary registration records that fire when the received type equals the receiver's own law, all observed as registered. By the method of types (Cover and Thomas 2006, Theorems 11.1.2 and 11.1.3), the registration probability given source j is |T_{q_i}| exp(−n[H(q_i) + D_ij(x)]); the posterior over source labels equals the Gibbs row of eq:dyn-gibbs because the factor |T_{q_i}| e^{−nH(q_i)} cancels; for point recognition at x, T times the free energy equals E(x, β, γ) + C_n(x) + T log|X_n| with receiver-only C_n in [0, |supp| log(n+1)/n]; and minimizing rows gives E_red(x) = −T log P_n(o | x) − C_n(x), a collective evidence bound. Residuals are at most 4.4e-16 at n = 4 to 7 on ternary alphabets (X3 s1). A general standard Borel version is sketched through Sanov's theorem in the τ-topology (Csiszár 1984; Dembo and Zeitouni, Thm 6.2.10; not re-opened). L03-DYN-06 obtains the same identity at frozen sources: at T = 1/N the Gibbs row is the exact source-label posterior given an N-sample sequence of type q_i (error 1.1e-16), and the row block equals T KL(β ⊗ q^N ‖ π ⊗ ν_J^N) (residual 0.0). The lenses state the consequences as: the row temperature is the reciprocal message count, Gibbs rows are exact posteriors of which source was heard, the incoming force is a likelihood gradient in a source's emission law, and the identification is at configuration level, with laws as emission parameters, not as recognition marginals of a sample posterior (which X3-PIFB-01 proves impossible, see H1).

**Proposed fix.** State parts (i) to (iv) as a theorem in chapter 3 for finite alphabets, with the restrictions X3 lists (equal row temperatures and a common n, otherwise a weighted composite likelihood or power posterior; a uniform configuration prior on the lattice, otherwise −T log ρ(x) is added; exactness only on the type lattice), and the general-space limit as a remark. Add L03-DYN-06 as the frozen-source corollary, with the tempered case as a power likelihood. Difficulty as estimated by the lenses: low for the finite theorem, medium for the general-space double limit.

### H1. No normalized generative model, no evidence and no persistent record term; the departure from PIFB2's collective free energy is unstated

**Sources:** L03-DYN-01 (high, weakness); X3-PIFB-01 (high, weakness); X5-IMPL-03 (high, weakness). Related: I1 (L01-FOUND-01), L03-DYN-11 and X4-PRIOR-10 (the "inference" title), C1.

**Location:** chapters/03_dynamics.tex:4, :35-49 (def:dyn-potential), :151-184 (eq:dyn-fixed-vfe, eq:dyn-affine-obstruction); appendices/record_process.tex:33-55; chapters/07_smooth_closure.tex:206-219 (smooth:feedback); chapters/10_boundary_closure.tex:144-153; frontmatter.tex:5; chapters/06_outlook.tex.

**Finding.** The potential is declared "a constitutive choice at the level of agent laws" and contains no record; by L03's grep, "evidence" and "ELBO" occur nowhere in 2.0. **Proposed fix.** L03: add a subsection to chapter 3 that inventories these losses, defines the agent process with records as a piecewise-deterministic process (Davis 1984) that is not monotone across records, and either adopts a restoration or states that evidence-based identification is given up. X3: add (a) and (b) as a proposition replacing the informal last paragraph of Section 3.4, and add one abstract sentence and one outlook paragraph. ## 4. Cross-lens themes

### T1. Recurring weaknesses in the foundations

The foundational chapters define more structure than the later chapters use, and leave unstated some of the regularity that the later chapters need. On the structural side, L01-FOUND-03 and X1-GAUGE-05 independently find that def:agent-section makes every agent a section of one associated bundle with one structure group, whereas chapter 2 and later chapters use agent-indexed fibers, independent per-agent and per-sector frame changes, agent-dependent sample spaces and, in chapters 11 and 12, a group that ties the belief and model sectors; the manuscript never declares which structure it uses. L01-FOUND-07 and X1-GAUGE-01 find that per-agent covariance holds automatically (dressing), L01-FOUND-08 that the exponential-link layer reaches a dynamical theorem only in a scalar commuting case, L02-GEOM-10 that the base-connection appendix records the transformation law of a supplied form rather than constructing it from links, and L02-GEOM-04 that for a transitive fiber the per-agent tensor records the connection rather than the section. L01-FOUND-12 and X5-IMPL-11 both observe that the general joint agent law is never evolved: every dynamical chapter uses product recognition, and the joint Fisher flow would generate dependence at unit rate, so the product dynamics is a projection that the text does not identify as such. On the regularity side, L01-FOUND-05 shows that the bundle's measurable structure needs a jointly Borel action (with a Hamel-function counterexample under the weaker reading), L01-FOUND-06 that configuration-dependent channels need joint measurability, L01-FOUND-13 that smoothness in a nonseparable Banach chart does not give measurability, and L01-FOUND-04 that record readouts carry no covariance requirement. L03-DYN-03, L07-BND-04 and G2-01 to G2-03 find, for chapters 9, 10, 12 and 7, that the Fisher flow on general densities is used but defined and proved well-posed only on finite tables, that chapter 7's bounded-log hypothesis must be uniform in the public coordinate, and that chapter 7's closure needs uniqueness of the forced coarse flow. Several lenses supply the missing pieces at low cost: global existence on finite tables (L03-DYN-05, X5-IMPL-18), global bounds by maximum principles for bounded-kernel and identity channels (G2-04, L07-BND-15), uniqueness of the weak spectral equation (G2-05), Brezis well-posedness for fusion (L05-FUS-14, X6-LIT-07), and one measurability lemma together with one Banach-chart Fisher-flow proposition in chapter 3 (L01 extensions, L03-DYN-03, G2). Equal unit mobility is a constitutive choice in the text; L03-DYN-04 and X6-LIT-14 both note that it follows, up to one time scale, from requiring the kinetic metric to restrict a Markov-invariant metric on the joint law of all tables (Čencov), and L03-DYN-04 adds that it makes the row relaxation rate equal to the row temperature.

### T2. The objective and the place of records

The second theme is the one H1 records. 2.0 replaces a normalized generative model with a declared law-level potential and lets records act only by conditioning. The lenses agree on the facts and differ in the restorations they propose. X3 proposes the configuration-level registered-agreement model (C1), L03-DYN-06 its frozen-source special case, L03-DYN-07 a normalized law-point model (a Jeffreys-reference Gibbs prior at temperature τ whose zero-temperature MAP flow is the 2.0 flow plus record terms), X4-PRIOR-07 an exact identity between the 2.0 potential and the free energy of a normalized label model whose rows coincide with the Gibbs rows exactly when the Jensen gaps are source independent, I1 (L01-FOUND-01) a content law on fields with covariant record kernels, and X5-IMPL-03 an evidence-augmented potential. These routes give different meanings to an agent's law (an emission parameter, a latent state, a recognition marginal), and the lenses do not settle which the program needs. Two further structural facts about the single potential bear on the interpretation: one potential forces a reverse influence along every directed edge, the receiver's likelihood ratio pulled back to the source, and makes the agents play an identical-interest game, whereas the agent-wise alternative is not a gradient flow in any metric (L03-DYN-02); and the incoming force splits exactly into a receiver log-linear pool and a source arithmetic pool, the latter equal to PIFB2's optimal aggregate prior (X3-PIFB-05). L01-FOUND-11 gives an exact chain-rule accounting of how far any variational record process departs from the Bayesian one, a first quantitative bridge from records to evidence comparisons.

### T3. The scope of the formation results

The lenses that examined the formation constructions report the same pattern from different directions. Selection is read from prepared data: the chapter 11 schedule is a closed-form function of the initial shapes and independent of the coupling (H3), the chapter 12 membership in the proof regime is reproduced by a no-peer-force control (H4), and X4-PRIOR-01 classifies the chapter 6 and 11 constructions as supplied-scale sequences under the earlier R14 standard. Inheritance is built in: X2-FRAME-05 finds that each architecture (Wishart convolution, the H_I quotient, tree automorphisms, a flat gate, shield channels) gives exact inheritance for every partition at every time, so the selected partition is not distinguished by inheritance, and that the construction chapters cite 4 of the 61 numbered general results and re-prove their own commuting squares. Compression is symmetry reduction or projection: H7 for chapter 10, L09-OPEN-01 for chapter 12, L06-ERA-04 for chapter 8 (a decoupled autonomous factor and a finite label quotient), and L04-INH-11 proposes stating equivariant descent once as the general mechanism, while L04-INH-07 proves that in the program's gradient dynamics a coordinate projection is exact only for energetically isolated blocks. Persistence is transient: fusion synchronizes every datum in finite time (L05-FUS-01), the "persistent heterogeneous pairs" of chapter 8 converge to each other (L06-ERA-01), the chapter 10 contrast decays at rate 3 + 2/(N − 1) (L07-BND-02), chapter 11 converges to global consensus (L08-CND-06), and in chapter 12 every block relation fails eventually and the selector forces a final merge (L09-OPEN-03). The definition of formation is weak enough to be met by these constructions and by trivial ones (H2). The lenses also report positive evidence: X2-FRAME-09 and X3-PIFB-04 find interaction-necessary two-stage formation numerically in the chapter 12 limit field away from the proof regime, X2-FRAME-09 classifies the nonsmooth fusion construction as passing the weak interaction-necessity clause, and L06-ERA-09, L05-FUS-18 and L03-DYN-14 describe variants that would make heterogeneity or several parents persist (heterogeneous couplings, bounded-range contacts, bounded-confidence rows). ### T4. What the compressions discard

Across chapters 7 to 12 the lenses find that the discarded information is information that no agent, channel or energy term reads. In chapter 7 the private conditionals relax along uncoupled e-geodesics and reach the public flow only as precomputable open-loop forcing (L05-FUS-02, G2-01), the spectrum identifies conditionals only up to the symmetry group of the declared private reference and is injective for generic finite references (L05-FUS-03), and the full spectrum is not minimal for the public flow (L05-FUS-06). In chapter 11 the information-loss witness lies in a model-shape allocation that influences no other variable, and the belief allocation the selector reads is recoverable from the event record (L08-CND-03). In chapter 12 the discarded offsets are invisible to every agent and channel and are resolved only by an external response protocol whose resolution changes at each merge, and pre-merge records estimate them consistently (H5). ### T5. The contextual geometry is prepared and decays

Five lenses report that the rank-two, nonzero-curvature contextual geometry of chapters 11 and 12 is assigned by hand in the initial sections and transported pointwise by a context-independent flow (L08-CND-04, L09-OPEN-05, X1-GAUGE-04, X2-FRAME-06, X4-PRIOR-05). The curvature is present at t = 0 and at zero coupling (L08 computes F = −1.716 at κ = 0), the minimized tensor decays exponentially (rate 12 − 4√2 in chapter 11, e^{−4t} in chapter 12), and preservation under the retention follows from an isometry that holds for every partition. The lenses add three refinements. X1-GAUGE-04 finds that the curvature exists only because belief and model angles are declared to share one structure group: with the independent group that the constitutive data permit, the minimizing connection is flat and the tensor has rank one. L09-OPEN-08 and X4-PRIOR-05 find that in chapter 12 the curvature on the reference section converges to a nonzero limit, −2λ²κ/(κ² + λ²)², so the infinite-time obstruction is loss of rank, not loss of curvature, while L08-CND-04 finds that in chapter 11 the curvature limit is nonzero only when α_0 = η_0. L02-GEOM-03 shows that the collective connection is singular where isotropy jumps, which is where agents agree. Proposed repairs are anisotropic, context-dependent references that keep rank two and curvature at infinite time (L08-CND-12, with limiting F = −1.689), an evaluator that couples the belief and model angles so that the tie is derived (X1-GAUGE-04, L09-OPEN-16), and a context coupling through which the connection acts (E10).

## 6. Prioritized roadmap of improvements and extensions

The roadmap merges the extension and improvement sections of all seventeen reports with the explicit recommendations of X3, X4 and X5. Priority follows two rules that the lenses state or imply: decisions that change what later results mean come before results that depend on them, and statements that cost little and repair a reported defect come before research problems. Difficulty estimates are the lenses' own. Items are ordered by priority; the dependencies are stated at the end of each paragraph.

### R1. Decide the objective and state the generative status (C1, H1, X3-PIFB-01, X3-PIFB-02, L03-DYN-01, L03-DYN-06, L03-DYN-07, X4-PRIOR-07, X5-IMPL-03, L01-FOUND-01, L01-FOUND-11, X3 roadmap item 1)

The lenses propose to begin by stating, in chapter 3, the abstract and the outlook, that the law-level potential is not the free energy of a normalized sample-level population model, with X3's boundedness and mixed-derivative arguments as the proof, and then to adopt one of the available restorations. The configuration-level route (X3-PIFB-02) builds a normalized model from n-message batches and type-registration records; on the type lattice the potential is then T = 1/n times the collective free energy at a point recognition, up to receiver-only terms of order log n/n, the Gibbs rows are exact source posteriors, and the reduced potential is T times a negative log-evidence; its frozen-source special case is L03-DYN-06, and its general-space version is a Sanov double limit. The law-point route (L03-DYN-07) uses the Gibbs law e^{−E/τ} against the Jeffreys measure, which is normalizable on finite alphabets because E is nonnegative and the Jeffreys volume is finite; records then enter through a likelihood, MAP descent is the Fisher flow of E − τ log l, and Riemannian Langevin dynamics samples the posterior. The label-model identity (X4-PRIOR-07) states exactly when the Gibbs rows are recognition optima of a normalized label model. The content-law route (L01-FOUND-01) places a recognition law Q on fields and compares the law-level potential with F(Q; o). X5-IMPL-03 asks, whichever route is chosen, for a persistent evidence term or a reference-learning rule, and L01-FOUND-11 supplies an exact accounting of the departure of any variational record process from the Bayesian one. Dependencies: none for the finite statements (method of types, prop:dyn-gibbs); the general-space versions need R3. Difficulty: low for finite alphabets, medium to high on general spaces.
