# G2: General-space well-posedness and uniqueness behind chapter 7's exact spectral closure

Read in full: `chapters/07_smooth_closure.tex` 1-247 (scope 7-186; 188-247 for context), `chapters/03_dynamics.tex` 1-185 (baseline thm:dyn-finite-flow at 119-149), `chapters/05_inheritance.tex` 55-115 (inh:fiber-test, inh:vector-square), `chapters/09_public_compression.tex` 20-65 (the bounded log-density chart used there), `companion/c08_smooth.tex` 1-66, the shared BRIEF, and the context findings L03-DYN-03 and L05-FUS-06, -08, -09, -12, -13. Skimmed: the chapter-7 lines of `README.md` (18) and `AUTHORING.md` (17, 25), `frontmatter.tex`, `Research/docs/research/2026-10-21-smooth-kl-formation.md` (lines 3, 27, 39, 45), and a grep of `D:\MultiAgentELBO_Theory` for a general-space Picard-Lindelöf result (none applicable). Verdict: nothing in 07:7-186 is false, and every result in scope holds on general standard Borel spaces once three hypotheses that the chapter does not state are added; under them I reconstructed local well-posedness of the closed system, the fine/retained correspondence, global well-posedness for two channel classes, and uniqueness of the weak spectral equation. As written, the general-space dynamical claims are conditional on the prose hypothesis at 07:14 ("unique regular solutions"), the bounded-log assumption at 07:91 constrains only the private sector and must be read uniformly in the public coordinate, and no public-sector regularity is specified anywhere. Answers to the four questions: (a) gap, closed by (H1)-(H3) of G2-01; (b) holds for the private part (the bound propagates and the interval qualifier is redundant) and is a gap for the public part, which I close globally for bounded-kernel and identity channels (G2-04); (c) gap: both claims need uniqueness of the forced coarse flow, which is available under (H2)-(H3) but supplied nowhere in 2.0 for non-finite public spaces (G2-02); (d) holds: the range is forward invariant, but the uniqueness proof must be added (G2-05).

Scripts and saved outputs are in `docs/reviews/2026-10-22-magent-theory-2.0-review/scripts/G2/`: `g2_spectral.py` (parts A-E), `g2_public_flow.py` (parts 1, 1b, 2, 3, 4), `g2_identity_bound.py`, each with a `.out.txt`. All ran single-threaded under a 20 s timeout (the longest took about 5 s). Grids of 16 to 512 public points stand in for a non-finite public space; they illustrate the dimension-free estimates, and the general-space statements rest on the proofs given below, not on the numerics.

## Findings

### G2-01: The closed system's well-posedness on non-finite public spaces rests on public-sector hypotheses the chapter never states
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/07_smooth_closure.tex:14, :44, :58, :91, :93-113 (smooth:split-dynamics), :133-162 (smooth:spectrum-flow); baseline chapters/03_dynamics.tex:124-145 (thm:dyn-finite-flow)
**Evidence:** Question (a). The only analytic hypothesis attached to the formulas is 07:91, "assume these log densities are bounded on the time interval in question", and "these log densities" are the private ℓ^q_{i,z}, ℓ^s_{i,w} of smooth:conditional-entropies. The public force h_i = δE_c/δμ_i is, by eq:dyn-kl-differential and eq:dyn-channel-differential, the sum of (1) the evaluator term log(dμ_i/dρ_i)(z) − ∫σ_i(dw) log g_i^c(w,z); (2) receiver terms β_ij[log(dμ_i/dρ_i) − log(dν_ij/dρ_i)]; (3) source pullbacks −β_ki ∫(dμ_k/dν_ki)(z′) B_ki(z,dz′); and (4) channel-variation terms −β_kl ∫(dμ_k/dν_kl) d(μ_l δB_kl/δμ_i(z)). None of these is controlled by 07:91. Line 44 asks only for positive public densities, and 07:58's "with the regularity needed for the original variation" points back to chapter 3, whose only well-posedness statement (thm:dyn-finite-flow) assumes finite alphabets and C² channel entries. On a non-finite public space the vector field of the closed system is therefore not shown to exist in any Banach chart, and 07:14 assumes the conclusion ("on an interval where the fine and proposed retained equations have unique regular solutions").

The stated hypotheses do not imply it. On Z = (0,1] with Lebesgue ρ take B_ij[μ](z′,dz) = (1 + c(y_j)φ(z′)φ(z))ρ(dz), φ = cos 2πz, y_j = ∫g dμ_j with g(z) = log(1/z) ∈ L¹ \ L^∞, and c smooth with |c| ≤ 1/2. The channel is normalized, strictly positive, law dependent, Gateaux-smooth along bounded-log paths, and C^∞ on every finite discretization, and the private log densities may all be bounded. Its first variation adds β_ij (∂D_ij/∂y_j)(g(z) − y_j) to h_j, with ∂D_ij/∂y_j = −c′(y_j) x_j ∫(dμ_i/dν_ij)φ dρ and x_j = ∫φ dμ_j, which is generically nonzero. So h_j ∉ L^∞ at every state where that coefficient is nonzero. The equation then forces ∂_t u_j(0) = −(h_j − E_{ρ_j}h_j) ∉ L^∞, so no C¹ curve in the bounded-log chart solves the system from such data: the chart vector field is not defined there, and any solution in a weaker sense leaves the chart at once.

Reconstruction under added hypotheses (the positive answer to (a)):

Proposition A. Use the chart of 09:24-33 for every public law: u_i ∈ L^∞_0(ρ_i) = {u ∈ L^∞(ρ_i) : E_{ρ_i}u = 0}, μ_i = e^{u_i}ρ_i / E_{ρ_i}e^{u_i}, the same for σ_i against ϱ_i, and log-odds coordinates for interior rows. Assume
(H1) M := max_i ess sup_{(z,v)} |ℓ^0_{i,z}(v)| < ∞, the essential supremum taken with respect to ρ_i ⊗ A_i^q, and likewise in the model sector (see G2-03; for the public equation alone, ess sup_z KL(κ^0_{i,z} ‖ A_i^q) < ∞ suffices);
(H2) G_i^c(w,dz) = g_i^c(w,z)ρ_i(dz) and r_i^c = r_i ϱ_i, with log g_i^c ∈ L^∞(ϱ_i ⊗ ρ_i) and log r_i ∈ L^∞(ϱ_i);
(H3) the map from the chart to L^∞(ρ_i) that sends the state to h_i (the first variation of E_c with Fisher rows, or of E_c,red with Gibbs rows) is Lipschitz on bounded subsets, and likewise in the model sector. Sufficient: each B_ij[μ] has a kernel density b_ij[μ] with respect to ρ_i whose logarithm is bounded on bounded subsets, and μ ↦ b_ij[μ] is C¹ into L^∞(ρ_j ⊗ ρ_i), with derivative Lipschitz on bounded subsets and a jointly bounded first-variation kernel. Identity channels between agents sharing (Z,ρ) also satisfy (H3).
Then, for every initial state in the charts, the closed system (μ, σ, ξ, rows) has a unique maximal solution on [0,T*) in the following class: (μ,σ,rows) is C¹ into the chart; z ↦ ξ_{i,z}(t) is measurable, narrowly continuous in t, supported in [−M,M], and satisfies smooth:spectrum-weak. The fine Fisher system has a unique maximal C¹ solution in L^∞_0(ρ_i ⊗ A_i^q) on the same [0,T*). It is the lift q_i(t) = μ_i(t) ⊗ exp(aℓ^0_i − log Z_i(a)) A_i^q, with a = e^{−t}.

Proof. (1) The private part decouples. By G2-05 each ξ_{i,z} is the explicit solution, so H_i(z,t) = aΛ_z′(a) − Λ_z(a), where Λ_z = log Z_z. Since d/da[aΛ′ − Λ] = aΛ″ ≥ 0, H_i(z,t) is nonincreasing in t, and 0 ≤ H_i(z,t) ≤ H_i(z,0) ≤ M. (2) The forcing enters additively. In the centered chart, u̇_i = −(h_i + H_i − E_{ρ_i}(h_i + H_i)). Put K_i(z,t) = ∫_0^t H_i(z,s)ds. It is jointly measurable and satisfies |K_i(z,t) − K_i(z,s)| ≤ M|t − s|, so t ↦ K_i(·,t) is Lipschitz into L^∞. The substitution w_i = u_i + K_i − E_{ρ_i}K_i gives ẇ = F(t,w), with F continuous in t and Lipschitz in w on bounded sets: the normalizer and the centering u ↦ E_μ f are smooth on the chart, and (H2)-(H3) control h. The Banach-space Picard-Lindelöf theorem then gives a unique maximal solution with the blow-up alternative (for example Dieudonné, *Foundations of Modern Analysis*, Ch. X; I did not recheck the theorem numbers). The Fisher row block is the finite-dimensional row ODE of thm:dyn-finite-flow, with costs D_ij that are Lipschitz on bounded sets of the chart; Gibbs rows are smooth functions of the D_ij. (3) The lift is a fine solution. With φ_i = h_i(z) + ℓ_{i,z}(v), which is the fine first variation (checked below), ∂_t log q_i = ∂_t log μ_i + ∂_t ℓ_i = −(h_i + H_i − E_{μ_i}(h_i + H_i)) + (H_i − ℓ_i) = −(φ_i − E_{q_i}φ_i), because E_{q_i}φ_i = E_{μ_i}h_i + E_{μ_i}H_i. In the fine chart the marginal map lq ↦ log ∫e^{lq(·,v)}A(dv) is smooth from L^∞(ρ ⊗ A) to L^∞(ρ), so the fine field is Lipschitz on bounded sets and fine chart solutions are unique. Every fine solution projects onto a retained solution, and the lift of a retained solution is a fine solution, so the two maximal intervals coincide.

Numerical support (finite grids, illustrative only). A finite-difference check of the full fine first variation, built directly from the four-index receiver-reconstruction kernel K_ij((z′,v′),(z,v)) = b_ij[μ](z′,z)κ_{i,z}(v) with law-dependent b_ij, gives residual 2.2e-10. The fine Fisher flow on 32×24 tables against the lift of the retained flow gives 1.0e-11 (μ), 8.3e-12 (ℓ) and 1.0e-13 (Fisher rows) at T = 1.5 (`g2_public_flow.py` parts 1b and 2).
**Fix:** Insert (H1)-(H3) and Proposition A after 07:91, or better, as the general Fisher-flow proposition that L03-DYN-03 asks chapter 3 to supply, and cite it in chapter 7. Replace "with the regularity needed for the original variation" (07:58) by (H3), and strengthen 07:44 from "positive public densities" to "public densities with bounded logarithms".
**Falsifies:** A data set satisfying (H1)-(H3) whose chart vector field is not Lipschitz on bounded sets, or two distinct chart solutions of the closed system from one initial state.

### G2-02: The "autonomous" closure and the "same public trajectory" sentence need uniqueness of the forced coarse flow, which is assumed only in prose
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/07_smooth_closure.tex:14, :24 (proof of smooth:mean-force-criterion), :133-135 and :161 (smooth:spectrum-flow), :166-170 (smooth:equal-spectra), :178; chapters/05_inheritance.tex:73-82 (inh:vector-square)
**Evidence:** Question (c). The proof of smooth:spectrum-flow ends: "All private contributions ... are now functions of the retained spectra. The remaining scores and row equations depend only on retained public data, proving closure." This shows that the retained velocity is a function of the retained state. "Autonomous" in the sense the chapter needs, namely that the retained state determines its future as at 07:178 and in the inheritance framework, needs in addition uniqueness of the retained equation in a class that contains the projection of every fine solution under consideration. Two fine states with the same (μ, σ, ξ, rows) project to two solutions of μ̇_i = −μ_i[h_i + H_i(t) − E_{μ_i}(h_i + H_i)] with the same explicit H_i(t), and these coincide only if this H-forced coarse equation has unique solutions. In smooth:equal-spectra, the statement that the projected trajectory solves the E_c system needs no uniqueness. The sentence "Both have the same public trajectory in this architecture" (07:178), however, needs uniqueness of the unforced E_c flow from the common initial public state and rows.

The manuscript's own differentiable criterion, inh:vector-square, does not apply as written. It requires R to be continuously differentiable and the parent vector field to have unique local solutions. Here the retention q ↦ (ℓ_z)_#A takes values in kernels of probability laws on ℝ and is differentiable only weakly (as a map into the dual of C¹), and the parent law of motion for the spectra is the weak continuity equation smooth:spectrum-weak, not an ODE on a Banach manifold. The intertwining must therefore be proved directly; the explicit lift of G2-01 does this.

Availability of uniqueness. For finite public alphabets with C² channels, the proof of thm:dyn-finite-flow applies to the forced public system. The system is non-autonomous through H_i(t), which is smooth in t, so the theorem itself does not literally apply, but its Picard-Lindelöf argument does. The private spaces may be arbitrary standard Borel spaces, and this case covers the bit example at 07:172-178. For non-finite public spaces nothing in 2.0 supplies uniqueness; Proposition A of G2-01 supplies it under (H1)-(H3). The lift also shows that the two hypotheses at 07:14, fine uniqueness and retained uniqueness, are equivalent here, so only the public one needs to be assumed or proved.
**Fix:** State smooth:spectrum-flow as a semiflow intertwining. Under (H1)-(H3), R∘Φ_t = Φ^c_t∘R on [0,T*), where Φ^c_t acts on spectra by smooth:spectrum-solution and on (μ,σ,rows) by the H-forced public flow, and T* is the maximal chart time of that flow. Add "on the common maximal interval" to smooth:equal-spectra, and put the uniqueness hypothesis in the statements rather than only in 07:14. For smooth:mean-force-criterion, combine this with the invariance requirement of L05-FUS-13.
**Falsifies:** An argument for 07:178 on a non-finite public space that does not use uniqueness of the E_c flow (for instance a direct coupling of the two fine trajectories), or a result elsewhere in 2.0 that already supplies that uniqueness and that chapter 7 cites.

### G2-03: The bounded-log hypothesis at 07:91 must be uniform in the public coordinate
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/07_smooth_closure.tex:91; :14 ("square-integrable scores"); :104 (first line of smooth:split-flow)
**Evidence:** This bears on question (b), private part, and is a precondition for (a). "These log densities are bounded" can be read per public value z, and under that reading the condition does not justify "the exponential moments and derivatives used below". Exact example (`g2_spectral.py` part D): Z = (0,1] and V = [0,1] with Lebesgue ρ and A, p(z) = z/2, and κ_z with density (1 − p)/p on [0,p) and p/(1 − p) on [p,1]. Each ℓ_z is bounded (|ℓ_z| ≤ log((2 − z)/z)), and the private energy ∫H(z,0)ρ(dz) = 1.0000 is finite. For fixed a ∈ (1/2,1], the explicit solution gives H(z,t) = log(2/z) + o(1) as z → 0, uniformly for t ≤ t_0 < log 2. The table shows H(10^{−15}, 0.2) = 35.232 against log(2·10^{15}) = 35.232. Take two identical agents with identity public channels, each the other's only source, and a product public reference. At symmetric states the receiver forces vanish identically and the source pullback is the constant −1, so the public log density obeys ∂_t m = −(m + H − E_μ(m + H)) and m(z,t) = −∫_0^t e^{−(t−s)}H(z,s)ds + c(t) = −(1 − e^{−t}) log(2/z) + O(1) as z → 0, for t < log 2. The measured slopes are 0.0952 and 0.2592 at t = 0.1 and 0.3, against 1 − e^{−t} = 0.0952 and 0.2592. The public law stays positive, but its log density is unbounded below for every t > 0, so the bounded-log public chart is not invariant. With p(z) = exp(−z^{−2/3}) instead, H(z,0) ≈ z^{−2/3} is integrable but not square integrable. The public Fisher score h + H − E(h + H) is then not in L²(μ_0), and the "square-integrable scores" of 07:14 fail at t = 0 although every ℓ_z is bounded.

The sharp requirement for the decoupled public equation is that K(t) = ∫_0^t H(·,s)ds be continuous into L^∞ at t = 0. Boundedness of H(·,0) is sufficient but not necessary. The family with κ_z-mass 1/2 on a set of A-measure p has sup_z H(z,0) = ∞, yet the time integral tends to log 2 for every fixed t > 0 as p → 0 (part E: 0.6919 at log(1/p) = 640 and t = 0.1). Its K(t) is bounded, but it jumps at t = 0 in L^∞.
**Fix:** Replace 07:91 by "assume ess sup_{(z,v)} |ℓ^0_{i,z}(v)| < ∞ with respect to ρ_i ⊗ A_i^q, and similarly in the model sector." By G2-04 the bound then holds for all t ≥ 0, so "on the time interval in question" can be deleted. Note that for the public equation alone the weaker ess sup_z KL(κ^0_{i,z} ‖ A_i^q) < ∞ suffices, because H(z,t) is nonincreasing in t.
**Falsifies:** A reading of "bounded" at 07:91 that the text already makes uniform in z (it gives none), or an error in the explicit H(z,t) of the example.

### G2-04: The bounded-log property propagates: the private range is forward invariant, and public bounds are global for bounded-kernel and identity channels
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/07_smooth_closure.tex:91 ("on the time interval in question"), :140-147 (smooth:spectrum-solution), :110 and :242 (rows); chapters/03_dynamics.tex:131-144
**Evidence:** Question (b).

Private part (holds; proved). Let m_0 = ess inf ℓ^0_z ≤ 0 ≤ M_0 = ess sup ℓ^0_z; normalization forces these signs. For a ∈ (0,1], Hölder's inequality gives Z(a) = ∫(dκ/dA)^a dA ≤ 1, and e^{ar} ≥ e^{r}e^{−(1−a)M_0} for r ≤ M_0 gives Z(a) ≥ e^{−(1−a)M_0}. Hence ℓ_t = aℓ^0 − log Z(a) satisfies m_0 ≤ am_0 ≤ ℓ_t ≤ aM_0 + (1 − a)M_0 = M_0, and the width of the essential range is exactly a(M_0 − m_0). The same conclusion follows from a maximum principle for ∂_tℓ = H − ℓ, since H = E_κℓ lies in the current range. Numerically, 3000 random atomic spectra and three grid spectra show no excursion beyond [m_0, M_0], and the width residual is 7e-15 (`g2_spectral.py` part A). Consequences: the uniform bound of G2-03 at t = 0 holds for all t ≥ 0; 0 ≤ H ≤ M_0; and |∂_tH| = Var_{κ_t}(ℓ_t) ≤ a²(M_0 − m_0)²/4 (maximum ratio 1.000000 in part C, attained by two-atom spectra), so the forcing is Lipschitz in t into L^∞.

Public part, class (i): kernels with two-sided bounds. Suppose 0 < b_− ≤ b_ij[μ] ≤ b_+ for all states, the channel-variation term (4) of G2-01 has oscillation at most L_i in z, and (H2) holds. Rows are normalized, so the coefficient of log(dμ_i/dρ_i) in h_i is 1 + Σ_jβ_ij = 2 for Fisher and Gibbs rows alike, and h_i = 2 log(dμ_i/dρ_i) + R_i with osc R_i ≤ C_i := 2‖log g_i^c‖_∞ + log(b_+/b_−) + n_i b_+/b_− + L_i + M_0. Here n_i is the number of receivers attending to i; each ν_ij has density in [b_−, b_+], and each source pullback lies in [−b_+/b_−, 0]. Variation of constants in ∂_t log μ_i = −(2 log μ_i + R_i) + c(t) gives osc log(dμ_i/dρ_i)(t) ≤ e^{−2t} osc log(dμ_i/dρ_i)(0) + (1 − e^{−2t}) C_i/2. With the blow-up alternative this gives T* = ∞, and smooth:row-ratio keeps Fisher rows interior for all time because the D_ij stay bounded. Numerical illustration with a law-dependent channel on n = 16, 64 and 256 public grid points: the bound holds with margin at least 0.26, and its constants do not depend on n (`g2_public_flow.py` part 4).

Public part, class (ii): identity channels among agents sharing (Z,ρ), the class used in chapters 9, 10 and 12. The source term −β_ki dμ_k/dμ_i is now unbounded, but a max/min principle with energy-controlled centering closes. Let u(t) = max_i ess sup log(dμ_i/dρ) and l(t) = min_i ess inf log(dμ_i/dρ). For a C¹ curve m in L^∞, D⁺ ess sup m ≤ lim_{ε→0} ess sup_{{m > ess sup m − ε}} ∂_t m; to see this, expand m(t + h) = m(t) + h∂_tm + o(h) in L^∞ and split by the level set. At near-maximal points of a maximizing agent i, m_k ≤ u for every k and m_i ≥ u − ε, so h_i ≥ u − ‖log g^c‖_∞ − n_i − O(ε). Moreover, E_{μ_i}h_i ≤ KL(μ_i ⊗ σ_i ‖ R_i^c) + Σ_jβ_ijD_ij + ∫H_i dμ_i ≤ E(t) ≤ E(0), because these are disjoint nonnegative summands of the energy and E is nonincreasing. Hence D⁺u ≤ −u + ‖log g^c‖_∞ + max_i n_i + E(0). At near-minimal points the source term is at most 0 and E_{μ_i}h_i ≥ −n_i, so D₊l ≥ −l − (max_i n_i + M_0 + ‖log g^c‖_∞). Both u and l therefore stay bounded for all t ≥ 0, and T* = ∞. Numerical illustration with four agents, Fisher rows, and n = 32, 128 and 512: both bounds hold, with margins 0.039 and 0.033, and the energy decreases monotonically (`g2_identity_bound.py`).

Outside these classes, for example with law-dependent channels whose dependence is C^{1,1} but whose relative first variation is unbounded, or with kernels lacking a positive lower density, only the local statement of Proposition A is available.
**Fix:** Replace "on the time interval in question" by an initial uniform bound, and add a proposition containing the two global statements. In these classes the equal-spectra conclusion and the sentence at 07:178 then hold on [0,∞), and the proposition supplies the global windows that chapters 9, 10 and 12 currently establish case by case.
**Falsifies:** A trajectory in class (i) or (ii) whose public log-density oscillation exceeds the stated bound. None appeared on the grids tried; that does not prove the bound, which rests on the argument above.

### G2-05: The weak spectral equation is adequate as stated, but its uniqueness is not proved
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/07_smooth_closure.tex:134-140 (smooth:spectrum-weak), :154-160
**Evidence:** Question (d). The proof derives the weak equation from the fine conditional equation and shows that the explicit formula solves it. It never shows that the weak equation, read as the law of motion of the retained spectra, has no other solution. That is needed whenever the spectra are used as a state without reference to a fine law, which is what the closure claim does.

Reconstruction. Let W be the class of narrowly continuous curves t ↦ ξ_t of probability laws supported in I = [m_0, M_0] = conv supp ξ_0, with ∫e^r dξ_0 = 1, that satisfy smooth:spectrum-weak for all f ∈ C¹(I), where H[ξ] := ∫re^r ξ(dr). Then H(t) = H[ξ_t] is continuous, and ξ solves the linear continuity equation with velocity v(t,r) = H(t) − r, which is smooth and globally Lipschitz in r. Every f ∈ C¹_c(ℝ) restricts to a test function in this class, so the uniqueness theorem for continuity equations with Lipschitz velocity fields (Ambrosio, Gigli and Savaré, *Gradient Flows in Metric Spaces and in the Space of Probability Measures*, 2nd ed., Prop. 8.1.7 and 8.1.8; proposition numbers from memory, not rechecked) gives ξ_t = (X_t)_#ξ_0 with X_t(r) = e^{−t}r + c(t) and c(t) = ∫_0^t e^{−(t−s)}H(s)ds. Substituting X_t into H(t) gives the closed scalar equation c′ = −c + e^{c}[aZ′(a) + cZ(a)], c(0) = 0, a = e^{−t}, whose right side is smooth in c. Its unique solution is c = −log Z(a): indeed c′ = aZ′/Z, and the right side equals log Z + aZ′/Z − log Z. This is smooth:spectrum-solution. Numerically, the ODE solution matches −log Z(e^{−t}) to within 1.4e-12 on [0,5], and the weak form holds to 4e-9 for f = sin 3r and f = r³ (`g2_spectral.py` part B).

The "bounded range in use" moves with t, but by G2-04 it stays inside I and contracts by the factor a, so the fixed initial range is adequate for all t ≥ 0. Normalization is not an extra constraint: f = e^r gives Ṅ = H(N − 1) for N = ∫e^r dξ_t, so N ≡ 1. If the bounded-log hypothesis is dropped as L05-FUS-09 proposes, the test class should be C¹_c(ℝ) together with the moment condition ∫_0^T∫|r|(1 + e^r)dξ_t dt < ∞, and the same argument then applies.
**Fix:** After 07:140, state the solution class W ("narrowly continuous, supported in the initial range, which is forward invariant"), and add the three-line uniqueness argument to the proof.
**Falsifies:** A second curve in W that solves smooth:spectrum-weak from the same ξ_0.

### G2-06: "General-law" wording overstates what chapter 7 establishes on general spaces
**Category:** inflation
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/07_smooth_closure.tex:91 ("allows arbitrary dominated law shapes"); companion/c08_smooth.tex:44 ("applies to arbitrary dominated laws under the stated regularity conditions"); README.md:18 ("an exact general-law state completion"); AUTHORING.md:25
**Evidence:** The completion is general in the private conditionals: arbitrary within bounded log densities, and by L05-FUS-09 beyond them. **Fix:** Use wording such as "an exact state completion for arbitrary private conditionals with uniformly bounded log densities, under the public regularity (H2)-(H3)", and name those conditions in the companion.

## Extensions and improvements

The most useful single change is one Banach-chart proposition for the Fisher flow of eq:dyn-potential on standard Borel spaces, placed in chapter 3 as L03-DYN-03 recommends, stated with (H2)-(H3) and proved as in Proposition A without the spectral part: centered L^∞ log-density charts, local Lipschitz continuity of the first variation, Picard-Lindelöf with the blow-up alternative, and the dissipation identity dE/dt = −ΣVar. Chapter 7 would then add only the decoupled private flow, the additive forcing K(t), and the lift. Chapters 9, 10 and 12, which currently reprove local existence in their own charts (09:59, 10:100, 12:65), could cite it. The proof is short, and the only real choice is how (H3) is phrased for law-dependent channels; the relative-first-variation form used in G2-04 is the one that also yields global bounds.

The two global statements of G2-04 can be combined into a proposition: for channels with two-sided kernel bounds and bounded relative first variation, and for identity channels, the closed system exists for all t ≥ 0 with explicit time-uniform log-density bounds, and Fisher rows stay interior. This upgrades smooth:equal-spectra and 07:178 to statements on [0,∞) in those classes. Convergence as t → ∞ is a separate question: bounded sets of L^∞ are not compact, so LaSalle-type arguments need extra structure, for example a metric public space with smoothing kernels that make the log densities equicontinuous. I would estimate it as medium difficulty and did not attempt it.

The minimal private-side input to the public dynamics is the time integral K_i(z,t) = ∫_0^t H_i(z,s)ds, and only its z-dependence modulo constants matters. This connects with L05-FUS-06: the public flow is determined by the public laws together with the CGF differences Λ_{i,z} − Λ_{i,z_0} on (0,1]. A retained state built on these differences needs only that K_i be continuous into L^∞ (G2-03), which holds whenever ess sup_z KL(κ^0_{i,z} ‖ A_i) < ∞. Combined with L05-FUS-09, this would remove the bounded-log hypothesis from the whole public-closure statement, while keeping it, or a moment condition, for the spectral weak equation (G2-05).

Finally, the uniqueness class could be widened from chart solutions to the "dominated differentiable paths with square-integrable scores" of 07:14 by a weak-strong uniqueness argument: a Grönwall estimate on a relative entropy or a weighted L² distance between a chart solution and any regular solution from the same data. Whether the source pullbacks and channel-variation terms are Lipschitz in such a weaker norm is not clear to me, and I did not attempt it; without it, 07:14 should name the chart class explicitly.
