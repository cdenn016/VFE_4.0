### L09-OPEN-01: Formation and compression are dynamically decoupled; the handoff loss is created by the interface switch
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** high
**Location:** chapters/12_open_formation.tex:4, 263-303, 336-345, 414-420 (thm open:formation, prop open:parent-flow, eq open:response); frontmatter.tex:7; companion/c12_open_formation.tex:5, 137-143
**Evidence:** I reconstructed three facts from the chapter's own formulas. (i) The native rows depend only on the bit marginals (eq open:row; numerically, rows from the full $D_{ij}$ agree with rows from $\mathrm{KL}(\mu_i\Vert\mu_j)$ to 7.0e-16, script s4, T1). On the product family the bit equations are exactly the binary field and contain no angular data (s1 check [2], residual about 1e-16; s4 T2, bit means agree to 7e-9 h). For correlated laws the only angular input to the bit force is each agent's own $2\,\mathrm{KL}(\kappa_i(\cdot\mid z)\Vert\mathrm{Haar})$, which is invariant under the whole group $(S^1)^N$. Hence rows, the selector and all event times are functions on the $(S^1)^N$ quotient. (ii) The $\mathsf H_I$ quotient, the moving-representative equation (open:moving-flow), the cost equality (open:cost-equality) and the aggregate-response descent use only $(S^1)^N$ equivariance, never the selection; they hold verbatim for every partition $\mathcal P$ and every time $t_0\geq0$, including $t_0=0$. (iii) What ties compression to formation is the response rule (open:response): a query to a current singleton returns $(Z_i,\Theta_i)$, an individual absolute angle, while after a merge only $\sum_{i\in I}\Theta_i$ is admitted. Numerically (s4, T4) a $(+11,-11)$ grid-step rotation of agents 0,1 leaves rows equal to 3.4e-12 and the aggregate first moment equal to 1.7e-16, while the individual first moment changes by 0.61. So the distinction discarded at the handoff is exactly the one the interface stops measuring at the handoff. The chapter concedes that "the symmetry exists before the selected events" (line 4), but the theorem and abstract present orientation loss, inherited dynamics, histories and geometry as accompanying the native selection, whereas the selection contributes only the times at which the declared interface coarsens.
**Fix:** Add a proposition before thm open:formation: for every partition $\mathcal P$ and every $t_0$, the $\mathsf H_{\mathcal P}$ retention descends the flow, the aggregate-response histories, and the collective minimizing costs. Then restate the theorem as the conjunction it is: (a) native routing selects membership and times on an open law class; (b) at those times the declared interface coarsens from constituent to aggregate angles; (c) the $\mathsf H$ quotient compatible with the coarsened interface exists for all partitions. The abstract sentence "Fixed selective channels and anchored contextual profiles permit continuous physical orientation loss while preserving ..." should say that the loss is relative to an interface whose resolution is changed by the merge rule. A stronger result needs an architecture in which the compressible directions affect routing (see L09-OPEN-16).
**Falsifies:** Exhibit a dependence of rows or event times on $\mathsf H_I$-orbit coordinates, or an interface fixed independently of the current partition under which the stated loss and history descent both hold.

### L09-OPEN-02: "Loss" is only deterministic non-identifiability; carried pre-formation records estimate the discarded offsets consistently
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/12_open_formation.tex:341-345 (eq open:response and the paragraph after it), 415 (thm open:formation, "loses ... after retaining the event times and admitted finite records")
**Evidence:** Before a merge, each agent is a current singleton block, so (open:response) returns samples $\Theta_i$ from its angular marginal. "Queries are observational and do not reset the law state", "Prior records are carried forward", and policies may be adaptive. Each record carries rotation Fisher information $\mathcal I(\rho_t\kappa)>0$ about $\vartheta_i$, and on the product reference the phases are constant in time (eq open:angular-flow keeps phases fixed), so the post-merge discarded offsets $\vartheta_i-\vartheta_j$ equal the pre-merge ones. The circular sample mean of $n$ pre-merge records estimates them with error $O((n\mathcal I)^{-1/2})$. The chapter's argument ("all the densities are positive, leaving continuously many compatible configurations") establishes only that no finite record determines the offsets exactly; for each fixed $n$ the posterior is nondegenerate, but the information retained in the records grows without bound in $n$. Off the product reference the phases move (s4, T5: $\omega_0=1.1\times10^{-4}$), but deterministically and continuously, so the same estimate holds up to a drift computable from the retained state.
**Fix:** State loss quantitatively, for example as the conditional Fisher information about the $\mathsf H_I$ coordinates given the retained parent state and $n$ admitted records, and say that it is finite for every $n$ but grows linearly in $n$. Alternatively bound the number of pre-merge queries in the protocol, or discard records at merges and say so. Restricting singleton queries to bits from $t=0$ would make the angle never observable and the loss vacuous, which shows the tension is structural.
**Falsifies:** A proof that the admitted policies cannot make more than a bounded number of pre-merge singleton angle queries, or that the conditional Fisher information about the offsets stays bounded uniformly in the number of records.

### L09-OPEN-03: Persistence is provably transient and the fixed selector later merges the exterior parent
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/12_open_formation.tex:86-94 (def open:selector), 113-123 (lemma open:interior), 125-126 (prop open:routing), 422; chapters/06_outlook.tex:8
**Evidence:** Lemma open:interior gives $x,y\to0$ for every initial condition, hence $\beta_{ij}\to1/6$. For a first-stage pair the primitive contrast tends to $1/6-4/6=-1/2$; for the two core parents against the third, $B_{IJ}-B_{IK}\to2/6-2/6=0<\delta$. Once the partition is $\{I\cup J,K\}$, the exterior sum in (open:contrast) is empty, so the rule reduces to $B>1/10$ in both directions and sectors; the limits are $4/6$ and $2/6$, so every trajectory in the admitted class merges into one block at a finite time $T_3>L$. Integrations: limit field at the proof's reference ($A=4096$, $R=1.7546\times10^{7}$), merges at $t=0.589$ (three pairs), $8.213$ (core parents), $15.997$ (all six) (s2); exact binary field at $h=10^{-9}$ with independent perturbations of all twelve coordinates, $0.590/0.592/0.592$, $8.214$, $15.998$ (s3); moderate parameters $A=6,R=60$, final merge at $t\in[3.4,3.9]$ (s3, s4). The chapter says only that "finite windows do not imply ... permanent separation of the exterior parent", which understates a provable statement: the "nonempty exterior" exists only on $(t_2,T_3)$, and the two-block contrast test has no content.
**Fix:** Add a corollary: under def open:selector every admitted trajectory reaches the one-block partition at a finite time, and the last merge has an empty exterior. Either accept this as a third level (it discards one more relative orientation, since $\mathsf H_{I\cup J}\times\mathsf H_K\subsetneq\mathsf H_{\{1,\ldots,6\}}$), or declare that merges require a nonempty exterior. State in the outlook that no block relation in this architecture is forward invariant.
**Falsifies:** An admitted initial law for which some contrast in (open:contrast) holds for all $t\geq0$, which would contradict lemma open:interior.

### L09-OPEN-04: The open class is nonempty only by a nonconstructive argument at astronomically small temperature
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/12_open_formation.tex:127-231 (proof of prop open:routing), 253-259 (prop open:open-laws), 414-417
**Evidence:** The order of choices is $A\geq4096$, then $\varepsilon_b$ (not computed), then $R$ with $D_R=Re^{-L}-A-6$ large, then $h$ with $h|u_i|<1$ and $C^1$ closeness. Computing the proof's own quantities (s2): $t_A=6.93$, $S=1.427$, $L=8.358$; taking $D_R=12$ gives $R=1.75\times10^{7}$, so admissibility alone forces $h<5.7\times10^{-8}$, i.e. $T<3.3\times10^{-15}$. At the parent event the within-pair half-gap is about $9.3\times10^{-9}e^{-3\cdot1.28}\approx2\times10^{-10}$ in $u$ units (estimate from the measured $b(t_A)$ and the rate-3 contraction), i.e. bit-mean differences near $4\times10^{-19}$ at $h=10^{-9}$. The neighborhood radius in the density chart is of order $h$ times a margin times $e^{-CL}$ and is never quantified; bit-angle correlations of size $\varepsilon_c$ perturb the bit force at order $\varepsilon_c$, i.e. order $\varepsilon_c/h$ in the routing coordinates, so admissible correlations are at most of order $h$. Numerics show the mechanism is not confined to this regime: with $A=6$, $R=60$ the sequence holds for $T$ from $10^{-6}$ to $10^{-4}$ in the binary field (s3) and, in the discretized full model at $T=2.5\times10^{-5}$, for correlations up to $\varepsilon_c=2h$ (s4, T3). At $h=0.015$ the third pair forms after the core join (s3), so the event order is sensitive near the boundary of $(-1,1)$. These runs are evidence about qualitative behavior, not a proof.
**Fix:** Either give explicit constants ($\varepsilon_b$, $R$, $h$, neighborhood radius in a stated norm), or replace the asymptotic choice by a validated-numerics witness at moderate parameters (see L09-OPEN-15). State in the theorem that the witness temperature is not quantified.
**Falsifies:** Explicit constants showing that $T$ of order $10^{-4}$ or larger satisfies all strict windows with a quantified neighborhood radius.

### L09-OPEN-05: The nonzero curvature is supplied by the declared contextual family, not produced by formation
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/12_open_formation.tex:359-410 (eqs open:curvature, open:rank, open:equivariant-lift); frontmatter.tex:7
**Evidence:** The contexts $(\kappa,\chi)$ label the initial belief concentration and phase; each context carries an independent copy of the six-agent system, routing is read only at the anchor, and the flow acts on the family only through $\kappa\mapsto\rho_t\kappa$ (eq open:angular-flow). The connection $A=-a/(a+b)\,d\chi$ and the curvature are those of a von Mises rotation family against a context-independent model factor and are already nonzero at $t=0$. Preservation follows from the isometry (open:cost-equality), which holds for every partition at every time (L09-OPEN-01). I recomputed both formulas from the discretized flow (s5, G1): for blocks of size 2 and 4, $A_\chi$ agrees to 1e-6, $F$ agrees to 7e-6 (finite-difference error), and $h_{\chi\chi}$, $h_{\kappa\kappa}$ agree to the printed digits at $t=0,1,2,3$; with correlated laws through the lift, $\det h>0$ and $F\approx-0.284$ on $[0,3]$ (G2). The result is correct; its content is that an isometric retention preserves a designed curvature.
**Fix:** Present the geometry statement as "the anchored retention is isometric for all collective costs on any section, and the following declared section has $F\neq0$". To make geometry interact with formation, use a context base on which the channels or rows depend (for example contexts entering the evaluator), so that the selected partition changes the minimizing connection.
**Falsifies:** A context family on which the formation events change the connection class or the curvature.

### L09-OPEN-08: The infinite-time statement conflates decaying inertia with curvature
**Category:** consistency
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/06_outlook.tex:8 ("finite persistence and curvature do not imply ... a uniform infinite-time bound"); chapters/12_open_formation.tex:388, 422; counterexample-register.md R11
**Evidence:** On the reference section $F_t=-b_t\rho_t\mathcal I'(\rho_t\kappa)/(a_t+b_t)^2$ with $\mathcal I(v)\sim v^2/2$ and $\mathcal I'(v)=v(1-(I_1/I_0)^2)\sim v$, so $F_t\to-2\lambda^2\kappa/(\kappa^2+\lambda^2)^2\neq0$. Since $F_t$ is continuous, nonvanishing and has a nonzero limit, $\inf_{t\geq0}|F_t|>0$. **Fix:** Say that the inertia and minimized tensor tend to zero while the curvature coefficient in the $(\kappa,\chi)$ chart has a uniform positive bound on the reference section; the infinite-time obstruction is degeneration of rank, not of curvature.
**Falsifies:** A reference pair $(\kappa,\lambda)$ with $\liminf_{t\to\infty}|F_t|=0$.

### L09-OPEN-14: Arbitrary finite recursive depth by self-similarity of collapsed clusters
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** high
**Location:** chapters/12_open_formation.tex:201-207 (late comparison), 422; chapters/06_outlook.tex:16
**Evidence:** In the limiting field, two collapsed clusters of $m$ coincident agents at $\pm a$ have inherited cross mass $B=v/(1+v)$, $v=e^{-2a^2}$, and $\dot a=-a(1+4B)$, independently of $m$: each member's row has $m$ unit entries and $m$ entries $v$. I checked this numerically for $m=1,2,4$ to 1e-9 (s6). Within a merged cluster the internal half-gap contracts at a rate near 3 while inter-cluster distances contract at rate 1, so the late comparison of prop open:routing applies verbatim at every level. A probe with ten agents (dyadic core at scales $2$, $A_2=50$, $A_3=2500$, plus an exterior pair) gives merges at $t=0.589$ (five pairs), $3.808$ (two quadruples), $7.720$ (octet, with the exterior pair present), $13.281$ (all) (s6). This is numerical exploration, not proof.
**Fix:** Sketch: for $N=2^d+2$ agents at positions $\sum_{k=1}^ds_kA_k$ ($s_k=\pm1$, $A_1=2$, $A_{k+1}/A_k\geq C$) plus a far pair at $R$, induct on the level: when level-$k$ half-distances reach $a=4$, the clusters below have collapsed to gap $O((A_{k-1}/A_k)^3)$; apply the $b=0$ comparison with its $m$-independent constants; separate the exterior by the moving-coordinate estimate (open:distant-bound); choose $A_d$, then $R$, then $h$ with $T=h^2\lesssim(RC^d)^{-2}$. The $\mathsf H$ compression extends level by level with the same phase recursion. Difficulty: moderate (bookkeeping of nested tube estimates). It would replace "finite windows do not imply unlimited recursion" by a theorem giving every finite depth on a finite horizon.

### L09-OPEN-15: A validated-numerics witness at moderate parameters
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/12_open_formation.tex:125-259
**Evidence:** At $A=6$, $R=60$, $T=2.5\times10^{-5}$ the binary field and the discretized full bit-circle field (64 angles, correlated laws) both produce the event sequence, with correlation tolerance at least $2h$ (s3, s4). This yields an explicit nonempty open set with a stated radius. Difficulty: low to moderate.

## Extensions and improvements

The most valuable next step is the finite-depth extension in L09-OPEN-14. The calculation that makes it plausible is exact: in the limiting field a collapsed cluster of $m$ coincident agents behaves, with respect to a second collapsed cluster of the same size, exactly like a single agent with its self-mass, since each row carries $m$ unit entries and $m$ entries $e^{-2a^2}$, giving $B=v/(1+v)$ and $\dot a=-a(1+4B)$ with no dependence on $m$. Because internal gaps of a merged cluster contract at a rate near 3 while inter-cluster distances contract at rate 1, the ratio of internal gap to external distance decays like $e^{-2t}$, so a geometric sequence of scales $A_{k+1}\geq CA_k$ suffices to separate the levels. The induction reuses three existing ingredients: the early-phase bounds for the first level, the $b=0$ comparison with its explicit crossing $a_c=\sqrt{\log9/2}$ at every level, and the moving-coordinate decoupling for the exterior. The cost is a temperature $T\lesssim(RC^d)^{-2}$, so depth is bought with an exponentially smaller temperature; that tradeoff should be part of the statement, since it is the quantitative form of the "recursive continuation" the outlook leaves vague.

Second, the theorem would be stronger and easier to referee if the decoupling in L09-OPEN-01 were stated as a proposition and the significance claim moved to where it is earned. The symmetry reduction is a freezing-method construction for the group $(S^1)^N$ acting on the angular parts of independent agents, and it descends for every partition; native routing contributes a law-determined, relabeling-natural choice of partition and times, and the interface rule contributes the coarsening that makes the discarded directions invisible. With that separation explicit, the open problem becomes (P3) of L09-OPEN-07: an architecture in which the compressible directions influence routing. A concrete test case is the channel $\mathcal K_\epsilon((z,\theta),d(z',\theta'))=\delta_z(dz')p_\epsilon(\theta'-\theta)d\theta'/2\pi$ with small concentration $\epsilon$. The symmetry breaks to the diagonal $S^1$, row costs acquire an $O(\epsilon^2)$ dependence on relative phases, and one can ask whether the selected blocks are those whose relative phases are aligned, which would make the compression approximately exact on exactly the selected blocks, with a computable defect.

Third, the loss statement in L09-OPEN-02 can be made quantitative at low cost. For a singleton whose angular marginal has rotation inertia $\mathcal I$, each pre-merge record contributes Fisher information $\mathcal I$ about its phase, so the posterior variance of a discarded relative offset given $n_i$ and $n_j$ records is about $(n_i\mathcal I_i)^{-1}+(n_j\mathcal I_j)^{-1}$. Reporting this, together with the exact statement that it is positive for every finite record set, gives a precise version of "loses a continuous distinction" and shows how the protocol (number of pre-merge queries, whether records are carried forward) controls it.

Fourth, a validated-numerics witness (L09-OPEN-15) would replace the nonconstructive choice of $A$, $R$, $h$ with an explicit open box at $T$ near $10^{-5}$. The binary routing system is a 12-dimensional analytic ODE on a horizon of about 2.5 at $A=6$, $R=60$, which is within reach of interval Taylor integrators; transversality of each crossing is certified by enclosing the time derivative of the block rows; and the transfer to correlated bit-circle densities needs only the first-order bound that a correlation of sup-size $\varepsilon_c$ perturbs each bit force by at most $2\varepsilon_c$, plus the Lipschitz constant of the density field on the tube.

Finally, two small statements would sharpen the chapter at no cost: the forced final merge (L09-OPEN-03), which gives a third level with empty exterior and one more discarded relative orientation, and the curvature limit $F_\infty=-2\lambda^2\kappa/(\kappa^2+\lambda^2)^2$ together with $h\propto e^{-4t}$ (L09-OPEN-08), which places the infinite-time obstruction in loss of rank rather than loss of curvature.
