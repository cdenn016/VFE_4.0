### X6-LIT-01: The Fisher dynamics and its explicit solutions are the Shahshahani/replicator flow and the known Fisher-Rao KL flow, uncited
**Category:** citation
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/03_dynamics.tex:124-149, thm:dyn-finite-flow, eq:dyn-dissipation; chapters/07_smooth_closure.tex:16-25, smooth:mean-force-criterion; 07_smooth_closure.tex:133-162, smooth:spectrum-flow, eq. smooth:spectrum-solution; 12_open_formation.tex:243-251, open:angular-flow; 11_conditional_formation.tex:111-190, form:full-flow (shape equation)
**Evidence:** eq:dyn-fisher-flow, $\dot u_z=-u_z(h_z-\sum_w u_w h_w)$, is the replicator equation with fitness $-h$, and its identification as the gradient of $\mathcal E$ for the metric $\sum v_z^2/u_z$ is Shahshahani's theorem (S. Shahshahani, Mem. AMS 17, no. 211, 1979; venue verified by the AMS catalog). The general gradient statement is in Hofbauer and Sigmund, *Evolutionary Games and Population Dynamics* (CUP 1998), as attributed by Harper (arXiv:0911.1383, Theorem 1); I did not read the book's locator. The dissipation identity eq:dyn-dissipation, $\dot{\mathcal E}=-\operatorname{Var}_u(h)$, is Fisher's fundamental theorem in this form (Harper, Theorem 2). smooth:mean-force-criterion, $\dot\mu=-\mu(\E[h\mid C]-\E h)$, is the aggregation of replicator dynamics over types with the class-mean fitness of the Price equation (G. R. Price, Nature 227:520-521, 1970; not re-verified). The solution in smooth:spectrum-solution and smooth:conditional-solution, $d\kappa_t/dA=\exp(e^{-t}\ell_0)/Z(e^{-t})$, and the angular solution $f_t\propto f_0^{e^{-2t}}$ in open:angular-flow are the explicit solution of the Fisher-Rao gradient flow of $\KL(\cdot\Vert\pi)$, $\rho_t=\rho_0^{e^{-t}}\pi^{1-e^{-t}}/Z_t$: Chen, Huang, Huang, Reich and Stuart, arXiv:2310.03597v3, Appendix B.1, Eq. (B.1) (read), with exponential convergence in their Theorem 4.1 and in Lu, Lu and Nolen, arXiv:1905.09863, Theorem 3.3 (as cited by Chen et al.). The normalizer $Z_z(a)=\int e^{ar}\xi^0_z(dr)$ is the cumulant generating function used by Domingo-Enrich and Pooladian (TMLR 2023; arXiv:2302.12229; venue verified) to expand KL along this flow. I recomputed the log-density equation, $\dot H=-\operatorname{Var}$, and the replicator dissipation (`lit_identity_checks.py`, blocks 2 and 5, residuals below $10^{-10}$). The 2.0 results are correct. thm:dyn-finite-flow is a special case of the Shahshahani gradient theorem on a product of simplices, and smooth:spectrum-flow is a fiberwise instance of the known Fisher-Rao solution; the new content there is the spectrum retention, not the solution.
**Fix:** At 03_dynamics.tex:126-144 add: "On one simplex this is the replicator equation and the metric is Shahshahani's (Shahshahani 1979; Hofbauer and Sigmund 1998); eq:dyn-dissipation is the corresponding form of Fisher's fundamental theorem." (arXiv:2310.03597, App. B.1), Lu, Lu and Nolen (2019) and Domingo-Enrich and Pooladian (2023) for the explicit Fisher-Rao solution and its cumulant generating function.
**Falsifies:** A demonstration that the cited flows use a different metric normalization or energy so that the formulas do not coincide. My reconstruction found none.

### X6-LIT-02: Conditional reconstruction and common recovery re-derive Jeffrey conditioning, Halmos-Savage sufficiency and Petz recovery
**Category:** citation
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/04_prediction.tex:56-73, prop:conditional-lift; 04_prediction.tex:75-114, prop:common-family-recovery; 04_prediction.tex:116-132, cor:local-family-recovery; 04_prediction.tex:23-35, thm:predictive-chain; supplements/s01_probability.tex:37-49
**Evidence:** $\widehat q=\int p(\cdot\mid h)\nu(dh)$ is Jeffrey's rule of conditioning of the prior $p$ onto the marginal $\nu$. Diaconis and Zabell (JASA 77:822-830, 1982), Theorem 5.1 (read), prove for countable spaces that Jeffrey's update is the unique minimizer of $\KL(Q\Vert P)$ (and of the Hellinger distance) among laws with the prescribed partition marginal, with minimum equal to the marginal divergence. Combined with eq:conditional-lift-loss this gives the Pythagorean identity $\KL(q\Vert p)=\KL(q\Vert\widehat q)+\KL(\widehat q\Vert p)$ with $\KL(\widehat q\Vert p)=\KL(\nu\Vert T_\#p)$ (Csiszar, Ann. Probab. 3:146-158, 1975), which 2.0 does not state; checked numerically (block 3, residual $6\times10^{-17}$). Baez and Fritz (Theory Appl. Categ. 29(16):421-456, 2014; abstract read) characterize the quantity $\KL(q\Vert s(T_\#q))$, for a measure-preserving map with a stochastic section $s$, as the unique functorial, convex-linear, lower semicontinuous relative-entropy functor vanishing at the optimal section; eq:conditional-lift-loss is that functor's value with $s=p(\cdot\mid h)$. prop:common-family-recovery says that one kernel $P_0(\cdot\mid C)$ recovers every $P_\alpha=L_\alpha P_0$ exactly when each $L_\alpha$ has a $\sigma(C)$-measurable version. That is the definition of sufficiency of $C$ for $\{P_0\}\cup\{P_\alpha\}$ combined with the Halmos-Savage factorization theorem for a family dominated by one of its members (Halmos and Savage, Ann. Math. Stat. 20:225-241, 1949; the abstract Fisher-Neyman version is Fritz 2020, Theorem 14.5, read). The equality case of thm:predictive-chain (zero conditional term) is the classical case of Petz's sufficiency theorem: $\KL(sK\Vert tK)=\KL(s\Vert t)$ exactly when the Bayes inverse of $K$ at $t$ maps $sK$ back to $s$ (Petz, Q. J. Math. 39:97-108, 1988). The 2.0 statements are correct; prop:common-family-recovery is a rediscovery, and prop:conditional-lift and thm:predictive-chain are special cases. cor:local-family-recovery adds the conditional-independence clause, which is the Markov-property form of the same sufficiency statement.
**Fix:** After prop:conditional-lift add: "This is Jeffrey's rule; it is the unique KL projection of $p$ onto laws with $T$-marginal $\nu$ (Diaconis and Zabell 1982, Theorem 5.1), so $\KL(q\Vert p)=\KL(q\Vert\widehat q)+\KL(\nu\Vert T_\#p)$." After prop:common-family-recovery add: "That is, $C$ is sufficient for $\{P_0,P_\alpha\}$; the equivalence is the Halmos-Savage factorization theorem." After thm:predictive-chain cite Petz 1988 for the recovery form of the equality case. The earlier theory's bibliography already contains HalmosSavage1949, Petz1988 and Blackwell1953 records.
**Falsifies:** A reading of prop:common-family-recovery that is not equivalent to sufficiency of $C$ for the extended family, for example because $P_0$ need not belong to it. It does belong (take $L\equiv1$), so I found none.

### X6-LIT-03: The collective minimizing connection is the mechanical connection, its residual tensor is the Riemannian-submersion quotient metric, and the Berry-phase literature is the closest antecedent
**Category:** citation
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/02_geometry.tex:149-198, geo:collective-data, geo:collective, geo:minimal-pullback; 12_open_formation.tex:325-331, open:two-metrics; 11_conditional_formation.tex:441-513, form:geometry; references.bib entry LewisNigamOlver2003
**Evidence:** The normal equation $\mathfrak k(A^F(X),\xi)=-\mathsf G(d\boldsymbol p(X),J\xi)$ is $A^F=-\mathbb I^{-1}\mathcal J(d\boldsymbol p)$ with locked inertia $\mathbb I=\mathfrak k$ and momentum $\mathcal J=J^*\mathsf G$; the label geo:locked-inertia already uses Marsden's term. This is Smale's mechanical connection (Invent. Math. 10:305-331, 1970), with the locked-inertia formulation of Marsden, Montgomery and Ratiu (Mem. AMS 88, no. 436, 1990) and the n-body gauge-field account of Littlejohn and Reinsch (Rev. Mod. Phys. 69:213-275, 1997). For a free proper action, $h^{\min}$ is the pullback of the O'Neill quotient metric of the Riemannian submersion $\mathcal N\to\mathcal N/G$ (O'Neill, Michigan Math. J. 13:459-469, 1966), and geo:minimal-pullback is the split of kinetic energy into shape and locked-rotation parts. The quantum analogue is exact: for normalized states $\psi(c)$ with phase group $U(1)$, minimizing $\Vert d\psi+ia\psi\Vert^2$ over $a$ gives the Berry connection $a=i\langle\psi\vert d\psi\rangle$, the residual is the Fubini-Study (Provost-Vallee) metric, and $da$ is the Berry curvature (Berry, Proc. R. Soc. A 392:45-57, 1984; Provost and Vallee, Commun. Math. Phys. 76:289-301, 1980). form:curvature and open:curvature are classical statistical instances of that curvature. open:two-metrics is the standard difference between the O'Neill metric and the metric induced on a section (checked, block 10). 2.0 cites Guichardet 1984 (correctly: the molecular rotation-vibration connection) and Lewis-Nigam-Olver, but none of the general sources. The published Lewis-Nigam-Olver (Commun. Contemp. Math. 7(3):341-374, 2005; venue verified by catalog) also shows that Lie-algebra-valued connection forms are necessarily discontinuous where the isotropy dimension jumps, which is why geo:collective needs constant rank; the text does not say so.
**Fix:** After geo:collective add: "For a free action this is the mechanical connection of Smale (1970) and Marsden et al. (1990), and $h^{\min}$ is the pullback of the O'Neill quotient metric; the quantum analogue is the Berry connection with the Provost-Vallee metric." At 02_geometry.tex:198 add that Lewis, Nigam and Olver (2005) show the constant-rank hypothesis cannot be dropped for algebra-valued forms. Update the bib record to the journal version.
**Falsifies:** A weight choice $w_i$ or a non-isometric action under which $A^F$ is not the mechanical connection of the metric $\mathsf G$. The weights only change the metric, so the identification holds for each declared $\mathsf G$.

### X6-LIT-07: The fusion chapter is a one-dimensional aggregation equation with kernel $|x|$ on weighted atoms, and its global well-posedness is classical

**Severity:** low

**Fix:** In fus:uniqueness replace the existence qualification by "For every initial datum there is a unique global solution (Brezis 1973)." ## Extensions and improvements

The most useful single addition is a related-work section that says, result by result, what is old and what is new. The Fisher dynamics on positive tables is the Shahshahani gradient flow, its fiberwise solution is the known geometric-interpolation solution of the Fisher-Rao KL flow, the collective connection is the mechanical (Berry-type) connection with the O'Neill quotient metric, the conditional reconstruction is Jeffrey conditioning, common recovery is Halmos-Savage sufficiency, the Chapter 12 representative is a first-Fourier-mode slice, the routing limit is a Gaussian log-sum-exp gradient flow, and the fusion chapter is a one-dimensional sticky aggregation equation. What 2.0 adds to each is the inheritance bookkeeping: exact descent of laws, channels, rows, declared responses and contextual geometry through a retention at the original time. Saying so makes the contribution sharper and protects the theory against a referee who recognizes any one of these.

Two structural improvements cost little. Cencov's theorem (X6-LIT-14) can replace the constitutive choice of equal mobilities by a covariance requirement on the joint law of all represented variables and source labels; the statement is one paragraph, and it fits AUTHORING's insistence on the joint-law Fisher metric. The observation that $C_\#$ is a Fisher-Rao Riemannian submersion whose horizontal leaves are Jeffrey lifts (X6-LIT-15, checked numerically) connects inh:gradient-inheritance, smooth:mean-force-criterion and prop:conditional-lift; it gives the general sufficient condition $\mathcal E=\mathcal E_c\circ C_\#$ for same-time inheritance and identifies the retained force as the pushforward of the horizontal projection of the fine gradient.

For the long-term program two bridges are concrete. The attention-dynamics literature (X6-LIT-05, X6-LIT-18) applies directly to the routing limit and suggests how to reach arbitrary finite depth by scale separation, composing the moving-representative correction level by level through open:phase-recursion; this is the shortest path from two selected stages to depth $d$ for every finite $d$ on an open set. The auxiliary-gauge-field reading of the collective connection (X6-LIT-17) is the closest physical precedent for PIFB2's request that link dynamics be derived rather than supplied: in $CP^{N-1}$ models a connection that is eliminated algebraically acquires a kinetic term from fluctuations. 2.0 would need a declared ensemble to follow that route, which is a substantive modeling decision and should be treated as one.

The spectral construction of Chapter 7 reads more cleanly in the language of comparison of experiments (X6-LIT-10, X6-LIT-19). The retained object is the Blackwell class of each private dichotomy, the flow acts on its Renyi curve by a reparametrization, and finite-dimensional closure reduces to an invariant-subspace condition on that curve. This is the natural place to connect 2.0's dynamical sufficiency with statistical sufficiency, which the manuscript currently separates by assertion (07_smooth_closure.tex:40).
