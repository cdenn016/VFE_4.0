### L04-INH-01: The formation obligation is met by the identity retention on linear Gaussian consensus
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** high
**Location:** chapters/05_inheritance.tex:776-792, `inh:recursive-formation`; chapters/06_outlook.tex:4 ("Two constructions satisfy the formation obligation"); contrast chapters/07_smooth_closure.tex, `smooth:formation-open` ("a noninjective collective retention").
**Evidence:** The definition asks for dynamics that "selects nontrivial blocks", a "declared persistence criterion", a handoff "determined by the state or accessible history", inherited "belief and model laws, evaluators, boundary responses, and dynamics", and a second selection "without resetting their source data or supplying a newly tuned attraction". It never requires that a retention lose information. The identity retention passes every test in the chapter (`inh:fiber-test`, `inh:lumpability`, `inh:boundary-square`, the channel square), and with the identity sample channel its parent law is the single law $\bigotimes_{i\in I}q_i$ on the product content space, so it also meets `inh:two-retentions`. Construction (`check_trivial_formation.py`): four agents $q_i=N(x_i,1)$ with peer potential $E=\sum_{i<j}[\mathrm{KL}(q_i\Vert q_j)+\mathrm{KL}(q_j\Vert q_i)]=\sum_{i<j}(x_i-x_j)^2$ and the unit-variance location Fisher metric (Euclidean). The flow is $\dot x=-2Lx$ with $L=4I-\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top$, so $\frac{d}{dt}(x_i-x_j)=-8(x_i-x_j)$, and any block relation $|\bar x_I-\bar x_J|<\delta$, once entered, persists. Selector: merge current parents whose block means differ by less than $\delta$. Retention: the identity on each block's tuple. With $x(0)=(0,0.3,2,2.5)$ and $\delta=0.05$ the script records selection of $\{0,1\}$ at $t=0.224$, $\{2,3\}$ at $t=0.2879$ and their union at $t=0.4673$, matching $\ln 6/8$, $\ln 10/8$, $\ln 42/8$. Children stay distinct at every finite time (backward uniqueness on the equality diagonals, as in `inh:smooth-no-finite-entry`). Every clause holds, nothing is compressed, and the second stage uses the unchanged fine interaction. Chapter 7's open problem contains the missing clause; the definition that chapter 6 says is satisfied does not.
**Fix:** Add to `inh:recursive-formation`: (a) each handoff retention $R_I$ is noninjective on the post-handoff invariant set $\Omega_I$, with at least one fiber containing configurations that a declared child-level probe distinguishes (optionally, a bound on the parent state independent of $|I|$); (b) the protocol class is declared before $R_I$ is chosen (L04-INH-05); (c) "no newly tuned attraction" means the second-stage selector is the first-stage rule applied to inherited states and the parent interaction is the descended one, with no new constitutive parameter. Then restate chapters/06_outlook.tex:4 relative to the strengthened definition.
**Falsifies:** A clause in the main text that excludes product parent laws or the identity sample channel from "inherited belief and model laws". I found none in ch05, s04 or c05.

### L04-INH-02: A canonical parent exists: the coarsest exact retention containing a declared interface
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** high
**Location:** chapters/05_inheritance.tex:14-25 (`inh:two-retentions`, "the additional data needed for its declared interactions and update"), 44-66, 172-206, 562-571.
**Evidence:** Sketch with proof. Deterministic case: let $F:\mathsf S\to\mathsf S$ and an interface $Q:\mathsf S\to\mathsf Y$ (parent laws, the response kernels $x\mapsto K_a(x,\cdot)$ for $a\in A$, channels and evaluators read by the parent). Put $x\sim x'$ iff $Q(F^nx)=Q(F^nx')$ for all $n\geq0$ (for a flow, $Q\circ\Phi_t$, $t\geq0$). The quotient map $\pi=(Q\circ F^n)_{n\geq0}$ satisfies the fiber test by the shift, $Q$ factors through $\pi$, and any retention $R$ through which $Q$ factors and which satisfies `inh:fiber-test` obeys $R(x)=R(x')\Rightarrow x\sim x'$ (apply the fiber test $n$ times and then $Q$). Thus $\pi$ is the unique coarsest exact parent containing the interface; on standard Borel spaces it is Borel into $\mathsf Y^{\mathbb N}$. For analytic $B$ and $Q$, $\sim$ is equality of all Lie derivatives $L_B^kQ$ (the observability construction of Hermann and Krener, already cited in ch10). Finite Markov case: start from the partition $\ker Q$ and repeatedly split each block by the vector of row masses into the current blocks; every strongly lumpable refinement of $\ker Q$ refines every iterate (induction), so the fixed point is the unique coarsest strongly lumpable refinement. This is probabilistic bisimulation (Larsen and Skou 1991; algorithm in Derisavi, Hermanns and Sanders 2003). `check_lumpability_variants.py` (B): a 6-state chain with planted equal rows; refinement returns $\{\{0,1\},\{2,3\},\{4\},\{5\}\}$, the unique coarsest among the 4 lumpable refinements of $\{\{0,1,2,3\},\{4,5\}\}$ found by brute force over all partitions.
**Fix:** State both propositions in ch05 after `inh:finite-lumpability`. The "additional data needed" in `inh:two-retentions` then becomes a theorem, and formation with compression becomes the precise question whether $\pi$ is noninjective on the admitted invariant set. The supplied retentions of ch11 and ch12 can be compared with $\pi$ (a coarser exact parent may exist). Difficulty: low for the statements; computing $\pi$ for the smooth KL model is the research problem.
**Falsifies:** An exact retention containing $Q$ that does not factor through $\pi$; the argument above excludes it.

### L04-INH-03: Rogers–Pitman intertwining gives the exact handoff class between strong lumpability and filtering
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** high
**Location:** chapters/05_inheritance.tex:196-206 ("Closure for every initial law is stronger than closure for one chosen preparation"), 564-571, 641-646; supplements/s04_inheritance.tex:55-109.
**Evidence:** Let $\Lambda:\mathsf S^c\rightsquigarrow\mathsf S$ satisfy $\Lambda(z,R^{-1}\{z\})=1$ and $\Lambda T=T^c\Lambda$ with $T^c=\Lambda T\Phi_R$, where $\Phi_R$ is the deterministic kernel of $R$. For every initial law $\nu\Lambda$ the retained process is Markov with kernel $T^c$ and the filter equals $\Lambda(Z_n,\cdot)$ at every step (Rogers and Pitman 1981). Discrete-time proof from `sinh:filter-update`: if $\widehat p_{h_n}=\Lambda(z_n,\cdot)$ then $\widehat p_{h_n}TP_z=(T^c\Lambda)(z_n,\cdot)P_z=T^c(z_n,z)\Lambda(z,\cdot)$, because $\Lambda(w,\cdot)P_z=\delta_{wz}\Lambda(z,\cdot)$. `check_lumpability_variants.py` (A): $\mathsf S=\{1,2,3\}$, $R(1)=R(2)=a$, $R(3)=b$, rows $(0.3,0.5,0.2)$, $(0.4,0.2,0.4)$, $(0.25,0.25,0.5)$, $\Lambda(a)=(1/2,1/2,0)$, $\Lambda(b)=\delta_3$. Strong lumpability fails ($0.2\ne0.4$), $\Lambda T=T^c\Lambda$ holds with $T^c=\begin{pmatrix}7/10&3/10\\1/2&1/2\end{pmatrix}$, retained path laws of length 5 agree exactly for initial $\nu\Lambda$, and differ by $53/2000$ for initial $\delta_1$.
**Fix:** Add this as a proposition. It is the precise class of handoffs "from its declared conditional law" (ch05:641) that yields a memoryless exact parent; inherited predictive responses are $\Lambda K_a$. The continuous-time and standard Borel versions follow Rogers and Pitman. Difficulty: low.
**Falsifies:** A failure of the induction above; none is possible once $\Lambda T=T^c\Lambda$ and the support condition hold.

### L04-INH-04: Inheritance identities have no declared domain; along one trajectory they are vacuous
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/05_inheritance.tex:776-786, `inh:recursive-formation` ("an admitted class of initially unpartitioned configurations", "a handoff determined by the state or accessible history"); 83-86 (moving $R_t$).
**Evidence:** Lemma: let $x(t)$ be a smooth solution on $[0,T]$ and $R$ smooth with $z=R\circ x$ an embedded arc ($z$ injective, $\dot z\ne0$). Extending the tangent field of the arc through a tubular neighborhood gives a smooth $B^c$ with $\dot z=B^c(z)$, so $R(\Phi_t x_0)=\Phi^c_t(Rx_0)$ holds with unique parent solutions. Hence if the admitted class is one configuration, if inheritance is checked only along realized trajectories, or if the retention may depend on the trajectory, dynamical inheritance holds for every retention whose image of the trajectory is an embedded arc. The same is true on a prepared invariant family on which $R$ is injective. The fiber test is informative only over a set containing pairs $x\ne x'$ with $R(x)=R(x')$. The definition does not say how large the admitted class must be, nor over which set the fiber tests are imposed after a state-dependent handoff. Chapter 12 states an open class; the prepared family of chapter 11 should be checked against this requirement.
**Fix:** Require (i) an admitted class that is open in a declared topology, or an invariant family declared in advance; (ii) for each selected block and event, an invariant set $\Omega_I$ containing every post-handoff state that arises from the admitted class with that block, with the inheritance identities for all $x,x'\in\Omega_I$ such that $R_I(x)=R_I(x')$; (iii) the handoff a measurable function of the state at the event, the same for every trajectory that selects the same block.
**Falsifies:** An explicit requirement in ch05 that the fiber tests hold on an open set or on a declared invariant family; I found none.

### L04-INH-05: No quantified definition of an inherited parent; the protocol and preparation classes are free
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/05_inheritance.tex:4-41, 172-206, 208-225, 267-276, 776-786.
**Evidence:** The chapter defines retentions (`inh:two-retentions`), dynamical squares (`inh:update-square`, `inh:vector-square`, `inh:lumpability`), the boundary square for "a declared boundary protocol $a$", and the channel square, but never combines them into a definition of "parent" with quantifiers. Three choices are left open: the protocol class $A$ for which `inh:boundary-square` must hold; the class of initial laws (all, a Rogers–Pitman class as in L04-INH-03, or one preparation); and whether law-dependent channels must factor through $R$ (the channel paragraph treats one fixed channel). These choices decide the outcome: for $A=\varnothing$ response inheritance is vacuous, while the Gibbs identifiability result of ch09 (`sec:public-gibbs-identifiability`) and `prop:boundary-observable` in ch10 show that unrestricted identity-channel probes force the retention to be injective on exposed child laws. The formation definition says "boundary responses" without naming $A$.
**Fix:** Definition (inherited parent). Data: a block $I$, a retention $R_I:\Omega_I\to\mathsf S^c_I$, a protocol class $A_I$ fixed independently of $R_I$, and a preparation class $\mathcal P_I$. Conditions: (D) the dynamical square on $\Omega_I$ for all initial laws in $\mathcal P_I$; (B) for each $a\in A_I$, the joint kernel of response and next retained state factors through $R_I$ (L04-INH-08); (C) every channel and evaluator read by $A_I$ or by the parent update factors through $R_I$, including its dependence on $x$; (G) optionally `geo:retained-connection`. These are the separate conditions that AUTHORING.md names.
**Falsifies:** A passage that fixes $A$ and $\mathcal P$ for the formation definition.

### L04-INH-06: The gradient-inheritance hypothesis freezes the discarded dynamics; a sharp criterion is available
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/05_inheritance.tex:88-133, `inh:gradient-inheritance`; companion/c05_inheritance.tex:55-69.
**Evidence:** The proposition is correct (reconstructed; `check_gradient_criterion.py` conformal case residual 0). Its hypothesis $E=\chi\circ E_c\circ R$ makes $dE$ vanish on $\ker DR$, so $\operatorname{grad}_gE$ is horizontal and each fine trajectory is the horizontal lift of its projection: the discarded state does not move relative to the horizontal distribution (for a product metric and $R(a,b)=a$, $\dot b=0$). For noninjective retentions that are not symmetry quotients, peer potentials depending on child differences do not satisfy it, and the proposition is cited nowhere outside ch05. Sharp criterion: for a Riemannian submersion $R$, $DR\operatorname{grad}_gE=(\operatorname{grad}_{g_c}E_c)\circ R$ if and only if $d(E-E_c\circ R)(v)=0$ for every horizontal $v$. Proof: for horizontal $v$ with $DRv=z$, $g_c(DR\operatorname{grad}_gE,z)=g((\operatorname{grad}_gE)^H,v)=dE(v)$, and equality with $dE_c(z)$ for every $z$ is the condition. Example (script): $R=(x_1+x_2)/\sqrt2$ on Euclidean $\mathbb R^2$, $E=x_1^2/2+x_2^2/2+W(x_1-x_2)$ with arbitrary $W$: $DR\operatorname{grad}E-R=0$ symbolically, although $\partial_sE(x_1+s,x_2-s)|_{s=0}=x_1-x_2+2W'(x_1-x_2)\ne0$, so $E\ne\chi(E_c\circ R)$. With quartic self-energy the pushed field equals $\sqrt2/8$ and $\sqrt2/2$ at $(1/2,1/2)$ and $(1,0)$ on one fiber, so descent fails. Necessity in the uniform sense: if descent is required for every potential of the form $E_c\circ R$, then $DR\,g^{-1}DR^*=g_c^{-1}\circ R$, which is the Riemannian submersion condition (horizontal conformality when a positive factor is allowed). The text says the condition is sufficient, which is true; it does not say that the metric condition is necessary in this uniform sense and not necessary for a given $E$. No passage in ch05, c05 or the README claims necessity.
**Fix:** Replace the potential hypothesis by $d(E-\chi\circ E_c\circ R)|_H=0$, with the dilation factor in the conformal case; add the necessity remark; note that a $G$-invariant $E$ and $g$ with the quotient metric are the natural instance (L04-INH-11); cite O'Neill (1966) and Baird and Wood (2003).
**Falsifies:** A use of the original hypothesis in ch09 to ch12 with a noninjective retention that is not an orbit map.

### L04-INH-13: Remove the measurable right-inverse hypothesis and state the smooth existence criterion
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/05_inheritance.tex:44-86 (`inh:deterministic-descent`), 196-199, 220-223.
**Evidence:** On standard Borel spaces with Borel surjective $R$, if `inh:fiber-test` holds then $\operatorname{graph}(F^c)$ is the image of $\mathsf S$ under the Borel map $x\mapsto(R(x),R(F(x)))$, hence analytic, and a function between standard Borel spaces with analytic graph is Borel (Souslin; Kechris 1995, Theorem 14.12, numbering from memory). The same argument for $x\mapsto T(x,R^{-1}(\cdot))\in\mathcal P(\mathsf S^c)$ and $x\mapsto(K_aD_a)(x,\cdot)$ gives measurable lumped and boundary kernels without $\iota$, so the "qualification" at line 198 is automatic. Borel surjections need not admit Borel right inverses (only universally measurable ones, by Jankov–von Neumann), so the present hypothesis is a real restriction. Smooth case: if $R$ is a $C^{k+1}$ surjective submersion with connected fibers and $B\in C^k$, $k\geq1$, then $B$ descends iff $[V,B]$ is vertical for every vertical field $V$ (projectable fields; in coordinates $R(a,b)=a$ this is $\partial_bB_A=0$), and $B^c=DR\,B\circ\sigma$ is $C^k$, so parent solutions are unique. For linear $R=M$ this is the Li–Rabitz condition $DB(x)\ker M\subseteq\ker M$.
**Fix:** Replace the right-inverse hypothesis by "standard Borel spaces" and add the smooth existence criterion after `inh:vector-square`.
**Falsifies:** A failure of the analytic-graph theorem for the spaces used; it holds for all standard Borel spaces.

### L04-INH-14: Separator elimination is proved; a gluing statement needs more hypotheses than two blocks

### L04-INH-15: The selector-symmetry result holds for the stabilizer of the law, and its |V| >= 3 hypothesis is unused
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/05_inheritance.tex:750-772, `inh:selector-symmetry`.

## Extensions and improvements

The most useful change in this scope is to make the parent canonical. Given the fine update and a declared interface $Q$ (parent laws, the responses for a declared protocol class, the channels the parent reads), the future-trajectory equivalence $x\sim x'\iff Q(F^nx)=Q(F^nx')$ for all $n$ yields the unique coarsest exact retention containing $Q$ (L04-INH-02); in the finite Markov case it is the coarsest strongly lumpable refinement of $\ker Q$, computed by partition refinement. With this proposition the formation problem takes a form that the identity retention cannot meet trivially: on the admitted invariant set, is the observability quotient of the declared interface noninjective, and does it remain so after the next handoff? It also makes the protocol class visibly decisive, because a larger interface gives a finer quotient, and it explains the identifiability results of ch09 and ch10 as the limit of an unrestricted interface.

The second addition is the Rogers–Pitman class of handoffs (L04-INH-03). The chapter currently offers two extremes: strong lumpability for every initial law, and a filter on the simplex of fine states for one preparation. An intertwining kernel $\Lambda$ with $\Lambda T=T^c\Lambda$ gives an exact, memoryless parent for exactly the preparations of the form $\nu\Lambda$, with filter $\Lambda(Z_n,\cdot)$ and inherited responses $\Lambda K_a$. This gives a precise meaning to "a handoff initializes the parent from its declared conditional law", and it is the natural stochastic setting for the PIFB2 "predictive parent state" obligation (24_section.tex:7), whose projected memory kernel vanishes in this class.

Third, the exact mechanisms should be classified in ch05 rather than rediscovered in later chapters. There are three: equivariant descent through an orbit map (L04-INH-11), which covers the phase and rotation compressions of ch10 and ch12; aggregate maps whose horizontal differential is basic (the sharp criterion of L04-INH-06, which covers center-of-mass inheritance for quadratic self-energies with arbitrary translation-invariant pair interactions); and coordinate projections, which by the reciprocity theorem of L04-INH-07 are exact in the program's Fisher gradient dynamics only for energetically isolated blocks. Together these say where exact compression can come from in the smooth KL model: from symmetry or from special energy structure, and not from discarding reciprocally interacting constituents.

Fourth, the formation definition needs its quantifiers (L04-INH-01, 04, 05): noninjectivity on the post-handoff invariant set, fiber tests over a declared open or invariant admitted class, a protocol class fixed before the retention, joint descent of responses with transitions (L04-INH-08), and a formal meaning for "no newly tuned attraction". If unbounded recursion is a goal, type closure (L04-INH-12) is the property that lets a level-0 theorem be applied at every level; checking whether the ch11 additive Wishart parents are type-closed is a concrete first step of moderate difficulty.

Smaller improvements: drop the right-inverse hypothesis by the analytic-graph theorem and state the projectable-field criterion (L04-INH-13); state the stabilizer version of the selector result, which ties directly to the PIFB2 four-cycle pairing problem (L04-INH-15); state the actual two-block gluing theorem with the junction-tree caveat and the XOR and four-cycle counterexamples (L04-INH-14); and add the JVP lumping-defect diagnostic to VFE_4.0 so that the reductions it already computes can be tested against the exactness criteria (L04-INH-19).
