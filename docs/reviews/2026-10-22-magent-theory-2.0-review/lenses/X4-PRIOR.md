# X4-PRIOR: Continuity with the earlier MultiAgentELBO theory

Verdict: every earlier result that 2.0 imports was reconstructed and none is misstated, but 2.0 dropped the earlier counterfactual standard for recursion (SPEC R14), and by that standard its three formation constructions are relaxation schedules of initially supplied scales with descriptive membership, in which parents never act back and links never carry holonomy. Two defects found on 2026-10-12 (missing formation prior art; structure that does no dynamical work) are re-imported, while several earlier constructions (quotient-gradient inheritance, the torsion obstruction for symmetric anchors, the Gaussian soldering example, source-prior learning, the label-model row, a covariant loop defect) would turn declared or unexplained ingredients of 2.0 into derived ones at low cost; exact identities supporting those imports are given with scripts. Read in full: the 2.0 main manuscript (`frontmatter.tex`, `chapters/01_foundations` through `12_open_formation`, `06_outlook`, and the four appendices, 4,334 lines), `README.md`, `AUTHORING.md`, the 2026-10-12 review `REPORT.md`, and the findings section of lens `L10-framing-citations.md`; in the earlier theory `D:/MultiAgentELBO_Theory/`, the whole of `CONTENT.md` and the ranges `SPEC.md:225-275` (R13 to R16), `b02_base_direction_geometry.tex:1-60, 325-600, 820-1075`, `c09_coarse_channels.tex:99-230, 568-720`, `c06_attention.tex:1-270`, `c03_probability.tex:1-60, 515-665`, `c08_two_timescales.tex:1-40, 629-700`, `c08b_lie_links.tex:1-80`, `b04_covers_descent.tex:510-660`, `c07_fiber_geometry.tex:1078-1100, 1440-1510`, `c10_network_state.tex:983-1150`, `c12_multiscale_vfe.tex:1090-1110`, `c13_formation.tex:1533-1560`, `c14_interpretation.tex:180-300`, `g07_coarse_maps.tex:429-500`, `c11_network_coarsegraining.tex:1780-1800`. ## Findings

### X4-PRIOR-01: The formation obligation dropped the earlier counterfactual-handoff clause; by that clause the 2.0 formation constructions are supplied-scale sequences with descriptive membership
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** high
**Location:** `chapters/05_inheritance.tex:776-803` (`inh:recursive-formation`); `chapters/11_conditional_formation.tex:277-348` (`form:selector`, `form:selection`); `chapters/12_open_formation.tex:125-232` (`open:routing`), `:414-423` (`open:formation`); `chapters/06_fusion.tex:286-330` (`fus:two-stages`); `frontmatter.tex:9`; earlier `D:/MultiAgentELBO_Theory/SPEC.md:232-249` (R14), `c13_formation.tex:1533` (`open:rg-formation-general`), `c12_multiscale_vfe.tex:1475` (`open:full-graph-hierarchy`).
**Evidence:** The 2.0 definition requires that parents "select a block containing at least two first-stage parents under those inherited interactions, without resetting their source data or supplying a newly tuned attraction at the next level." The earlier standard (R14, carried into the settling conditions of `open:rg-formation-general` and `open:full-graph-hierarchy`) adds that if the second-stage event with its block occurs uniformly over starts in a handoff class that contains counterfactual handoffs, the sequence "is a supplied-scale sequence, not recursion", and that "a second-stage proof that uses only hypotheses on the handoff state that also hold for counterfactual handoffs in the class establishes a supplied-scale sequence". 2.0 omits this clause, and its constructions are of exactly that kind.
(i) Membership is descriptive in all three constructions. The fine vector field contains no membership variable (ch. 11: "Each additive quotient is available initially ... No law is reset at that event"; ch. 12: `open:full-field` reads only the fine laws; ch. 6: blocks are attained equalities of the fine inclusion), so first-stage formation has no dynamical effect on the second stage.
(ii) Every fine flow contracts globally. In ch. 6 all blocks collide at finite auxiliary time for every admitted datum. In ch. 11 every block score obeys $b_I(t)=e^{-2t}b_I(0)$, so the union of all blocks forms from every start whose gaps exceed $\delta$. In ch. 12, `open:interior` sends every logit to zero.
(iii) The schedule is a reparameterization of initially supplied scales. Script `s3_selector_geometry.py` (output `s3_selector_geometry.out`) confirms that the ch. 11 rule is count-weighted centroid-linkage (UPGMC) clustering of the initial shapes with event times $t_k=\tfrac12\log(h_k/\delta)$: zero mismatches in random one-dimensional trials with all initial gaps above $\delta$, and the manuscript's example reproduces $\tfrac12\log2$, $\tfrac12\log3$, $\tfrac12\log(17/2)$. The threshold $\delta$ only fixes the time origin. Script `s2_open_formation_scales.py` integrates the ch. 12 limit field `open:limit`: the pair event occurs at $t\approx0.59$ for every $A$, while the parent event occurs at $t_{\rm parent}=\log A-0.103\pm0.002$ for $A=64,\dots,16384$ (four agents, and six agents with $R=1000A$). Script `s2b_threshold_sweep.py` gives the same blocks and order for $(\varepsilon,\delta)$ from $(0.02,0.01)$ to $(0.24,0.05)$, with all event times inside a band of width $0.43$. These are numerical observations in the scaled limit, not proofs.
Consequently ch. 6 and ch. 11 are supplied-scale sequences under R14 (the second-stage block forms from every start). In ch. 12 the second stage depends on the first only through the selector's bookkeeping (its exterior excludes the declared partner), not through the dynamics; whether R14 would classify it as recursion depends on a handoff class that 2.0 never declares, but its dynamical margin is zero. The chapter title "Conditional Recursive Formation", the theorem name "Open-law preferential recursive formation", and the abstract's "selects two parents and then their union" use a weaker standard than the earlier theory without saying so. AUTHORING.md forbids claiming recurrent formation from a supplied example, but the definition admits scales supplied in the initial data.
**Fix:** Add a dynamical-efficacy clause to `inh:recursive-formation`, for example: "after the first handoff, the inherited interactions depend on the retained first-stage state through a term that the fine potential does not contain before the handoff, or the second-stage event or its time differs by a stated margin from that of the counterfactual fine trajectory without the first-stage retention." Otherwise rename the 2.0 notion (for example "two-stage selection with exact inheritance") and reserve "recursive formation" for the R14 standard. In ch. 11 state the UPGMC equivalence and that $\delta$ is a time origin; in ch. 12 state that the second stage occurs when the supplied core separation $A$ has contracted to order one; in the abstract say that the stages follow the order of initially supplied scales. X4-PRIOR-09 sketches a construction that could meet the stronger clause.
**Falsifies:** A 2.0 construction in which the fine vector field after the first handoff differs from the membership-free field, or a proof that the ch. 12 parent time is not $\log A+O(1)$ in the limit field.

### X4-PRIOR-02: Primitive self channels contradict chapter 2 and the recorded identity convention, and the chapter 12 selector depends on the nonidentity choice
**Category:** consistency
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** `chapters/02_interactions.tex:69` ("The primitive self-comparison is $L_{ii}^a=0$"); `AUTHORING.md` ("Primitive identity self-comparisons and inherited coarse loop information must be distinguished"); `chapters/11_conditional_formation.tex:88-89`; `chapters/12_open_formation.tex:12, 34-41, 65, 139`; earlier `c06_attention.tex:43-75` (`def:obs-attention-label-model`: an agent-level self edge carries the identity link) and `c06_attention.tex:211-260` (`prop:obs-attention-constant-row-gap`, self-copy clause); 2026-10-12 `REPORT.md` section 1 (author convention $\Theta_{ii}=I$).
**Evidence:** Chapters 11 and 12 put nonidentity channels on primitive self edges ("These self channels are nonidentity channels"; in ch. 12 the selective channel $\mathcal K$, with $D^q_{ii}=H^q_i$). In ch. 12 the receiver angular entropy $H^q_i$ cancels from the Gibbs normalization (`open:row`) only because the self edge uses $\mathcal K$. With the identity self channel of ch. 2, $D_{ii}=0$ while $D_{ij}=H_i+\KL(\mu_i\Vert\mu_j)$, so every peer weight is multiplied by $e^{-H_i/T}$ relative to self. This is the earlier self-copy clause ("the receiver's own uncertainty penalizes every other source and not the self copy"). `s2_open_formation_scales.py`: for a von Mises angular profile with $\kappa=1$ ($H=0.2105$) and $T=0.01$ the factor is $7.2\times10^{-10}$; at $\kappa=2$ it is $1.5\times10^{-25}$. Since `open:routing` fixes $A$, $R$ and its horizon first and then takes $T=h^2$ with $h$ small, while `open:open-laws` uses $\kappa>0$ for the rank-two geometry, the routing windows do not occur on the stated horizon under the identity convention unless $H_i=O(T)$: the suppression relaxes only as the angular concentration decays ($H\approx\kappa^2e^{-4t}/4$ at small concentration), which postpones order-one peer weights by roughly $\tfrac12\log(1/h)$, beyond any fixed horizon as $h\to0$. The identity convention also removes the self term from the force, so the fine field itself changes. Excluding self instead also fails: without the self summand the limit weights renormalize over distant agents, each agent's nearest neighbor receives weight near one at $t=0$, and the hypothesis "every initial off-diagonal native weight is below $1/10$" is false. Ch. 11's final parent relies on its nonidentity self channel as well (`11_conditional_formation.tex:259-262`). The 2.0 supplement (`s03_variation.tex`, "Identity self-comparisons and independent snapshots") already warns that the self convention must be fixed before computing a cost.
**Fix:** State in ch. 2 and ch. 3 that a primitive self channel is constitutive data, with the identity as one choice; amend AUTHORING.md accordingly; and in ch. 12 state that the selective self channel is what removes $H^q_i$ from the rows and that the construction requires it.
**Falsifies:** A proof that `open:routing` holds with identity primitive self channels at fixed $\kappa>0$ as $T\to0$ on its stated horizon.

### X4-PRIOR-04: The imported statistical selection results have no admissible input in any 2.0 dynamics
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** `chapters/05_inheritance.tex:648-772` (`inh:finest-independent`, `inh:conditional-partition`, `inh:independence-discontinuity`, `inh:selector-symmetry`); `chapters/03_dynamics.tex:9`; earlier `c09_coarse_channels.tex:585-720` (`prop:cg-finest-independent-partition` to `open:cg-natural-selector`).
**Evidence:** The four results are faithful imports (reconstructed; see the table below), and they apply to a declared cross-agent joint law. 2.0's dynamics evolves a tuple of agent laws with product recognition. The chapter itself notes (05:650-653) that the tuple specifies no joint law and that its product imposes independence, for which the finest partition is all singletons. The one joint law that the dynamics does produce is the pushforward $(\Phi_t)_\#\mu_0$ of a preparation ensemble, and coupling makes it dependent across interacting pairs. For example, for $\dot x=Ax$ with $A=\begin{psmallmatrix}-1-\lambda&\lambda\\\lambda&-1-\lambda\end{psmallmatrix}$ and $x(0)\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$, $\operatorname{Cov}(x_1(t),x_2(t))=\tfrac12(e^{-2t}-e^{-2(1+2\lambda)t})>0$ for $\lambda,t>0$. With Gibbs rows every allowed edge keeps positive weight (`smooth:row-ratio`), so the dependence graph of the pushed law is generically connected and its finest independent partition is $\{V\}$: the instability of `inh:independence-discontinuity` in dynamical form. The earlier theory reached this conclusion in `open:cg-natural-selector`; 2.0 imports the propositions without saying that they cannot select membership in any of its constructions, which is why chapters 11 and 12 need declared thresholds.
**Fix:** State after `inh:selector-symmetry` which joint law the selectors would read in 2.0 and that for the natural candidates they return all singletons or $\{V\}$; present them as constraints on admissible selectors, not as formation mechanisms.
**Falsifies:** A 2.0 construction whose generated joint law has a nontrivial intermediate finest independent partition.

### X4-PRIOR-05: The contextual geometry is transported initial data and decays; the earlier uniform-preservation obligation is not met
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** `chapters/11_conditional_formation.tex:421-514` (`form:geometry`); `chapters/12_open_formation.tex:359-402` (`open:curvature`, `open:rank`), `:414-423`; `frontmatter.tex:9`; earlier `b02_base_direction_geometry.tex:574` (`open:pb-collective-interface-preservation`); 2026-10-12 L10-08.
**Evidence:** The constitutive data are context independent and the dynamics acts pointwise in $c$, so contextual jets are transported initial data; the base still does no dynamical work (L10-08, now in dynamical form). In ch. 11 both eigenvalues of $\mathsf P$ obey the same scalar equation with $f_p\le-2$ (`form:eigenvalue`), so $|p_+-p_-|$ and $\partial_ap_\pm$ are $O(e^{-2t})$, and $g_{aa}$ and $k_1$ are $O(e^{-4t})$. In ch. 12 on the product reference family, `s3_selector_geometry.py` (part b) gives $h_{\chi\chi}=ab/(a+b)$ falling from $1.7\times10^{-1}$ at $t=0$ to $8\times10^{-19}$ at $t=10$, and $h_{\kappa\kappa}=n\rho_t^2\operatorname{Var}$ also decays like $e^{-4t}$. The statements "rank two at every finite time" and "nonzero curvature" are true, but the smallest eigenvalue decays exponentially, so the earlier problem's requirement of a uniform positive lower bound on the singular values over a horizon is not met. The connection does not degenerate: it converges to $A^F_\infty=-\kappa^2/(\kappa^2+\lambda^2)\,d\chi$ with curvature $-2\kappa\lambda^2/(\kappa^2+\lambda^2)^2\,d\kappa\wedge d\chi$ ($-0.2681$ at $\kappa=1.3$, $\lambda=0.7$, matching finite differences), which follows from $\mathcal I(x)\sim x^2/2$.
**Fix:** State the decay rates next to `form:geometry` and `open:rank`, state the stronger true fact that the curvature has a nonzero limit and that $e^{4t}h$ converges on the reference family, and say that the earlier uniform-preservation obligation remains open. Persistent contextual rank needs context-dependent constitutive data or a base-coupling term, such as the gauged Dirichlet energy $\tfrac12\int\operatorname{tr}_\gamma h^{\omega}_\zeta\,d\mathrm{vol}_\gamma$ with a declared base metric $\gamma$, whose Fisher flow is a gauged harmonic-map heat flow; the earlier b02 scope paragraph lists exactly these missing declarations.
**Falsifies:** A uniform positive lower bound for the smallest eigenvalue of $h$ on the ch. 11 or ch. 12 families over $[0,\infty)$.

### X4-PRIOR-09: Make links dynamical with a covariant law-metric loop defect, and use it for downward action
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** `chapters/02_interactions.tex:62-139` (`def:int-generator`, `def:int-moment-family`, `eq:int-nongaussian-loop`), `:145`; `chapters/03_dynamics.tex:23-31`; `chapters/06_fusion.tex:127-137`; `chapters/05_inheritance.tex:44-87`; earlier `c08b_lie_links.tex:284` (`cor:link-no-invariant-defect`), `:309` (`def:link-loop-defect`), `:1072` (`prop:link-field-equation`); `c12_multiscale_vfe.tex:345` (`rem:full-graph-anchored-star`), `:1090` (`prop:full-graph-downward-coupling`); 2026-10-12 `REPORT.md` section 1 (2-cycles in the loop family).
**Evidence:** No 2.0 dynamical construction uses a link with nontrivial holonomy: the ch. 6 links have trivial cycle product, and chapters 7 to 12 use identity, fixed selective, engineered, or moment-target channels. The exponential links of ch. 2 are therefore inert in the formation theory, and the author's convention that 2-cycles enter a loop penalty has no counterpart. With the base-vertex law supplying the fiber metric,
$$Q_p=\operatorname{tr}\bigl((\Theta_p-I)^{\mathsf T}M_i^{-1}(\Theta_p-I)M_i\bigr)=\bigl\Vert M_i^{-1/2}(\Theta_p-I)M_i^{1/2}\bigr\Vert_F^2$$
is invariant under passive vertex changes and every reference change, nonnegative, and zero exactly at $\Theta_p=I$. It escapes `cor:link-no-invariant-defect` because it is not a class function of $\Theta_p$ alone (the unipotent $\begin{psmallmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{psmallmatrix}$ has $Q=1$ at $M=I$). `s5_law_metric_loop_defect.py`: invariance error $8\times10^{-12}$ over 200 random $\GL(3)$ trials, and $Q=3$ for the ch. 2 two-cycle holonomy $\begin{psmallmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{psmallmatrix}$. Separately, in every 2.0 construction $R\circ\Phi_t=\Phi^c_t\circ R$ with a membership-free $\Phi_t$, so a parent never enters a child's vector field; the ch. 11 global-moment channel ($DC_q[H]\ne0$ in `form:coupling`) acts before and regardless of formation.
**Fix:** Two sketches. (a) Add $\sum_pc_pQ_p(\Theta[\nu])$ to `eq:dyn-potential`; reciprocity and flatness forces then enter every law through the generator derivative already displayed in `eq:int-total-variation`. Alternatively promote $L_e$ to state variables with an anchor term $\tfrac12\Vert L_e-\mathcal L_e(\nu)\Vert^2$, giving a 2.0 analog of `prop:link-field-equation` through the supplement's Fréchet-derivative lemma. To re-prove: reference covariance of the augmented potential (needs reference equivariance of $\mathcal L$ and of $M$), regularity on the moment family, and the inheritance squares with dressed loop data. Difficulty: moderate. (b) For downward action, use a state-dependent architecture: after a handoff the compared law of $i\in I$ becomes $(1-\theta)q_j\mathcal K+\theta\,q_I\mathcal K_I$, reading the retained parent law. The switched fine field then differs from the counterfactual one, R14's margin can be nonzero (X4-PRIOR-01), and the parent acts back through a nonzero cross-derivative, the falsifier of L10-01. Requirements: a well-posed switched flow (hysteresis or Filippov), the moving-map form of `inh:vector-square`, and response squares for the switched channels. Difficulty: high.
**Falsifies:** For (a), a reference change under which $Q_p$ is not invariant on the moment family.

### X4-PRIOR-10: Chapter 3 calls the declared flow inference
**Category:** consistency
**Severity:** low

## Extensions and improvements

The most valuable next step in this scope is to decide which formation standard 2.0 wants and to build one construction that meets the stronger one. Under R14 a recursion claim needs a second stage that depends on the first through the dynamics. In 2.0 terms this means a fine potential whose interactions read retained parent states after a handoff, for instance compared laws $(1-\theta)q_j\mathcal K+\theta\,q_I\mathcal K_I$ for members of a formed block $I$ (X4-PRIOR-09b). The inheritance square must then be proved for the switched field through the moving-map form of `inh:vector-square`, and the counterfactual margin is the difference between the switched and the unswitched second-stage times. A first test case is the ch. 11 Wishart family, where the parent law $W_2(A_I,\mathsf P)$ is explicit. A parent-reading channel $W_2(n_I\alpha_0,\Lambda_I)$ with a block moment $\Lambda_I=I_2+\kappa M_I/n_I$ keeps the members of each block at a common precision, but blocks then acquire different precisions, so the union is no longer a common-precision Wishart sum; computing how the union is retained in that case, and whether the union time differs from the counterfactual one by a margin bounded below, is the first calculation. Difficulty: high, but the closed form of ch. 11 makes the first computation feasible.

The second step is to derive the selector instead of declaring it. Two earlier routes transfer exactly. Source-prior learning is the unit-Fisher flow of the reduced potential in $\pi$ at rate $T$ (X4-PRIOR-08), so an emergent slow selector exists inside 2.0 once self is excluded from the learned row. The label-model bridge (X4-PRIOR-07) shows which Gibbs rows are recognition optima of a normalized model, namely those with $j$-independent Jensen gaps. A third, geometric route makes links state variables with a covariant law-metric loop defect (X4-PRIOR-09a), which would let holonomy, absent from every 2.0 construction, influence routing.

The third step is low cost: restore the free, proper, isometric clause of gradient inheritance (X4-PRIOR-06), which turns the ch. 12 parent flow into a gradient flow of the descended reduced energy in the quotient Fisher metric with equal Fisher duration, and import the earlier soldering example, isotropy criterion, frame-sector obstruction, and witnesses (X4-PRIOR-12, X4-PRIOR-13).
