Verdict. Every identity in my scope that I recomputed holds (sympy residual 0, or numerical residuals of order 1e-14 or smaller), and I found no false statement in chapters 3 and 4, the supplement chapter, or the two companion chapters. The problems are structural: the dynamics of 2.0 has no normalized generative model, no evidence and no record term in its potential, so records act only through separately declared assimilation events whose composition with the flow is used later but never defined; the single-potential gradient structure is bought with a reverse influence from receivers to sources that the communication model of chapter 4 does not supply; the Fisher flow is defined and shown well-posed only on finite tables while later chapters use a density-level version; and unit mobility makes row speed equal to row temperature. The finite-table theorem can be strengthened at low cost to global existence and convergence for fixed channels.

## Findings

### L03-DYN-01: The 2.0 dynamics has no normalized generative model, evidence, or record term, and the record/flow composition used later is undefined
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** high
**Location:** chapters/03_dynamics.tex:4, :49, :151-184 (def:dyn-potential, eq:dyn-fixed-vfe); appendices/record_process.tex:33-55; chapters/07_smooth_closure.tex:206-216 (smooth:feedback); frontmatter.tex:5
**Evidence:** Chapter 3, line 49: "This potential is a constitutive choice at the level of agent laws. No distribution over these law configurations is required to define it." The potential eq:dyn-potential contains no record. A grep of every 2.0 source file finds "variational free energy" only at ch3:160 ("This is the variational free energy of a fixed model"), in the section that presents it as a different objective; "evidence" and "ELBO" occur nowhere. Records reach law dynamics only as Bayesian conditioning events (eq:predict-and-assimilate; smooth:feedback), and ch7:216 composes "records, feedback updates, and original smooth-flow intervals" without a definition of that composite process in chapter 3. No functional is decreased by both parts: in `s6` (1) one agent sits at the product minimizer of E_0 = KL(q x s || rG) (E = 0.146075); one informative record and the Bayesian update of s raise E to 0.510172.

What the earlier theory had and 2.0 gives up: one fixed normalized joint (c04 prop:gen-exact-normalization) whose generative factors may not read recognition laws (req:gen-typing-prohibition), the exact evidence identity (c05 thm:elbo-exact-identity), block updates that are coordinate descent of one collective free energy (thm:obs-local-global-potential, cor:obs-potential-game), and evidence monotonicity of accepted M-coordinates (prop:elbo-evidence-monotonicity). The law-dependent channels of 2.0 are exactly the factors the typing prohibition excluded, and prop:gen-no-distinguished-target already showed that a family of joints indexed by recognition laws selects no evidence; the new eq:dyn-affine-obstruction adds that no fixed sample energy reproduces a peer divergence. The concrete losses are (i) evidence and model comparison, (ii) a posterior that the dynamics targets (stationary points of E are compromises among references and peers, not posteriors), (iii) one objective spanning interaction and data, so no monotonicity across records, and (iv) a learning criterion for r_i, G_i, pi and T. What survives exactly (verified in `s4`): E_0 is the record-free mean-field free energy of R_i (evidence 1); at frozen sources and T = 1/N the row block equals T KL(beta (x) q^N || pi (x) nu_J^N) (residual 0.0), and the Gibbs row is the exact source-label posterior given an N-sample sequence of type q_i (error 1.1e-16).
**Fix:** Add a short subsection to chapter 3 that states this inventory; defines the agent process with records as a piecewise-deterministic process (the flow of E between record times, declared assimilation at record times; Davis 1984, JRSS-B 46) and says that E is not monotone across records; and either adopts one of the restorations in L03-DYN-06 and L03-DYN-07 or states that 2.0 gives up evidence-based identification.
**Falsifies:** A fixed normalized joint, not indexed by the current laws, whose extended free energy equals E, or E plus a record term, along the full coupled flow including source-role forces; or a passage of 2.0 in which records enter the potential.

### L03-DYN-02: One potential forces a reverse influence along every edge; the agent-wise alternative is not a gradient flow; neither fact is stated
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/03_dynamics.tex:109-117 (eq:dyn-incoming), :124-145 (thm:dyn-finite-flow); chapters/04_prediction.tex:41-46; companion/c03_dynamics.tex:56
**Evidence:** The source-role force on q_j from an edge j to i is -beta_ij K_ij(q_i/nu_ij) (eq:dyn-incoming). The velocity of agent j therefore depends on the density ratio of receiver i pushed back through the adjoint of the channel, whether or not j attends to i. Line 117 says the gradient is not one-way, but not that this is a reverse message from i to j, which the sending model of chapter 4 (nu_j = (T_j)_# q_j, one direction) does not supply, nor that every agent descends the shared total E, so that the agents play an identical-interest game rather than minimizing their own costs. The alternative in which each agent descends only its own terms F_i = KL(q_i x s_i || R_i) + sum_j beta_ij D_ij + T KL(beta_i || pi_i) is not a gradient flow. (a) Cross-derivative test for F_1 = KL(q_1||q_2), F_2 = KL(q_2||q_1): the mixed partials are -1/y_1 and -1/x_1, which differ unless q_1 = q_2 (sympy, `s3`); this is the derivative criterion for exact potential games (Monderer and Shapley 1996, Thm 4.5, as cited by secondary sources; I did not check the primary text). (b) A directed 3-cycle of Bernoulli agents with F_i = KL(p_i||r_i) + w KL(p_i||p_{i+1}) gives, in log-odds, dl_i/dt = -(l_i - rho_i) - w(l_i - l_{i+1}), with Jacobian eigenvalues -1 and -1 - 3w/2 +/- i sqrt(3) w/2 (sympy). Complex eigenvalues at an equilibrium are impossible for a gradient flow in any Riemannian metric, because the linearization -g^{-1} Hess is similar to a symmetric matrix. At w = 2 the chapter-3 flow of the summed E has real spectrum (-1.037, -6.990, -7.085) and gJ symmetric to 1e-10, while the agent-wise flow has -4 +/- 1.732i and gJ asymmetry 0.497 (`s3`).
**Fix:** After eq:dyn-incoming, state that under one potential each directed edge carries influence both ways, the reverse influence being the receiver likelihood ratio through the adjoint channel, supported on what the channel reads (for a deterministic readout T the force on q_j is a function of T(v), so only the message marginal of the source moves); that the dynamics is an identical-interest game; that the non-reciprocal agent-wise dynamics has no Lyapunov function in general (the 3-cycle); and that chapter 4 must model the reverse message if the global flow is to be realized by communicating agents. Cite Fruchart, Hanai, Littlewood and Vitelli (Nature 592, 2021) for non-reciprocal dynamics.
**Falsifies:** A Riemannian metric in which the agent-wise 3-cycle field is a gradient (excluded by the complex spectrum), or a passage of 2.0 stating the reverse-message requirement that I missed.

### L03-DYN-03: The Fisher dynamics is defined and shown well-posed only on finite tables and finite-dimensional families, but the abstract and later chapters use it on general densities
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/03_dynamics.tex:119-149 (thm:dyn-finite-flow); frontmatter.tex:5; chapters/09_public_compression.tex:23, :59; chapters/10_boundary_closure.tex:100; chapters/12_open_formation.tex:12, :65
**Evidence:** thm:dyn-finite-flow assumes finite alphabets, and line 149 treats regular finite-dimensional families. The abstract says "A declared interaction potential generates coupled Fisher dynamics." Chapter 9, line 59: "Centering a logarithmic Fisher equation against A or B removes its scalar normalizer and gives an ordinary differential equation in the Banach chart"; chapter 12 uses "equal unit Fisher mobilities" for C^r densities on the bit-circle. The density-level equation, its identification as the Fisher-Rao gradient, and its dissipation identity are stated nowhere in chapter 3; each later chapter reproves local existence in its own chart and relies on an undefined general flow.
**Fix:** Add to chapter 3 a definition and a proposition. For q = e^l lambda / E_lambda e^l with l in a ball of L^infinity(lambda) modulo constants, the Fisher-Rao flow is dl/dt = -(phi_q - E_q phi_q), where phi_q is the first variation of E (eq:dyn-kl-differential supplies it: log(a/b) for a receiver, -a/b pulled back through the channel for a source). If q to phi_q is locally Lipschitz into L^infinity, Picard-Lindelof in the Banach space gives unique local solutions and dE/dt = -sum Var_q(phi_q). KL terms satisfy the hypothesis on bounded log-density balls for fixed channels whose compared densities stay bounded away from zero; law-dependent channels need a Lipschitz hypothesis on their first variation. The Fisher-Rao metric is, up to scaling, the diffeomorphism-invariant metric on densities (Chen, Huang, Huang, Reich, Stuart 2023, arXiv:2310.03597; I read the abstract only).
**Falsifies:** A general-law definition of the Fisher flow elsewhere in 2.0 that chapters 9, 10 and 12 cite.

### L03-DYN-04: Unit mobility makes the row relaxation rate equal to the row temperature, so sharp source selection implies slow rows
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/03_dynamics.tex:147 and thm:dyn-finite-flow; chapters/07_smooth_closure.tex:110; chapters/10_boundary_closure.tex:103; companion/c03_dynamics.tex:68
**Evidence:** For a row at frozen costs, h_j = D_j + T(log(beta_j/pi_j) + 1) = T log(beta_j/beta*_j) + T(1 - log Z) (sympy residual 0, `s5`). Under unit Fisher mobility this gives d/dt (l_j - l_k) = -T (l_j - l_k) for l = log(beta/beta*): log-odds contrasts relax at rate exactly T (measured 0.25000000 at T = 0.25, `s2`). Rows are therefore slow exactly when they select sharply (small T), which is the regime in which the Gibbs-reduced flow (rows held at beta*) and the full flow differ most in their transients. Chapter 7, line 110 ("Source rows evolve by their original Fisher equations with the public costs, or may be exactly optimized") and chapter 10, line 103 offer the two as alternatives without noting that their transients differ; they share equilibria and Morse indices (L03-DYN-13) but are different flows. Line 147 asserts unit mobility without justification, although one is available: unit weights are the Fisher metric of the single product law of all tables (geo:joint-fisher applied to a product), and replicating a table c times multiplies its metric by c, so a block mobility 1/c is the Fisher metric of c independent copies. The Cencov uniqueness theorem fixes the metric of each simplex only up to scale (Cencov 1982; not rechecked in the primary text), so without the product-law reading the relative mobilities are constitutive constants.
**Fix:** State the row relaxation identity and its consequence; describe exact Gibbs rows as the limit of row mobility tending to infinity (a singular perturbation in the sense of Tikhonov), not as a property of the unit-mobility flow; give the product-law justification of unit mobility or present mobilities as constitutive constants equivalent to block replication counts.
**Falsifies:** A computation showing that the beta-block relaxation rate under eq:dyn-fisher-flow at frozen costs differs from T.

### L03-DYN-05: Global existence, a uniform interior floor, and convergence hold for fixed channels without null columns; thm:dyn-finite-flow can state them
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/03_dynamics.tex:124-145 (thm:dyn-finite-flow, last sentence of the proof)
**Evidence:** Sketch for the belief sector (the model sector is identical; channels fixed, with column sums c_z > 0). Let m(t) be the minimum over agents i and states z of log q_i(z). At a minimizing pair, q_i(z) is no larger than any entry of any belief table, so nu_ij(z) is at least c_z e^m and the receiver term beta_ij(log(q_i/nu_ij) + 1) is at most beta_ij(1 - log c_z); the incoming term is nonpositive; the E_0 term is at most m + 1 + C_G with C_G = max(-log G). The mean of h under q_i is at least 1 - log|K| - N (incoming terms average to -beta_ki, KL terms are nonnegative). Hence the upper Dini derivative of m is at least -m - C with C = log|K| + N + 1 + C_G + log(1/c_min), and m(t) stays above min(m(0), -C) for all time. All D_ij are then bounded, and the same argument gives uniform floors for beta and gamma. The trajectory remains in a compact subset of the interior, so it is global; E is real analytic there and the metric is uniformly equivalent to the Euclidean one, so the Lojasiewicz gradient inequality gives convergence of every trajectory to one critical point (Lojasiewicz 1963; Absil, Mahony and Andrews, SIAM J. Optim. 16, 2005). Since E is at least E_red, which is at least E_0, which is nonnegative (Z_i is at most the sum of pi, which is 1, because D is nonnegative), the integral of sum Var over [0, infinity) is at most E(0). Numerics (`s2`): from initial entries 1e-9 with identity channels and with sparse permutation-type channels, the q and s blocks never fall below their initial minima, E is nonincreasing to 1e-15, and the field norm at t = 60 is 2e-13 and 9e-13. For law-dependent channels the channel-derivative term can be unbounded and nothing of this kind follows. This is a sketch with supporting numerics, not a written proof.
**Fix:** Add a part (ii): for law-independent channels whose compared laws are positive whenever the sources are positive, the maximal solution is global, its entries are bounded below uniformly in time, and it converges to a single critical point of E.
**Falsifies:** A trajectory with fixed channels without null columns whose minimum entry tends to zero in finite time, or whose belief-block minimum falls below min(m(0), e^{-C}).

### L03-DYN-06: Gibbs rows have an exact Bayesian identification at frozen sources and rational temperature
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** prop:dyn-gibbs (chapters/03_dynamics.tex:54); sec:dyn-likelihood (:151-184); supplements/s03_variation.tex:33-64
**Evidence:** For a sequence x^N with type q, P(x^N | nu_j) = exp(-N(H(q) + KL(q||nu_j))) (method of types; Cover and Thomas 2006, Thm 11.1.2, cited from memory). With J drawn from pi and x^N drawn independently from nu_J, P(J = j | x^N) = pi_j e^{-N D_j} / sum_k pi_k e^{-N D_k}, which is the Gibbs row at T = 1/N exactly (`s4`: 1.1e-16), and -T log P(x^N) = H(q) - T log Z (`s4`: 2.2e-16). At frozen sources the row block equals T KL(beta (x) q^N || pi (x) nu_J^N) (`s4`: 0.0). Hence for rational T_q and T_s the terms of one agent (its local term and its own rows) at frozen sources are a positive multiple of the record-free free energy of a fixed normalized product model (the agent model R_i replicated, and the source mixture replicated) under a tied-copy recognition family. Records attached to the copies give an ELBO with evidence for each frozen episode. The cost is that freezing the sources removes the source-role force, which returns the agent-wise dynamics of L03-DYN-02.
**Fix:** Add as a proposition after prop:dyn-gibbs, with the tempered (irrational T) case stated as a power likelihood. Difficulty: low for finite alphabets.
**Falsifies:** Failure of the type-class identity (it is exact).

### L03-DYN-07: A normalized law-point model restores evidence and Bayesian identification for the coupled flow
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/03_dynamics.tex:184 ("an explicitly different probability model whose variables are law points")
**Evidence:** On finite alphabets the configuration space U (the product of the open simplices of all q, s, beta, gamma tables) has finite Jeffreys volume (Gamma(1/2)^n / Gamma(n/2) per simplex). Since E is nonnegative, P_tau(du) = e^{-E(u)/tau} mu_J(du) / Z_tau is a normalized prior for every tau > 0, with Z_tau in (0, vol(U)]. Records with likelihood l(o | u), for instance o drawn from the predictive law s_i K_i^rec of eq:predictive-evaluator, give a normalized joint P_tau(du) l(o | u), evidence Z_tau(o)/Z_tau and posterior proportional to e^{-E/tau} l mu_J. The MAP flow is the Fisher gradient flow of E - tau log l, so records add a data term of size tau times count times KL(empirical || predicted), and Riemannian Langevin dynamics with the Fisher metric (Girolami and Calderhead, JRSS-B 73, 2011; on a simplex a Wright-Fisher type diffusion) samples the posterior. Law-dependent channels are admissible because they are functions of latent law points, which the earlier typing prohibition allows (latent links in the earlier theory). Numerics (`s6`): two Bernoulli agents, identity channels; Z_tau = 1.2800 at tau = 0.5 and 0.1326 at tau = 0.1 (Jeffreys volume pi^2 = 9.87); log evidence of 20 records -14.07 and -14.33. What changes: q_i becomes a latent state of a hierarchical model rather than a recognition density for a sample posterior.
**Fix:** Present this model as the normalized object whose zero-temperature MAP limit is the 2.0 flow plus record terms. Difficulty: low on finite alphabets; hard for infinite-dimensional laws, which need a prior on log-densities.
**Falsifies:** A potential E in the admissible class that is unbounded below or infinite on a set of full Jeffreys measure (excluded on finite alphabets by E being nonnegative and finite on the interior).

### L03-DYN-13: The full and Gibbs-reduced flows have the same equilibria with the same Morse indices

## Extensions and improvements

The most valuable next step is to decide what object the theory minimizes once records exist. Two exact options follow from the computations above. In the frozen-source option (L03-DYN-06), each interaction episode holds the compared laws nu_ij fixed; for rational temperatures the terms of each agent are then a positive multiple of the free energy of a fixed normalized model, namely the agent model R_i together with the source mixture J ~ pi_i, X ~ nu_iJ, both replicated N = 1/T times, under a recognition family that ties the replicated copies to one law. Attaching record kernels to the copies yields an ELBO with evidence, and prop:dyn-gibbs becomes Bayesian inference of the source label. The price is the loss of source-role forces, so the episode sequence is the agent-wise dynamics of L03-DYN-02 and has no global Lyapunov function in general; an exact sequential update of one agent with its sources frozen can raise the summed potential through the rows that attend to that agent, so the theorem to aim for is a sufficient condition (for example a symmetric divergence on reciprocal edges with equal fixed rows, which makes each pair term common to both agents and the game an exact potential game, or a small-coupling regime; reciprocal edges with the asymmetric KL do not suffice, by the cross-derivative test of L03-DYN-02) under which sequential frozen-source episodes decrease the summed potential, in the spirit of the damped block descent of the earlier c05 prop:elbo-exact-e-coordinate (iv). In the law-point option (L03-DYN-07), the Gibbs law e^{-E/tau} with respect to the Jeffreys measure is normalized on finite alphabets because E is nonnegative and the Jeffreys volume is finite; records enter through a likelihood of the predicted record law, MAP descent is the Fisher flow of E minus tau times the log-likelihood, and Fisher-Langevin dynamics samples the posterior over law configurations. This keeps the full coupled flow (including source-role forces) as the tau-to-zero limit of an inference procedure for one fixed normalized model, at the cost of reading q_i as a latent state. On finite alphabets both options are elementary; the infinite-dimensional law-point version needs a prior on log-densities and is a research problem.

The second step is to make the dynamics chapter carry the general well-posedness that later chapters use. A density-level definition (L03-DYN-03) plus a Banach-space Picard-Lindelof proposition would replace the per-chapter local arguments in chapters 9, 10 and 12. For finite tables, the min-entry argument of L03-DYN-05 gives global existence with a time-uniform floor and, by Lojasiewicz, convergence for every fixed channel without a null column; it costs one paragraph. Together with L03-DYN-13 (equal Morse indices for the full and reduced flows) this would let later chapters move between full and Gibbs-reduced rows for equilibrium statements while keeping the transient differences of L03-DYN-04 explicit.

The third step concerns interpretation. Under one potential, directed attention does not produce non-reciprocal forces: each edge carries the receiver likelihood ratio back to the source through the adjoint channel, and the agents descend a shared energy. The manuscript should say this and should model the reverse message in chapter 4 if the agents are to be realized as communicating systems; otherwise the dynamics presupposes that each source can read the laws of every receiver that attends to it. If the author prefers self-interested agents, the agent-wise dynamics is the honest model, and its loss of a Lyapunov function (the Bernoulli 3-cycle spirals) is a theorem worth stating rather than an obstacle to hide.

Finally, two concrete bridges. Identity self-sources make the reduced interaction a smooth bounded-confidence model (L03-DYN-14), which is a plausible endogenous route to the cluster structure that the formation chapters currently supply by hand; the first theorem would be stability of well-separated k-cluster equilibria at small T. And the VFE_4.0 implementation computes rows from expected comparison likelihoods; prop:sup-row-equivalence already gives the exact conditions under which its rows coincide with the 2.0 rows and the exact covariance-force discrepancy, which makes a two-mode regression test a cheap and decisive check of whether the code can serve as a test bed for 2.0 (L03-DYN-15).
