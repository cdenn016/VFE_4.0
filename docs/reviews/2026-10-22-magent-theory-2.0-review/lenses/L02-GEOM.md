# L02-GEOM: Statistical and contextual geometry

I read in full `chapters/02_geometry.tex` (lines 1-335), `appendices/base_connection.tex` (1-73), `appendices/determinant_example.tex` (1-62), `companion/c06_geometry.tex` (1-190), `companion/figures/link_transport.tex`, `chapters/01_foundations.tex` (1-119, for `def:agent-section` and `eq:statistical-bundle`), and PIFB2 `18_section.tex` (1-102), `19_section.tex` (1-215) and `20_section.tex` (1-109). I skimmed the downstream uses in `chapters/05_inheritance.tex:295-315` and `chapters/11_conditional_formation.tex:440-500`, the notation appendix, README and AUTHORING, the geometry integration record `Research/docs/research/2026-10-21-geometry-integration.md`, `VFE_4.0/docs/model-contract.md`, the vault theme page on information geometry, and the primary sources Pollard (2013, Theorem 7 and its Section 5 proof) and Ay, Jost, Le and Schwachhofer (Bernoulli 2018, introduction). The two other companion figures belong to other chapters and were not opened. Verdict: I found no false statement and no failed proof step in the geometry chapter or its two appendices; every identity I recomputed holds (symbolic residual 0, or floating residuals at or below 1e-10 against quantities of order one). The chapter is weaker than its own mathematics permits at three load-bearing points: the collectively selected tensor is the pullback of the orbit-space (shape-space) metric, so its radical is automatically integrable and the tensor automatically basic, which the chapter does not observe; the minimal tensor contracts under every fixed equivariant Markov retention with an exact two-term defect, which the chapter does not state; and the collective connection is singular exactly at agreement configurations where isotropy jumps. One remark about dominating measures is contradicted by the source it cites, and the determinant appendix cannot meet its own rank hypothesis with fewer than four proper complex Gaussian agents.

The regular statistical tier, as defined at `02_geometry.tex:9-34`, is a finite-dimensional smooth manifold of laws on a standard Borel space that is differentiable in quadratic mean (Le Cam and Pollard form, with singular part o(|delta|^2)) in every chart at every point, has a smooth positive-definite Fisher tensor after removal of redundant directions, and is stable under the smooth pushforward action of a finite-dimensional Lie group. On that tier the Fisher metric is well defined, chart independent (DQM is preserved by C^1 reparameterization) and G invariant. Infinite-dimensional families are not covered, and non-dominated families cannot occur in it (L02-GEOM-09).

## Findings

### L02-GEOM-01: The collectively selected tensor is the pullback of the shape-space metric; its radical is always integrable and the tensor always basic
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** high
**Location:** chapters/02_geometry.tex:139-145 (descent paragraph and contact example after `geo:quotient`), 173-198 (`geo:collective`, `geo:normal-equation`, `geo:minimal-pullback`)
**Evidence:** Let theta be the mechanical connection of the diagonal G-action on the collective family, theta(v) = k^{-1} G(v, J .), defined wherever ker J = 0. The normal equation `geo:normal-equation` reads A^F(X) = -k^{-1} G(dp(X), J .) = -theta(dp(X)), so A^F = -p^* theta. Pullback commutes with d and with the bracket of Lie-algebra-valued forms, so with the chapter's convention F = dA + [A ^ A] one gets F^{A^F} = -p^* Omega, where Omega = d theta - [theta ^ theta] is the curvature of theta for a left action (with [xi^*, eta^*] = -[xi, eta]^* one checks Omega(xi^*, eta^*) = 0). G-equivariance of theta makes Omega horizontal. If Z lies in ker r, then dp(Z) = -J A^F(Z) is an orbit direction, so F^{A^F}(Z, .) = 0. By the structure identity in L02-GEOM-05, ker r is then involutive and L_Z h^min = 0 for every section Z of ker r. Where the action is free and proper, H is the horizontal space of the Riemannian submersion pi: N^n -> N^n/G (O'Neill, Michigan Math. J. 13 (1966) 459-469), so h^min = (pi o p)^* g_shape, with g_shape the quotient metric and radical ker d(pi o p). Numerical check (`s07_minimal_radical.py`): Aff(1) acting on two normal laws over R^3, rank r = 2, max |F^{A^F}(Z, e_b)| = 1.75e-11 for Z in ker r against max |F| = 0.239 (central differences, step 1e-5); h^min(v + J xi) - h^min(v) = -8.9e-16 against h^min(v) = 3.35. The chapter uses the contact form dz - x dy to show that "even involutivity is an additional condition"; that example uses a non-minimal connection on a transitive family, whose minimal tensor is identically zero.
**Fix:** Add a proposition after `geo:minimal-pullback`: if ker J = 0 then A^F = -p^* theta_mech and F^{A^F} = -p^* Omega_mech, the radical of h^min is involutive, h^min is basic, and under a free proper action h^min = (pi o p)^* g_shape. Restrict the contact counterexample to non-minimal connections, and cite Littlejohn and Reinsch, Rev. Mod. Phys. 69 (1997) 213, beside Guichardet (1984) for the mechanical connection and its curvature.
**Falsifies:** A locally free isometric action and a section with constant-rank h^min for which F^{A^F}(Z, X) is nonzero for some Z in ker r, or for which ker r is not involutive.

### L02-GEOM-02: The minimal tensor contracts under every fixed equivariant Markov retention, with an exact two-term defect
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** high
**Location:** chapters/02_geometry.tex:322-334 (`geo:retained-connection`, `geo:connection-retention`, and the example "Contraction alone is insufficient"); companion/c06_geometry.tex:170-183
**Evidence:** With R, Delta = g - R^* gbar and Jbar = dR J as in `geo:retained-connection`, hbar^min(X,X) = min_a ||dR dp(X) + Jbar a||^2 <= ||dR r_X||^2 = g(r_X, r_X) - Delta(r_X, r_X). Applying `geo:minimal-pullback` at the coarse level to the representative A^F gives the exact identity

    h^min(X,X) - hbar^min(X,X) = Delta(r_X, r_X) + kbar((A^F - Abar^F)(X), (A^F - Abar^F)(X)),

independent of the coarse representative Abar^F because kbar annihilates ker Jbar. Both terms are nonnegative when Delta is positive semidefinite, in particular for a fixed Markov morphism with Fisher metrics in the same normalization. The second term vanishes exactly when `geo:connection-retention` holds. On the manuscript's own example (means (x,-x), unit noise on the first sample): h^min = 2, hbar^min = 4/3, Delta(r,r) = 1/2, kbar shift = (3/2)(1/3)^2 = 1/6, and 2 = 4/3 + 1/2 + 1/6. Random checks (`s03_collective.py`, part B: four normal laws, translation group, independent N(0, tau_i^2) noise, min eigenvalue of Delta between 1.5e-2 and 7.4e-2) give residuals at most 1.8e-15 with h^min - hbar^min between 1.56 and 4.35. So the sentence at line 334 concerns the connection; the minimal tensor itself always contracts, and the connection shift is measured exactly by the kbar term. When both bundles use collective minimizers over the same base, the horizontal defect of `geo:signed-comparison` is a_Psi = Jbar(Abar^F - A^F) and its two defect terms sum to -kbar(A^F - Abar^F) <= 0, so `geo:contraction-criterion` holds automatically.
**Fix:** State the identity as a corollary of `geo:retained-connection` and `geo:minimal-pullback` and use it in `ch:inheritance` as the geometric monotonicity statement. Combined with L02-GEOM-01 it says that the map induced by R on shape spaces is 1-Lipschitz for the quotient metrics.
**Falsifies:** A fixed equivariant retention with Delta positive semidefinite and a first jet with hbar^min(X,X) > h^min(X,X).

### L02-GEOM-03: The collective connection is singular at agreement, where infinitesimal isotropy jumps
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/02_geometry.tex:173-198 (constant-rank hypothesis of `geo:collective`); curvature claims downstream, e.g. chapters/11_conditional_formation.tex:440-500 (`form:geometry`)
**Evidence:** For GL(3) acting linearly on two Gaussian laws N(mu_i, Sigma_i), the orbit dimension is 9 for distinct generic laws and 8 when the laws agree, since a common law has stabilizer O(2) (`s08_consensus_singularity.py`). Moving the second law to distance eps from the first with a fixed first jet gives smallest eigenvalue of k equal to 9.0e-4, 9.2e-6, 9.2e-8, 9.2e-10 and |A^F| equal to 4.5, 41.6, 415, 4145 for eps = 1e-1, 1e-2, 1e-3, 1e-4, while h^min stays between 0.912 and 0.921 and the normal-equation residual stays below 4e-12. Thus |A^F| grows like 1/eps, and generically the curvature like 1/eps^2, along base loci where two sections cross transversally into agreement; this is the collision singularity of the mechanical connection. h^min stays bounded because it is the shape-space pullback (L02-GEOM-01). The program's formation chapters are about agreement, which is where the constant-rank hypothesis fails.
**Fix:** Add a remark after `geo:collective` that the hypothesis fails along agreement loci with isotropy jumps, that h^min extends continuously there while A^F and its curvature do not, and that curvature claims about the collective connection need an isotropy-free region or should be restated for h^min.
**Falsifies:** A proof that, for every admissible pair of sections reaching agreement, the jet component along the isotropy-breaking direction vanishes at rate eps, which would keep A^F bounded.

### L02-GEOM-04: For a homogeneous fiber the per-agent tensor records the connection, not the section
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/02_geometry.tex:65-102 (`geo:pullback`, `geo:gauge-connection`) and 198 ("A single transitive family has H=0")
**Evidence:** If G acts transitively on N, the orbit map is a submersion, so a local gauge g(c) makes the section constant, p = p_0. Then D^A p = J_{p_0} A and h^A = A^* k_{p_0}, with k_{p_0}(xi, eta) = g^F(J_{p_0} xi, J_{p_0} eta). As A ranges over local connection forms, h^A ranges over every positive semidefinite form of rank at most dim N; nothing gauge invariant about the section enters. More generally rank h^min <= dim H = codimension of the orbit. Counts (`s03_collective.py`, part D, rank of J at random laws): Aff(K) acts transitively on single Gaussians N(mu, Sigma) (dim H = 0 for K = 2, 3); GL(K) acting linearly has cohomogeneity one on a single K-dimensional Gaussian (dim H = 1, the invariant being mu^T Sigma^{-1} mu); for two laws under GL(K), dim H = 3K (6 and 9 for K = 2, 3); SO(K) leaves dim H = 4 and 6 for one law. VFE_4.0 admits GL(K, R) acting linearly (`docs/model-contract.md:19,65`), so for one K-dimensional Gaussian factor the minimal tensor has rank at most one.
**Fix:** State rank h^min <= dim N^n - dim(orbit) as a corollary of `geo:collective`, and note after `geo:gauge-connection` that for transitive fibers h^omega_zeta = A^* k_{p_0} in the constant gauge, so per-agent contextual geometry is a property of the supplied connection (`open:conn-derived`).
**Falsifies:** A transitive G-family and section for which h^omega_zeta records a gauge-invariant function of zeta alone, independent of omega.

### L02-GEOM-05: Integrability and basicness of the radical are exact curvature conditions for any connection
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/02_geometry.tex:122-147 (`geo:quotient`, `geo:rank`, the descent paragraph and the contact example)
**Evidence:** In a local trivialization write V = D^A p and j(p, xi) = J_p xi. For arbitrary base vector fields X, Y,

    V([X,Y]) = X(VY) - Y(VX) - d_p j(VX, A(Y)) + d_p j(VY, A(X)) - J_p F(X,Y),   F = dA + [A ^ A].

With nabla^A_X W = X(W) + Gamma(VX, W) + d_p j(W, A(X)), where Gamma are the Christoffel symbols of g^F (equivalently, the pullback Levi-Civita derivative plus nabla_W of the Killing field A(X)^*, hence metric), this reads V([X,Y]) = nabla^A_X VY - nabla^A_Y VX - J_p F(X,Y), and

    (L_Z h)(X,Y) = g(nabla^A_X VZ + J_p F(Z,X), VY) + g(VX, nabla^A_Y VZ + J_p F(Z,Y)).

Hence ker V is involutive exactly when J_p F(X,Y) = 0 for X, Y in ker V, and an involutive radical carries a basic h exactly when g(J_p F(Z,X), VY) + g(VX, J_p F(Z,Y)) = 0 for Z in ker V. Symbolic check (`s02_covariant_pullback.py`, parts c and d): Aff(1) acting on N(mu, sigma^2), random rational polynomial section, connection and fields on R^3, residual exactly 0 for both identities at a rational point where V([X,Y]) = (44.5, 86.7) and L_Z h(X,Y) = -984.4; metric-compatibility residual 0. The contact example satisfies V([d_x, d_y + x d_z]) = 1 = -F(d_x, d_y + x d_z) with F = -dx ^ dy. Flat connections, including the VFE_4.0 reference connection A = -dU U^{-1} (`model-contract.md:9`), therefore always give an involutive radical and a basic tensor, h being the Fisher pullback of the reference section. I did not locate a primary-source locator for this structure equation of the vertical differential; it is verified here symbolically, which supports but does not prove the general identity, whose derivation is three lines from the definitions.
**Fix:** Replace the counterexample-only discussion by this criterion, keep the contact example as the case F = -dx ^ dy, and record the flat case.
**Falsifies:** A nonzero residual of either identity for another smooth isometric action, or a flat connection with a non-involutive constant-rank radical.

### L02-GEOM-06: The determinant soldering cannot be an isomorphism with fewer than four proper complex Gaussian agents
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** appendices/determinant_example.tex:7-43 (`geo:det-moment`, `geo:determinant-metric`); chapters/02_geometry.tex:212
**Evidence:** For centered proper complex Gaussians CN(0, M_i) with Fisher metric w_i tr(M_i^{-1} h M_i^{-1} k) and GL(2,C) acting by M -> g M g^dagger, the Fisher-orthogonal complement of the orbit is H = {h : sum_i w_i h_i M_i^{-1} = 0}; the central U(1) acts trivially, so rank J = 7 for n >= 3; and dM(h) = sum_i w_i h_i. Numerically (`s04_determinant.py` part 4, `s05_det_rank_probe.py`): (rank J, dim H, rank dM|_H) = (4,0,0), (6,2,2), (7,5,3), (7,9,4) for n = 1, 2, 3, 4 in five random draws each; for n = 3 the fourth singular value is 5.6e-16 and 3.5e-16 against 0.39 to 1.17 for the others. For n = 3 the deficit is structural: with k_i = w_i h_i, A = M_1^{-1} - M_3^{-1} and B = M_2^{-1} - M_3^{-1}, the kernel of k -> (sum k_i, sum k_i M_i^{-1}) contains (A^{-1}, -B^{-1}, B^{-1} - A^{-1}) and (A^{-1} B A^{-1}, -A^{-1}, A^{-1} - A^{-1} B A^{-1}) (residuals at most 2e-15), one more than the generic count 12 - 4 - 7 = 1, so rank dM|_H <= 5 - 2 = 3 at generic triples and, by lower semicontinuity of rank on the constant-rank set, at every triple with rank J = 7. The isomorphism hypothesis of `geo:determinant-metric` on a four-dimensional base, and the availability sentence at `02_geometry.tex:212`, therefore need at least four agents in this family. The appendix already notes that the phase component Im tr A^F is undetermined for it.
**Fix:** Add a worked proposition: for proper complex Gaussian target laws, rank e <= 0, 2, 3 for n = 1, 2, 3, with generic rank 4 for n = 4 (numerical); and state which families (non-circular, non-Gaussian, with shape parameters coupled to second moments) relax the bound.
**Falsifies:** A triple of positive-definite 2x2 Hermitian matrices with rank J = 7 and rank dM|_H = 4.

### L02-GEOM-07: The determinant scale has a canonical choice, kappa = det M, which writes the Lorentzian form as half a Fisher metric minus a log-volume term and makes exact solderings curved
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** appendices/determinant_example.tex:21-43 (`geo:determinant-metric`, `geo:determinant-form`); PIFB2 19_section.tex:163 and 167
**Evidence:** det(g M g^dagger) = |det g|^2 det M, so kappa_det = det M is an admissible compensator whenever M is positive definite, constructed from the same data as e. Then gamma(X,X) = -det(e(X))/det M = (1/2) tr((M^{-1} e(X))^2) - (1/2) (tr M^{-1} e(X))^2 (residual -3.1e-16), and (1/2) tr((M^{-1} e)^2) is one half of the Fisher metric of CN(0, M) evaluated on e (checked through the real 4x4 representation, residual 0). The negative direction is the log-volume rate tr M^{-1} e, so the indefinite signature is a rank-one subtraction from a Fisher-type metric, a DeWitt-type metric on positive Hermitian matrices; the dilation direction e = M is timelike (gamma = -1). For an exact soldering e = dM, gamma is the pullback by M of the GL(2,C)-invariant metric -det(dM)/det M on the positive cone, which is R x H^3; its scalar curvature is -6 (`s04_determinant.py` part 2, sympy, exact). PIFB2 19:163 states that an exact soldering gives a flat metric; that holds for constant kappa or after reduction to |det g| = 1, not for this scale.
**Fix:** Record kappa = det M as the canonical scale (or say why it is excluded), add the Fisher-minus-trace decomposition to the appendix, and restrict PIFB2's flatness remark to constant kappa.
**Falsifies:** A nonzero residual in the decomposition, or a scalar curvature other than -6 for -det(dM)/det M on the positive Hermitian cone.

### L02-GEOM-08: A global minimizing connection exists when infinitesimal isotropy is nonzero, and the curvature on the radical is class invariant
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/02_geometry.tex:198; companion/c06_geometry.tex:150
**Evidence:** At constant rank the minimizers at c form an affine subspace A^F(c) + T_c^* U (x) stab(p(c)), and being minimizing is a frame-independent property of a connection (last sentence of `geo:collective`). Given local smooth minimizing connections omega_alpha and a partition of unity phi_alpha, omega = sum phi_alpha omega_alpha is a connection (an affine combination) and is minimizing at every c (an affine combination of points of an affine subspace). So a global smooth representative exists on any paracompact domain; what requires ker J = 0 is uniqueness and naturality, not gluing. Applying the structure identity of L02-GEOM-05 to A and A + beta with beta stabilizer valued (same V) gives J_p F^{A+beta}(X,Y) = J_p F^A(X,Y) - d_p j(VX, beta(Y)) + d_p j(VY, beta(X)), so J_p F restricted to the radical does not depend on the representative, while the full curvature does.
**Fix:** Replace "If ker J = 0, the unique local forms ... glue" by: global minimizing representatives always exist by a partition of unity; uniqueness holds exactly when ker J = 0; curvature statements about a class should be made for J_zeta F on the radical or after a representative is fixed.
**Falsifies:** A constant-rank configuration over a paracompact base admitting local minimizing connections but no global one.

### L02-GEOM-09: Every admitted regular family is dominated, and the cited source says so
**Category:** inflation
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/02_geometry.tex:26 (remark after `geo:regular-family`)
**Evidence:** The text says the hypotheses "allow parameter-dependent supports satisfying (geo:dqm), without a common dominating measure". DQM at each theta gives a squared Hellinger distance O(|delta|^2) between P_theta and P_{theta+delta}, so theta -> P_theta is continuous in total variation. A finite-dimensional manifold is second countable; for a countable dense set {theta_n}, mu = sum_n 2^{-n} P_{theta_n} dominates every P_theta (if mu(A) = 0 then every P_{theta_n}(A) = 0, and P_theta(A) is a limit of such values). The cited Ay, Jost, Le and Schwachhofer, Bernoulli 24 (2018), introduction (arXiv:1510.07305, page 2), states: "While the existence of a measure mu dominating all measures p(xi) is satisfied for example, if M is a finite dimensional manifold, the condition that all measures p(xi) have the same null sets is a more severe restriction". The generality is in the null sets, not in domination.
**Fix:** Replace the sentence by: "allow parameter-dependent supports, that is, laws with different null sets; such finite-dimensional families are dominated by a countable mixture, but no common positive density is required".
**Falsifies:** A finite-dimensional family satisfying `geo:dqm` at every point with no sigma-finite dominating measure.

## Comparison with PIFB2 sections 18 to 20

PIFB2 18:59 asks for a reason to select the comparison rule. 2.0's collective least squares supplies one, and by L02-GEOM-01 the selected tensor is connection free (the shape-space pullback), while the per-agent tensor remains connection data (L02-GEOM-04).

Section 19 (Lorentz geometry). ## Extensions and improvements

Shape-space formulation of the collective tensor (L02-GEOM-01). The cleanest restatement of the collective section is to define the minimal tensor without a connection: for a collective section p: U -> N^n and a free proper isometric action, h^min = (pi o p)^* g_shape, where g_shape is the quotient metric of the Riemannian submersion pi: N^n -> N^n/G. The connection then becomes a derived object, A^F = -p^* theta_mech, with curvature -p^* Omega_mech, and the radical is the kernel of the shape map, so local descent is automatic and the only remaining global issue is the leaf space of a regular foliation. For two Gaussian laws under GL(K) the shape space has dimension 3K (`s03` part D), with coordinates given by the K generalized eigenvalues of Sigma_2 relative to Sigma_1 and the two means written in a simultaneous whitening frame, modulo the finite sign group; this gives explicit coordinates in which h^min can be computed. Difficulty: low for the general proposition, moderate for explicit Gaussian shape metrics.

Monotone inheritance of the minimal tensor (L02-GEOM-02). For a chain of fixed equivariant retentions R_01, R_12 the identity telescopes: h^min_0 - h^min_2 = [Delta_01(r_0, r_0) + k_1(A_0 - A_1)] + [Delta_12(r_1, r_1) + k_2(A_1 - A_2)], with r_1 the level-one residual and A_a the minimizers at level a, each bracket nonnegative. This is the collective analogue of `geo:defect-composition` and gives `ch:inheritance` a quantitative geometric monotonicity with an exact accounting of connection drift. Difficulty: low.

Probabilistic reading of the weights. The weighted metric G = sum_i w_i g^F_{p_i} is not the Fisher metric of the product law unless all w_i = 1. It is the Fisher metric, in the p-directions, of the labeled family R = sum_i w_i delta_i (x) p_i, which is PIFB2's eq:labeled_collective_fisher. If the weights depend on context, the weight score d log w_I has zero Fisher pairing with every orbit score because G acts on records and not on labels, so A^F is unchanged and h^min gains the term sum_i dw_i (x) dw_i / w_i (`s03` part C: pairing 0.0 and identity residual 0.0 by Gauss-Hermite quadrature). This lets attention-dependent weights enter the collective geometry without changing the selected connection, and gives "the same normalization" in `geo:retained-connection` a definite meaning. Difficulty: low.

Agreement loci (L02-GEOM-03). Three routes are available. Statements can be made for h^min alone, which is continuous across agreement. One can stratify by orbit type and work on the agreement stratum with its larger stabilizer. Or one can regularize the normal equation by A_lambda = -(k + lambda B)^{-1} J^T G dp; this is gauge covariant only if B is an Ad-invariant positive form on the Lie algebra, which exists for compact G but not for GL(K), so for the program's group a regularized connection breaks passive covariance. The first route is the one compatible with the chapter's covariance standard. Difficulty: moderate.

Determinant geometry (L02-GEOM-06, L02-GEOM-07). With kappa = det M the conditional Lorentzian form is a DeWitt-type metric built from the complex-Gaussian Fisher metric of the aggregate moment, with the log-volume rate as its timelike direction. Two concrete next results follow. First, for n >= 4 proper complex Gaussian targets, show that first jets with invertible e form an open dense set (numerically the generic rank is 4) and then test whether the dynamics keeps det e nonzero on a finite window. Second, the central phase that leaves Im tr A^F undetermined for proper laws is broken by improper (non-circular) complex laws: the pseudo-covariance P = E[z z^T] transforms as g P g^T, so a central phase e^{i phi} multiplies it by e^{2 i phi}, J(iI) is nonzero when P is nonzero, and the Fisher normal equation then determines the phase component, making omega_det selected rather than supplied. Difficulty: moderate.

Bridge to VFE_4.0. The implementation uses GL(K, R) acting linearly and the flat reference connection A = -dU U^{-1} (`docs/model-contract.md:9,19`). By L02-GEOM-05 its per-agent contextual tensor is then the Fisher pullback of the reference coefficient section, with an involutive radical and a basic tensor, so no quotient obstruction arises in the implemented model. A population diagnostic for h^min needs only G and J on the patch coefficient Gaussians and finite differences of the reference coefficients between neighboring grid contexts, one solve with the K^2 x K^2 locked-inertia matrix per context; its rank, bounded as in L02-GEOM-04, is the observable that the formation chapters' "rank-two contextual geometry" claims would predict. Difficulty: low.

Justifying the fiber metric. `geo:fisher-loss` is the monotonicity property that characterizes the Fisher metric up to scale: Cencov (1982), Theorem 11.1, p. 159, on finite sample spaces, and the extensions of Ay, Jost, Le and Schwachhofer (Probab. Theory Relat. Fields 162 (2015); Springer 2017) and Le (2017) beyond finite spaces, under their stated hypotheses. Citing these would justify the choice of g^F in `geo:regular-family`; they do not fix the collective weights w_i, which remain constitutive. I did not re-verify the locators of the extension theorems.
