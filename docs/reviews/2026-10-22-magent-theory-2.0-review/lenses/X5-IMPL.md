# X5-IMPL: Implementability and alignment with VFE_4.0

I read the following in full. In VFE_4.0: `README.md`, `docs/model-contract.md` (1-85), `docs/implementation/progress.md` (1-129), and the modules `vfe4/state.py`, `objective.py`, `inference.py`, `model.py`, `latent_links.py`, `gaussian.py`, `source_learning.py`, `analytics/*.py`, `geometry/supports.py`, `geometry/transport.py`, `geometry/groups.py`, `exchange.py`, and the `DEFAULTS` dictionary of `presets.py`. In the manuscript M: chapters 01, 02_interactions, 02_geometry, 03, 04, 05, 06, 07, 09, 10, 11 and 12 (every line), supplement `s03_variation`, appendices `record_process` and `base_connection`, `README.md`, `AUTHORING.md` and `frontmatter.tex`. I skimmed `vfe4/config.py` and `run_simulation.py` by grep, the 2026-10-22 derivation record (`final-report.md`, grep only), the earlier L11 implementability lens (its headings and first finding), and companion `c03_dynamics` (grep). I did not read chapter 08, the other companion chapters, supplements s01, s02 and s04, or the appendices `determinant_example` and `notation`. The verdict leads. Every 2.0 identity I recomputed within scope holds, to machine precision or to the finite-difference floor. VFE_4.0 and 2.0 nevertheless implement different objects. VFE_4.0 realizes the fixed-record-model free energy, which 2.0 sets aside as "a second construction". Its Gaussian agents are not an invariant family of the 2.0 peer-KL flow, it advances in CAVI iterations rather than Fisher time, its external evidence has no place in the 2.0 potential, and most of its analytics have no 2.0 definition. Four 2.0 constructions are exact finite tests today: depth-two boundary closure, the four-agent Wishart example, lognormal fusion, and the binary public-phase subfamily. I built working prototypes of their core identities. The six-agent bit-circle construction is not a finite test as stated, because its constants are nonconstructive and its native scaling is numerically degenerate. A moderate-size instance of its limiting field does reproduce the event structure numerically.

## Findings

### X5-IMPL-01: VFE_4.0 implements the fixed record-model VFE, not the 2.0 law potential, and no configuration reaches the 2.0 route
**Category:** consistency
**Severity:** high
**Location:** VFE_4.0 `vfe4/objective.py:28-55` (evaluate_vfe), `vfe4/model.py:128-147` (bank_costs), `vfe4/model.py:236-258` (synthetic communication records), `vfe4/objective.py:23-25` (optimal_rows); M `chapters/03_dynamics.tex:35-49` (def:dyn-potential), `:151-162` (sec:dyn-likelihood), `:164-176` (prop:dyn-jensen), `supplements/s03_variation.tex:33-64` (prop:sup-row-equivalence)
**Evidence:** In VFE_4.0 the interaction term is `bank_costs`. It equals E[-log N(o; A k_i - B k_j, Gamma)], with the complete residual covariance (this is exactly prop:sup-residual-cost) and a synthetic record o generated from the truth fixture for each row. Rows are softmax(log pi - cost) at unit temperature. In 2.0 the dynamics chapter uses the law potential of eq:dyn-potential. Its peer divergence is D_ij = KL(q_i || q_j K_ij), with temperatures T_q and T_s and no record. The chapter calls the record model "a second construction" (03_dynamics.tex:4), and the companion repeats this (c03:86). The two costs differ by an exact, source-dependent term. For A = B = I, o = 0, independent Gaussian laws and K(v,.) = N(v,R), prop:dyn-jensen gives C = H(q_i) + KL(q_i || q_j K) + E_{q_i} KL(q_j || q_j^w). Script `s8_vfe4_vs_2p0.py` (a) evaluates both sides in closed form for a random K=2 example, with residual 0.0 and a Jensen gap of 8.238 nats. Because the gap depends on the source covariance, the rows change their preferred source. In s8 (b), the VFE_4.0 row is (0.849, 0.016, 0.135), the 2.0 identity-channel Gibbs row at T = 1 is (0.077, 0.919, 0.004), and the noisy-channel row is (0.274, 0.624, 0.102). The self candidate also differs. VFE_4.0 merges A - B (model.py:96-101), so the self cost is the law-independent c_R + o^T R^{-1} o/2 with the row's own record (eq:sup-self-cost), whereas 2.0 assigns D_ii = 0 to an identity self-channel. No key in `presets.DEFAULTS` selects a law-to-law comparison. The local term is aligned: in point mode, model_prior + bridge + recognition_entropy equals the sum over i of KL(q_i (x) s_i || r_i G_i), which is eq:dyn-local-joint. I checked this by reading the code.
**Fix:** Add a separately named objective route, `law_potential`. It evaluates the local KL, the receiver peer divergences D^q_ij = KL(N(mu_i,Sigma_i) || N(Theta mu_j, Theta Sigma_j Theta^T + Lambda)) in closed form, T_q KL(beta||pi), and the model-sector analogues. Keep the record route as the declared fixed-model alternative, and rename its scalar "fixed-record VFE" in outputs and figures.
**Falsifies:** A VFE_4.0 configuration whose interaction term is KL(q_i || q_j K), or a 2.0 statement that identifies the expected record NLL with the peer divergence outside the restricted case of prop:sup-row-equivalence (common held W, R = TW).

### X5-IMPL-02: The Gaussian family is not invariant under the 2.0 smooth peer-KL flow once an agent is a source, and 2.0 states no finite-dimensional realization for vector agents
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** high
**Location:** M `chapters/03_dynamics.tex:102-117` (eq:dyn-channel-differential, eq:dyn-incoming), `:149` (regular-family flow), `chapters/09_public_compression.tex:184-188` (eq:public-restricted-force); VFE_4.0 `vfe4/inference.py:69-122` (_assemble)
**Evidence:** Let agent k attend to source i through an identity channel. The functional derivative of beta KL(q_k || q_i) with respect to q_i is -beta q_k/q_i. The unit Fisher flow therefore contains the inflow d/dt q_i = ... + beta (q_k - q_i), the mixture-geodesic term that 2.0 itself displays in eq:public-restricted-force. For Gaussian q_k different from q_i, log(q_k/q_i) is a nonconstant quadratic, so q_k/q_i is the exponential of a quadratic and lies outside span{1, v, v v^T}. A linear-Gaussian channel N(Theta v, Lambda) gives the same conclusion: its force is the integral of q_k(u)/nu(u) N(u; Theta v, Lambda) du, again an exponential of a quadratic. Script s8 (e) uses q_i = N(0,1), q_j = N(0.5,1), q_k = N(1,1.3^2) and r = N(0,4). With the receiver and local terms only, the Fisher norm of d/dt log q_i lying outside the Gaussian tangent space is a fraction 1.3e-13 of the total. With the sender role added it is 0.926. The sender block is also non-conjugate: grad_{Sigma_j} KL(q_i || N(Theta mu_j, S)) = Theta^T [S^{-1} - S^{-1}(Sigma_i + delta delta^T) S^{-1}] Theta / 2, with S = Theta Sigma_j Theta^T + Lambda. It has no closed-form block minimizer, so the precision/information assembly of VFE_4.0's CAVI has no 2.0 counterpart for the sender role. 2.0 admits restricted natural-gradient flows on regular families (03_dynamics.tex:149), but the inheritance and formation theorems of chapters 07, 09, 10 and 12 are proved on full density charts. Chapter 11 avoids the issue only because its channel ignores the source sample, which kills the incoming term.
**Fix:** In chapter 03, define the declared restricted-family flow theta-dot = -g(theta)^{-1} dE on an admitted family N, with the Gaussian formulas mu-dot = -Sigma grad_mu E and Sigma-dot = -2 Sigma (grad_Sigma E) Sigma. State that chapters 05 to 12 concern full charts unless their results are re-proved for N. Alternatively, identify the channel classes that keep a finite family invariant, namely constant-in-input kernels as in form:channel.
**Falsifies:** A nontrivial sender term under which the Gaussian product family is invariant under the full flow, or a 2.0 theorem stated for projected Gaussian dynamics.

### X5-IMPL-03: The 2.0 potential has no persistent evidence term, so records act only transiently and observation-driven inference has no 2.0 formulation
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** high
**Location:** M `chapters/03_dynamics.tex:35-49` (def:dyn-potential), `:151-160` (eq:dyn-fixed-vfe), `chapters/04_prediction.tex:48-54` (eq:bayesian-reception), `chapters/07_smooth_closure.tex:206-219` (smooth:feedback), `chapters/10_boundary_closure.tex:144-153` (eq:boundary-tree-feedback); VFE_4.0 `vfe4/model.py:220-225` (private records), `vfe4/analytics/observables.py:83` (field_rmse)
**Evidence:** The stationary set of the law potential is determined by the references, evaluators, channels, priors and temperatures. None of these depends on records. In 2.0, records enter only as exact conditioning events: q goes to l q/Z in eq:bayesian-reception, eq:boundary-tree-feedback and smooth:feedback, after which the flow continues to dissipate the same E (eq:dyn-dissipation). For example, in chapter 10 the profile force h^q of eq:boundary-tree-flow does not depend on o after f+ = L f/Z_o. In chapter 07, the spectrum solution xi^t = (r -> a r - log Z(a))_# xi^0 with a = e^{-t} relaxes back toward the reference whatever the conditioned initial spectrum. Chapter 07:219 requires only that any record-updated reference "must also be retained", and gives no such rule. VFE_4.0's purpose is reconstructing a field from persistent private and communication observations, scored by field_rmse. That purpose cannot be stated inside the 2.0 potential.
**Fix:** Declare an evidence-augmented potential, E_o = E + sum_i E_{q_i}[-log l_i(o_i | .)], which is compatible with eq:dyn-fixed-vfe, or a reference-learning rule. Then state which constructions survive it; X5-IMPL-14 shows that chapter 10 does.
**Falsifies:** A 2.0 passage that defines persistent record dependence of E, or of the references, through a stated rule.

### X5-IMPL-05: VFE_4.0's default damped CAVI does not run in Fisher time; only `fisher_natural` with equal weights approximates the 2.0 flow, and only for VFE_4.0's own objective
**Category:** consistency
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** VFE_4.0 `vfe4/gaussian.py:51-61` (damp_moments), `vfe4/inference.py:151-165`, `vfe4/presets.py:28-29`; M thm:dyn-finite-flow, `chapters/03_dynamics.tex:147` (unit mobility), form:first-times, form:second-time, open:routing, fus:collision-times
**Evidence:** `damp_moments` interpolates the expectation parameters (mu, Sigma + mu mu^T). That is an Euler step of the mixture-coordinate relaxation eta-dot = -(eta - eta*), not of the Fisher flow theta-dot = -(theta - theta*) in natural coordinates. `fisher_natural` interpolates (P, P mu), the explicit Euler step of the Gaussian Fisher flow for a conjugate block. Script s8 (c) relaxes one scalar block from N(0, 10) toward N(1, 0.1) and records the time to reach mean 0.5. Moment interpolation takes 0.69 to 0.70 per unit of cumulative weight at every w, natural interpolation takes 0.0100 for w <= 0.01, and the exact flow takes 0.0100. The 2.0 formation claims are original-time trajectory claims: t_L, t_R and t_2 in chapter 11, the windows in chapter 12, and t_1, t_2 in chapter 06. Only a Fisher-time integrator can test them. Chapter 03 states unit mobility for both sectors. The 2.0-consistent setting is reachable in VFE_4.0 (fisher_natural, belief_weight = model_weight = dt, model_every = 1), but it integrates the fixed-record VFE (X5-IMPL-01). The default model_weight = 0.1 is a working toggle, not a defect.
**Fix:** Record the cumulative flow time (the sum of weights per sweep) in history. For any 2.0 time-level test, require `fisher_natural` with equal weights and model_every = 1. Add an RK integrator in natural or log-density coordinates for time-accurate tests.
**Falsifies:** A demonstration that the moment-interpolated mean path coincides with the Fisher-flow mean path when P differs from P*. The s8 example shows it does not.

### X5-IMPL-06: No discretization, event-location rule or tie convention anywhere in 2.0, while its main claims are event times and windows
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** M `chapters/06_fusion.tex:370-371` ("No discrete numerical update has been specified"), thm:dyn-finite-flow, `chapters/11_conditional_formation.tex:277-290` (form:selector, "first reaches delta from above", simultaneous edges), `chapters/12_open_formation.tex:86-94` (open:selector, "strict inequalities hold immediately afterward"), open:anchor (phase lifts), thm:boundary-tree-inheritance (representatives "by recursively ordering"); VFE_4.0 `vfe4/inference.py:210-212`

### X5-IMPL-07: The six-agent construction is nonconstructive and numerically degenerate in its native coordinates, so it is not a finite test as stated
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** M `chapters/12_open_formation.tex:128-139` (T = h^2, "one sufficiently small fixed h"), `:179` (A >= 4096), `:216-231` (R "sufficiently large and finite"); derivation record `docs/derivations/2026-10-22-unprepared-recursive-formation/final-report.md:6` ("no formal proof system or numerical experiment was used")
**Evidence:** The proof needs h max|u| << 1 with max|u| >= R > (A + 6) e^L, where L >= log(A/4)/2 + S. Since t_A lies between log(A/4)/2 and log(A/4) and 0 < S <= log(4/sqrt(log(4)/2)), at A = 4096 L is at least 3.5 and can reach about 8.5, so R is of order 10^5 to 10^7 and h must be about 1e-7 to 1e-9 or below. In these coordinates the Gibbs costs d(x,z)/T are ratios of quantities near 1e-14 to 1e-18. In s5, the naive evaluation of h^{-2} d(hu, hv) for u = 3, v = 1 returns 2.0000998 at h = 1e-6 and 2.3347 at h = 1e-8, against the exact limit 2. The log1p form returns 2.0000000172 at h = 1e-8. **Fix:** State the construction in the rescaled coordinates u, with the exact h-dependent field written through stable divergence evaluations. Give one explicit instance (for example A = 6, R = 30, b_0 = 2 and a stated h) and certify its windows and crossing signs by validated interval integration. The existence theorem then becomes a reproducible finite test.
**Falsifies:** An explicit (A, R, h) in the manuscript or derivation record, together with a certificate of the event windows.

### X5-IMPL-08: VFE_4.0 agents are laws on fields with spatial records, whereas 2.0 agents are sections of pointwise laws with no context coupling in the potential
**Category:** consistency
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** VFE_4.0 `vfe4/state.py:72-115` (patch Gaussian with full PK by PK covariance), `vfe4/objective.py:39-40` (patch entropy), `vfe4/model.py:157-176, 226-231` (spatial zero-difference records); M def:agent-section, `chapters/01_foundations.tex:29-31`, geo:pullback, geo:transported-kl
**Evidence:** 2.0 defines an agent as a section c -> zeta_i(c) of pointwise laws. It states that "a collection of pointwise marginals also does not determine a probability law on fields". It formulates dynamics at one context c*, and contexts relate only through connections and the pullback tensor. VFE_4.0 instead puts one Gaussian on the whole coefficient patch, with a per-node prior and bridge and the patch joint entropy. By the chain rule KL(Q || (x)_c R_c) = sum_c KL(Q_c || R_c) + KL(Q || (x)_c Q_c), its local term exceeds the sum of 2.0 pointwise local terms by the patch multi-information. It also adds zero-difference Gaussian records between neighboring contexts, which have no counterpart in def:dyn-potential. Only point mode, or contexts evolved independently, aligns with 2.0.
**Fix:** For a 2.0 route, use pointwise laws, or declare a contextual coupling in 2.0. One option is sum over c ~ c' of w KL(zeta(c) || (T_{c'->c})_# zeta(c')), whose expansion is w delta^2 h^omega(X,X)/2 + o(delta^2) by geo:transported-kl. It would then need its own inheritance analysis.
**Falsifies:** A 2.0 definition of an agent law on fields, or a context-coupling term in the potential.

## Extensions and improvements

The most valuable next step for the program is the conformance suite of X5-IMPL-04. It is built from finite computations that need no Gaussian engine, and it pins every claim that VFE_4.0 will later be asked to realize. The specifications follow, one construction per paragraph.

**Depth-two boundary closure** (chapter 10, d = 2, N = 4). Take a finite base r with uniform A and a finite model base w. Choose any positive profiles f on r x {+1,-1}^2 and g on w x {+1,-1}^2, and a coupled rho(r,w) = exp(J phi psi)/cosh J. Lift to fine laws on r x {+1,-1}^3, where the signs are root, left and right. The circles and phase functions enter the dynamics only through these fair signs (X5-IMPL-15). Integrate the fine four-agent identity-channel flow, with dynamic rows or Gibbs-reduced rows, alongside the profile flow eq:boundary-tree-flow and the row equations eq:boundary-tree-rows. Everything is a finite sum, and nothing is sampled. The hazards are the incoming ratio P_k f/f, which becomes stiff when profile values spread, so the flow should be integrated in the log chart, and the representatives obtained by sorting, which are discontinuous at ties. The phase-rotation statement eq:boundary-tree-tv is not visible in the finite reduction and needs circle quadrature. The observables are: the commutation of lifted and fine trajectories, not only velocities (s3 gives 4.4e-16 at t = 0); D_k against the fine KL; the class-0 Jensen gap (positive) and class-1 gap (zero); response densities f_l; commutation of feedback with a base likelihood; invariance of descriptors under branch exchanges; and persistence of the witness eq:boundary-tree-witness over a positive interval.

**Four-agent Wishart** (chapter 11). The shapes are closed form, alpha_i(t) = alpha_0 + e^{-2t}(alpha_i(0) - alpha_0), and so are the selector events t = (1/2) log(coefficient/delta) and the parent equations. The only integration is the nonautonomous scalar equation form:eigenvalue, together with its variational equation for d p_+-/da, which supplies k_1 and the curvature. Responses are sampled. In this convention W_2(nu, P) is the standard Wishart law with 2 nu degrees of freedom and scale P^{-1}/2. Bartlett sampling uses chi^2 variables with 2 nu and 2 nu - 1 degrees of freedom, which are generally non-integer; this conversion is worth stating in the text. The hazards are shapes near 1/2, where Gamma(nu - 1/2) diverges; eigenvalues near 0, where 2 kappa alpha-bar/p is large but bounded by the epsilon barrier; the measure-zero simultaneity case d_1 = d_3; and the "from above" event semantics. The observables are: the law e^{-2t} for P_i - P_j; the natural gradient against form:ode (s4); the effect of freezing the channel derivative (1.61 in s4); event times; equality of parent and fine velocities; the fine-energy excess example; the model-shape loss witness, which leaves every event time unchanged; exposure of shape sums by queries that cut a block, Cov(tr U X_C, tr V X_D) = A_{C cap D} tr(P^{-1} U P^{-1} V); and the sign of the curvature form:curvature.

**Lognormal fusion** (chapter 06). Work in log coordinates, where the laws are Gaussian with fixed variance 1/n. On the common-offset family, the exact solution is event-driven sticky motion in tau, mapped back by t = log(1 + kappa tau)/kappa. General initial data, where the two sectors stick at different times, need the proximal step of X5-IMPL-06. Event times, v(t) and the energies are closed form. The boundary messages B_n and the joint reconstruction kernel fus:reconstruction are Gaussian algebra and can be tested exactly or by sampling. The conditional covariance -1/n off the diagonal is the classic error to test for. The hazards are explicit-step chatter (s7), and the multiplicity scaling n_i n_j in the pair terms against the mobility 1/n_i. The observables are t_1 and t_2, the classification by the open gap (necessary and sufficient, X5-IMPL-17), the contraction rate a - c, the identity for dF/dt, and the handoff identity with constant (n_I - 1) C.

**Public-compression phase** (chapter 09). The finite test is the invariant binary two-agent subfamily q_i = s_i. Integrate the fine equation eq:public-phase-binary-fine forward and the diagonal equation f_b backward, averaging in the logit chart. Backward integration amplifies errors by at most e^{LT} with L = 1 + 2|J|, while the residual decays as e^{-2bT}, so T near 6 suffices (s1). The observables are the coefficient c, the intertwining Pi(Phi_t x) = phi_t(Pi x) along trajectories, the response mismatch between m and p, and Gibbs identifiability through the Prony reconstruction of chapter 12 from m_0, ..., m_3. That inverse is ill-conditioned when the within-pair KL is small, which is a quantitative test of the "not exactly redundant" remark. A finite version of the Banach theorem itself uses A and B finite, a balanced two-point factor times an n-point space, with N agents.

**Six-agent bit-circle** (chapter 12). Integrate the binary 12-dimensional field, which splits into two identical 6-dimensional sectors, in the rescaled coordinates u with stable divergence evaluation (X5-IMPL-07). The angular factors are closed form (open:angular-flow, von Mises with concentration kappa e^{-2t}). The open-law transfer needs a spectral circle grid in the log-density chart. The parent needs anchor phases, continuous unwrapping, and the moving-representative term of open:moving-flow, with a spectral derivative in theta. The geometry is closed form in Bessel functions. Responses are sampled; the first moment of the angle sum is the product of the u_i. The observables are the block rows and contrasts, event times and coexistence windows; the difference between the parent flow and the canonicalized fine flow, which should sit at integrator tolerance; equality of response laws; F_t and h_chi-chi; and the 32/3 generated-loss example (s5).

Beyond the suite, three items have the highest scientific payoff. The first is the 2.0-native Gaussian route with its projection oracle (X5-IMPL-12). It turns the qualitative mismatch of X5-IMPL-02 into a measured number, |P_perp V|_g/|V|_g, and tells the author whether a Gaussian implementation can stand in for the full-density theory in any regime. Sender roles with small within-pair KL are a natural candidate for small projection error, since the inflow beta (q_k - q_i) is then nearly tangent. The second is evidence. 2.0 must decide whether agents learn from records persistently. X5-IMPL-14 shows that the exact boundary-closure hierarchy survives a persistent base-only likelihood, which is a concrete first theorem in that direction. The analogous question for chapter 11 is open. A Wishart likelihood in X alone, for example a Wishart observation of the precision, adds a force linear in log det X and X, so the family stays invariant, but the common-precision invariance then needs the record to act identically on all agents. The third is replacing the old analytics with the collective-connection observables of X5-IMPL-13. These are the only 2.0 geometric quantities that VFE_4.0's spatial sections can already supply. A nonzero curvature of A^F in a VFE_4.0 run would be the first empirical bridge between the implementation and the geometry chapter.
