### D20-CTXC-01: Frames and rows propagate $H^s$, so the asymmetric class is a proof device and $H^s$ law data give $H^s$ law fields
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/15_contextual_coupling.tex:672-673 (remark after `ctx:c:thm:local`), :827-834 (`ctx:c:eq:reconstruction`); AUTHORING.md:87; VFE_4.0/docs/derivations/2026-10-23-continuum-context-coupling/README.md:21
**Evidence:** The theorem is correct for $u_0\in H^s$ and $r_0,b_0\in H^m$, $m=s-1$. The remark "The law map itself will generally have only $H^m$ regularity", placed directly after "Smooth initial data satisfy these assumptions", and the derivation record's statement that a common $H^s$ frame estimate "loses derivatives", suggest a structural one-derivative loss in the frame. There is none. Sketch under the chapter's hypotheses and threshold: suppose in addition $r_0,b_0\in H^s$. For $|\alpha|=s$,
$$\partial^\alpha r(t)=\partial^\alpha r_0-\int_0^t C\,\partial^\alpha u_\tau\,d\tau-\int_0^t[\partial^\alpha,C]u_\tau\,d\tau+\int_0^t\partial^\alpha Q\,d\tau .$$
Integrate the top-order term by parts in time: $\int_0^tC\partial^\alpha u_\tau=C(t)\partial^\alpha u(t)-C(0)\partial^\alpha u_0-\int_0^t(\partial_\tau C)\partial^\alpha u$. Here $\|C\partial^\alpha u\|_{L^2}\le\|C\|_\infty\|u\|_{H^s}$ with $u\in CH^s$, and $\partial_\tau C=D_uC\,u_\tau+D_rC\,r_\tau$ with $u_\tau,r_\tau\in L^2H^m\subset L^2L^\infty$. The Kato-Ponce commutator bound $\|[\partial^\alpha,C]w\|_{L^2}\le c(\|\nabla C\|_\infty\|w\|_{H^{s-1}}+\|C\|_{H^s}\|w\|_\infty)$ applies with $w=u_\tau\in L^2H^m$, with $\|\nabla C\|_\infty$ controlled by $\|(u,r)\|_{H^m}$ because $m>d/2+1$, and with $\|C\|_{H^s}\le K(1+\|u\|_{H^s}+\|r\|_{H^s})$. The row equation $b_t=L(z)$ is pointwise. Hence $y=\|r\|_{H^s}+\|b\|_{H^s}$ satisfies $y(t)\le A+\int_0^ta(\tau)y(\tau)d\tau$ with $a\in L^1$, and Gronwall bounds $y$ on the whole existence interval (a Friedrichs mollifier makes the computation rigorous). Consequently $p=\Psi(u,r)\in CH^s$ and $A_*\in CH^{s-1}$ whenever the law data are in $H^s$. Exact illustration (`scope_witnesses.py`, part c): for a coupled system of the theorem's form with $h=I_2$, $k=1$, $C=(-u_2/2,\,u_1/2)$ (a horizontal distribution with curvature; a location family on the Heisenberg group with its central translation has this structure with $k$ a positive constant, which does not change the computation because $V_r=0$), $V=|u|^2/2$, $u_1(0)=1$, $u_2(0)=g$, the frame displacement is the Fourier multiplier $m_t(l)=\tfrac{\lambda l^2}{2}\,\tfrac{1-e^{-(2+\lambda l^2)t}}{2+\lambda l^2}\in[0,\tfrac12)$ applied to $g$, so $\|r(t)-r_0\|_{H^s}\le\|g\|_{H^s}/2$. Along lacunary data with $\|g\|_{H^3}\le0.91$ the naive bound $\int_0^T\|r_t\|_{H^s}dt$ grows without bound (ratio naive/actual 1.26, 1.56, 1.92, 2.14, 2.30, 2.43 for $J=4,8,16,24,32,40$ dyadic modes) while $\|r(T)-r_0\|_{H^3}$ stays between 0.29 and 0.33. The naive ODE estimate loses a derivative; the equation does not.
**Fix:** Add a persistence proposition after `ctx:c:thm:local`: if also $r_0,b_0\in H^s$, then $r,b,p\in C([0,T_*);H^s)$ and $A_*\in C([0,T_*);H^{s-1})$ on the same maximal interval. Replace "The law map itself will generally have only $H^m$ regularity" by "the law map has the regularity of the frame data: $H^m$ in general and $H^s$ when $r_0\in H^s$". Update AUTHORING.md:87, which encodes the asymmetric class as a scope rule.
**Falsifies:** Data with $r_0,b_0\in H^s$ for which $\|r(t)\|_{H^s}=\infty$ at some $t<T_*$; such data would have to defeat either the time integration by parts or the commutator estimate.

### D20-CTXC-02: No locally gauge-invariant contextual energy can diffuse frames once the connection is eliminated; rows can be diffused by a gauge-invariant row Dirichlet term
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/15_contextual_coupling.tex:544, :594, :673, :961 ("Independent frames and source rows acquire no separate diffusion"); chapters/02_geometry.tex:265 (`open:geo-gauged-flow`); future_TODO.md:78
**Evidence:** Structural statement with proof sketch. Let $E$ be any functional of $H^m$ law fields $p:\mathbb T^d\to\mathcal N_0$ with $E[g\cdot p]=E[p]$ for every $H^m$ gauge map $g:\mathbb T^d\to G$. If $\pi\circ p_1=\pi\circ p_2$, freeness and properness give a unique $g(x)$ with $p_2=g\cdot p_1$, and $g\in H^m$ because the division map $\mathcal N_0\times_Q\mathcal N_0\to G$ of the principal bundle is smooth; so $E$ factors through the shape map $u=\pi\circ p$. Then $dE(J_p\xi)=0$ for every $\xi:\mathbb T^d\to\mathfrak g$, the Fisher gradient of $E$ is horizontal, and together with the pointwise potential the vertical velocity is $\theta(p_t)=-\mathfrak k_p^{-1}J_p^*dV$, a pointwise quantity. Therefore no locally gauge-invariant contextual term of any order (the Dirichlet energy, a curvature-squared term of $A_*$, higher derivatives) produces frame diffusion after elimination: the absence of frame diffusion in this chapter is forced by local gauge invariance together with elimination, not by the particular quadratic energy. Frame smoothing needs a gauge-variant term, such as the ungauged energy $\tfrac\lambda2\int\|\partial_\mu p\|^2_{\mathsf G}$ (the harmonic-map flow into $\mathcal N$ itself, uniformly parabolic) or a mass term $\epsilon\|A\|^2$ for the auxiliary field (reduced principal symbol $\lambda(I-J(\mathfrak k+\epsilon)^{-1}J^*)$, positive definite), or a connection that is not eliminated and carries its own kinetic term and evolution (Yang-Mills-Higgs type), so that frame variation relative to $A$ is penalized. Rows are not acted on by $G$ (the action is defined on laws only, :418-424), so a gauge-invariant row term $\tfrac{\lambda_b}2\int\sum_\mu\partial_\mu b^TB(b)\partial_\mu b$ is admissible; its Fisher gradient adds $\lambda_b\tau_B(b)$, the harmonic-map heat flow into the open Fisher-Rao simplex (the sigma flow of `CasselEtAl2024`), and the local theory extends with $b$ moved into the parabolic block.
**Fix:** State the no-go as a remark at :544 or :961 and in `open:geo-gauged-flow`, and record in future_TODO Item 4 that frame smoothing is excluded for every locally gauge-invariant eliminated construction, while row smoothing is available by adding a row Dirichlet term, a constitutive choice the chapter does not make.
**Falsifies:** A locally gauge-invariant functional of law fields, $C^1$ on $H^m$, whose Fisher gradient has a nonzero vertical second-order part.

## Extensions and improvements

The most valuable change at the lowest cost is to state the regularity theory the proof already supports. The asymmetric class $u\in H^s$, $r,b\in H^{s-1}$ is the natural space for a Picard map whose input paths carry no time derivative; it is not a structural loss. Integrating the top-order frame term by parts in time and estimating the remaining commutator (D20-CTXC-01) shows that $H^s$ frames and rows persist on the whole existence interval, so the law field and the eliminated connection inherit the regularity of their data. Combined with the observation that the existence proof itself needs only $m>d/2$ (D20-CTXC-06), the theorem can be restated with three regimes: existence and uniqueness for $s>d/2+1$; persistence and the tame continuation criterion for $s>d/2+2$, which is where the chapter's threshold is actually used; and smooth dependence on data from the implicit function theorem.

The continuation alternative should be rewritten so that it detects only genuine singularities. With the frame written as a group-valued field, $p=g\cdot\sigma(u)$, the frame equation is $g_tg^{-1}=-\mathfrak k_p^{-1}J_p^*dV-\operatorname{Ad}_g(\sigma^*\theta)(u_t)$, a right-invariant ODE on the Banach Lie group $H^m(\mathbb T^d;G)$ driven by the shape velocity through the local connection form of the quotient. Its solution is the pointwise parallel transport of the frame along the shape trajectory, corrected by the reference torque; for invariant references it is the geometric phase of the temporal shape path at each context. Together with the tame Gronwall argument of D20-CTXC-03, the alternative reduces to blow-up of $\|u\|_{H^s}$, exit of the shape from compact subsets of the quotient chart, escape of the frame in a noncompact $G$, or loss of finite smooth costs. This form is the right starting point for any global question.

The singular orbit deserves a separate construction rather than a disclaimer. D20-CTXC-04 shows that the chapter's own polar example and the two-agent witness model with shifted references reach the zero-mean orbit in finite time with bounded norms and decreasing energy, and that for homogeneous data the law flow is regular there. A workable regularization is the mass term $\tfrac{\lambda\epsilon}2\|A\|^2$ in the auxiliary minimization, which gives $A_\epsilon=-(\mathfrak k_p+\epsilon)^{-1}J_p^*\partial p$ and the reduced density $\tfrac\lambda2(\|\partial p\|^2-\langle J_p^*\partial p,(\mathfrak k_p+\epsilon)^{-1}J_p^*\partial p\rangle)$; this is smooth in $p$ across changes of orbit type and its principal symbol has eigenvalues $1$ (horizontal) and $\epsilon/(\kappa_i+\epsilon)$ (vertical), so the regularized flow is uniformly parabolic on the whole family. It breaks local gauge invariance, as D20-CTXC-02 says it must if frames are to diffuse. The question is then whether the $\epsilon\to0$ limit selects a passage rule through the singular orbit, first for the polar example in $d=1$. Difficulty: moderate for the regularized theory, open for the limit.

The vanishing-coupling limit is accessible (D20-CTXC-05). An $H^m$ energy method that absorbs the quasilinear term into the $\lambda$-dissipation, plus the time integration by parts for the frame, should give an existence time and bounds uniform in $\lambda\in[0,\lambda_0]$ and an $O(\sqrt\lambda)$ rate in $C([0,T];H^{m-1})$. This would make precise the sense in which weak contextual coupling perturbs the context-local reciprocal flow that the formation chapters use, which matters for transferring any formation statement to a weakly coupled base. Difficulty: moderate.

For the program's interest in gauge-invariant contextual structure (2026-10-22 E10), the regular one-chart class already contains nontrivial observables. In $d=1$ the base-cycle holonomy of $A_*$ equals the geometric phase of the closed shape loop and changes by the swept curvature flux; in $d=2$ the curvature is the pulled-back quotient curvature (D20-CTXC-08). A short proposition recording these formulas, an explicit holonomy witness on $S^1$ for two planar Gaussian location laws, and the statement that homogeneous data generate no contextual geometry would answer what the dynamics does to the connection. Quantized flux needs the nontrivial bundles the chapter excludes; the same local proof applies chart by chart there, and the topological sector is then conserved while the solution stays regular. Difficulty: low for the formulas, moderate for nontrivial bundles.

A refinement theorem is less distant than the chapter's wording suggests, if it is posed as numerical consistency rather than $\Gamma$-convergence. On the lattice $(2\pi/N)\mathbb Z^d/(2\pi\mathbb Z)^d$ with $m_v=N^{-d}$ and nearest-neighbor weights $w_e=m_v(N/2\pi)^2$, the finite reduced edge cost is $c_e^*=\tfrac{\varepsilon^2}2\|P_H\partial_\mu p\|^2+O(\varepsilon^3)$ with lattice spacing $\varepsilon=2\pi/N$ near agreement, where the finite chapter's near-agreement branch exists for compact $G$ acting freely (:157), and the finite contextual force $\tfrac\lambda{m_v}\sum_ew_e\operatorname{Log}$ is a consistent second-order discretization of $\lambda P_H\Delta p$. For smooth solutions in the regular domain, consistency plus an energy-stability estimate should give $O(\varepsilon^2)$ convergence of the finite flow to the continuum flow on compact time intervals. Convergence of nonsmooth solutions or of minimizers would need $\Gamma$-convergence of the reduced energies and the Sandier-Serfaty framework for gradient flows (Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 57 (2004) 1627-1672), which is harder. Difficulty: moderate for the smooth consistency theorem.
