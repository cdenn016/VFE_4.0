### D13-ERAPUB-01: The erasure selector is a preference map, not a partition; the histories that (F5) compares are undefined during switching, and their claimed equality is unproved
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/08_smooth_erasure.tex:261 (selector of `sec:erase-status`), :263 ("The sequence of selected pairings coincides with that of the reciprocal flow, and only the switching time changes"); companion/c08_smooth.tex:80; `inh:null-flow` (chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1380), which defines H(x) as a sequence of selected partitions.
**Evidence:** The selector "assigns to each agent the source of smallest reciprocal row cost". This is a map from the four agents to sources, and it is a partition only when it is an involution. Along both flows an agent i switches from its crossed partner c(i) to its box-mate b(i) when, to first order, `alpha(H_q(t)) rho |z_i| (|z_b(i)| + |z_c(i)|) = D_eps(x_i, x_c(i)) - D_eps(x_i, x_b(i))`. The right side differs between agents for generic public data, so the four switches happen at distinct times and the map is not an involution in between. In s02 (an independent 24-dimensional implementation; analytic gradient against central differences of E_red: 7.1e-10 and 7.5e-10), with x = y = (0.4990, -0.4950, 0.5060, -0.5040), z = w = 0.5(1, 1, -1, -1), Delta = 0.04, rho = 0.01, eps = 0.015, T = 1, the reciprocal flow switches agents 2, 3, 0, 1 at t = 0.01071446, 0.01071746, 0.01076034, 0.01076606 and passes through the maps (1,0,0,2), (1,0,0,1), (2,0,0,1), none an involution; the null flow passes through the same maps at t = 0.0218925 to 0.0219965. No grid time had an argmin outside {c(i), b(i)}. The chapter proves only the initial (crossed) and final (box) pairings. The R-ERA null-flow script sampled every 1e-4, longer than the 5.2e-5 reciprocal-flow window found here, which explains why it recorded a single switch.

A partition rule must be supplied, and the conclusion depends on it. With connected components of the preference graph both flows give {01|23} -> {0123} -> {02|13}. With mutual pairs they give {01|23} -> {01|2|3} -> {02|1|3} -> {02|13}, but not on every preparation: s02b locates, for a shift eta of x_0 = y_0, the value at which agents 0 and 1 switch simultaneously, eta_c = -0.0039680660 for the reciprocal flow and -0.0039681236 for the null flow, the same to ten digits under LSODA (rtol 1e-11) and DOP853 (rtol 1e-12, s02c). For eta strictly between these values the switch orders are [2,3,1,0] and [2,3,0,1], and the mutual-pair sequences differ in their third partition ({0|13|2} against {02|1|3}). Switch times depend continuously on the initial data and the inequalities are strict, so this is a nonempty open subset of the admitted family on which P differs from P^0 under the mutual-pair rule; the window is 5.8e-8 wide in one coordinate. Under the component rule all 33 scanned preparations (eta in [-0.008, 0.008]) had equal sequences.
**Fix:** Declare a partition-valued selector, for example the connected components of the preference graph in each sector with a stated rule for combining sectors. Then prove, on the explicit crossed preparation, that each agent switches exactly once with argmin in {c(i), b(i)}: the cost difference is affine in alpha to first order, alpha decreases monotonically along both flows, and public costs move by O(Delta) during the window. Conclude P = P^0 off a closed set with empty interior. Otherwise restrict the null-flow statement to that preparation and label the switch-time comparison a numerical observation. The companion sentence at c08:80 needs the same rule.
**Falsifies:** A proof that the declared preference map is an involution at all times on an open dense set of admitted preparations, or a recomputation giving eta_c(full) = eta_c(null).

### D13-ERAPUB-03: The phase chapter's null-flow control does not separate the flows on the existence of an exact compressing phase; the uncoupled binary flow has one
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/09_public_compression.tex:251 ("The spectral gap kappa_N that makes the phase a compression of differences is therefore produced by the peer costs"), :137 (last two sentences); companion/c09_public_compression.tex:25 ("the construction below loses its foundation").
**Evidence:** Under the null flow each agent of the invariant binary subfamily q_i = s_i follows `xdot = f_b(x)` (eq:public-phase-binary), a one-dimensional analytic field with a hyperbolic sink at 0 and rate lam = 1 - J. Koenigs' function `h(x) = x exp(int_0^x [-lam/f_b(s) - 1/s] ds)` satisfies `h(phi_t x) = e^{-lam t} h(x)`. For every c >= 0 the map `Pi0_c(x) = h^{-1}((h(x_1) + h(x_2))/2 + c |h(x_1) - h(x_2)|)` is a Lipschitz retraction onto the diagonal that intertwines the null flow with phi_t, by homogeneity of degree one, and has one-dimensional fibers; `Pi0_0` is analytic. The phase formula itself also converges under the null flow, since the averaging residual is O(e^{-2 lam t}) against backward growth e^{lam t}. s05(c) at J = 0.2: Koenigs residual 7e-17; intertwining residuals at most 6e-17 for c = 0 and c = 0.3 at t = 1 and 3; the fiber of `Pi0_0` through (0.04, 0) contains (0.03, 0.0100105123); `phi_{-T}(mean(Phi0_T x)) - Pi0_0(x)` is -1.9e-11 and 3.1e-16 at T = 8 and 16; replacing the mean by the Lipschitz retraction `mean + 0.3 |x_1 - x_2|` gives the different limit `Pi0_0.3(x) = 0.0320053794` against `Pi0_0(x) = 0.0200070057`. Under the coupled flow the two retractions give the same phase, 0.0200045030, to 4.5e-13, as `cor:public-phase-unique` requires.

On any finite-alphabet subfamily, Hartman's C^1 linearization of an attracting fixed point (Bol. Soc. Mat. Mexicana 5 (1960) 220-241; cited from memory, not re-read) gives the same kind of exact null-flow retraction, `h^{-1}(N^{-1} sum_i h(z_i))`. So exact, non-injective, intertwining collective coordinates exist without interaction. What the peer gap adds is uniqueness among Lipschitz intertwining retractions and strong-stable fibers: two configurations in one fiber approach each other at rate at least omega > 1 - |J| under the coupled flow, but only at the common rate under the null flow. The failure of `eq:public-phase-gap` reported for the null flow is a failure of the proof hypothesis, not of the construction.
**Fix:** "Without peer costs the binary subfamily still admits exact analytic intertwining retractions, such as the Koenigs mean, but they are not unique in the Lipschitz class and their fibers are not strong-stable leaves. The peer gap kappa_N makes the Lipschitz intertwining parent unique (cor:public-phase-unique) and makes the discarded differences decay faster than the parent signal." Make the same correction at c09:25.
**Falsifies:** An error in the homogeneity argument for `Pi0_c`, or a definition of compression under which `Pi0_0` does not count.

### D13-ERAPUB-04: erase:pair-convergence holds with one eps_0 for all temperatures, so pair convergence also covers the sharp-selection regime T <~ eps^2
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/08_smooth_erasure.tex:181-199 (`erase:pair-convergence`: "Fix T>0 ... There is eps_T>0", and "the derivatives of the Gibbs rows are O(eps^2/T)"); :171-177 (near one-hot selection requires T of order eps^2 or smaller).
**Evidence:** As stated, convergence is proved where the Gibbs rows are within O(eps^2/T) of uniform, while sharp preference (`erase:row-bound`) needs T <~ eps^2, so the two results cover disjoint regimes. The row-derivative estimate can be sharpened. For one Gibbs row, `|d beta_j| <= (2/T) max|dD| beta_j (1 - beta_j)`. On the post-closure region Q, `erase:cost-comparison` gives `beta_j (1 - beta_j) <= min(1/4, 2 exp(-Delta_eps/T))` for every source (same-box source: `1 - beta <= 2 exp(-Delta_eps/T)`; cross source: `beta <= exp(-Delta_eps/T)`), with `Delta_eps = c_2 eps^2`, `c_2 = 0.479930` (s01). With `max|dD_eps| <= C_1 eps^2` this gives `|d beta_j| <= 4 C_1 sup_s s e^{-c_2 s} = 4 C_1/(e c_2)`, independent of T and eps. The peer part of the post-closure public field therefore has C^1(Q) norm O(eps^2) uniformly in T > 0, and the implicit-function and LaSalle steps of the proof hold with one eps_0 for all T; box invariance in `erase:pairs` already uses only row weights in [0,1]. s03: `sup_Q ||D V_peer||_inf / eps^2` stays in [2.1, 7.0] for T from 1 to 1e-8 (eps = 0.015 and 0.05). For eps = 0.015 the zero in Q is unique, symmetric (box-mates equal to 7e-16, boxes opposite to 6e-16) and stable (real eigenvalues, the largest -0.174) at every T in {1, 1e-2, ..., 1e-6}, and a heterogeneous trajectory at T = 1e-6 has in-box separation 2.6e-2 at t = 0 and 1.1e-13 at t = 150. At eps = 0.05 no zero lies in Q for T >= 1e-3 (the equilibrium leaves the boxes, consistent with the small-eps hypothesis), while for T <= 1e-4 it does; small T relaxes the eps requirement instead of tightening it.
**Fix:** Replace "Fix T>0 ... There is eps_T>0" by "There is eps_0>0 such that for all T>0 and 0<eps<eps_0", and put the row-derivative bound above into the proof.
**Falsifies:** An admitted configuration with T << eps^2 at which `||D V_peer||` is much larger than eps^2, or a second zero in Q for small eps and small T.

### D13-ERAPUB-05: Near the common reference the phase cannot be refined to a proper sub-block, because the block fixed-point subspace has its normal spectrum inside its tangential spectrum
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/09_public_compression.tex:246 ("for proper sub-blocks ... neither condition has been checked"), `def:public-controlled-projection` (:232-244), `sec:public-recursive-requirement`.
**Evidence:** For a proper block I the set S_I on which the block members are equal is the fixed-point subspace of the permutations of I, hence invariant. At the reference every zero-sum mode carries the operator `A - kappa_N` (eq:public-phase-linearization), so intra-block differences, normal to S_I, decay at rates {1 + kappa_N +- J, 1 + kappa_N}, and the same rates occur tangentially to S_I through block-versus-exterior and exterior differences. The rate condition for a strong-stable projection onto S_I, normal contraction stronger than every tangential contraction, needs `1 + kappa_N - |J| > 1 + kappa_N + |J|`, which fails for every J (equality at J = 0). s07 (N = 4, I = {0,1}, nonuniform four-point references, T_q = 0.8, T_s = 1.5, J = 0.5): tangential rates {0.5, 1 (x4), 1.5, 3.1667 (x2), 3.6667 (x8), 4.1667 (x2)}, normal rates {3.1667, 3.6667 (x4), 4.1667}. A hierarchy of phase parents therefore cannot be built from this theorem with uniform all-to-all rows. Separating intra-block from inter-block rates needs another mechanism, for example suppressed cross-block Gibbs rows near a multi-well equilibrium; the 2026-10-22 lens found a normal-to-tangential rate ratio of 2.29 at J = log 3, T = 0.05. The next obstruction is a conjecture with a sketch, not checked: where a sub-block foliation exists, the strong-stable leaf through x meets the invariant set at a point whose exterior component is a shadow state e^c different from e, so the block parent is driven by e^c, and `eq:public-controlled-projection` with R_ext the exterior state generally fails unless the exterior retention also carries its phase.
**Fix:** Add to `sec:public-recursive-requirement`: "At the common reference with uniform nonself rows no proper sub-block admits a rate-condition phase, since every zero-sum mode has the spectrum of A - kappa_N." Pose the sub-block problem at a multi-well equilibrium instead (Extensions below).
**Falsifies:** A proper block whose normal rates at the reference exceed its tangential rates, or a mechanism other than a rate condition that gives a unique sub-block phase there.

### D13-ERAPUB-06: "its level sets are isostables" holds only when the diagonal is one-dimensional, and the noncompactness rationale misplaces the obstacle
**Category:** citation
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/09_public_compression.tex:137.
**Evidence:** Mauroy, Mezic and Moehlis (Physica D 261 (2013) 19-30; checked at arXiv 1302.0032 through ar5iv for this review) define the isostables of a stable fixed point as the level sets of |s_1|, the Koopman eigenfunction of the slowest eigenvalue; they are codimension-one sets (their Definition 2). Intersections of level sets of several eigenfunctions are a separate construction (their Eq. 14), which gives fibrations when the remaining eigenvalues are faster. The fibers of Pi have codimension dim E, infinite here: they are the joint level sets of the Koopman eigenfunctions of the whole diagonal spectrum {-1 +- J, -1}. They are isostables only in the binary example, where the diagonal is one-dimensional. Separately, the text justifies the direct proof by the diagonal being "a noncompact subset of a Banach space". The theorem is local; what blocks the cited compact-manifold theorems is that a neighborhood of 0 in the infinite-dimensional diagonal is not compact. Local invariant-foliation theorems at an equilibrium with a spectral gap do exist for semiflows on Banach spaces; a candidate primary source is Chen, Hale and Tan, J. Differential Equations 139 (1997) 283-318, whose bibliographic record I found but whose content I did not read. I did not re-read Fenichel 1974, Guckenheimer 1975, Bates-Lu-Zeng 2000 or Eldering-Kvalheim-Revzen 2018 in this session.
**Fix:** "its fibers are the joint level sets of the Koopman eigenfunctions of the diagonal spectrum (the fibrations of Mauroy et al. 2013, Eq. 14), and they are isostables when the diagonal is one-dimensional". Replace "noncompact" by "infinite dimensional near the reference", and check whether a Banach-space foliation theorem at an equilibrium covers the statement, which would make the direct proof a verification of hypotheses.
**Falsifies:** A definition in Mauroy et al. 2013 of isostables as fibers of a projection onto a higher-dimensional invariant subspace.

### D13-ERAPUB-08: Companion c09 calls |J|<1 "exactly the condition for the common reference to be stable"; at |J| = 1 the reference is still asymptotically stable
**Category:** error
**Severity:** low
**Location:** companion/c09_public_compression.tex:11.
**Evidence:** For balanced phi, psi the span u = a phi, v = b psi is invariant under the diagonal flow, with `adot = -a + J tanh b`, `bdot = -b + J tanh a` (because E_{q_u} phi = tanh a). At |J| = 1, `V = a^2 + b^2` gives `Vdot = 2(-a^2 - b^2 + J a tanh b + J b tanh a) <= -2(|a| - |b|)^2`, with equality only at the origin because |tanh s| < |s| for s different from 0; the complementary directions and the difference modes decay at rates 1 and kappa_N. So the reference is asymptotically stable at |J| = 1, though not exponentially. For |J| > 1 the eigenvalue -1 + |J| is positive and it is unstable. The theorem needs exponential stability (mu > 0), which is the condition |J| < 1; the main text does not make the companion's claim.
**Fix:** "The bound |J|<1 is exactly the condition for the common reference to be exponentially stable, which the construction uses."
**Falsifies:** A trajectory on the invariant span at |J| = 1 that does not converge to the reference from a small initial condition.

## Extensions and improvements

The cheapest repair of the erasure status is to turn the null-flow comparison into a theorem. Declare the selector as the partition into connected components of the preference graph in each sector, combined across sectors by a stated rule. On the explicit crossed preparation, the cost comparison that decides agent i's switch is, to first order, `eps^2 [A_{i c(i)} - A_{i b(i)}] - alpha rho |z_i| (|z_b(i)| + |z_c(i)|)`, an affine function of alpha whose slope has a fixed sign. Along both flows alpha(H_q(t)) decreases monotonically, and the public and private states move by O(Delta) while alpha crosses the thresholds. With a quantitative bound on those drifts, each agent switches exactly once, from c(i) to b(i), and the component sequence is {crossed} -> {all four} -> {box} under both flows whenever the switch times are not all equal. Then P = P^0 outside a closed set with empty interior, and (F5) fails as a theorem on this preparation. The mutual-pair completion shows why the rule must be declared: there a reordering of switch events inside a window of length O(Delta) changes P, so the formal clause can be met by a timing artifact (D13-ERAPUB-01). The formation-standard review may want (F5) to compare only partitions that persist for a declared time, or only final partitions; I flag this for chapter 5. Difficulty: low on the explicit preparation, moderate on the full admitted class, where an agent can switch twice.

With the uniform C^1 bound of D13-ERAPUB-04, the heterogeneous-coupling variant at 08:201 becomes an implicit-function argument valid for every T > 0. At eps = 0 agent i sits at the nondegenerate well x*(J_i) = y*(J_i), the solution of atanh x = J_i x near 1/2, separated from its box-mate by 2.130188 |eta_i - eta_k| + O(eta^2) per coordinate (s01(g)); the box margins survive while |eta_i| l stays below the margin 2.26e-3. The peer field stays C^1-small uniformly in T as long as the same-box cost remains below the cross-box cost by a margin proportional to eps^2, which holds when |x*(J_i) - x*(J_k)| is small against 2l. The perturbed zero is then unique in Q and attracting, with box-mates at distinct states, so the in-pair separation converges to a positive limit. This gives persistent heterogeneous preferred pairs from heterogeneous constitutive data in the sharp-selection regime T <~ eps^2. Difficulty: low.

For retention results without selection (chapters 9 and 10) the formal (F5) is vacuous, and D13-ERAPUB-03 shows that the existence of an exact intertwining collective coordinate does not depend on the interaction. A meaningful control is spectral. Call a retention onto an invariant set S interaction-made if its slowest normal decay rate exceeds its fastest tangential contraction rate under Phi but not under Phi^0. For the phase the ratio is (1 + kappa_N - |J|)/(1 + |J|) > 1 under the reciprocal flow and (1 - |J|)/(1 + |J|) < 1 under the null flow. Under the inequality the Lipschitz intertwining retraction is unique, by the argument of `cor:public-phase-unique`, and its fibers are strong-stable leaves; these are the two properties the null flow lacks. The definition is computable from linearizations and would make the sentence at 09:251 precise. Difficulty: low.

A hierarchy of phase parents has to start away from the common reference (D13-ERAPUB-05). The natural test case is the chapter 8 public system without the erasure gate: N = 4, J = log 3, identity channels, small T, two blocks in opposite wells. There the cross-block Gibbs weights are O(exp(-c/T)). The tangential rates of S_I x S_J (block-versus-block and block common modes) are close to the single-agent rates 0.176 and 1.824, while the intra-block normal rates carry the full peer gap; the 2026-10-22 lens reported tangential rates (0.173, 0.176, 1.822, 1.824) and normal rates (4.174, 5.823) at T = 0.05. A Fenichel-type phase onto S_I x S_J then follows from the same direct proof. Restricted to that fixed-point subspace the dynamics is a two-agent law-pair KL system with a zero-cost self source of prior weight (n_I - 1)/(N - 1), so the parents are agents of the children's type. The open question is controlled projectability for one block: the strong-stable leaf through x meets S_I x S_J at a point whose other-block component is a shadow state, so the block parent is driven by the shadow and not by the actual exterior. I expect the discrepancy to be of order exp(-c/T) times the intra-block differences. That would give approximate but not exact controlled projectability, and an exact statement would require retaining the joint phase of both blocks. Membership stays supplied by the initial wells, so the result would be a retention theorem, not a selection theorem. Difficulty: moderate.

The phase theorem extends to the ordered regime |J| > 1 at little cost. In the A-centered chart the receiver term log(q_i/q_j) is exactly linear in u_i - u_j up to constants, and the source term linearizes to the same complete-graph operator at every diagonal point, because the Gibbs rows are uniform with zero first variation at equality. At any exponentially stable diagonal equilibrium z* of f the normal spectrum is therefore the spectrum of Df(z*) shifted by -kappa_N, independent of the temperatures. The proof of `thm:public-phase` uses only these rates, the global Lipschitz bound L = 1 + |J| of the backward diagonal flow, and smoothness, so it applies whenever kappa_N plus the slowest tangential rate at z* exceeds L. For the binary wells of chapter 8 (J = log 3, single-agent rates 0.176 and 1.824, L = 2.0986) this holds for every N >= 2, since kappa_N > 2. The parent then carries a persistent nonzero public state, although the children still synchronize. Difficulty: low.

The erasure parent satisfies both conditions of `def:public-controlled-projection` for proper blocks, in the weak form of `erase:empirical-flow`. With R_I = eta_I and R_ext = eta_J, the field V[v; M] with M = 2 eta_I + 2 eta_J determines the evolution of eta_I from (eta_I, eta_J), and joining descends because eta_{I u J} = (|I| eta_I + |J| eta_J)/(|I| + |J|). It is the only construction in this scope that meets both conditions for proper blocks, and it does so only because it discards no public information. Saying this in `sec:public-recursive-requirement` would show that the two recursive requirements are compatible with exact inheritance and that they say nothing by themselves about compression.
