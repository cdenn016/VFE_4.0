## Findings

### D04-DYN-01: The companion and AUTHORING.md read "not a gradient flow" as "no global Lyapunov function", and the example offered has one
**Category:** error
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** companion/c03_dynamics.tex:10; AUTHORING.md:38; the supporting result is prop:dyn-nonreciprocal (chapters/03_dynamics.tex:359-368)
**Evidence:** c03:10: "Such non-reciprocal forces need have no global Lyapunov function: on a directed ring of three Bernoulli agents, each pulled toward its successor, the linearized motion spirals, with complex eigenvalues that no gradient flow can have." AUTHORING.md:38 says the per-agent dynamics "generally has no global Lyapunov functional". Complex eigenvalues exclude a gradient structure in every Riemannian metric, which is what `prop:dyn-nonreciprocal` proves; they do not exclude a Lyapunov function. The ring dynamics `eq:dyn-three-cycle` is exactly linear in log odds, l' = A(l - l*) with A = (-(1+w)I + wS)/(1+w) and S the cyclic shift. The symmetric part of A has eigenvalues -1/(1+w) once and -(1+3w/2)/(1+w) twice, all negative, so d/dt |l - l*|^2 <= -(2/(1+w)) |l - l*|^2: the squared distance to the equilibrium is a strict global Lyapunov function and every trajectory converges exponentially. In s7(4), at w = 0.5, 2 and 10, the symmetric part has eigenvalues (-1.1667, -1.1667, -0.6667), (-1.3333, -1.3333, -0.3333) and (-1.4545, -1.4545, -0.0909), while the spectrum is complex (agreement with the stated eigenvalues to 1.5e-15). More generally, every per-agent Bernoulli dynamics with fixed rows, identity channels and positive self weight, on any directed graph, reads l_i' = -beta_ii (l_i - rho_i) - sum_{j != i} beta_ij (l_i - l_j); its matrix has log-norm mu_inf(A) = -min_i beta_ii < 0, so max_i |l_i - l_i*| is a strict Lyapunov function. On 2000 random directed graphs (s7(5)) the Lyapunov equation A^T P + PA = -I also has P positive definite (smallest eigenvalue over all graphs 0.2504), while spectra reach imaginary part 0.764. Nothing in the manuscript exhibits a per-agent dynamics without a Lyapunov function.
**Fix:** Replace the c03:10 sentence by: "Such non-reciprocal forces need not be the gradient of any function in any metric: on a directed ring of three Bernoulli agents ... the linearized motion has complex eigenvalues, which no gradient flow can have, although this ring still contracts to its equilibrium." Change AUTHORING.md:38 to "is in general not a gradient flow". A claim of no Lyapunov function needs a construction with recurrence (for example a periodic orbit with learned rows or law-dependent channels), which the program does not have.
**Falsifies:** A per-agent dynamics in 2.0 shown to admit no Lyapunov function, or an error in the symmetric-part eigenvalues above.

### D04-DYN-02: The free-energy obstruction is announced for every normalized model, but prop:dyn-no-global-vfe proves it only on finite alphabets or for fixed channels
**Category:** inflation
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/03_dynamics.tex:4; companion/c03_dynamics.tex:12; the same sentence at frontmatter.tex:5 and AUTHORING.md:39 (outside my scope); result prop:dyn-no-global-vfe (chapters/03_dynamics.tex:375-385)
**Evidence:** ch3:4 says the chapter will "prove that the reciprocal potential is not the free energy of any fixed normalized model in which the agent laws are recognition laws"; the abstract and c03:12 repeat this without a qualifier. Part (a) of `prop:dyn-no-global-vfe` assumes K_i = K_j finite and a model "over finitely many finite-valued variables"; its proof bounds KL(Q‖Π_o) by max(-log Π_o), which fails on infinite spaces. Part (b) assumes fixed channels and the product recognition whose factors are the agent laws, and its non-vanishing computation is on two-point alphabets (by my reconstruction the third-derivative argument extends to general spaces for fixed channels). Two cases are not covered: law-dependent channels on non-finite spaces, which the abstract advertises ("interactions that may depend on the current belief and model laws"), and non-product recognition rules on non-finite spaces. The obstruction also has no content in the mean directions. On the slice of unit-variance Gaussian beliefs with identity channels and fixed rows, E - F(Q;o) is a constant (s9, sympy). Here F is the free energy of the normalized model y_i ~ G_i independently, o_ij given y ~ N(y_i - y_j, 1/beta_ij) registered at 0, and Q = product of N(m_i, 1). Off the slice the difference depends on the means. This recomputes 2026-10-22 X3-PIFB-01(c). On that restricted family the reciprocal potential is a mean-field global free energy whose recognition factors are the agent laws.
**Fix:** In ch3:4, c03:12 and the abstract write: "prove that on finite alphabets the reciprocal potential is not the free energy of any fixed normalized model in which the agent laws are recognition laws, and that for fixed channels it is not a mean-field free energy with the agent laws as factors on any open set". After `prop:dyn-no-global-vfe`, add that the obstruction lives in the variance and shape directions, and cite the unit-variance slice.
**Falsifies:** A proof of (a) on general standard Borel spaces with law-dependent channels and arbitrary recognition rules.

### D04-DYN-03: Global existence in bounded log-density charts follows for fixed channels by an essential-infimum and essential-supremum maximum principle; law-dependent channels can leave the interior in finite time
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/03_dynamics.tex:228-234 (prop:dyn-chart-flow and "Global statements on general spaces need maximum-principle estimates for particular channel classes and are stated where they are used"); :180-186 (prop:dyn-global-flow); REMEDIATION.md author decision 2
**Evidence:** The min-entry argument of `prop:dyn-global-flow` transfers to the chart of `prop:dyn-chart-flow` with essential infima and suprema. Because densities are taken against the probability reference λ, the constants no longer depend on alphabet size. Sketch for the belief sector, under the sufficient conditions of `prop:dyn-chart-flow` with every channel fixed. Assume abs(log g_i) <= C_g and abs(log r_i) <= C_r, each channel is either an identity between agents sharing λ or a kernel with density in [c, C_K], and N is the number of agents. Let m(t) and M(t) be the essential infimum and supremum over all agents of log(dq_i/dλ); since λ is a probability, m <= 0 <= M.

Near a minimizing point:
- the evaluator term is at most C_g;
- log(q_i/ν_ij) <= max(0, -log c) for kernel edges (ν >= c) and <= 0 for identity edges (ν = q_j >= e^m);
- the incoming terms are nonpositive;
- the q_i-mean of the force is at least KL(q_i‖λ) - C_g - N >= -C_g - N.

Hence the lower Dini derivative of m is at least -m - A with A = 2C_g + N + max(0, -log c), so m(t) >= min(m(0), -A).

Near a maximizing point:
- the receiver terms are at least -max(0, log C_K);
- the incoming terms are at least -N max(1, C_K/c), because the integral of (q_k/ν) k(z,.) dλ is at most C_K/c, and for identity edges q_k/q_i <= 1 at the global maximum;
- the q_i-mean of the force is at most KL(q_i‖λ) + C_g + sum_j beta_ij KL(q_i‖ν_ij) <= 2E(0) + C_r + 2C_g. This uses that E is nonincreasing, that sum_j beta_ij D_ij <= E, and that KL(q_i‖λ) <= KL(q_i⊗s_i‖R_i) + C_r + C_g.

Hence dM/dt <= B - M with B = 2E(0) + C_r + 3C_g + max(0, log C_K) + N max(1, C_K/c), so M(t) <= max(M(0), B).

The model sector is identical with r_i, and the rows follow as in `prop:dyn-global-flow`. The chart coordinates stay bounded, so the continuation alternative of `prop:dyn-chart-flow` gives t* = infinity, and the integral of sum Var over [0, infinity) is at most E(0). The only step beyond the finite proof is the essential-infimum Dini estimate. It holds because t -> log q(t) is C^1 into L^infinity and the force at a point depends on log q at that point plus global integrals. s8 supports the uniformity in the sample space. On a circle discretized with L = 50, 200 and 800 points, with three agents, fixed cosine kernels with densities in [0.2, 1.8], identity edges including a self edge, and learnable rows, the log-density range over t in [0, 30] is [-3.6908, 1.3248], [-3.6908, 1.3255] and [-3.6908, 1.3255].

The fixed-channel restriction cannot be dropped. In s2(e), two binary agents use a binary symmetric channel whose flip probability depends on the receiver's Bhattacharyya coefficient B = sum_z sqrt(q_1(z)). Its entries are C^infinity on the interior and the compared law is positive, so every hypothesis of `thm:dyn-finite-flow` holds. The trajectory reaches the boundary at t* = 0.0187935 from q_1(0) = 1e-5 and at t* = 0.9083564 from 1e-3, with E finite there. The quantity d(log q)/dt times sqrt(q) tends to -0.373, matching the prediction -κ (∂D/∂ε)/2 = -0.373. The channel-derivative force grows like q^(-1/2), which is the mechanism behind the caveat at ch3:186.
**Fix:** After `prop:dyn-chart-flow`, add: "Under its sufficient conditions with fixed channels, every chart solution is global, with log densities in [min(m(0), -A), max(M(0), B)]." For law-dependent channels, add the hypothesis that the channel-derivative contribution to the force is bounded above by C(1 + abs(log q)) near the essential infimum, which gives global existence with an exponential-in-time floor; the Bhattacharyya example shows that some such hypothesis is needed. Difficulty: low for the statement and a careful proof. Convergence on general spaces would need a Łojasiewicz–Simon inequality and is open.
**Falsifies:** A fixed-channel chart trajectory whose essential-infimum log density tends to minus infinity in finite time, or an error in the upper-bound step.

### D04-DYN-04: "Share their stability" claims more than the inertia statement it cites
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/03_dynamics.tex:194 ("they share their critical points and their stability (\cref{prop:dyn-morse})"); prop:dyn-morse (ch3:196-200)
**Evidence:** `prop:dyn-morse` proves equal numbers of negative and zero Hessian eigenvalues. I confirmed it in s3 at a saddle, with inertia (1,0,3) for E and (1,0,2) for E_red, and at two minima, (0,0,4) and (0,0,3). The Schur-complement residual is at most 1.2e-14, and the row Hessian equals T times the row Fisher metric exactly. Equal inertia settles linear stability, and therefore stability at nondegenerate critical points. At degenerate critical points the Hessian does not decide the Lyapunov stability of a gradient flow. For C^infinity costs, stability is not even equivalent to local minimality (Absil and Kurdyka, Systems & Control Letters 55 (2006) 573-577; I know the statement from the abstract and did not open the paper). Under `thm:dyn-finite-flow` the channels are only C^2, so the claim is unproved at degenerate points. For fixed channels it is true, by an argument the text omits. First, E = E_red + T sum KL(β‖β*) >= E_red with equality on the Gibbs graph, so local minima of E and E_red correspond. Second, for analytic costs and a metric uniformly equivalent to the Euclidean one, stability is equivalent to local minimality. I reconstructed that step by the Łojasiewicz argument of Absil and Kurdyka but did not check it in their text.
**Fix:** "... share their critical points, Morse indices and nullities (\cref{prop:dyn-morse}), hence their linear stability. For fixed channels, where E is analytic, they also share Lyapunov stability, because local minima correspond and for analytic costs stability is equivalent to local minimality."
**Falsifies:** A proof that equal inertia implies equal Lyapunov stability at degenerate critical points of C^2 potentials.

### D04-DYN-05: The full velocity of a Gaussian source can lie outside the Fisher tangent space, and no well-posedness result covers the full flow from Gaussian data
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/03_dynamics.tex:213 ("The restricted flow is the Fisher-orthogonal projection of the full velocity"); prop:dyn-gaussian-noninvariance (ch3:215-219); prop:dyn-chart-flow (ch3:228-232)
**Evidence:** With identity channels, the source term of the first variation is -beta_ki q_k/q_i (ch3:218), so the squared Fisher norm of the full velocity contains beta_ki^2 χ²(q_k‖q_i). For one-dimensional zero-mean laws, χ²(q_k‖q_i) = v_i/sqrt(v_k(2v_i - v_k)) - 1 when v_k < 2v_i, and it is infinite when v_k >= 2v_i. In s5(b) the x^2 coefficient of log(q_k^2/q_i) is (v_k/2 - v_i)/(v_i v_k), and the closed form agrees with quadrature at 0.1547, 1.2942 and 6.0888 for v_k = 1.5, 1.9 and 1.99. In d dimensions χ² is infinite unless 2Σ_k^(-1) - Σ_i^(-1) is positive definite. When a receiver is at least twice as wide as its source in some direction, Var_{q_i}(φ) is infinite, the dissipation identity gives dE/dt = -infinity at that state, and "Fisher-orthogonal projection" is not an L^2(q_i) projection. The restricted velocity is still well defined as the score-pairing projection, because E_{q_i}[ψ q_k/q_i] = E_{q_k}[ψ]. s5(d) confirms that it equals -G^(-1) ∂_θ E to ten digits both for v_k = 1.5 (χ² = 1.37) and for v_k = 3 (χ² infinite). Gaussian laws with different parameters never share a bounded log-density chart, so `prop:dyn-chart-flow` does not cover the full flow from Gaussian data, and `thm:dyn-finite-flow` is finite only. `prop:dyn-gaussian-noninvariance` remains correct as a statement about the velocity field at one state.
**Fix:** At ch3:213 write: "the restricted velocity is -G^(-1) times the score pairing of the first variation; this is the Fisher-orthogonal projection of the full velocity whenever the latter has finite Fisher norm (for identity channels, when 2Σ_k^(-1) - Σ_i^(-1) is positive definite for every receiver k)". Then add that no result in the chapter covers the full density-level flow from Gaussian initial data.
**Falsifies:** A chart in which Gaussian laws with unequal covariances and the full source velocity are both admissible, together with a well-posedness result there.

### D04-DYN-06: The registered-agreement model supplies no kinetic metric, its type lattice has no interior points at prime n when both sectors are nontrivial, and its stochastic temperature is 1/(2n)
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/03_dynamics.tex:194 (Čencov justification of equal mobility); :389 (definition of X_n); :403 (stochastic-temperature remark); thm:dyn-registered (:391-401)
**Evidence:** (1) In the registered-agreement model the configuration X is uniform on a finite set X_n and the recognition is the point mass δ_x, so the model has no smooth parameter and no Fisher metric for x. The unit-mobility metric is justified at ch3:194 only by reading the tables as one product law of represented contents and labels. Under that reading, `prop:dyn-no-global-vfe` says E is not a free energy. In the model where E is T times a free energy, the kinetic metric is an extra constitutive choice, and the Fisher flow is a continuum interpolation of a search over a lattice. (2) X_n requires q_i⊗s_i to be an n-type. With both q_i and s_i interior, the entries a_k b_m/n^2 lie in (1/n)Z only if n divides every product a_k b_m with 0 < a_k, b_m < n, which is impossible for prime n. At T = 1/n with n prime, the theorem therefore covers only configurations in which one sector is a point mass, outside the interior on which `thm:dyn-finite-flow` works. The natural lattice, marginals that are m-types with n = m^2, is not mentioned; s4 uses it with m = 3. (3) At ch3:403, resampling n messages per unit time gives increments with covariance (diag p - pp^T)/n = (1/n) G^(-1). That identity is correct (s4: 2.1e-17). A Langevin equation u' = -G^(-1)∇E + sqrt(2 T_eff) G^(-1/2) dW/dt with this noise has 2 T_eff = 1/n, so T_eff = 1/(2n). This is the Wright–Fisher convention, with stationary weight e^(-2nE). It equals the row temperature 1/n only if one time unit is two resampling rounds.
**Fix:** At ch3:194 add: "in the registered-agreement model of \cref{thm:dyn-registered} the configuration is a lattice variable, and the model does not supply a kinetic metric". At ch3:389, give the m-type lattice with n = m^2 and note that interior configurations require composite n. At ch3:403, replace "the same message count would set the temperature" by "the message count would set the temperature, up to the factor 2 fixed by the time unit".
**Falsifies:** A smooth parametrization of the registered-agreement model whose Fisher information for the configuration is the unit-mobility metric, or an interior n-type product q⊗s at prime n (excluded by divisibility).

## Extensions and improvements

The most useful next result is the global chart theorem of D04-DYN-03. It turns the finite-table `prop:dyn-global-flow` into a statement on general standard Borel spaces for the same channel class (identities between agents sharing λ, and fixed kernels with two-sided density bounds). This is the class for which `prop:dyn-chart-flow` already verifies its Lipschitz hypothesis, so the new theorem closes remediation author decision 2 for every downstream chapter whose channels are fixed. The proof needs only the two Dini estimates written out in D04-DYN-03, together with a short measure-theoretic lemma: if t -> f(t) is C^1 into L^infinity, and the time derivative is bounded below by a function of f on the sublevel set {f <= ess inf f + ε}, then the essential infimum satisfies the corresponding Dini inequality. Because the upper bound uses only E(t) <= E(0), the theorem also gives two-sided density bounds that are uniform in time, which is the compactness that later convergence arguments need. Convergence of the trajectory, as opposed to its global existence, would need a Łojasiewicz–Simon inequality for E on the chart. That is plausible for analytic fixed-channel potentials but is a separate project. For law-dependent channels the finite-time exit of s2(e) marks the boundary of what can hold. Either the channel-derivative force is log-Lipschitz near the essential infimum, which gives global existence with an exponential floor, or floors must be proved for each channel class, as ch3:234 says.

A cheap improvement to D04-DYN-04 states shared Lyapunov stability of the full and Gibbs-reduced flows for fixed channels. The proof has three lines. E(u, β) = E_red(u) + T sum_i KL(β_i‖β_i*(u)) shows that (u*, β*(u*)) is a local minimum of E exactly when u* is a local minimum of E_red. For an analytic cost, Lyapunov stability of an equilibrium of a gradient flow in a metric uniformly equivalent to the Euclidean one is equivalent to local minimality: one direction uses the finite-length bound from the Łojasiewicz inequality, and the other uses the local constancy of the cost on the critical set. Together with `prop:dyn-morse` this makes the full and reduced flows interchangeable for every equilibrium statement, while their transients still differ at the rate T of `prop:dyn-row-relaxation`.

The unit-variance slice of D04-DYN-02 answers part of `op:dyn-three-pictures`, and it generalizes. For beliefs N(m_i, Σ) with a common fixed covariance Σ, identity channels and fixed rows, E restricted to the means equals, up to a constant, the mean-field free energy of the normalized model with independent Gaussian priors G_i and records o_ij given y ~ N(y_i - y_j, Σ/β_ij), registered at zero. This is the mean-sector form of `prop:sup-row-equivalence`, where R = TW with a common held W. Both say that a peer divergence and a normalized comparison likelihood agree exactly when the record noise is the held source covariance scaled by the coupling. On that restricted family the reciprocal and global objectives therefore have the same stationary points. The full reciprocal flow leaves the family, by `prop:dyn-gaussian-noninvariance` and the variance force at ch3:148, so the statement concerns the restricted flow of `def:dyn-restricted-flow` only. Stating it as a proposition would give the open problem its first positive case, and would identify exactly what the obstruction of `prop:dyn-no-global-vfe` uses: law-dependent record noise. Difficulty: low.

The stochastic version mentioned at ch3:403 has a natural candidate: the Riemannian Langevin diffusion on the product of simplices with the Fisher metric. It is reversible with respect to e^(-E/T_eff) times the Riemannian volume, which on each simplex is the Jeffreys (Dirichlet(1/2)) density. That stationary law is the normalized prior of the second identification at ch3:387, so one diffusion would join the two identifications of that paragraph. Two details must be settled first. With noise from n-message resampling per unit time, T_eff = 1/(2n), not 1/n (D04-DYN-06), so the row temperature and the configuration temperature agree only under a stated time unit. The first-order part of the Laplace–Beltrami generator corresponds, in Wright–Fisher language, to a specific mutation-like drift, so whether the diffusion reaches the faces of the simplex must be decided by Feller's boundary classification of the associated Jacobi diffusions. I did not do that classification. Difficulty: moderate on finite alphabets.
