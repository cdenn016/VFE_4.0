### D03-GEOM-01: With at most three proper complex Gaussian targets no section has an invertible aggregate jet on an open set; the lower-strata remark after geo:det-rank suggests otherwise
**Category:** error
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** appendices/determinant_example.tex:73 (remark after `geo:det-rank`); :61-72 (`geo:det-rank`, `geo:det-horizontal`); :22 (hypothesis of `geo:determinant-metric`); chapters/02_geometry.tex:279
**Evidence:** The remark reads: "The bound for three agents holds only on the stratum of maximal orbit dimension. Off it the horizontal space grows: two agents with proportional covariances $M$ and $2M$ have a four-dimensional orbit, $H$ of dimension four, and $d\mathcal M|_H$ surjective by the same choice of $k$. A section supplying an invertible $e$ is still required; the proposition shows only where one can exist." `geo:determinant-metric` needs $e=d\mathcal M_{\boldsymbol p}\circ r:T\mathcal U\to\operatorname{Herm}(W)$ to be a bundle isomorphism on a four-dimensional $\mathcal U$, with $r_X=P_Hd\boldsymbol p(X)$. Surjectivity of $d\mathcal M|_H$ at a single tuple of smaller orbit dimension does not give that, and no section can.

Reconstruction. Let $k(c)=\operatorname{rank}J_{\boldsymbol p(c)}$. It is lower semicontinuous, so the set $\mathcal U'$ where it is locally constant is open and dense. (i) Where $k=7$ ($n=3$, maximal stratum), `geo:det-rank` gives $\operatorname{rank}e\le3$. (ii) For proper complex Gaussian targets the stabilizer of $(M_i)$ is conjugate to the group of unitaries commuting with every $M_1^{-1/2}M_jM_1^{-1/2}$; it has dimension 1, 2 or 4 according as these Hermitian matrices fail to commute, commute without all being scalar, or are all scalar. So $k=7$, $6$ or $4$, with $k=6$ exactly on the jointly diagonalizable locus (every non-proportional pair when $n=2$) and $k=4$ on the proportional locus. These are orbit-type strata of a proper action, hence embedded submanifolds, and a component of $\mathcal U'$ with $k<7$ maps into one of them. In the normal frame $M_1=I$, $M_j=D_j$ diagonal, the non-orbit tangent directions of the stratum are diagonal triples; at diagonal base points diagonal and off-diagonal Hermitian matrices are orthogonal for $\sum_iw_i\operatorname{Tr}(M_i^{-1}hM_i^{-1}k)$, so $P_H$ of a diagonal triple is diagonal. Hence $e(X)=\sum_iw_ir_{X,i}$ lies in a fixed two-dimensional real subspace at each point (one-dimensional, the multiples of $M$, on the proportional stratum): rank $e\le2$, respectively $\le1$. (iii) For $n=1$, $H=0$. So $\det e=0$ on the dense set $\mathcal U'$, and a continuous $e$ has $\det e\equiv0$. At an isolated tuple of smaller orbit dimension where $d\mathcal M|_H$ is onto, a first jet with $r$ spanning $H$ must leave the stratum, the orbit dimension jumps, and $e$ is discontinuous there.

Numerical support (`scripts/D03-GEOM/s09_det_sections.py`, output in `s09_det_sections.out`): a two-agent section through $(I,2I)$ with transverse jet has four singular values of $e$ equal to $0.25$ at the crossing, but at distances $10^{-3}$ and $10^{-4}$ from it only two nonzero singular values (about $0.25$; the other two at most $2.2\times10^{-9}$). At distance $10^{-5}$ the near-stabilizer locked inertia (about $10^{-10}$) falls below a pseudoinverse cutoff of $10^{-9}$ and the solver reproduces the crossing value, which illustrates the ill-conditioning near the isotropy jump. Sections kept on the proportional stratum give rank 1 (singular values $0.28$ and below $2\times10^{-11}$), sections on the jointly diagonalizable three-agent stratum give rank 2 (third singular value at most $1.4\times10^{-12}$), and generic triples give rank 3 (fourth at most $8.4\times10^{-14}$). The 2026-10-22 L02-GEOM-06 conclusion, that the isomorphism hypothesis on a four-dimensional base needs at least four agents in this family, is therefore correct for solderings. The R-GEOM "corrected form" (two agents with covariances M and 2M give a surjective map; "three agents (I, 2I, generic) give rank 4") concerns the map at single tuples. My first run of `s05_determinant.py` reported rank 4 for generic triples; that was a sign error in my own Hermitian basis, fixed in the saved script, after which rank 3 is reproduced (fourth singular value $3.5\times10^{-16}$).
**Fix:** Replace the three sentences by: "For $n\le3$ centered proper complex Gaussian targets, no section over an open four-dimensional base has an aggregate jet that is a continuous bundle isomorphism: rank $e\le3$ on the maximal stratum, $\le2$ on the jointly diagonalizable stratum and $\le1$ on the proportional stratum, and the orbit dimension is locally constant on an open dense set. At an isolated tuple of smaller orbit dimension $d\mathcal M|_H$ can be onto, but a section realizing it leaves that stratum and its $e$ is discontinuous there. Four agents are the minimum in this family, and the witness above shows that four suffice on an open set." Add "at least four agents for proper complex Gaussian targets" to the sentence at `02_geometry.tex:279`.
**Falsifies:** A smooth section of at most three centered proper complex Gaussian laws over an open subset of a four-manifold on which $e$ is continuous and invertible at some point.

### D03-GEOM-02: The recommended class-invariant curvature, "J F on the radical", vanishes identically; J F on all pairs is the invariant of the minimizing class
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/02_geometry.tex:230 (paragraph after `geo:collective`, last sentence), using `geo:structure-identity`
**Evidence:** The text says: "Curvature statements about a class should be made for $J_{\boldsymbol p}F$ on the radical, which by \cref{geo:structure-identity} is the same for every representative, or after a representative has been fixed." I reconstructed the following by hand for any smooth minimizing representative $A$ at constant rank of $J$, so that $V=D^A\boldsymbol p=r$ takes values in $H=(\operatorname{im}J)^{\perp_{\mathsf G}}$.

(a) Representative independence on every pair. For $\beta$ with values in $\ker J_{\boldsymbol p(c)}$, `geo:structure-identity` applied to $A$ and $A+\beta$ (same $V$) gives $JF^{A+\beta}(X,Y)-JF^A(X,Y)=d_pj(r_Y,\beta(X))-d_pj(r_X,\beta(Y))$. For $\beta\in\ker J_p$ the map $v\mapsto d_pj(v,\beta)$ is the linearization at the zero $p$ of the Killing field $\beta^*$, that is, the linear isotropy action. It is $\mathsf G$-skew and preserves $T_p(\text{orbit})$, so it preserves $H_p$. The difference therefore lies in $H\cap\operatorname{im}J=0$.

(b) Vanishing on the radical. For $Z$ with $r_Z=0$ one has $d\boldsymbol p(Z)=-JA(Z)$. Differentiating $\mathsf G(r_X,J\xi)=0$ along $Z$, and using the Killing equation for $A(Z)^*$ together with $[\xi^*,\eta^*]=-[\xi,\eta]^*\in\operatorname{im}J\perp r_X$, gives $P_{\operatorname{im}J}(Z(r_X)+d_pj(r_X,A(Z)))=0$. Inserting this in `geo:structure-identity` with $VZ=0$ gives $JF(Z,X)=0$ for every $X$. So $JF$ restricted to the radical, in either argument, is zero for every minimizing representative, and the recommended statement carries no information.

Numerical check (`s03_isotropy_class_curvature.py`): $U(2)$ acting on two complex location laws with $\psi_2=z\psi_1$ and fixed norm of $\psi_1$, base $\mathbb R^4$; rank $J=3$ and $\ker J$ is one-dimensional with a direction that varies with $c$. Structure-identity residual $3.9\times10^{-12}$ (convention check); the maximum of $|JF^A-JF^{A+\beta}|$ over all pairs is $2.3\times10^{-13}$ against a maximum $|JF|$ of $2.08$, while the full curvatures differ by $0.12$; the maximum of $|JF(Z,\cdot)|$ is $1.2\times10^{-12}$ for $Z$ in the two-dimensional radical; $JF(\partial_1,\partial_2)$ has norm $2.33$ and lies along the phase orbit direction $J(iI)$ (absolute cosine 1.000000), a Berry-type class curvature; a non-minimizing control connection changes $JF$ by $0.48$. Chapter 11's `form:geometry` uses an $SO(2)$ action with distinct model eigenvalues, hence zero infinitesimal isotropy, so no current downstream claim relies on the misdirected sentence. The sentence implements the fix proposed by 2026-10-22 L02-GEOM-08, which carried the same recommendation.
**Fix:** Replace the sentence by: "For minimizing representatives $J_{\boldsymbol p}F$ is the same on every pair of directions and vanishes whenever one argument lies in the radical; curvature statements about a class should be made for $J_{\boldsymbol p}F$, or after a representative is fixed, since the stabilizer-valued part of $F$ depends on that choice." Add the proofs (a) and (b).
**Falsifies:** A constant-rank configuration with nonzero infinitesimal isotropy and two minimizing representatives whose $JF$ differ on some pair, or a minimizing representative with $JF(Z,X)\ne0$ for some $Z$ in the radical.

### D03-GEOM-03: Involutivity of the radical and basicness of the minimal tensor need only constant rank of J; geo:shape-pullback assumes ker J = 0, and the word "always" at line 177 cites it

### D03-GEOM-07: Five wording slips in worked examples and terminology
**Category:** exposition
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/02_geometry.tex:58, :151; companion/c06_geometry.tex:69, :187, :193
**Evidence:** (i) 02_geometry:58, "Summing its squared score over the four outcomes gives $1/(1-\rho^2)$": the unweighted sum is $4(1+\rho^2)/(1-\rho^2)^2$ (sympy, `s01`); the Fisher information is the $\zeta_\rho$-weighted sum. (ii) 02_geometry:151, "A basic semidefinite tensor of this kind is a bundle-like transverse metric": in the standard terminology (Reinhart 1959; Molino 1988, Ch. 3, not re-read here) the basic semidefinite tensor is a transverse metric, and a bundle-like metric is a Riemannian metric on the whole manifold whose transverse part is such a tensor. (iii) c06:187, "Expanding its squared norm gives the fine information loss minus two cross terms and minus $\|a\|^2$": it is the difference of the fine and coarse tensors, not the squared norm of the coarse derivative, that equals that expression. (iv) c06:193, "the metric is then one half of a complex-Gaussian Fisher metric minus the square of the entropy rate": `geo:det-canonical` has $\gamma=\tfrac12(h^{\mathcal{CN}}-\vartheta\otimes\vartheta)$, both terms halved; the companion's own timelike criterion matches the main text, so only the phrase is off. (v) c06:69, "the only metric on finite statistical models invariant under Markov congruent embeddings": Cencov's theorem concerns the family of metrics on all finite simplices; the main text says "finite statistical simplices".
**Fix:** (i) "Its expected squared score gives"; (ii) "is a transverse metric of the foliation by the fibers; extended by any metric along the fibers it gives a bundle-like metric"; (iii) "Subtracting its squared norm from the fine one gives"; (iv) "one half of the difference between a complex-Gaussian Fisher metric and the square of the entropy rate"; (v) "on finite statistical simplices".
**Falsifies:** For (i), a reading in which the outcomes are weighted; the others are terminology and phrasing.

## Extensions and improvements

A four-agent theorem for determinant solderings, and a test of whether it belongs to the circular family. D03-GEOM-01 gives the statement: for centered proper complex Gaussian targets an aggregate jet that is a continuous bundle isomorphism on an open four-dimensional base exists only for n at least 4, and the witness of `geo:det-rank` shows that 4 suffices on an open set. The bound comes from circularity: the central phase acts trivially, the stabilizers are large on the jointly diagonalizable locus, and residuals there are diagonal. For improper complex Gaussian laws CN(0, M, P), with pseudo-covariance P transforming by g P g^T, each agent law has ten real parameters, the central phase acts on P, and the generic stabilizer in GL(2, C) is expected to be finite, so dim H = 10n - 8 and n = 2 is the first count at which rank e = 4 is dimensionally possible. Whether it is attained, and whether the normal equation then fixes the phase component of the connection (as the appendix anticipates), is a finite rank computation of the kind in `s05` and `s09`. A positive answer would show that the four-agent requirement is a property of the proper family and not of the determinant construction. Difficulty: low for the theorem, low to moderate for the improper computation.

Representative-free curvature of the collective class. D03-GEOM-02 and D03-GEOM-03 show that J F is the invariant of the minimizing class and vanishes on the radical. In the free case it has a classical reading: F^{A^F} = -p^*Omega with Omega horizontal, Omega(v, w) = -theta([v~, w~]) for horizontal extensions, so J F(X, Y) = V[r~_X, r~_Y] = 2 A_{r_X} r_Y, twice the O'Neill integrability tensor of the submersion evaluated on the residuals (sign fixed by the chapter's conventions; I derived this by hand and did not compute it numerically). The collective connection is then nonflat along the section exactly when the horizontal lifts of its shape velocities fail to be integrable, and O'Neill's formula K_quotient = K + 3|A_X Y|^2 links that curvature to the sectional curvature of shape space that `open:geo-gauged-flow` mentions. On an orbit-type stratum of a proper action the same holds for the stratum's own submersion, which covers the isotropy case of `s03` (where J F lies along the phase orbit, a Berry-type curvature). This gives the formation chapters a curvature test that needs only G, J and the residuals. Difficulty: low.

Stratified shape pullback. With constant rank of J, the residual lies in the tangent space of the orbit-type stratum S containing the section, and P_H restricted to TS is the horizontal projection of the orbit foliation of S. Hence h^min = (pi_S o p)^* g_{S/G} for the quotient metric of the stratum, which extends `geo:shape-metric` beyond free actions and explains the rank bounds of D03-GEOM-01 (the shape space of the jointly diagonalizable stratum has dimension 4 but its residuals span only a two-dimensional image under dM). Difficulty: low.

Geometric inheritance in chapter 5. `geo:minimal-contraction-identity` already gives an exact monotone statement for any fixed equivariant Markov retention in the same normalization: h^min - hbar^min = Delta(r, r) + kbar(A^F - Abar^F, A^F - Abar^F), both terms nonnegative and telescoping over a chain. Exact preservation of the minimal tensor holds exactly when both terms vanish, i.e. no Fisher loss along the shape residual and retention of the connection. Writing clause (G) of `inh:inherited-parent` with this identity (D03-GEOM-04) would make geometric inheritance quantitative instead of a yes/no condition. Difficulty: low.

Graph refinement. D03-GEOM-08 sketches the energy limit from the finite edge cost of chapter 15 to `geo:context-energy`. The next step is a Gamma-convergence theorem on a flat torus in a fixed chart with uniform constant rank and locked-inertia floor, and after it a convergence theorem for the gradient flows. The second is the obstacle; the frame directions carry no diffusion in the limit (chapter 15), so the discrete frame equations must be shown to converge without a parabolic estimate. Difficulty: moderate for energies, high for flows.
