### XC6-PROG-01: Formation under the collective free energy with a latent structure variable
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** critical
**Location:** `chapters/03_dynamics.tex:236-270` (sec:dyn-likelihood, prop:dyn-global-local); `chapters/01_foundations.tex:199-226` (def:content-law, prop:content-law); `chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1396-1399` (inh:formation-open); PIFB2 `05_section.tex:6-13` (eq:population_joint), `10_section.tex:18-36` (eq:hierarchical_normalized_joint, eq:cavity_predictive_prior); earlier `SPEC.md:72-75` (random membership as a structural mark), `CONTENT.md:824-902`
**Evidence:** No selection, formation or parent-dynamics result in 2.0 is proved under the global free energy. The only parent object under a global joint is the supplied rigid-cluster restriction of chapter 19, which AUTHORING calls "supplied-cluster reductions, not selection" (`AUTHORING.md:91`). Yet the global picture is the only one of the three objectives with an evidence, one objective fixed across records, and an exact meaning for top-down influence: PIFB2's cavity prior $p_i^{\rm cav}(dk_i)\propto\int p_i(dk_i\mid z)r(z)\prod_{j\ne i}m_j(z)dz$ uses each child's record once (`10_section.tex:28-32`). Construction sketch. Extend the model of prop:dyn-global-local by a structural latent $\Pi$, a partition of the agents (or a nested pair of partitions) with a proper prior on a finite catalog; block latents $z_B\sim r_B$; children $y_i\mid z_B\sim p(y_i\mid z_B)$ for $i\in B$; and the comparison records of eq:sample-comparison. The posterior of $\Pi$ given the records is a derived selector. Under recognition $Q(\Pi)\otimes Q(z)\otimes Q(y)$ the structure factor is $Q(\Pi)\propto\pi(\Pi)e^{-F_\Pi}$ with $F_\Pi$ the block free energy, of which the "structure free energy" of `Item_7_formation_feedback.tex:52-54` is a special case. For linear-Gaussian children with conjugate block latents the block posterior depends on the children only through summed natural statistics, so the parent recognition factor obeys a closed equation; this exactness is a Gaussian-family feature and must be stated as one. Deleting the comparison records leaves $Q(\Pi)$ at its prior, so selection is caused by the records (this satisfies weak (F5); the strong form needs more). The second-level partition posterior reads first-level block statistics, so a counterfactual first-level handoff changes it, which is counterfactual dependence in the sense of the earlier R14. Obstacles: (i) (F2) asks the dynamics to select; with records arriving over time the selection becomes posterior concentration, and persistence must be an evidence statement with a horizon clause (earlier R15); (ii) exact inheritance fails beyond conjugate linear-Gaussian families (Item 7 reports that closure of the pooled parent for every configuration forces a Gaussian location family, `Item_7_formation_feedback.tex:28`), so general laws need an augmented parent state or a graded defect (XC6-PROG-10); (iii) a factorized label recognition discards label-state dependence (PIFB2 `05_section.tex:122`). Prior art to cite and to separate from: Bayesian hierarchical clustering (Heller and Ghahramani 2005), the nested Chinese restaurant process (Blei, Griffiths and Jordan 2010), partitioned variational inference (Ashman et al. 2022; vault record read). Difficulty: medium for a static Gaussian theorem (exact posterior on a finite catalog, cavity feedback, exact block inheritance, counterfactual dependence of the second level); high for a dynamical version meeting (F1)-(F6) on a non-Gaussian family.
**Fix:** Pose this construction as the first case of inh:formation-open under the global free energy, with the derived selector, the cavity feedback and the graded persistence clause stated in the theorem.
**Falsifies:** A proof that in this model the second-level partition posterior is the same for every first-level handoff state in an open set of record configurations (no counterfactual dependence), or that the parent recognition factor fails to close even for conjugate linear-Gaussian children.

### XC6-PROG-02: The no-free-energy obstruction is stated well beyond what prop:dyn-no-global-vfe proves, and its unqualified reading is false on atomless spaces
**Category:** inflation
**Severity:** high
**Location:** `frontmatter.tex:5`; `chapters/01_foundations.tex:197`, `:226`; `chapters/03_dynamics.tex:4`, `:52`, `:375-387` (prop:dyn-no-global-vfe); `chapters/06_outlook.tex:6`; `AUTHORING.md:39`; `README.md:34`; `companion/c03_dynamics.tex:12`; `companion/glossary.tex:20`; `frontmatter.tex:24`
**Evidence:** The proposition proves two restricted statements. (a) On finite alphabets no model over finitely many finite-valued variables, with any recognition rule, reproduces $\mathcal E$: a finite free energy is bounded by $\max_{\operatorname{supp}\Pi_o}(-\log\Pi_o)$ while $\mathcal E\ge\beta_{ij}\KL(q_i\Vert q_j)$ is unbounded on the interior. (b) With fixed channels, no model with the product recognition $\bigotimes_k\zeta_k$ reproduces $\mathcal E$ on an open set: the mixed derivative $\partial_{q_i}\partial^2_{q_j}$ vanishes for a mean-field free energy and equals $\beta_{ij}v_1^3[q_j(1)^{-2}-q_j(2)^{-2}]$ for $\mathcal E$ (recomputed, s2(c): 8.67199 by finite differences against 8.67187 from the formula; the mean-field value is $-2\times10^{-7}$). Both proofs are correct. The abstract says "we prove that it is not the free energy of any fixed normalized model whose recognition laws are the agent laws", and `01_foundations.tex:226` says the proposition "shows that it is not $\mathcal F(Q;o)$ for any fixed normalized model with these marginals as recognition laws". The second sentence is false on atomless spaces, and the first is false under the same marginal reading. Counterexample: fix positive rows, take the fixed normalized model $P=\bigotimes_iR_i$ with no record ($Z_o=1$), put $\Delta(x)=\mathcal E(x)-\mathcal E_0(x)\ge0$, and let $Q(x)$ be the Gaussian-copula coupling of the agent laws $\zeta_1,\dots,\zeta_N$ with equicorrelation $r(x)$ solving $-\tfrac12[(N-1)\log(1-r)+\log(1+(N-1)r)]=\Delta(x)$. The chain rule gives $\KL(Q\Vert\bigotimes_iR_i)=\sum_i\KL(\zeta_i\Vert R_i)+\operatorname{TC}(Q)$, and total correlation is invariant under the strictly increasing marginal maps that define a copula, so $\mathcal F(Q(x);o)=\mathcal E(x)$ for every interior $x$, with the marginals of $Q(x)$ equal to the agent laws. s1: maximal residual $2.5\times10^{-12}$ over 340 two-agent Gaussian instances, $4.6\times10^{-7}$ over 100 four-agent instances with fixed rows and the row-entropy constant, and $1.2\times10^{-5}$ for a Gaussian-mixture and a logistic marginal by grid quadrature (the quadrature resolution: mass defect $2.6\times10^{-6}$, copula mutual information 0.2367032 against 0.2367152). On finite alphabets the construction fails exactly where (a) says it must: the total correlation of any coupling of two binary laws is at most $\log2$, while $\KL(q_1\Vert q_2)$ reaches 9.90 at $t=0.9999$ (s1(d)). The rule $x\mapsto Q(x)$ is reverse-engineered; that is the point, since clause (a) quantifies over every rule and the general theory works on standard Borel spaces. Consequences. (1) What separates the reciprocal and global objectives is the recognition family (product versus coupled, part (b)) and the kinetic metric: along $\{Q(x)\}$ the reciprocal flow is the gradient flow of the fixed-model free energy $\mathcal F\circ Q$ in the sum of marginal Fisher metrics, not in the Fisher metric of $Q(x)$. (2) Product recognition with law-dependent channels on atomless spaces is covered by neither (a) nor (b), so the abstract's claim is unproved there even under the product reading. (3) `frontmatter.tex:24` ("Finite alphabets ... enter only where an example or a differential calculation requires them") is contradicted: here the finite alphabet is what makes a theorem true. (4) The 2026-10-22 X3-PIFB-01 summary that the sample-level identification "is impossible" overstated its own finite-alphabet result, and the C1 entry repeated it.
**Fix:** Replace every occurrence by the proved scope, for example "on finite alphabets for every recognition rule, and with fixed channels for the product recognition whose factors are the agent laws". In `01_foundations.tex:226` delete "with these marginals as recognition laws" or restrict the sentence to finite alphabets. Add the copula identity as a remark after prop:dyn-no-global-vfe stating that on atomless spaces the obstruction is a property of the product recognition family and of the metric. Optionally settle (b) for law-dependent channels.
**Falsifies:** A failure of copula invariance of total correlation for atomless marginals (there is none: it is invariance of mutual information under coordinatewise bimeasurable maps), or a reading of the abstract restricted to product recognition with fixed channels (the abstract would then still omit "fixed channels").

### XC6-PROG-03: The formation program sits on the least normalized of the three objectives; one normalized objective must be primary
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** high
**Location:** `chapters/03_dynamics.tex:4`, `:134`, `:426`, `:437-438` (op:dyn-three-pictures); `AUTHORING.md:35-41`; `chapters/06_outlook.tex:6`, `:16`; `chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1394`; PIFB2 `01_section.tex:10`, `05_section.tex:4`, `:29`, `:223`; earlier `SPEC.md:64-66`
**Evidence:** Chapters 6 to 12, which hold every multi-stage construction, use the reciprocal potential; chapters 13 and 14 give one-stage per-agent results; nothing is proved under the global free energy (XC6-PROG-01). The manuscript lists what the reciprocal potential gives up, "an evidence for model comparison, a posterior that the dynamics targets ..., one objective that spans both interaction and data and is monotone across records, and a learning criterion" (`03_dynamics.tex:426`), notes that its stationary set does not depend on records (same line), and that a communicating population realizes it only if each source can read the laws of every receiver that attends to it (`:134`). PIFB2 takes "the normalized joint as the foundation" (`01_section.tex:10`) and calls the normalized collective model "the principal construction of the monograph" (`05_section.tex:223`); the earlier theory's "program's dynamics is joint descent of the collective free energy" (`SPEC.md:64`). AUTHORING keeps the three objectives "side by side" without a decision. The separations are now sharper than op:dyn-three-pictures suggests: by XC6-PROG-13 the law-level per-agent dynamics is never coordinate descent of any global functional on an open set when an agent attends to a peer, and its stationary points are critical points of $\mathcal F_{\rm tot}=\sum_i\mathcal F_i$ only where every source is a deconvolution fixed point of its receivers' pool. Results therefore do not transfer between objectives by default, and the choice of primary objective decides which results count for the program.
**Fix:** The reciprocal potential should not be primary. Make the per-agent free energy the primary agent dynamics, with peers' communications entering each agent's fixed normalized model as records (messages) rather than as prior components; PIFB2 objects to the latter ("Replacing a generative factor by another agent's current posterior changes the model during inference", `05_section.tex:29`), and cor:dyn-frozen-source already shows the law-level per-agent row terms are $T$ times a fixed-model free energy only per frozen episode. Make the global free energy of a block the object that defines when its members have a common generative model, which is where formation lives (XC6-PROG-01). Keep the reciprocal potential as the configuration-level device of thm:dyn-registered and label its results as such. Record the decision in AUTHORING and the outlook, and re-prove one formation result under the primary objective before extending the reciprocal track.
**Falsifies:** A transfer theorem showing that the conclusions of chapter 11 or 12 hold under the per-agent or global dynamics.

A selector that thresholds rows above the prior therefore never fires along $\Phi^0$, and (F5) holds on the whole admitted class for every such selector. Chapter 12 does something else: it reads "the same rows" recomputed from the full peer costs at the null laws ("the rows at time $t$ are a Gaussian kernel of bandwidth $e^t$ on $u(0)$", `12:249`); chapter 8 reads "the source of smallest reciprocal row cost" along the null (`08:261-263`). **Fix:** State in inh:null-flow that the selector is a declared measurable function of the matter configuration and the accessible records (laws, records, declared interfaces), that rows and costs enter it only as declared functions of the laws, and that it is evaluated identically along $\Phi$ and $\Phi^0$ (the readout convention, which chapters 8 and 12 already use). ### XC6-PROG-05: The formation standard leaves its topology, its (F5) quantifier, the (F4) probe class and the (F6b) counterfactual class to the constructor
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** high
**Location:** `chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1385-1392` (F1, F4, F5, F6), `:1394`, `:155-166` (inh:trajectory-remark); `chapters/06_fusion.tex:683-684`; `chapters/11_conditional_formation.tex:393`; `chapters/08_smooth_erasure.tex:261`; earlier `SPEC.md:232-249` (R14)
**Evidence:** (i) (F1) asks for a class "open in a declared topology on a declared space", and (F5) for $P\ne P^0$ "on a nonempty open subset of the admitted class". With the discrete topology both are vacuous. The alternative "or a declared invariant family" makes openness relative: chapter 8 ("(F1) holds in its invariant-family form"), chapter 11 ("open within it") and the chapter 13 angular construction use it, so "open" there means open in a finite-dimensional supplied family, which has empty interior in law space, while chapter 12 is open in its $C^r$ chart. inh:trajectory-remark explains why thin admitted sets carry little inheritance information, but the standard does not grade them. (ii) "$P\ne P^0$ on a nonempty open subset" does not say whether the inequality holds at every point of the subset or only somewhere on it. (iii) (F4) requires that "some admitted child-level probe distinguishes" two configurations of a fiber; that probe cannot lie in the protocol class $A_I$ of (F3) (whose responses factor through $R_I$), and no rule requires its class to be declared before the retention, so (F4) reduces to non-injectivity. (iv) (F6b) requires that the second stage "does not occur from every counterfactual handoff in the inherited class", and "inherited class" is defined nowhere. Chapter 11 evaluates it on handoffs "with sufficiently separated parents" (`05:1394`, `11:393`), chapter 6 on every handoff (`06:683`). Any bounded-confidence dynamics satisfies (F6b) once the class contains a handoff whose parents are far apart. The earlier R14 closed this: the class "contains every first-stage handoff and, with probability bounded below, the handoffs of histories without first-stage formation", and "a second-stage proof that uses only hypotheses on the handoff state that also hold for counterfactual handoffs in the class establishes a supplied-scale sequence". The 2026-10-22 H2 and X4-PRIOR-01 are therefore only partly resolved.
**Fix:** Fix the topology to the chart topology in which the flow is well posed and state it per construction; grade (F1) as full (open in the law chart) or family (open in a declared invariant family), and report the grade. Write (F5) as "there is a nonempty open $U$ with $P(x)\ne P^0(x)$ for every $x\in U$". Require the child-level probe class of (F4) to be declared with $A_I$. Define the counterfactual class of (F6b) as the post-handoff states reachable from the admitted class by histories in which the first-stage block was not selected, or adopt R14.
**Falsifies:** A definition elsewhere of the topology or of the inherited class (grep for "inherited class", "handoff class" and "counterfactual handoff" over chapters, companion and Item 7 finds only uses, and Item 7's own notion of walled states).

### XC6-PROG-06: The standard does not require what the program means by a further agent: type closure, top-down efficacy, and a parent-level free energy
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** high

The standard also admits purely descriptive membership. **Fix:** Add a type-closure clause (the inherited parent is type-closed for the declared class, inh:type-closed, so the construction iterates) and state the depth it supports; add an efficacy grade (after the handoff the fine field depends on the retained parent state through a term absent before it, with Item 7's control without feedback as its null). **Falsifies:** A proof that (F1) to (F6) imply type closure for the constructions they admit (they do not: the chapter 11 parent is not shown type-closed, inh:type-closure-open).

### XC6-PROG-09: One message-level model whose limits are the three objectives
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** high
**Location:** `chapters/03_dynamics.tex:389-419` (thm:dyn-registered, cor:dyn-frozen-source), `:437-438` (op:dyn-three-pictures), `:236-268` (prop:dyn-global-local)

## Extensions and improvements

The decision that unlocks the rest is the choice of a primary objective. The reciprocal potential cannot carry the program: it has no evidence, its stationary set ignores records, and its source force exists only if sources read their receivers' laws. Its separation from a free energy is also not what the abstract says; on atomless spaces it equals the free energy of the fixed model $\bigotimes_iR_i$ at a copula coupling of the agent laws with total correlation $\mathcal E-\mathcal E_0$, so the separating features are the product recognition family and the sum of marginal Fisher metrics. The per-agent free energy is the natural agent objective once peers' communications enter as records: then each $\mathcal F_i$ is the free energy of a fixed normalized model, records persist, and the law-level per-agent free energy is the frozen-episode, large-message limit (cor:dyn-frozen-source). A meta-agent is then a block whose members' models are conditionals of one block model, which is where the global free energy enters.

The first construction to attempt in that setting is XC6-PROG-01. Put a structural latent $\Pi$ on a finite catalog of partitions, conjugate block latents and coded comparison records into one normalized model; its partition posterior is a derived selector, its cavity priors are top-down feedback that use each record once, and in the linear-Gaussian family the block posterior is a closed function of summed natural statistics. The theorem to prove is a static one first: concentration of $Q(\Pi)$ on a nontrivial partition for an open set of record configurations, exact block inheritance, and dependence of the second-level partition on the first-level block statistics. A dynamical version then replaces (F2) by posterior concentration as records accumulate, with an evidence horizon clause in the sense of the earlier R15.

Recursion needs type closure, and XC6-PROG-07 gives the cheapest route. In the per-agent picture a shared-label block is a single receiver at temperature $T/|B|$ holding its pool (Item 7, general laws). If the pooled mean parameter, the dispersion of the members' mean parameters and the record sums form a closed finite state on a declared invariant submanifold of an exponential family, the block is again a per-agent agent and induction gives arbitrary depth. Item 7's characterization limits full closure to the Gaussian location family, so the target is closure on an invariant submanifold or with a finite augmentation.

Participation is the missing physics of the program. XC6-PROG-08 makes the first action in 2.0 a choice of whom to query, scored by an expected free energy; in the shared-target architecture of inh:common-clock, where passive attention gives a common clock, receiver-dependent querying should break the clock and give interaction-necessary selection on an open set of a small Bernoulli instance. This is also the place where the per-agent free energy becomes active inference rather than its perceptual half.

Two low-cost statements tighten chapter 3. XC6-PROG-13 settles the first part of op:dyn-three-pictures negatively by the Monderer-Shapley mixed-partial test and gives the exact criterion for per-agent stationary points to be critical points of $\mathcal F_{\rm tot}$: every source must be a fixed point of the maximum-likelihood deconvolution of its receivers' pool. XC6-PROG-21 identifies the reciprocal peer cost at such points with a static Schrodinger cost. Both rest on prop:dyn-compensation, whose source term is the EM or Richardson-Lucy direction (XC6-PROG-14).

On the formation standard, the immediate repairs are definitional (XC6-PROG-04 and -05): selectors read only the matter configuration and records, the chart topology is fixed, the (F5) quantifier is explicit, the (F4) probe class is declared with $A_I$, and the (F6b) class is the set of post-handoff states reachable from histories without first-stage formation. The program-level repairs are a type-closure clause and an efficacy grade (XC6-PROG-06) and a graded inheritance by exact defect functionals (XC6-PROG-10), so that interaction-created compression can be stated with its defect computed rather than excluded by exactness.
