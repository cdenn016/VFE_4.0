### XC7-IMPL-03: The Gaussian obstruction belongs to the reciprocal potential only; the global and per-agent flows preserve Gaussian products exactly, and chapter 3 does not say so
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** high
**Location:** `chapters/03_dynamics.tex:204-221` (def:dyn-restricted-flow, eq:dyn-gaussian-flow, prop:dyn-gaussian-noninvariance, scope sentence at 221), `:249-268` (prop:dyn-global-local), `:319-357` (def:dyn-per-agent, prop:dyn-per-agent, def:dyn-per-agent-flow), `chapters/18_link_dynamics.tex:7`
**Evidence:** 2026-10-22 H8 is resolved for the reciprocal potential. `def:dyn-restricted-flow` gives the Gaussian formulas, `prop:dyn-gaussian-noninvariance` has a correct proof, which I reconstructed, and 03:221 confines chapters 7 to 12 to full density charts. The chapter does not state the converse, which is what any Gaussian implementation needs. In the per-agent free energy `eq:dyn-per-agent-free-energy` the peers' laws are data. The first variation in q_i is therefore sum_j beta_ij (log q_i - E_{s_i} log kappa_ij(m,.)) - E_{s_i} log l_i, and the first variation in s_i adds beta_ii KL(q_i || G_i(m,.)) as a function of m. Suppose the evaluators are Gaussian with mean affine in m and fixed covariance, the channels are linear Gaussian (so every nu_ij is Gaussian), and the record log-likelihoods are quadratic in (k,m). Then every one of these terms is a polynomial of degree at most two, and the Gibbs envelope preserves this. In the global free energy, the first variation e-bar_i = E_{Q_{-i}} log p is quadratic in y_i for a Gaussian prior and linear-Gaussian comparison records, in both the receiver and the sender role, because peers enter only through expectations (`prop:dyn-global-local`). So in both pictures the full Fisher velocity is tangent to the Gaussian product family, and the restricted flow is the full flow. Script `s1` measures, at a three-agent state, the fraction of the Fisher norm of d/dt log q_1 lying outside span{1, x, x^2}. For the reciprocal potential it is 0.807 with identity channels and 0.390 with a linear-Gaussian channel. For the per-agent free energy it is 2.0e-30 and 2.5e-30. For the global free energy it is 1.5e-30, and for the reciprocal potential with its source role deleted it is 4.0e-31. Three consequences follow. In chapter 18 the global and per-agent tangent flows (`it2:global-flow`, `it2:per-agent-flow`) are exact flows on Gaussian laws, whereas the reciprocal coupled flow (`it2:coupled-flow`) is a restricted flow, as 18:7 says. VFE_4.0's CAVI is exact coordinate descent with no projection error (XC7-IMPL-01). A Gaussian engine can therefore realize two of the three objectives exactly. Separately, the hypothesis "exactly one receiver" of `prop:dyn-gaussian-noninvariance` is not needed. Take any finite set of receivers with positive weights and some q_k different from q_i, and a line on which log(q_k/q_i) is nonconstant. On that line each nonconstant term beta_ki e^{Q_k} either grows faster than any polynomial at one end or decays at both ends, and a positive sum of decaying terms tends to zero while staying positive, so the sum is not a polynomial. The second row of `s1` shows that the same holds for linear-Gaussian channels.
**Fix:** After `prop:dyn-gaussian-noninvariance`, add a Gaussian-family proposition. Under the per-agent free energies and the global free energy, with Gaussian references, evaluators affine in m with fixed covariance, linear-Gaussian channels and log-quadratic records, the Fisher flow preserves Gaussian products and `eq:dyn-gaussian-flow` is exact. Noninvariance is a property of the source role in `def:dyn-potential` and `def:dyn-evidence-potential`. Label it a feature of the family, as the general-law rule of AUTHORING.md requires. Weaken "exactly one receiver" to "at least one receiver k with q_k different from q_i".
**Falsifies:** A per-agent or global configuration in this Gaussian class whose Fisher velocity has a component outside the Gaussian tangent space.

### XC7-IMPL-07: The finite contextual coupling needs Fisher-Rao distances and logarithm maps that have no closed form on the multivariate Gaussian family
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** `chapters/15_contextual_coupling.tex:92-105` (ctx:f:eq:C), `:107-126` (ctx:f:statement:1), `:136-153` (ctx:f:statement:3), `:190-199` (eq:B, ctx:f:eq:flow), `:299-362` (two-context witness), `:364-373` (cusp)
**Evidence:** The edge cost is c_e = (1/2) d_N(p_v, U_e . p_u)^2, where d_N is "the intrinsic Riemannian distance of the admitted family" (15:99), and the forces are logarithm maps (ctx:f:eq:endpointgrad, eq:B). For Gaussian laws N(mu, Sigma) in which both mean and covariance vary, no closed form of the Fisher-Rao distance is known in general. Closed forms exist only on submanifolds: fixed mean (the affine-invariant distance on SPD matrices), fixed covariance, and the univariate (hyperbolic) case; see the survey of [Pinele, Strapasson and Costa (2020)](https://www.mdpi.com/1099-4300/22/4/404), whose abstract states that for the multivariate normal model "there is generally no explicit expression". Every edge and every time step would then need a numerical geodesic boundary-value problem inside the outer minimization over G. The chapter's witnesses use fixed-covariance location families, where d_N is Euclidean (15:318) and Procrustes gives R_* in closed form (s3(a) reproduces ctx:f:eq:witnessvelocity exactly and the cusp ctx:f:eq:cusp to eight digits). No computable surrogate or error statement is given for other families. VFE_4.0's full-patch Gaussians under GL(K) lie outside the closed-form cases. The continuum construction (`ctx:c`) needs only the metric and the infinitesimal action J_p, which are closed form for Gaussians, so it is implementable. D19-CTXF-06 independently proposes a KL edge cost with closed-form Gaussian forces.
**Fix:** Add a remark after ctx:f:statement:3. For families without a closed-form distance, the finite construction can be run with (i) the second-order cost (epsilon^2/2)||dp(X) + J_p A(X)||^2_G of 15:285, declared as the finite edge cost, or (ii) a smooth invariant divergence (D19-CTXF-06). The proofs of ctx:f:statement:3 and ctx:f:thm:ode use only smoothness, invariance and the branch hypothesis, so they apply to such a declared cost. Cite Pinele et al. (2020) for the absence of a closed form.
**Falsifies:** A closed-form Fisher-Rao distance and logarithm map on the full multivariate normal family.

## Extensions and improvements

Each specification below says what is computed, which parts are closed form and which sampled, the numerical hazards, the choices the theory leaves open, and the observables. All of them run on CPU in seconds and need no Gaussian engine.

**Chapter 13, per-agent grouping.** State: two agents with four-state belief and presentation tables (pa:four-*), or three Bernoulli agents (pa:bit-*), or three agents in the seven-parameter angular family (pa:angular-family), with Gibbs rows at unit temperature. Closed form: the own-block field (pa:four-potentials, pa:bit-flow, pa:angular-ode), the rows (pa:rows, pa:angular-weights), the null rows (pa:null-row), the spectrum of pa:four-linear, the equilibrium u_* and the spectrum of M, and the epsilon = 0 geometry pa:nonflat-limit.
