### D19-CTXF-01: The maximal solution of the finite theorem has no declared phase space; in law space the regular branch has monodromy and can stop being the minimizer with every hypothesis intact
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/15_contextual_coupling.tex:132-133 (`ctx:f:ass:branch`, "The local branch is continued only while these conditions hold"), :220-221 (`ctx:f:thm:ode`, "It extends uniquely to a maximal solution in the regular domain"), :240 (continuation criterion), :4 ("its minimized contextual cost"), :377 ("after group alignment", "Pure common frame variation can have zero cost"); chapters/02_geometry.tex:7 ("adds the minimized contextual energy"); companion/c15_contextual_coupling.tex:50 and :84 (same "regular domain").
**Evidence:** `ctx:f:ass:branch` is stated at one reference configuration, and `ctx:f:thm:ode` asserts a unique maximal solution "in the regular domain", a set the chapter never defines. Read as a subset of the law space N^V on which the hypothesis holds, it does not carry a single-valued reduced energy or vector field. Script `s3_branch_monodromy.py` uses a family that meets every stated hypothesis. The family is one agent per context, a belief location factor N(mu, I_2) and a centered model factor N(0, Sigma), with SO(2) rotating both sectors. Its Fisher metric is flat on the belief factor and one half of the affine-invariant metric on the model factor, so the family is a Hadamard manifold with squared distance smooth everywhere and Log global. The local potentials have the chapter's form, with a self source and identity channel, so D = 0 and the rows are singletons: E_w = KL(q_w (x) s_w || r_w G_w), r_w = N(0, Sigma_ref), G_w(m, dk) = N(m_w, I)(dk). The script reports three results.

(A) At exact agreement p_u = p_v = (0.3 e_1, diag(4, 1/4)), where the action is free and the locked inertia is 14.1525, the edge cost has two nondegenerate local minima: theta = 0 (cost 0, Hessian 14.1525) and theta = pi (cost 0.18, Hessian 13.9725). The theta = pi branch satisfies `ctx:f:ass:branch` and gives pure common frame variation positive cost.

(B) Rotating Sigma_v by alpha from 0 to pi is a closed loop of laws (closure error 4.6e-16). Continuing the theta = 0 branch around it ends at theta = pi, with minimum Hessian 13.9725 along the loop, and the branch energy at the same law configuration changes from 0 to 0.18. This is monodromy.

(C) Under the coupled flow `ctx:f:eq:flow` with lambda = 0.1, the belief reference at v is rotated by 150 degrees. The continued branch ceases to be the global minimizer at t = 0.82. Throughout the run the branch Hessian stays at least 14.0095 and the norm of mu_v stays at least 0.0777 (free action), but c_branch - min_U c reaches 0.1039: at t = 5, c_branch = 0.1160 against min_U c = 0.0121. The branch total energy F_* decreases at every step (from 0.16794 to 0.01399; largest single-step change -8.75e-8), which confirms that the script integrates the gradient flow of the theorem. After the crossing, C_* is a branch energy, not the "minimized contextual cost" of :4 and 02_geometry:7, and the force pulls toward a non-optimally aligned neighbor. Nothing in the theorem detects the crossing. The compact-group supplement (:155) and the near-agreement statement (:157) guarantee global minimality only near the initial pair. The chapter's cusp example cannot exhibit this effect. For SO(2) on location families the cost is a pure first harmonic, const - rho cos(theta - theta_*), with a single local minimum whenever rho > 0. Competing nondegenerate minima need a sector with second-harmonic dependence, here the covariance sector.
**Fix:** Pose the theorem on the regular critical set R = {(p, U) in N^V x G^E : d_U C = 0, d_U^2 C > 0, squared distance smooth near every compared pair}. By the implicit-function theorem R is an embedded submanifold whose projection to N^V is a local diffeomorphism. State existence, uniqueness, maximality and the compact-subset continuation criterion of :240 on R; compactness must then be taken in (p, U), not in p alone. Define the open subset R_glob of R on which every U_e is the unique global minimizing transport. State that C_* = min_U C, and the readings at :4, :377 and 02_geometry:7, hold only while the lifted solution stays in R_glob. Add the product location-covariance witness next to the cusp: the cusp shows nondifferentiability, and this witness shows a silent Maxwell crossing. The Extensions section sketches a switching rule that continues the minimized energy past such crossings.
**Falsifies:** Two results would retract this finding. One is a proof that the continued branch is single-valued on its law-space domain; s3(B) contradicts that unless the loop leaves the domain, which the Hessian lower bound 13.97 excludes. The other is a proof that no trajectory of `ctx:f:thm:ode` reaches a configuration where another regular branch has lower energy; s3(C) contradicts that unless its cost or vector field differs from `ctx:f:eq:C` and `ctx:f:eq:flow`.

### D19-CTXF-02: For the program's GL(K) frames the regular-branch hypothesis fails at every intra-context consensus, and the near-agreement theorem needs a compact group
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/15_contextual_coupling.tex:155-164 (global version, near-agreement statement, isotropy paragraph), :318 and :364-373 (both examples use SO(2) on location families); AUTHORING.md:87; VFE_4.0 docs/model-contract.md:19 and :84 (GL_R frames on full-covariance Gaussian laws).
**Evidence:** At coincident endpoints the group Hessian of c_e is the locked inertia k_p = G(J_p., J_p.) (:282), so its kernel is ker J_p, the Lie algebra of the collective stabilizer. Script `s6_glk_consensus_isotropy.py` lets GL(K) act on Gaussian laws by sample pushforward, (mu, Sigma) -> (A mu, A Sigma A^T), which is a Fisher isometry. At consensus (n identical generic laws) it finds dim ker J = 0, 1, 3 for K = 2, 3, 4, independent of n = 1, 2, 5, matching (K-1)(K-2)/2, the dimension of the stabilizer {A : A mu = mu, A Sigma A^T = Sigma}, which is isomorphic to O(K-1). Generic distinct agents with n >= 2 give 0. So for K >= 3 `ctx:f:ass:branch` fails at every consensus collective and at every one-agent context, such as a context occupied by a single fused parent. The finite theorem therefore cannot follow a trajectory into the states the formation program targets. On the approach the branch is ill-conditioned: 2026-10-22 L02-GEOM-03 found the smallest locked-inertia eigenvalue 9.0e-4, 9.2e-6, 9.2e-8 at separations 1e-1, 1e-2, 1e-3 for GL(3). The positive near-agreement statement (:157) assumes a compact group acting freely; GL(K) is noncompact and is not free at consensus. Both chapter examples use SO(2) on location families, where agreement is free (02_geometry:246 says so), so for the program's group the analogous statement does not hold. The quotient route (:164) needs an invariant smooth stratum. With fixed references that are only passively covariant (:88), the local force at a consensus point need not lie in the stabilizer-fixed subspace, so a flow on the stratum is a different dynamics, not a restriction of `ctx:f:eq:flow`.
**Fix:** After :157, state that for GL(K) acting on Gaussian collectives with K >= 3, consensus and single-law contexts lie outside `ctx:f:ass:branch`, and give the dimension count. Then either adopt D19-CTXF-03 for free, non-consensus configurations, or develop the quotient version on a declared invariant stratum. Invariance holds when the local data are actively invariant under the stabilizer H, because the gradient at an H-fixed point is then H-fixed and tangent to the fixed-point set. The chapter's fixed references do not provide that.
**Falsifies:** A collective of identical Gaussian laws on R^K with K >= 3 for which J has trivial kernel under GL(K); the stabilizer computation and s6 exclude it. Alternatively, a declaration that the program's frame group acts freely at consensus, for example a restriction to a subgroup with trivial consensus isotropy.

### D19-CTXF-03: Compactness of G can be replaced by a proper isometric action on a complete family, with an explicit radius
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/15_contextual_coupling.tex:155 ("One sufficient global version assumes compact G ...") and :157.
**Evidence:** The argument at :155 uses compactness twice: for a positive cost gap off a neighborhood of the minimizer, and for uniform continuity. Both follow from weaker structure. Isometry and the triangle inequality give, simultaneously for all U in G, the bound abs(d(p_v, U p_u) - d(p_v^0, U p_u^0)) <= d(p_u, p_u^0) + d(p_v, p_v^0), which replaces uniform continuity with an exact 1-Lipschitz bound. If the action is proper and N is complete, then sublevel sets {U : d(p_v^0, U p_u^0) <= r} are compact: closed balls are compact by Hopf-Rinow (do Carmo 1992, Ch. 7, Thm. 2.8), and properness pulls compact sets back to compact sets. A unique global minimizing transported law therefore has a positive gap Delta = inf over U outside W of f_0(U) - f_0(U^0) for every neighborhood W of the coset U^0 G_{p_u^0}. Every global minimizer for (p_u, p_v) then lies in W whenever d(p_u, p_u^0) + d(p_v, p_v^0) < Delta/2. This covers the program's groups. GL(K) and Aff(K) act properly on Gaussian collectives, because congruence on SPD(K) = GL(K)/O(K) has compact isotropy and properness of one factor gives properness of the diagonal action. The full Gaussian Fisher manifold is homogeneous under Aff(K), hence complete (homogeneous Riemannian manifolds are complete; locator in Lee 2018, Introduction to Riemannian Manifolds, Ch. 6, not re-verified). Asking for a unique minimizing transported law, rather than a unique group element, also covers discrete stabilizers such as -I on centered laws, since B_v depends only on U p_u.
**Fix:** Replace the compact-group paragraph with a proposition. If G acts properly and isometrically on a complete N, and at (p_u^0, p_v^0) the minimizing transported law is unique and nondegenerate modulo the stabilizer, then for d(p_u, p_u^0) + d(p_v, p_v^0) < Delta/2 the implicit branch is the global minimizer. Restate :157 for proper actions that are free at p_0.
**Falsifies:** A proper isometric action on a complete family for which global minimizers escape W at pairs arbitrarily close to a pair with a unique nondegenerate minimizing law. The uniform bound above excludes this.

### D19-CTXF-04: The finite connection equation is a vanishing-momentum condition; the contextual force is horizontal, and the minimized energy is a discrete Dirichlet energy on shape space
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/15_contextual_coupling.tex:107-128 (`ctx:f:statement:1`, `ctx:f:eq:stationarity`), :190-198 (`ctx:f:eq:B`, `ctx:f:eq:flow`), :375-381 ("What the contextual energy controls").
**Evidence:** Let phi be any cost on pairs with phi(gx, gz) = phi(x, z). Differentiating this invariance at the identity gives dphi(J_x xi, J_z xi) = 0. Stationarity in U is dphi(0, J_z xi) = 0, hence dphi(J_x xi, 0) = 0: the endpoint force at x = p_v is Fisher-orthogonal to the orbit there. For phi = 1/2 d^2 this is conservation of the Noether momentum <gamma', xi^*> along the minimizing geodesic, since fundamental fields of an isometric action are Killing. So stationarity at either endpoint is equivalent to horizontality of the entire geodesic. Script `s4_curved_endpoint_noether.py` checks this on the curved product family with two agents. At the minimizing transport both momenta are at most 9.7e-11. At a transport rotated 0.7 rad away they are equal and opposite, for example (-4.3717, +4.3717). The momentum along the geodesic stays at 5.4867468623 to all printed digits at t = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1. This has two consequences the chapter states only in words. (a) G_{p_v}(B_v, J_{p_v} xi) = 0 for all xi: the contextual coupling exerts no torque, so the orbit (frame) component of the velocity at p_v is exactly that of the local flow. This sharpens "frames have no new evolution equation" (:381). (b) On R_glob (D19-CTXF-01) with a free proper action, c_e(U^*) = 1/2 d_{N/G}([p_u], [p_v])^2 by definition of the orbit-space metric. Log_{p_v}(U^* p_u) is the horizontal lift of the quotient logarithm, because horizontal geodesics project to geodesics of the quotient metric (O'Neill 1966). Hence lambda C_* is the weighted discrete Dirichlet energy of the shape map V -> N/G, and the contextual force is the horizontal lift of its discrete Laplacian. The abstract states the continuum counterpart ("a quotient Dirichlet energy"), but the finite section has no such statement.
**Fix:** Add a proposition after `ctx:f:statement:1` with the invariance argument and consequences (a) and (b). Replace the first paragraph of "What the contextual energy controls" by its statement, keeping the local-branch caveat of D19-CTXF-01, and cite O'Neill 1966 for horizontal geodesics.
**Falsifies:** A stationary transport with G_{p_v}(Log_{p_v}(U p_u), J_{p_v} xi) different from 0; the invariance identity excludes it, and s4 measures 9.7e-11 against momenta of order 4 at non-stationary transports.

### D19-CTXF-05: The holonomy of the finite transports around a small square converges to the curvature of the collective connection

**Evidence:** The chapter proves first-order consistency only. A second-order statement follows from what is already there. Reversal invariance (:104-105) makes the minimizing branch satisfy U_{u<-v} = (U_{v<-u})^{-1}, so the logarithm of the transport across an edge of length eps, centered at m, is odd in eps: log U = -eps A_m(X) + O(eps^3). With the composition U_4 U_3 U_2 U_1 around the square c -> c + eps X -> c + eps(X + Y) -> c + eps Y -> c, Taylor expansion of the midpoints plus the second-order BCH term give log Hol = -eps^2 (dA + [A wedge A])(X, Y) + O(eps^3), in the 02_geometry:153 convention, with A = A^F. By `geo:shape-pullback`, F^{A^F} = -p^* Omega, so log Hol = eps^2 Omega(dp(X), dp(Y)) + O(eps^3): finite holonomy reads the mechanical curvature of N -> N/G on the section's first jet. That is an absolute error of order eps^4, so the square's central symmetry also removes the eps^3 term. The edge check gives angle/eps = 0.53644953 against -A^F_1 = 0.53645078 at eps = 0.01, with error of order eps^2, as the odd expansion predicts. I did not run the nonabelian sign of the commutator term numerically; the BCH bookkeeping above gives +[A(X), A(Y)] inside the bracket.
**Fix:** Add a proposition after `ctx:f:eq:normal`: under the near-identity branch hypotheses, log Hol(square of side eps) = -eps^2 F^{A^F}(X, Y) + O(eps^3). The proof needs uniformity of the remainders, which follows from smoothness of the branch in (c, eps) by the implicit-function theorem at the diagonal. Difficulty: low. This gives the finite model a curvature observable tied to 02_geometry and to the curvature-hierarchy chapter, and answers the part of 2026-10-22 E10 that asks the dynamics to produce curvature.
**Falsifies:** A smooth section with free action for which the square holonomy differs from -eps^2 F at order eps^2. s5 excludes this numerically in the abelian case; a symbolic nonabelian check (for example SO(3) on three location vectors) would settle the commutator sign.

### D19-CTXF-06: The elimination theorem needs only a smooth invariant edge cost; a KL edge cost gives closed-form forces on full Gaussian laws and the same normal equation
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/15_contextual_coupling.tex:93-99 (`ctx:f:eq:C`), :130-153 (`ctx:f:ass:branch`, `ctx:f:statement:3`), :220-238 (`ctx:f:thm:ode`), :264-282; VFE_4.0 docs/model-contract.md:7-9 and :25.
**Evidence:** The proofs of `ctx:f:statement:3` and `ctx:f:thm:ode` use three properties of c_e: smoothness, invariance c(h_u p_u, h_v p_v, h_v U h_u^{-1}) = c(p_u, p_v, U), and a positive group Hessian. For the full Gaussian family N(mu, Sigma) on R^K with K >= 2, which is the family VFE_4.0 uses with GL(K) frames, no closed form of the Fisher-Rao distance between general pairs is known (Pinele, Strapasson and Costa 2020, Entropy 22:404, a survey of special cases and bounds; locator not re-verified). This is the horizontality of D19-CTXF-04, which needs only invariance. Because the second-order expansion of KL is one half the Fisher metric, the first-order transport recovers the Fisher normal equation: the distance from a(eps) to A^F is 2.34e-2, 1.17e-2, 5.83e-3, 2.92e-3, 2.35e-4 for eps = 0.1, 0.05, 0.025, 0.0125, 0.001, and Richardson extrapolation gives 5.97e-4, 1.46e-4, 3.78e-5. The present VFE_4.0 spatial difference record (model-contract.md:9) is a quadratic record transported by a flat reference connection. It is neither the chapter's cost nor a minimized-transport divergence, so the chapter has no implementation counterpart yet.
**Fix:** State `ctx:f:statement:3` and `ctx:f:thm:ode` for a declared smooth invariant edge divergence whose diagonal Hessian is the Fisher metric, keep 1/2 d^2 as the geometric case, and record KL or Jeffreys as the computable instance for full Gaussian families. **Falsifies:** An invariant divergence cost for which the implicit-function and envelope argument fails under smoothness and a positive Hessian, which is impossible; or a(eps) failing to converge to A^F, which s7 contradicts at O(eps).

### D19-CTXF-08: The planar alignment, its reduced energy and its degeneracy are the orthogonal Procrustes problem, unattributed

**Fix:** Cite Schonemann 1966 or Kabsch 1976 at `ctx:f:eq:procrustes`, and Kendall 1984 at :377 together with the shape-space reading of D19-CTXF-04.
**Falsifies:** An existing Procrustes or shape-theory citation in the chapter or its bibliography files; the grep found none.

## Extensions and improvements

The most useful repair is the phase-space statement of D19-CTXF-01, together with a dynamics for the minimized energy itself rather than for a local branch. Pose `ctx:f:thm:ode` on the regular critical set R and on its open subset R_glob. Then define the global-branch flow by following the branch while it is the global minimizer and switching to the new global branch at a Maxwell time. A one-line lemma makes this well posed at transversal crossings. Let F_1 and F_2 be the total energies on two regular branches, with metric inner products and norms in G. If -grad F_1 enters {F_2 < F_1}, then <grad F_1, grad F_2> > norm(grad F_1)^2. Cauchy-Schwarz then forces norm(grad F_2) > norm(grad F_1), hence <grad F_1, grad F_2> < norm(grad F_2)^2, so -grad F_2 also enters {F_2 < F_1}. Consequently a Maxwell hypersurface is never attracting from both sides. A trajectory that reaches it transversally crosses once, the switched solution is forward unique, and the minimized energy is continuous, nonincreasing, and has derivative minus the squared gradient norm almost everywhere. Non-uniqueness is confined to initial data on repelling parts of the Maxwell set and to degenerate global minima such as the cusp of D19-CTXF-07. The crossing lemma is low difficulty. A full hybrid well-posedness statement, excluding accumulating switches and tangential contacts, is moderate.

For the program's groups, combine D19-CTXF-03 (proper isometric actions on complete families) with D19-CTXF-06 (invariant divergence edge costs). With c_e = KL(p_v || U p_u) on full-covariance Gaussian collectives and U in GL(K), the sublevel sets are compact. U Sigma U^T bounded away from zero and infinity confines U to a compact set, since U = (U Sigma U^T)^{1/2} Q Sigma^{-1/2} with Q orthogonal. Global minimizers therefore exist, and a gap argument based on continuity on compact sets replaces the 1-Lipschitz bound, which KL lacks. This yields a closed-form, globally minimizing finite contextual coupling for the VFE_4.0 family at non-consensus configurations. Newton's method on gl(K) can compute the transports, with the locked inertia as the Hessian at agreement. Difficulty: low to moderate. The consensus stratum needs a separate construction. When the local data are actively invariant under the stabilizer H, the equivariant flow preserves the fixed set N^H, and the residual group N(H)/H acts there. The chapter's construction then applies to N^H with G replaced by N(H)/H; this is the slice-theorem reduction (Palais 1961, Annals of Mathematics 73:295-323) and the principle of symmetric criticality (Palais 1979, Communications in Mathematical Physics 69:19-30). With the chapter's fixed, non-invariant references, the passage through the stratum remains open, because the transports become ill-conditioned there (D19-CTXF-02).

The shape-space proposition of D19-CTXF-04 identifies the minimized energy with the weighted discrete Dirichlet energy, sum over edges of w_e/2 times d_{N/G}([p_u], [p_v])^2. On a cubic lattice of the flat torus with spacing h, masses h^d and weights h^(d-2), this is the standard discrete Dirichlet energy of the shape map. Energy convergence to the quotient Dirichlet energy of the continuum section would supply the energy half of the missing graph-refinement theorem. The plaquette statement of D19-CTXF-05 supplies the matching curvature consistency. I did not locate a primary source for Gamma-convergence of manifold-valued discrete Dirichlet energies with a varying target metric and do not cite one. Because the quotient target can be open and positively curved (02_geometry:265), only local statements on compact subsets of R_glob are realistic. Difficulty: moderate for the energy on the regular set, high for the coupled flow with its frame and row equations.

Two small additions would make the chapter's examples carry more weight. First, add the location-covariance product witness of s3 next to the cusp, since it is the only example in which competing nondegenerate minima and monodromy occur. Second, add an SO(3) version of the s5 plaquette check on three location vectors to fix the commutator sign in the nonabelian holonomy formula. Both are a few lines of exact or small numerical computation.
