I read the GAP09 section of `synthesis/COVERAGE.md`, the findings, checked tables and not-checked sections of D05-OBJ, D06-PRED, D07-IMEM1 and D08-IMEM2, the measurability rows of D02-LINKS and D04-DYN, XC6-PROG-08, XC7-IMPL-03, and 2026-10-22 L01-FOUND-06, L03-DYN-01 and L03-DYN-02. Primary sources opened by text extraction: Gray, Entropy and Information Theory (corrected first edition, revision of June 26, 2023, the URL in `references.bib`), Sections 5.2 and 5.3; Ghosal, Nutz and Bernton, arXiv:2106.03670v2, Section 5; the Mathlib docstrings for kernel disintegration and kernel densities. I did not read chapters 13 to 19, which use the per-agent free energy downstream.

Verdict: no result in the scope is false, and the measure theory that the per-agent model and both memory appendices need does hold on standard Borel spaces; I state it as four propositions with proofs in the Extensions section. The defect that matters is that the written proof of prop:dyn-per-agent covers only dominated sources and its finiteness hypothesis is misplaced, so it can exclude the optimum of the objective that five later chapters use. The other findings remove hypotheses (marginal uniqueness of the private-record state, jointly measurable disintegrations), settle the convergence question for cmem:product-label in the negative for global optimality, and supply the verified Dobrushin locator for the Borel measurability of relative entropy.

Answers to the focus questions. (1) The per-agent model is a probability on M×K×N^q×N^s×O for any kernels; its free energy is defined by eq:agent-free-energy for every recognition law at every record with 0 < P_i(o) < ∞, and the separated formula eq:dyn-per-agent-free-energy holds in (−∞, ∞] whenever KL(Q‖P_i^0) < ∞, because the Donsker–Varadhan inequality bounds the positive part of the log record density (GAP09-S1-01, Proposition A). (2) cmem:result-model needs a disintegration of a kernel, which exists for every presentation by the kernel disintegration theorem (GAP09-S1-05; the same theorem gives Proposition D); the alternating updates of cmem:product-label decrease F by exactly KL(old‖new) per block and converge to a stationary point on finite positive tables, but not to the structured minimizer in general (two attracting fixed points with F = 1.1941 and 1.5897), and a weak-coupling bound on log cross-ratios restores uniqueness (GAP09-S1-03). (3) Mutual absolute continuity of two product tilts with equal marginals is automatic; uniqueness then holds without integrability on finite private-record alphabets and whenever λ_0 is equivalent to the product of its marginals (Ghosal–Nutz–Bernton, Lemma 5.1(iii)); the general singular case on infinite spaces is not settled. The non-dominated clause of imem:history-theorem is correct: it has no denominator, the density form exists exactly when the message kernels are dominated by the predictive law, and then the denominator condition holds predictive-almost surely (GAP09-S1-02, GAP09-S1-07). (4) Relative entropy is Borel on Prob×Prob by Gray 1990, Lemma 5.2.2 (Dobrushin's theorem), p. 85, applied to a countable generating field; both measurability sentences are correct (GAP09-S1-06).

## Findings

### GAP09-S1-01: prop:dyn-per-agent is proved only for dominated sources, and its finiteness hypothesis is both too strong and placed where it can exclude the optimum
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/03_dynamics.tex:336-350 (prop:dyn-per-agent, eq:dyn-per-agent-free-energy, proof); :319-334 (def:dyn-per-agent); :61-87 (prop:dyn-gibbs, used for the row statement)
**Evidence:** (a) The proof begins "Write densities of ρ_il and κ_ij(m,·) against the references of s_i and q_i." The statement assumes neither a σ-finite reference dominating every source nor a jointly measurable version of (m,k) ↦ dκ_ij(m,·)/dλ_K(k). The density step fails while every displayed term is finite: take G_i(m,·) = ½N(am,1) + ½δ_{am} and q_i = N(c,v). Then q_i ≪ G_i(m,·) with dq_i/dG_i(m,·) = 2 dq_i/dN(am,1) off the atom, so KL(q_i‖G_i(m,·)) = KL(q_i‖N(am,1)) + log 2 < ∞, but G_i(m,·) has no Lebesgue density, and for a ≠ 0 and a continuous presentation law the family {G_i(m,·)} has uncountably many distinct atoms, so no σ-finite reference dominates it. The identity survives because the chain rule for relative entropy (Gray 1990, Lemma 5.3.1, eq. (5.17), pp. 100-101, read in [0, ∞]) needs no densities; the proof should use it.

(b) The hypothesis "assume that every term below is finite" is stronger than needed. Write P_i^0 for the record-free part of eq:dyn-per-agent-model. If KL(Q‖P_i^0) < ∞, the variational characterization of divergence (Gray 1990, Theorem 5.2.1, p. 98, due to Donsker and Varadhan), applied to the truncations of g = (log l_i(o|K,M))^+ with monotone convergence, gives E_Q[(log l_i)^+] ≤ KL(Q‖P_i^0) + log(1 + P_i(o)), because e^g ≤ l_i + 1 and E_{P_i^0} l_i(o|K,M) = P_i(o). So the record term is defined in (−∞, ∞] and eq:dyn-per-agent-free-energy holds there with no further hypothesis (Proposition A). `s1_per_agent_continuous.py` part (d) confirms the bound on 200 instances with record variances in [1e-4, 1e-2].

(c) The hypothesis can exclude the minimizer. Take one self source and a one-point presentation space, so that the product recognition family contains the exact posterior. Let G = Uniform(0,1) and O | U = u ~ N(0, σ(u)²) with σ(u) = u log(u)²/√(2π) on (0, 1/e] and σ(1/e) on (1/e, 1), so that l(0|u) = 1/(u log²u) near 0 and e on (1/e, 1) (the kernel of D06-PRED-04). `s2_extended_domain.py`: Z = P(o = 0) = e exactly; with s = −log u, ∫_0^{1/e} l log l du = lim ∫_1^S (1/s − 2 log s/s²) ds = lim (S(log S − 2) + 2 log S + 2)/S = ∞. Hence KL(Π_0‖G) = +∞ and the record term is −∞ at q = Π_0, so eq:dyn-per-agent-free-energy reads ∞ − ∞ at the optimum, while eq:agent-free-energy gives F(Π_0; 0) = −1. The truncated posteriors Q_ε have finite split terms, the split and the definition agree to all printed digits, and F(Q_ε) = −0.9453, −0.9798, −0.9920, −0.9954 at ε = 1e-3, 1e-8, 1e-20, 1e-60, approaching −1 at the rate log log(1/ε). This is the D06-PRED-04 phenomenon of cmem:free-energy, here in the objective of chapters 13, 14, 17, 18 and 19.

(d) The row conclusion cites prop:dyn-gibbs, whose hypothesis is that every source cost is finite. The proposition's own hypothesis admits a source with infinite cost and zero row weight (0·∞ = 0), for example a deterministic low-rank link (`chapters/02_interactions.tex:81`) compared with an absolutely continuous q_i. The minimizing rows are then the Gibbs rows with e^{−∞} = 0 on the face of finite-cost sources, unique when each sector has at least one finite cost; prop:dyn-gibbs does not state that case.

(e) "a declared density of ℓ_i" is used as a function of (k,m) at fixed o, which needs a jointly Borel version of the density in (o,k,m). A version exists when ℓ_i(k,m;·) ≪ λ for every (k,m) and the record space is countably generated (the kernel Radon–Nikodym theorem; Mathlib cites Kallenberg 2021, Theorem 9.27, which I did not check in the book). The statement does not ask for it.

The identity itself is correct. `s1_per_agent_continuous.py` builds a continuous instance (one-dimensional Gaussian presentation and content sources, a presentation-dependent evaluator N(am, g), a record N(o; k + bm, σ²), two sources in each sector) and finds the separated formula equal to tensor Gauss–Hermite quadrature of E_Q[log Q − log P_i(·, o)] to 6.6e-16 relative over 200 instances (the quadrature is exact for these quadratic integrands), the closed-form evidence equal to an independent quadrature to 6.3e-13, F + log P_i(o) = KL(Q‖Π_o) ≥ 0.556 on every instance, and the unit-temperature Gibbs rows below 2000 random rows (smallest excess 1.4e-3).
**Fix:** Define F_i by eq:agent-free-energy and replace the hypothesis and proof by Proposition A below: require a jointly Borel record density; state eq:dyn-per-agent-free-energy as an identity in (−∞, ∞] whenever KL(Q‖P_i^0) < ∞ (equivalently, every cost carrying positive row weight is finite), proved by the chain rule and the Donsker–Varadhan bound; say that the extended definition applies otherwise; state the row conclusion with e^{−∞} = 0, at least one finite cost per sector, and E_{s⊗q}(log l_i)^+ < ∞.
**Falsifies:** A product law Q with KL(Q‖P_i^0) < ∞ and E_Q[(log l_i)^+] = ∞ (excluded by the bound above); or a standing convention in chapters 1 to 3 that every source and evaluator has a jointly measurable density against declared references (I found none; `chapters/01_foundations.tex:178` declares only the record density).

### GAP09-S1-02: Marginal uniqueness of the private-record state needs neither mutual absolute continuity nor integrable log-ratios in every case the appendix uses
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** appendices/interacting_memory.tex:213 ("Keeping only the two private-record marginals would also discard the conditional pair law in \cref{imem:reduced-readouts}"), :580; companion/c04_interacting_memory.tex:89; the uniqueness argument of D07-IMEM1-02
**Evidence:** D07-IMEM1-01 shows that every reachable common state is a product tilt λ_t = F_0^t(d_0)F_1^t(d_1)λ_0(dd)/Z_t, and D07-IMEM1-02 proves that two such tilts with equal marginals coincide under mutual absolute continuity and integrable log-ratios. Both hypotheses can be removed in the cases that matter. Let P = f_0⊗f_1 λ_0/Z and P' = g_0⊗g_1 λ_0/Z' be probabilities with the same marginals μ_0, μ_1.
(a) Mutual absolute continuity is automatic. With S_i = {f_i > 0}, P is carried by S_0×S_1, and P'(S_0^c×D_1) = μ_0(S_0^c) = P(S_0^c×D_1) = 0, likewise for S_1, so P' is carried by S_0×S_1. On S_0×S_1 the density f_0 f_1/Z is positive, so λ_0 restricted there is ≪ P, and P' ≪ λ_0 gives P' ≪ P. By symmetry P ≪ P', and dP'/dP = a(d_0)b(d_1) with a = (Z/Z')g_0/f_0 and b = g_1/f_1 positive and finite μ_i-almost everywhere.
(b) Finite private-record alphabets, any λ_0 (zeros allowed): log a and log b are bounded on the common support, so the D07-IMEM1-02 computation KL(P'‖P) = E_{μ_0} log a + E_{μ_1} log b = −KL(P‖P') applies and gives P' = P.
(c) λ_0 equivalent to the product of its marginals, no integrability: then P ~ μ_0⊗μ_1 (P ~ λ_0 restricted to S_0×S_1 ~ λ_0^{D_0}|_{S_0}⊗λ_0^{D_1}|_{S_1}, and μ_i ~ λ_0^{D_i}|_{S_i}). Ghosal, Nutz and Bernton (arXiv:2106.03670v2, Lemma 5.1, read in the arXiv text) state for R ∈ P(X×Y) with R ~ μ⊗ν: "(iii) There exists at most one π ∈ Π(μ,ν) such that (π,R) is cyclically invariant", where cyclical invariance "is equivalent a factorization of the density dπ/dR into strictly positive functions", and the proof applies Nutz's lecture notes (Theorem 2.1, Corollary 2.9) "with π' as reference measure". Take R = P: P and P' both have strictly positive factorized densities against R, so P' = P. This covers every preparation whose private sensors have strictly positive densities (then λ_0 ~ σ_0⊗σ_1 and each marginal ~ σ_i), hence the Gaussian closure family, the Gaussian receiver examples and the binary example.
(d) If log a ∈ L¹(μ_0) and log b ∈ L¹(μ_1), P' = P for every λ_0, by the computation in (b).
`s4_marginal_uniqueness.py`: on 15 random sparse 3×3 preparations with zero patterns and tilts with zeros, all 58 scalings of other product tilts that reached the marginals of P ended at exact product tilts of λ_0, and all equal P (max deviation 9.9e-14); Sinkhorn from 100 random positive scalings of P returns P (9.6e-16); for a nondegenerate bivariate Gaussian λ_0, a quadratic product tilt keeps the cross-precision c, and the determinant equation σ_x²σ_y²D² − D − c² = 0 has roots with product −c²/(σ_x²σ_y²) < 0, so one positive root and one law.
Not settled: λ_0 with a part singular to the product of its marginals on infinite private-record spaces when log a or log b fails to be integrable. The two-graph structure R = ½(id,id)_#μ + ½(id,R_α)_#μ on the circle with irrational α shows that couplings absolutely continuous to R need not be unique (a one-parameter family) while product-form ones are, by Poincaré recurrence applied to the cocycle equation ψ∘R_α − ψ = const; I found no counterexample.
**Fix:** Add after imem:reduction a proposition "Product tilts are determined by their marginals" with (a) to (d), and replace the last sentence at :213 by "On the reachable family, the two private-record marginals together with the fixed λ_0 determine λ_t whenever the private-record alphabets are finite or λ_0 is equivalent to the product of its marginals; the product of the marginals alone does not." Use (c) in place of the integrability hypothesis of D07-IMEM1-02.
**Falsifies:** Two distinct product tilts with equal marginals of a finite λ_0, or of a λ_0 equivalent to the product of its marginals; or a misreading of Lemma 5.1(iii) of arXiv:2106.03670 (its hypotheses are R ∈ P(X×Y), R ~ μ⊗ν on Polish spaces, which standard Borel spaces satisfy after a Borel isomorphism).

### GAP09-S1-03: The alternating structured updates of cmem:product-label decrease F and converge, but not to the structured minimizer
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** low
**Location:** appendices/conditional_memory.tex:60-65 (cmem:product-label and the q_X update that follows it)
**Evidence:** On the structured family Q = s_a q(du)K_a(u,dv) the result kernel cancels and F(s,q) = KL(s‖π̂) + Σ_a s_a KL(q‖μ_a) − E_q log l(o|X), which I reconstructed; the two displayed updates are its block minimizers. Each block is a relative entropy to its own minimizer up to a constant, so F(s,q) − F(s*(q),q) = KL(s‖s*(q)) and F(s,q) − F(s,q*(s)) = KL(q‖q*(s)) exactly (`s3_product_label_bcd.py` (a): 1.8e-15 on 1000 random points). On a finite presentation set and a finite cue alphabet with π̂, μ_a and l(o|·) strictly positive, this gives: F decreases along the sweeps; Σ_k [KL(s^k‖s^{k+1}) + KL(q^k‖q^{k+1})] ≤ F(s^0,q^0) + log Z(o); successive differences tend to zero (Pinsker); after one sweep the iterates lie in a compact subset of the interior (q ∝ l∏_a μ_a^{s_a} and s ∝ π̂e^{−KL} are bounded below); every limit point is a fixed point, the limit set is connected, and the sequence converges when the fixed points are isolated, or in general by the Łojasiewicz property of the real-analytic F together with the exact sufficient-decrease identity (the Attouch–Bolte–Svaiter 2013 framework; theorem number not checked). Convergence to the structured minimizer fails. With π̂ = (0.4, 0.6), μ_1 = (0.98, 0.01, 0.01), μ_2 = (0.01, 0.01, 0.98) and l(o|·) = (0.5, 0.6, 0.5): two attracting fixed points s_1 = 0.007882 (F = 1.194097) and s_1 = 0.982237 (F = 1.589702), separated by a repelling fixed point s_1 = 0.525771 (sweep-map slope 4.88, F = 2.279464, tangent Hessian eigenvalue −2.09); 47 of 99 starts (s_1(0) ≥ 0.53) converge to the worse point, 0.396 above the structured minimum and 0.899 above −log Z(o) = 0.691149 (s3 (b), (c)). A uniqueness criterion holds: the tangent Hessian of F is Σσ_a²/s_a + Ση_u²/q_u − 2Σσ_aη_u log μ_a(u), and with σ_a = s_aφ_a, η_u = q_uψ_u the cross term is E_{s⊗q}[φψC̃] for the doubly centered C̃, an average of log cross-ratios. Hence if Λ = max |log(μ_a(u)μ_b(v)/(μ_b(u)μ_a(v)))| < 1, F is strictly jointly convex and every run converges to the unique structured minimizer. s3 (e): Λ = 9.17 for the bank above; for μ = ((0.4,0.3,0.3),(0.3,0.3,0.4)), Λ = 0.575, the smallest tangent-Hessian eigenvalue over 3000 interior points is 1.72, and 99 starts give one limit.
**Fix:** After the q_X update add: "Alternating these updates lowers F by KL(old‖new) per block and, on finite positive tables, converges to a stationary point of the structured family; distinct starts can reach distinct partial optima. If the log cross-ratios of the cue components are bounded by Λ < 1, the structured objective is strictly jointly convex and the stationary point is its unique minimizer."
**Falsifies:** A run of the s3 example whose limit is not a fixed point, or a two-presentation bank with Λ < 1 and two distinct structured fixed points.

### GAP09-S1-04: The proviso "whenever the normalizer is finite" names a condition that always holds and omits the one that can fail

**Severity:** low

### GAP09-S1-05: cmem:model and cmem:result-model leave σ-finiteness, joint measurability and normalized null-set extensions implicit, and cite Kallenberg without a locator for a kernel disintegration
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** low
**Location:** appendices/conditional_memory.tex:16 ("for a fixed reference measure"), :33-38 (cmem:result-model: "Disintegration on standard Borel spaces supplies measurable versions, with arbitrary extensions on null sets \citep{Kallenberg2021}")
**Evidence:** (i) The density L(u,do) = l(o|u)λ(do) is used through Tonelli and Bayes' formula, which need λ σ-finite and l jointly Borel in (o,u); `appendices/interacting_memory.tex:92` does say "a fixed sigma-finite τ" for the same role. (ii) ℓ_Y(v,m;do) = ∫ L(u,do)R_m(v,du) must be a kernel in (m,v), so the disintegration μ_m(du)K_m(u,dv) = G_Y(m,dv)R_m(v,du) is needed with R jointly measurable in (m,v). Disintegrating each μ_mK_m separately does not supply that. Two correct routes: disintegrate the joint law r(dm)μ_m(du)K_m(u,dv) along (M,V), which gives a kernel R: M×Y ⇝ X disintegrating μ_mK_m for r-almost every m (regular conditional distributions; Kallenberg 2021, Theorem 8.5 by a secondary citation in arXiv:2109.14749, not checked in the book); or disintegrate the kernel m ↦ (V,U)_#(μ_mK_m) for every m, which is the kernel disintegration theorem (the formal statement `ProbabilityTheory.Kernel.compProd_fst_condKernel` in Mathlib: a finite kernel α ⇝ β×Ω with Ω standard Borel and β countably generated equals its first marginal composed with a Markov kernel α×β ⇝ Ω; docstring read). (iii) "arbitrary extensions" must be normalized extensions, or ℓ_Y is not normalized on the null set; the interacting appendix says "fixed normalized extensions" (:52). (iv) The last claim is stronger than the text's "therefore" suggests: from the marginal identity alone one gets equality of result posteriors only for almost every record, but at every o with 0 < Z(o) < ∞, Tonelli gives ∫ l_Y(o|v,m)G_Y(m,dv) = Z_m(o) and r(dm)G_Y(m,dv)l_Y(o|v,m) = ∫_u r(dm)μ_m(du)K_m(u,dv)l(o|u), so the two result posteriors agree pointwise and for every version of R. I reconstructed (iv) by hand; D06-PRED s1 checked normalization and the marginal identity on random finite kernels.
**Fix:** Write "a fixed σ-finite reference measure λ and a jointly measurable density l"; cite the disintegration with its theorem number and say "a kernel R: M×Y ⇝ X, with fixed normalized extensions on null sets"; replace "therefore" by the pointwise Tonelli statement.
**Falsifies:** A standard Borel instance in which no jointly measurable R exists (excluded by either route), or a reading of "a fixed reference measure" as σ-finite by a standing convention.

### GAP09-S1-06: The KL measurability sentences are correct; the Gray citation needs its locator and the evaluation-σ-algebra step
**Category:** citation
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/03_dynamics.tex:26 ("relative entropy is Borel on pairs of laws through its supremum over finite partitions from a countable generating algebra \citep{Gray1990}"); chapters/02_interactions.tex:39
**Evidence:** Gray 1990 (the corrected first edition at the bibliography URL), Section 5.2, defines D(P‖M) as the supremum over finite measurable partitions (eq. (5.1)), and Lemma 5.2.2 on p. 85 reads: "Suppose that (Ω, B) is a measurable space where B is generated by a field F, B = σ(F). Then if P and M are two probability measures on this space, D(P‖M) = sup_{Q⊂F} H_{P‖M}(Q)." Gray attributes it to Dobrushin ("referred to as Dobrushin's theorem", following Pinsker's Chapter 2). The measurability argument then closes as follows. A standard Borel σ-algebra is generated by a countable family, so by the countable field F_0 it generates; there are countably many finite partitions with atoms in F_0; for each, (P,M) ↦ Σ_k P(A_k) log(P(A_k)/M(A_k)) is a [0, ∞]-valued Borel function of the evaluations P(A_k), M(A_k) (with 0 log 0 = 0 and p log(p/0) = ∞ for p > 0); and the supremum of countably many Borel functions is Borel for the evaluation σ-algebra on Prob(E)², which is the Borel σ-algebra of the weak topology (Kechris 1995, Section 17.E; theorem number 17.24 from memory, not checked). The chapter 2 sentence composes this with the Borel map x ↦ (ν_i(x), ν_j(x)K_ij[x]), which is Borel by the monotone class argument for jointly measurable kernels (reconstructed; D02-LINKS checked the same chain). `s5_dobrushin_nondominated.py` (A) illustrates the lemma: dyadic partition sums of KL(N(0,1)‖N(0.5,2)) increase from 0.146993 to 0.15907359023 (width 2^−14) against the closed form 0.15907359028, and for δ_0 against N(0,1) they grow without bound (23.10 at width 2^−32).
**Fix:** Cite "\citep[Lemma 5.2.2]{Gray1990}" at :26 and add "the Borel structure of Prob(E) is the one generated by the evaluations" with the Kechris locator after checking it.
**Falsifies:** A standard Borel space without a countable generating field (none exists), or a difference between the 1990 printing and the 2023 corrected edition in the numbering of Lemma 5.2.2 (the bibliography points to the corrected edition, which I read).

### GAP09-S1-07: A jointly measurable disintegration on law-valued families always exists, and the dominated Bayes update has a reference-free form
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** low
**Location:** appendices/interacting_memory.tex:52 ("An autonomous map on a larger family of law-valued states additionally requires a jointly measurable choice of disintegration on that family. Finite tables and the dominated formulas below provide it directly."), :92-104 (imem:history-theorem, imem:message-density, imem:bayes)
**Evidence:** (i) The identity map λ ↦ λ is a Markov kernel from the standard Borel space Prob(D_0×D_1) to D_0×D_1. The kernel disintegration theorem (stated above in GAP09-S1-05) applied to it gives a Markov kernel c: Prob(D_0×D_1)×D_0 ⇝ D_1 with λ = λ^{D_0}⊗c(λ,·) for every λ. So a jointly measurable choice exists on the whole law space, with no finiteness or domination, and the sentence at :52 describes a condition that always holds. (ii) In the dominated case, write p̄_t for the predictive law of H_{t+1} at the realized prefix. Then κ_{j,t}(d_j(e),·) ≪ p̄_t for Γ_t-almost every e, with density k_{j,t}(·|d_j)/k̄_{j,t} on {0 < k̄_{j,t} < ∞}: integrating κ(d_j(e), {k̄ = 0}) against Γ_t gives p̄_t({k̄ = 0}) = 0. ## Extensions and improvements

**Proposition A (the per-agent free energy on standard Borel spaces), to replace the hypotheses of prop:dyn-per-agent.** Let M_i, K_i, O_i be standard Borel, let N^q_i ∋ i and N^s_i ∋ i be finite with positive priors π^q, π^s, let ρ_il ∈ Prob(M_i), let κ_ij: M_i ⇝ K_i be Markov kernels, and let ℓ_i(k,m;do) = l_i(o|k,m)λ(do) with λ σ-finite and l_i ≥ 0 jointly Borel. Write P_i^0(dm,dk,j,l) = π^s_l ρ_il(dm)π^q_j κ_ij(m,dk) for the record-free model and P_i(o) = ∫ l_i(o|k,m) dP_i^0. Then (i) o ↦ P_i(o) is Borel with ∫P_i dλ = 1, so 0 < P_i(o) < ∞ for almost every record value under the record marginal; (ii) at such o, Π_o = l_i(o|·)P_i^0/P_i(o) is a probability and F_i(Q;o) = KL(Q‖Π_o) − log P_i(o) ∈ [−log P_i(o), ∞] is defined for every probability Q on M_i×K_i×N^q×N^s; (iii) for Q = s⊗q⊗β⊗γ, KL(Q‖P_i^0) = KL(β‖π^q) + KL(γ‖π^s) + Σ_l γ_l KL(s‖ρ_il) + Σ_j β_j ∫KL(q‖κ_ij(m,·))s(dm) in [0, ∞], with Borel integrands; (iv) if KL(Q‖P_i^0) < ∞, then E_Q[(log l_i(o|K,M))^+] ≤ KL(Q‖P_i^0) + log(1 + P_i(o)), the record term R(Q) = E_Q[−log l_i(o|K,M)] lies in (−∞, ∞], and F_i(Q;o) = KL(Q‖P_i^0) + R(Q), which is eq:dyn-per-agent-free-energy; if KL(Q‖P_i^0) = ∞ and E_Q(log l_i)^+ < ∞, then F_i(Q;o) = ∞; (v) consequently, for product Q with E_{s⊗q}(log l_i)^+ < ∞ (bounded record densities, or Gaussian records and laws with second moments), F_i(Q;o) < ∞ exactly when every cost carrying positive row weight is finite and E_{s⊗q}(log l_i)^− < ∞, while without that condition F_i can be finite with the split equal to ∞ − ∞ (`s2`); (vi) at fixed (s,q) with E_{s⊗q}(log l_i)^+ < ∞, R(Q) < ∞ and at least one finite cost in each sector, the unique minimizing rows are β_ij ∝ π^q_ij e^{−c^q_j} and γ_il ∝ π^s_il e^{−c^s_l} with e^{−∞} = 0; (vii) if the compared laws are Borel functions of the configuration x, then x ↦ P_i(o;x), x ↦ Π_o(x) on {0 < P_i(o;x) < ∞} and (x,Q) ↦ F_i(Q;o,x) are Borel. Proof. (i) is Tonelli, and the record marginal of {P_i(o) = 0} is ∫_{P_i=0} P_i dλ = 0. (ii) is eq:agent-free-energy. (iii) is the chain rule (Gray 1990, Lemma 5.3.1) over the finite labels and then over M_i, read in [0, ∞]; the integrands m ↦ KL(q‖κ_ij(m,·)) are Borel because m ↦ (q, κ_ij(m,·)) is Borel into Prob(K_i)² and relative entropy is Borel there (Gray 1990, Lemma 5.2.2, with a countable generating field). For (iv), apply the variational inequality E_Q g ≤ KL(Q‖P_i^0) + log E_{P_i^0}e^g (Gray 1990, Theorem 5.2.1) to g_n = min(n, (log l_i)^+), note e^{g_n} ≤ 1 + l_i, and let n → ∞; then, Q-almost surely on {l_i > 0}, log(dQ/dΠ_o) = log(dQ/dP_i^0) − log l_i + log P_i(o), the first term has integrable negative part and finite integral and the second has integrable negative part (the positive part of log l_i), so the integrals add; on Q(l_i = 0) > 0 both sides are +∞. The second clause of (iv) follows in the same way with the first term's integral equal to +∞. (v) combines (iii) and (iv). (vi) is (iv) plus strict convexity of the row objective on the face of finite costs. (vii) is composition of Borel maps. A further consequence, which I state without checking the locator: KL(·‖Π_o) is convex and weakly lower semicontinuous with weakly compact sublevel sets on Prob of a Polish model of the space (Donsker–Varadhan; Dupuis and Ellis 1997, Lemma 1.4.3 from memory), and the product family is weakly closed, so F_i attains its infimum over s⊗q⊗β⊗γ whenever that infimum is finite. That existence statement is what the per-agent dynamics of chapters 13, 14 and 17 to 19 needs before asking whether their flows converge to a minimizer.

**Proposition B (product tilts are determined by their marginals).** Let λ_0 ∈ Prob(D_0×D_1) on standard Borel spaces and let P = f_0⊗f_1λ_0/Z, P' = g_0⊗g_1λ_0/Z' be probabilities with the same marginals. Then P ~ P' with dP'/dP = a(d_0)b(d_1), a, b positive and finite; and P' = P if the alphabets are finite, or if λ_0 ~ λ_0^{D_0}⊗λ_0^{D_1}, or if log a and log b are integrable under the marginals. The proof is in GAP09-S1-02. Applied to λ_t = F_0^tF_1^tλ_0/Z_t, it makes the pair of marginals of λ_t, with the fixed λ_0, an exact changing state of dimension (n_0 − 1) + (n_1 − 1) on finite alphabets (D07-IMEM1-01 counts the same dimension from the frozen cross-ratios) and an exact changing state on continuous private records with positive sensor densities. The open problem is the singular case: λ_0 with a component singular to λ_0^{D_0}⊗λ_0^{D_1} on infinite spaces, with non-integrable log a or log b. A proof attempt should use the recurrence of the stationary reversible chain X → Y → X under P (its invariant law is the finite μ_0), since the irrational-rotation two-graph example shows that recurrence, not integrability, is what excludes nontrivial product-form couplings there. Difficulty: medium; the finite and product-equivalent cases already cover the appendix.

**Proposition C (structured coordinate updates).** For the structured family of cmem:product-label on finite positive tables, the sweeps satisfy the exact decrease identity, converge to a stationary point, and converge to the unique structured minimizer when the cue bank has log cross-ratio range Λ < 1 (GAP09-S1-03). The same second-variation computation applies to Gaussian banks on the space of all cue laws: on any convex set of pairs (s,q) on which E_{s⊗q}[C̃²] < 1, where C̃ is the doubly centered log cue density, F is strictly convex and has at most one stationary point there. For equal cue covariances L, C̃(a,u) = (ν_a − ν̄)^T L^{−1}(u − m_q), so E_{s⊗q}[C̃²] ≤ E_s‖L^{−1/2}(ν_A − ν̄)‖² · λ_max(L^{−1/2}Cov_q L^{−1/2}), a separation-versus-posterior-width quantity that a Gaussian implementation can monitor. This Gaussian version is a sketch from the same Cauchy–Schwarz step; I did not check it numerically, and turning it into a global uniqueness theorem needs an invariant convex set for the iteration. Difficulty: low for the finite case, medium for the Gaussian one.

**Proposition D (measurable disintegration on law spaces and the reference-free Bayes update).** GAP09-S1-07 gives a jointly measurable disintegration on all of Prob(D_0×D_1) and the identity Γ_{t+1} = [dκ_{j,t}(d_j(·),·)/dp̄_t](h_{t+1})Γ_t, whose domain is exactly the dominated case. Together with Proposition B this removes both measurability provisos of the retained-state discussion (:52 and :213), so the open problem imem:open can be posed on (F_0^t, F_1^t) modulo reciprocal scaling with no measurable-selection hypothesis. Difficulty: low; the statements are standard once located.
