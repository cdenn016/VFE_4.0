### D24-LDYN-01: Per-agent mean flows can be unstable; law-level widening guarantees stability under a signal-fraction bound
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/18_link_dynamics.tex:127-139 (`it2:per-agent-flow` (ii), (iv)), :153, :167-172 (`it2:separation` (ii)), :631-632 (`it2:open-convergence`); Item_2_link_dynamics.tex:143-169, 287-288; companion/c18_link_dynamics.tex:125
**Evidence:** (a) Plug-in, two agents, K=2. Take a two-cycle with codes T(1->0)=(1,0), T(0->1)=(0,1), R_e=1/q, P_0=[[10,3],[3,1]], P_1=[[1,3],[3,10]] (both positive definite) and c_i=0, which `it2:model` admits (the ring uses it). Agent 0's best response is y_0=A_0^{-1}(P_0m_0+q e_1(o+e_1^T mu_1)) with A_0=P_0+q e_1e_1^T, so the best-response matrix has blocks X_0=qA_0^{-1}e_1e_1^T and X_1=qA_1^{-1}e_2e_2^T. The nonzero eigenvalue of X_0X_1 is q^2 (A_1^{-1})_{12}(A_0^{-1})_{21}=9q^2/(1+q)^2 (sympy, s4). The per-agent mean Jacobian -(I-A^{-1}C) therefore has the eigenvalue -1+3q/(1+q)=(2q-1)/(q+1): the flow is unstable for q>1/2, and H is singular at q=1/2 (smallest singular value 2.5e-16). The instability persists with nonzero operating contents, a stiff anchor sigma^2=0.05, the two-cycle loop prior with lambda=2, and grounding (max Re +0.465). RK4 integration of the plug-in per-agent mean flow from a 1e-6 perturbation of its fixed point grows by a factor 5.9e5 over t=30, while the global flow of the same model contracts by 4.1e-6 (s4 output). The mechanism is the receiver's own prior: it regresses the unread coordinate on the read one with coefficient -3, and the partner reads exactly that coordinate. (b) Law-level. The same example is stable at its law-level covariance fixed point (max Re -0.73), because the sender's variance in the read direction widens R~_e. When lambda=0 this widening gives a sufficient condition for stability. For e: i->j put S_e=R_e+T_eSigma_iT_e^T and z_e=S_e^{-1/2}T_e dy_i. The response dx_j=A_j^{-1}sum_e L^_e^T S_e^{-1/2} z_e satisfies ||dy_j||^2_{Sigma_j^{-1}} <= ||dx_j||^2_{A_j} < sum_e |z_e|^2. The first inequality is the Schur-complement characterization of the marginal precision. The second holds because A_j >= A_j^0 + sum_e L^_e^T S_e^{-1} L^_e with A_j^0 positive definite. The matrix inversion lemma gives |z_e|^2 <= theta_e ||dy_i||^2_{Sigma_i^{-1}} with theta_e=s_e/(1+s_e), s_e=lambda_max(R_e^{-1/2}T_eSigma_iT_e^TR_e^{-1/2}) < infinity. Hence max_j sum_{e in in(j)} theta_e <= 1 makes the best-response map a strict contraction in max_i ||dy_i||_{Sigma_i^{-1}}, and the law-level mean flow is exponentially stable. In-degree one always satisfies the bound. Numerically (s7), 800 in-degree-one instances (two-cycle and directed three-ring, K=2, priors anisotropic over four decades, codes of rank 1 or 2, nonzero c, grounding, lambda=0) all have law-level max Re <= -0.089 and best-response spectral radius <= 0.911; the plug-in flow is unstable in 3.7% and 2.2% of them. At in-degree two the bound can fail: a three-agent complete exchange (s5, s6, parameters printed in the s6 output) has law-level max Re +0.0686 at its unique covariance fixed point (reached from two starts, difference 3.7e-13) and global max Re -0.0022. Of 1500 random complete exchanges (s10), 44 meet the bound and all 44 are stable; all 10 unstable ones violate it (values 1.50-1.99). The plug-in flow is unstable in 18.9% of the same 1500 (s5). For K=1 without loops, Gershgorin's theorem gives stability: after eliminating its own dictionaries, each content row has nonnegative weights q_eff,e/(p_i+sum q_eff) summing to less than one, and the ring's w is the special case. 300 K=1 instances with dictionaries, loops and grounding showed no instability (s5). (c) The author's check `item2_checks/checks.py:355-363` ("40 random complete exchanges ... largest real part -0.086") drew self precisions spd(K)/K = (aa^T+KI)/K, which are well conditioned; it is not evidence of general stability, and the text does not claim it is. (d) `it2:per-agent-flow` (iv) says "its fixed point need not equal x*", presupposing a unique fixed point; at q=1/2 there is none or a continuum. The hypothesis of `it2:separation` (ii) that the limiting content equations are uniquely solvable is therefore not vacuous: with c=0 they are exactly the singular system above.
**Fix:** Add to the chapter, as a proposition answering the first question of `it2:open-convergence`: the two-agent plug-in counterexample; the law-level contraction theorem under max_j sum theta_e <= 1 and lambda=0; and the in-degree-two law-level instance as a stated numerical example. Replace "its fixed point" in (iv) by "its fixed points, when H is invertible,". Note after `it2:separation` that the plug-in limit can be an unstable equilibrium of the per-agent flow.
**Falsifies:** An error in the loop gain 9q^2/(1+q)^2 (sympy in s4) or in the s4 spectra, or a hypothesis of `it2:model` excluding correlated self precisions or rank-one codes, would retract (a). A counterexample to the contraction inequality with lambda=0, or an unstable instance meeting the signal-fraction bound, would retract (b).

### D24-LDYN-02: The law-level covariance fixed point exists, is unique, and attracts the best-response iteration from every start
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/18_link_dynamics.tex:128 (hypothesis of `it2:per-agent-flow`), :153 ("at a fixed symmetric content variance Sigma"), :191-196 (`it2:echo` (iii)), :632 (`it2:open-convergence`, second question); Item_2_link_dynamics.tex:144, 169, 207-212, 288
**Evidence:** Let Phi send the peer content marginals Sigma and peer dictionary marginals V to the marginal blocks of A_i(Sigma,V)^{-1}, i in V, where A_i = A_i^0 + sum_{e in in(i)} L^_e^T(R_e+T_eSigma_jT_e^T)^{-1}L^_e + sum_{gamma in L_i} B^_gamma^T((lambda W_gamma)^{-1}+sum_f V_f)^{-1}B^_gamma and A_i^0 = P_i (+) (sigma^{-2}I+G_e)_e is positive definite. (1) Phi preserves the Loewner order, since X -> (R+X)^{-1} reverses it and principal blocks preserve it. (2) Phi is strictly subhomogeneous: for 0<t<1, R+tY >= t(R+Y) gives (R+tY)^{-1} <= t^{-1}(R+Y)^{-1}, hence A_i(tX) <= t^{-1}A_i(X)-(t^{-1}-1)A_i^0 < t^{-1}A_i(X) and Phi(tX) > tPhi(X). (3) Phi(0) <= Phi(X) <= U := blocks of (A_i^0)^{-1} for every X >= 0. Brouwer's theorem on the order interval gives existence. Uniqueness: if X and Y are fixed points and t* = max{t: tX <= Y} < 1, then Y = Phi(Y) >= Phi(t*X) > t*X, contradicting maximality; so X <= Y, and by symmetry X = Y. Convergence: Phi^n(Phi(0)) increases and Phi^n(U) decreases to fixed points, which coincide; any start lies in [Phi(0), U] after one step and is sandwiched. This is the standard argument for order-preserving, strictly subhomogeneous maps (nonlinear Perron-Frobenius theory); I give it in full and did not rely on a specific external theorem. Numerically (s2), the limits from starts 0 and 50I differ by 3.3e-14; over 20 random draws the minimum eigenvalue of Phi(tX)-tPhi(X) is 0.0042, and that of Phi(Y)-Phi(X) for Y >= X is -2e-16. Consequences: the hypothesis "Hold the peers' covariances at a fixed point of the per-agent covariance updates" is always satisfiable and fixes the covariances uniquely. In `it2:echo` (iii) the symmetric solution is the only fixed point, not only the only symmetric one; this can also be seen from v_e = phi(sum_f v_f - v_e), which has one solution for each total (s3). In the law-level ring, Sigma is the unique positive root of p s Sigma^2 + p(sR+c^2)Sigma - (sR+c^2) = 0, and the cyclic map is a contraction, phi'(Sigma) = s^2/(Rps+Sigma ps+c^2p+s)^2 < 1 (s1). Because A_i^0 increases with the grounding scale g, the fixed point X*(g) is nonincreasing in g and has a limit as g -> infinity, which is the first step toward the law-level grounded limit that line 179 excludes.
**Fix:** State a proposition "Law-level covariance updates" with this proof after `it2:per-agent-model` and cite it in `it2:per-agent-flow`, in `it2:echo` (iii), and in the ring remark (replace "at a fixed symmetric content variance Sigma" by "at the unique law-level covariance fixed point"). Then delete the second question of `it2:open-convergence`.
**Falsifies:** Two distinct law-level covariance fixed points for one admissible instance, or an admissible draw with Phi(tX)-tPhi(X) not positive definite.

### D24-LDYN-04: The per-agent tangent model is not the per-agent objective of chapter 3, and its law-level form averages each factor separately
**Category:** consistency
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/18_link_dynamics.tex:5, :35-52 (`it2:per-agent-model`); chapters/03_dynamics.tex:4, :317-352 (`def:dyn-per-agent`, `prop:dyn-per-agent`); chapters/06_outlook.tex:4, :6
**Evidence:** Chapter 18 opens with "Their dynamics depends on which of the three objectives in \cref{ch:dynamics} is chosen" and says the per-agent model is normalized "as in a per-agent generative model" (line 52). Chapter 3 defines the per-agent picture as one "in which its own evaluator and the communicated laws of its peers are competing sources of prior information" (03_dynamics.tex:4). It realizes that picture in `def:dyn-per-agent`, with source labels J, L and Gibbs rows at unit temperature (`prop:dyn-per-agent`), and the outlook repeats the description (06_outlook.tex:6). In `it2:agent-energy` the peers enter only through comparison-record likelihoods convolved with their marginals; there are no source labels or rows, and the registered records persist in the objective. The construction is a per-agent generative model in the sense of `def:generative-model`, but it is not `def:dyn-per-agent`. Chapter 3's unit-temperature identity between per-agent peer terms and reciprocal peer terms (03_dynamics.tex:352) has no counterpart here; the only bridge is the fixed-covariance quadratic match `it2:potential-forces` (iv). No conclusion is transferred, so this is a definitional gap, not a breach of the three-objective rule. A second point: line 50 calls the law-level terms "the record \cref{it2:record} and the loop factor integrated against the peers' laws". The model in fact averages each factor separately against the declared marginals q_j and the product of r_f. Two cases differ from integrating the product of factors against the peers' joint recognitions. The first is when a record and a loop factor of agent i read variables of the same peer, y_j and omega_f with f in in(j), which agent j's Gaussian block correlates. The second is when two loop factors of agent i share a peer edge. The phrase "for each loop factor" (line 50) discloses only half of this.
**Fix:** After `it2:per-agent-model` add: "This is a record-likelihood per-agent model. It is not \cref{def:dyn-per-agent}, it has no source rows, and the results of \cref{sec:dyn-per-agent} are neither used nor transferred. Each factor is averaged separately against the declared peer marginals." Amend 06_outlook.tex:6 to name both per-agent constructions.
**Falsifies:** A derivation of `it2:agent-energy` as a specialization of `def:dyn-per-agent`, for example with Gaussian evaluators and peer laws as mixture sources.

### D24-LDYN-06: The general-law edge cost needs a receiver kernel that is measurable in the dictionary
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/18_link_dynamics.tex:212-222 (`it2:law-potential`, `it2:law-cost`); companion/c18_link_dynamics.tex:192
**Evidence:** "Fixed normalized kernels give laws $A_e(\omega_e)=q_i\mathcal K_e^{\mathrm{rec}}(\omega_e)$ ... $C_e=\int\Delta_e(\omega_e)r_e(d\omega_e)$". The integral requires omega_e -> Delta_e(omega_e) = KL(A_e(omega_e)||B_e) to be Borel. That holds when K_e^rec is a kernel from Omega_e x K_i to the message space: omega_e -> A_e(omega_e) is then measurable by a monotone-class argument, and relative entropy is Borel through its supremum over finite partitions. **Fix:** Require K_e^rec : Omega_e x K_i -> H_e to be a normalized kernel jointly in (omega_e, k), and refer to the measurability argument of 03_dynamics.tex:26.
**Falsifies:** A statement in chapter 17 or 18 that the receiver kernel is jointly measurable in the dictionary and the content.

## Extensions and improvements

A per-agent stability theorem is the most useful addition, and D24-LDYN-01 gives its proof. With lambda = 0, define the signal fraction of an edge e: i -> j as theta_e = s_e/(1+s_e), with s_e the largest eigenvalue of R_e^{-1/2} T_e Sigma_i T_e^T R_e^{-1/2} at the law-level covariance fixed point. If sum_{e in in(j)} theta_e <= 1 for every j, the best-response map contracts the norm max_i ||dy_i||_{Sigma_i^{-1}} strictly, and the law-level per-agent mean flow converges exponentially to its unique fixed point. In-degree one is the special case. The plug-in analogue needs sum_e lambda_max(Sigma_i^{1/2} T_e^T R_e^{-1} T_e Sigma_i^{1/2}) <= 1, which is a genuine weak-coupling restriction. In the two-agent example of D24-LDYN-01, s_e = q(10+q)/(1+q), so the condition fails for q > (-9+sqrt(85))/2 = 0.11, and the flow is unstable for q > 1/2. Loop terms (lambda > 0) add cross-agent dictionary inputs. The same Schur-complement estimate then needs a bound on the loop sources; I expect the precision of Omega_{i gamma} relative to the peers' dictionary marginals to play the role of theta_e. That extension looks straightforward and is not done here. For K = 1 without loops the result also follows from Gershgorin's theorem. Difficulty: low for the stated theorem, moderate with loops.

D24-LDYN-02 makes a law-level grounded-limit theorem accessible. As g -> infinity the covariance fixed point X*(g) is monotone and converges. The record widening R~_e(g) then converges, and the Schur-complement argument of `it2:separation` applies verbatim to the law-level mean equations, provided their limiting content block is invertible. The O(1/g) rate needs X*(g) - X*(infinity) = O(1/g). I expect it from the implicit function theorem applied to the fixed-point equation in 1/g at 1/g = 0. The limit map has zero variance in the identified directions, so the cone argument must be run on the face where those blocks vanish. Difficulty: moderate. This would remove the exclusion at line 179 and the companion's "Coupled law-level covariance updates require a separate limit argument" (c18:127).

The multi-receiver form of the certainty result is a short step and is the case Item 7:415 actually needs. With identity codes, a common noise R on the outgoing edges and fixed rows, the source-role stationarity of Sigma_j over its outgoing edges is sum_e beta_e S_j^{-1}(S_j - C_e)S_j^{-1} = 0, that is S_j = sum_e beta_e C_e / sum_e beta_e. This is a beta-weighted pool of the receivers' predicted second moments, the Gaussian instance of the receiver pool of `prop:dyn-compensation`. Adding the sender's self term and its receiver roles, the scalar implicit-function argument of `it2:certainty` (iii) goes through for the pooled second moment. It gives "the stationary source variance increases with the weighted mean squared disagreement, other quantities frozen". That is the precise statement Item 7 can use, without claiming certainty loss along the flow.

For `it2:open-messages`, the cavity construction of `it2:echo` (iv) generalizes to Gaussian belief propagation on the factor graph of anchors, loop factors and records. By Weiss and Freeman (NeurIPS 12, 1999), converged Gaussian belief propagation has exact posterior means on every topology, and its variances are generally inexact. An echo-free protocol in which every receiver transmits, for each loop factor, the law with that factor's contribution removed is therefore a per-agent scheme whose fixed-point means equal the global posterior mean x*. In general that holds for neither the plug-in nor the law-level per-agent fixed point (D24-LDYN-01 shows that even their stability can fail). Stating convergence conditions needs the walk-summability theory of Gaussian belief propagation; I did not check that literature for this review. Difficulty: moderate.
