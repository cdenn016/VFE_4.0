### D09-INH-01: Interaction necessity (F5) is satisfied by agents that do not interact
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** high
**Location:** chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1379-1380 (`inh:null-flow`), :1389 (F5 of `inh:recursive-formation`); companion/c05_inheritance.tex:225-233. The two main-text locations lie just past the assigned line range; I examined them because they decide whether the "non-interacting instances" part of 2026-10-22 H2 (from X2-FRAME-01) is closed at the level of the definitions.
**Evidence:** The null flow deletes "every peer cost $D^q_{ij},D^s_{ij}$ with $j\neq i$". A peer cost whose channel has constant output, $\mathcal K^q_{ij}[\mathbf q](v,\cdot)=C$, equals $\mathrm{KL}(q_i\Vert C)$ for every source $j$. It couples no agents, but the null flow deletes it and so moves every agent's own target. Construction satisfying all hypotheses of `inh:common-clock` (shared model-independent evaluator, self excluded from belief sources, constant-output moment channel $C[M]$, here taken independent of $M$): three agents with belief laws $\mathrm{Ber}(p_i)$, $\theta_i=\operatorname{logit}p_i$, evaluator $G_0=\mathrm{Ber}(\sigma(\theta_0))$ with $\theta_0=0$, uniform rows over $j\neq i$ (the Gibbs rows equal the prior because all costs of a row coincide), and $C=\mathrm{Ber}(\sigma(2))$. From the manuscript's own reduction $h_i=\log q_i-\log G_0+\log q_i-\log C$ (proof of `inh:common-clock`), the replicator flow is $\dot\theta_i=-(2\theta_i-\theta_0-2)$, so each agent evolves independently of all others. The null flow is $\dot\theta_i=-(\theta_i-\theta_0)$. Selector, relabeling-equivariant: join two current blocks when the difference of their mean values of $d_i=\lvert\theta_i-\theta_0\rvert$ first falls below $\delta=0.1$. From $\theta(0)=(-1,1.2,3)$ the full flow selects $\{2,3\}$ and then $\{1,2,3\}$, and the null flow selects $\{1,2\}$ and then $\{1,2,3\}$. In closed form: under the null flow $\lvert d_1-d_2\rvert=0.2e^{-t}$ closes first, at $t=\ln2$; under the full flow $\theta_1=1-2e^{-2t}$ crosses $\theta_0$, $d_1$ and $d_2$ separate, and $\lvert d_2-d_3\rvert=1.8e^{-2t}$ closes first, at $t=\tfrac12\ln18$. `check_null_flow.py` gives $P\neq P^0$ at all 27 points of a $\pm0.02$ grid around $\theta(0)$ (24 of 27 on a $\pm0.05$ grid; the three agreements lie on the borderline $\lvert d_1-d_2\rvert=\delta$ at $t=0$). So (F5) holds on a nonempty open subset of the admitted class with zero interaction. The companion describes the control as deleting "every cost that couples one agent to another" (c05:225-227) and presents non-interacting agents as what it excludes (c05:229-232); the formal definition does not implement that description. A repaired control that freezes every peer law at its initial value in every cost and channel (script part 4) gives $P=P_{\mathrm{frozen}}$ on all 27 grid points for this uncoupled case, as intended.
**Fix:** Define the null flow by removing the dependence of each agent's force on the current laws of the other agents, not by deleting labeled costs: in every cost, channel and evaluator entering agent $i$'s force, replace each $q_j,s_j$ with $j\neq i$ by its initial value, and keep constant-output costs. Alternatively, require each deleted cost to have a nonzero derivative in some peer law on the admitted class. Align c05:225-233 and AUTHORING.md:45 with the repaired definition, and recheck the (F5) verdicts of the fusion and per-agent chapters against it.
**Falsifies:** A clause in `inh:null-flow`, `inh:recursive-formation` or AUTHORING.md that excludes peer costs independent of peer laws, or that forbids constant-output channels in (F5) tests. I found none; `inh:common-clock` admits such channels explicitly.

### D09-INH-02: The heading and remarks of `inh:no-underdamped` are false at parent equilibria not hit by critical points
**Category:** inflation
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/05_inheritance.tex:318 (heading of `inh:no-underdamped`), :337-340 (remark); companion/c05_inheritance.tex:77. Related: 2026-10-22 E7 (X3-PIFB-06), whose statement was restricted to "an interior critical point".
**Evidence:** The proposition assumes a critical point $x^*$ and concludes $\operatorname{spec}DB^c(R(x^*))\subseteq\operatorname{spec}L$. I reconstructed the proof (at $x^*$ the second-derivative term of $R$ multiplies $B(x^*)=0$, so $DR\circ L=L^c\circ DR$, $\ker DR$ is $L$-invariant, and $L^c$ is conjugate to the induced quotient map) and checked it on 200 random $g$-self-adjoint instances (`check_gradient.py` part D, residual $3.6\times10^{-15}$). The heading "Exact inheritance of a gradient flow creates no oscillatory mode", the remark "Oscillatory collective modes require nonreciprocal fields, explicit driving, or declared second-order kinetics", and c05:77 "exact inheritance cannot create oscillation" drop the critical-point hypothesis. Counterexamples, each with $R$ a surjective submersion with connected fibers and a pushed field depending on the retained value only, so `inh:projectable` holds and the parent is exact:
D3, a Fisher gradient flow of two non-interacting agents with agent-local metrics and a potential bounded below. Agent A has law $N(z,I_2)$, $z\in\mathbb R^2$ (Euclidean Fisher metric); agent B has law $\mathrm{Poisson}(e^{-y})$, whose Fisher metric in $y$ is $e^{-y}dy^2$. The potential $E=\mu\,\mathrm{KL}(N(z,I)\Vert N(0,I))+\mathbb E_{\mathrm{Poisson}(e^{-y})}[n]=\tfrac\mu2\lvert z\rvert^2+e^{-y}\geq0$ (an expected energy $V(n)=n$ for agent B) has no critical point. The flow is $\dot z=-\mu z$, $\dot y=1$, and the retention $w=\mathrm{Rot}(\kappa y)z$ (agent A read in a frame turned by agent B) gives $\dot w=-\mu w+\kappa Jw$, with eigenvalues $-\mu\pm i\kappa$. The script, with metric $e^{-y}/k$ and $k=2$, $\mu=1/2$, $\kappa=3/2$, returns the parent linearization $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}-1/2&-3\\3&-1/2\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ with eigenvalues $-\tfrac12\pm3i$.
D2, three unit-variance Gaussian location agents (Euclidean Fisher metric) with the coupled potential $\tfrac\delta2(a_1^2+a_2^2)+ca_3+\tfrac\mu2[(\mathrm{Rot}(\kappa a_3)a_{12})_1]^2$, for which $\partial_{a_1}\partial_{a_3}E\not\equiv0$, and the helical retention $w=\mathrm{Rot}(\kappa a_3)a_{12}$: parent linearization $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}-3/5&2\\-2&-1/10\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, eigenvalues $-\tfrac7{20}\pm\tfrac{3\sqrt7}4i$.
D1, one agent on $\mathbb R^3$ with a non-product metric and $E=\tfrac12\lvert z\rvert^2+e^{-y}\geq0$: parent eigenvalues $-\tfrac12\pm3i$; RK4 to $t=8$ gives $E$ nonincreasing (largest increment $-1.2\times10^{-5}$) and 3.82 turns of $z$ about the parent equilibrium.
None of these uses time-dependent forcing, and D1 and D3 have potentials bounded below. In D2 and D3 the fine field is equivariant under a free proper screw action, so `inh:equivariant-descent` (i) applies, while $E$ (D2) or $g$ (D3) is not invariant, so (ii) does not apply and the parent is not a gradient flow.
**Fix:** Keep the statement and add: "If $z^*$ is an equilibrium of $B^c$ and $E$ restricted to $R^{-1}(z^*)$ attains its infimum (for example, when the fiber is compact), then $R^{-1}(z^*)$ contains a critical point and the conclusion holds at $z^*$." Proof: $B^c(z^*)=0$ makes $B$ vertical along the fiber, so $dE$ vanishes on $H_x$ there, and at a fiber minimizer $dE$ also vanishes on $\ker DR$. Retitle to "...creates no oscillatory mode at images of critical points", replace the remark's list by one that includes parent equilibria whose fibers carry an unbounded descent, with D3 as the example, and correct c05:77.
**Falsifies:** A proof that every equilibrium of an exact parent of a gradient flow is the image of a critical point. D3 refutes it, since $\partial_yE=-e^{-y}$ never vanishes.

### D09-INH-03: Clause (B) of `inh:inherited-parent` disables the intertwining branch of (D)
**Category:** consistency
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/05_inheritance.tex:694-701 (`inh:inherited-parent`, clauses (D) and (B)); :431-435 (`inh:intertwining`, predictive response).
**Evidence:** (B) requires the joint kernel $x\mapsto\operatorname{law}(D_a(O_a),R_I(X'))$ to factor through $R_I$ on $\Omega_I$. Its second marginal is $x\mapsto T(x,R_I^{-1}(\cdot))$, so (B) for any $a\in A_I$ implies strong lumpability on $\Omega_I$. The intertwining branch of (D) exists for chains that are not strongly lumpable: in the manuscript's own example $T(1,R^{-1}b)=1/5\neq2/5=T(2,R^{-1}b)$ (`check_parent_definition.py` part 1), so (B) fails for every response, and the branch yields an inherited parent only when $A_I=\varnothing$, where (B) is vacuous. The notion stated after `inh:intertwining`, the predictive response $(\Lambda K_a)(Z_n,\cdot)$, is not what (B) says, and it is incomplete on its own: a response that reveals hidden state changes the filter. With $O=1\{X=\text{state }1\}$ the $\Lambda$-parent misstates the joint law of $(Z_0,O_0,Z_1,O_1,Z_2)$ by $0.06$ in total variation, while a response drawn from $K(R(x),\cdot)$ independently of the transition gives residual $10^{-17}$ (part 2). Two further problems sit in the same clause: $\mathcal P_I=\{\nu\Lambda\}$ is defined through the fibers of $R_I$ although $\mathcal P_I$ is declared before $R_I$, and the parenthetical presents intertwining as the condition for that class while it is only sufficient (D09-INH-09).
**Fix:** For stochastic dynamics replace (B) by output intertwining: for each $a\in A_I$, admitted action $u$ and measurable response set $o$, require $\Lambda T_{a,u,o}=T^c_{a,u,o}\Lambda$ with $T_{a,u,o}(x,dy)=\Pr(D_a(O)\in o,X'\in dy\mid X=x)$ and $T^c_{a,u,o}=\Lambda T_{a,u,o}\Phi_R$. The induction in the proof of `inh:intertwining` goes through with $TP_z$ replaced by $T_{a,u,o}P_z$, and when $\mathcal P_I$ contains all laws the condition reduces to the present (B). Declare $\Lambda$ together with $R_I$.
**Falsifies:** A reading of (B) in which "factors through $R_I$" refers to $\Lambda$-conditional laws. The text says "factors through $R_I$ on $\Omega_I$".

### D09-INH-04: H2 is closed for its recorded instances but reopens through declared quantifiers

**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/05_inheritance.tex:686-712 (`inh:inherited-parent`), :1385-1388 (F1, F3, F4); AUTHORING.md:45. Prior: 2026-10-22 H2, L04-INH-01, L04-INH-04, L04-INH-05.

(b) "Declared in this order" (687), "Fixing $A_I$ before $R_I$ is essential" (709) and "fixed before the retention is chosen" (F3) have no mathematical force: the definition is existential over tuples $(A_I,\mathcal P_I,\Omega_I,R_I)$, and every valid tuple can be declared in that order afterwards. (d) $\mathcal P_I$ is not tied to the laws that handoffs produce, so (D) can be checked for a preparation class that no handoff realizes.
(e) $\Omega_I$ is not required to be Borel. Post-handoff sets are images of measurable handoff maps, analytic in general, while `inh:deterministic-descent` is stated for standard Borel spaces and a surjective $R$.

(g) (D) is stated through `inh:fiber-test`, a discrete-time condition. For flows, the fiber test for every $\Phi_t$ and the well-posedness of the parent flow demanded by the regularity rule are not stated.
**Fix:** (a) Fix the topology to the chart or manifold topology in which the dynamics is posed; for an invariant family, require a declared submanifold and openness in it. (b) Declare once, for the whole construction, a rule that assigns to every block at every level its protocol class and response retentions from the children's data, and take $R_I$ to be the canonical parent of `inh:canonical-parent` for that interface; the required order then becomes a functional dependence. (c) Define child-level probes as that rule applied one level down, with a declared primitive class. (d) Require every post-handoff law to lie in $\mathcal P_I$. (e) Require $\Omega_I$ Borel and $R_I$ surjective onto a standard Borel $\mathsf S^c_I$. (f) Add a clause (L): the parent laws are functions of $R_I$ on $\Omega_I$. (g) State (D) for flows with the well-posedness hypothesis.
**Falsifies:** A passage that fixes the topology of (F1) or a probe-assignment rule. I found none in ch05, c05, the glossary or AUTHORING.md.

### D09-INH-07: The first step of `inh:type-closure-open` has a negative answer: additive Wishart retention is not horizontally conformal
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/05_inheritance.tex:738-743 (`inh:type-closure-open`), :732-736; chapters/11_conditional_formation.tex:17-23 (`form:wishart`), :223-243 (`form:parent`).
**Evidence:** For $W_2(\nu,\mathsf P)$ with log-density $\nu\log\det\mathsf P-\log\Gamma_2(\nu)+(\nu-\tfrac32)\log\det X-\operatorname{tr}(\mathsf PX)$, the Fisher metric is $g_W=\psi_2'(\nu)d\nu^2-2d\nu\operatorname{tr}(\mathsf P^{-1}d\mathsf P)+\nu\operatorname{tr}((\mathsf P^{-1}d\mathsf P)^2)$ with $\psi_2'(\nu)=\psi'(\nu)+\psi'(\nu-\tfrac12)$ (checked against a finite difference of the exact expected log-likelihood, relative residual $8.6\times10^{-9}$, `check_wishart_conformality.py`). On the common-precision family $(\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\mathsf P)$ with metric $g_W(\alpha_1,\mathsf P)+g_W(\alpha_2,\mathsf P)$, the retention $R=(\alpha_1+\alpha_2,\mathsf P)$ has vertical direction $(1,-1,0)$ and horizontal vectors $\psi_2'(\alpha_1)u_1=\psi_2'(\alpha_2)u_2$. The horizontal lift of $(dA,U)$ has fine norm $dA^2/(1/\psi_2'(\alpha_1)+1/\psi_2'(\alpha_2))-2dA\operatorname{tr}(\mathsf P^{-1}U)+A\operatorname{tr}((\mathsf P^{-1}U)^2)$, while the parent norm has $\psi_2'(A)dA^2$ in place of the first term. Taking $dA=0$ forces the conformal factor $d=1$, after which conformality would need $\psi_2'(A)=(1/\psi_2'(\alpha_1)+1/\psi_2'(\alpha_2))^{-1}$. Computed ratios $\psi_2'(A)(1/\psi_2'(\alpha_1)+1/\psi_2'(\alpha_2))$: 0.315 at $(0.8,0.8)$, 0.778 at $(2,2)$, 0.911 at $(2,7)$, 0.961 at $(10,10)$, 0.994 at $(50,80)$; random horizontal vectors at $(2,7)$ give norm ratios between 0.911 and 1. **Fix:** Record the negative answer and test type closure directly: the parent field $B^c$ is a $g_c$-gradient exactly when the one-form $g_c(B^c,\cdot)$ is exact (closed on a simply connected chart), a computable condition that needs no conformality. For the Wishart parent this means computing $d[g_W(A,\mathsf P)(B^c,\cdot)]$ for `form:parent-ode` with the precision equations. Difficulty: low to moderate.
**Falsifies:** A different Fisher metric for the parent (for example, one that includes $n_I$ as a coordinate with its own metric) that restores the harmonic identity; with the retained state of `form:parent-state` I do not see one.

### D09-INH-10: "Classify the mechanisms" and "must come from symmetry or horizontal energy structure" are not proved
**Category:** inflation

## Extensions and improvements

The most useful correction in this scope is to the null-flow control (D09-INH-01). Replace the deletion of labeled peer costs by a frozen-environment flow $\Phi^{\mathrm{fr}}$: in agent $i$'s force, every law of another agent is held at its value at time zero, in costs, channels and evaluators alike. Then constant-output costs survive, the uncoupled example fails (F5) on its whole grid, and the coupled example $C[M]=\mathrm{Ber}(\sigma(2+1.5M))$ still passes (27 of 27 grid points), so the control separates coupling from target displacement. A second control that deletes the costs, as now, can be kept to measure the effect of the peer terms as a whole. Both are cheap to report for every selection result, and the choice between them should be stated in `inh:null-flow`.

The exact statement behind `inh:no-underdamped` has a clean boundary, and the boundary is itself a mechanism (D09-INH-02). Let a Lie group $G$ act freely and properly, with $B=-\operatorname{grad}_gE$ equivariant but $E$ invariant only up to a character, $E\circ\phi_h=E+c(h)$ (as in D2), or with $g$ not invariant (as in D3). Writing $E=\tilde E+c\,\tau$ with $\tilde E$ invariant and $\tau\circ\phi_s=\tau+s$ for $G=\mathbb R$, the parent field is $B^c=-\operatorname{grad}_{g_c}\tilde E_c-c\,D\pi(\operatorname{grad}_g\tau)$ when $g$ is invariant; the second term is a vector field on the quotient that need not be a gradient, and in D2 it is the rotation $\kappa cJw$. The fine fiber then carries a constant descent and contains no critical point, which is exactly the case the proposition excludes. A proposition of this form, with the fiber-minimum hypothesis of the fix, would give PIFB2's inertia question a precise answer inside reciprocal dynamics: underdamped parent modes are impossible at images of critical points and possible at parent equilibria over fibers along which the fine state drifts, for example when a parent reads one agent in another agent's drifting frame. Difficulty: low for the statement and proof; whether such drifts occur in the program's law families (an agent law escaping to the boundary of its family at constant Fisher speed, as the Poisson agent of D3 does) is the research question.

For stochastic parents with responses, the right object is intertwining with outputs (D09-INH-03): $\Lambda T_{a,u,o}=T^c_{a,u,o}\Lambda$ for every protocol, action and response set. This is the hidden-Markov version of the Rogers–Pitman condition; it keeps the filter equal to $\Lambda(Z_n,\cdot)$ after every observed response and gives the predictive joint law of responses and retained states. The canonical stochastic parent for $\mathcal P_I$ equal to all laws is then the labeled bisimulation computed by partition refinement on $(J_a(x,A\times B))$ (D09-INH-05), and canonical parents compose along nested interfaces (D09-INH-14), which gives a canonical hierarchy for any declared tower of interfaces. Difficulty: low.

For type closure, the conformality route fails for the additive Wishart parent (D09-INH-07), and the exact replacement is the closedness of $g_c(B^c,\cdot)$. Computing $d[g_W(A,\mathsf P)(B^c,\cdot)]$ for `form:parent-ode` with its precision equations settles whether the Wishart parents are type-closed for the Fisher gradient class; if the form is not closed, the parents are exact but leave the class, and recursion by induction on levels is not available for that construction. Difficulty: moderate, a symbolic calculation on a five-dimensional chart.

Finally, the quantifier repairs of D09-INH-04 can be made uniform by requiring a single interface rule for the whole construction and taking every retention to be the canonical parent of that rule; (F3) and (F4) then become statements about one computable object, the order of declaration is replaced by functional dependence, and the topology of (F1) is fixed to the chart in which the dynamics is posed.
