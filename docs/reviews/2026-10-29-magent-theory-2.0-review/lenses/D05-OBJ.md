Verdict. Every identity in the scope that I recomputed holds: the mean-field local free energy identity, the per-agent free energy, the label-model identity, all four parts of the registered-agreement theorem and the frozen-source corollary (residuals at most 9e-16, by direct summation over the full product spaces), and prop:dyn-no-global-vfe is correct as stated. The defects are in what is said around these results. The no-go is advertised in the abstract, chapters 1 and 3, the outlook, README, AUTHORING and the companion as excluding every fixed normalized model in which the agent laws are recognition laws; that statement is false, because a countable normalized model whose recognition contains the agent laws as factors reproduces the reciprocal potential exactly on the whole interior, and the proposition proves only a finite-model statement and an exact-product statement. The companion and AUTHORING infer "no global Lyapunov function" for per-agent dynamics from an example that has one, and the record-interrupted process, the per-agent picture and the open problem relating the three objectives are not yet precisely defined.

## Findings

### D05-OBJ-01: The no-go is advertised far beyond prop:dyn-no-global-vfe, and the advertised version is false
**Category:** inflation
**Severity:** high
**Location:** frontmatter.tex:5; chapters/01_foundations.tex:197, :226; chapters/03_dynamics.tex:4, :375-387 (prop:dyn-no-global-vfe); chapters/06_outlook.tex:6; README.md:34; AUTHORING.md:39; companion/c03_dynamics.tex:12
**Evidence:** The proposition's quantifiers are narrow. (a): for finite K_i = K_j with at least two points, there is no normalized joint over finitely many finite-valued variables, record value, rule x -> Q(x) and a > 0, b with E(x) = a F(Q(x); o) + b on the whole interior. Its proof uses only that a finite-model free energy is bounded above by max(-log Pi_o) - log Z_o while E is unbounded on the interior (s3(a): E = 2.51, 4.83, 7.78, 13.31 along q_i -> one point mass, q_j -> the other); it has no local content. (b): with all channels fixed, no normalized joint with recognition exactly the product of the agent laws gives an affine identity on an open set; the proof compares the third mixed derivative d_{q_i} d^2_{q_j}, which vanishes for every mean-field free energy (sympy: 0 for an arbitrary positive table) and equals beta_ij times the integral of w (v/q_j)^2 for E (sympy: -2 x_j/(1 - x_j^2)^2, value -0.72455 at (0.1, 0.3)). Both arguments are correct.

The surrounding statements are broader. Chapter 3:4 and chapter 1:197 say the reciprocal potential "is not the free energy of any fixed normalized model in which the agent laws are recognition laws"; chapter 1:226 says it is not F(Q; o) "for any fixed normalized model with these marginals as recognition laws"; outlook:6 attributes the same to the proposition; AUTHORING:39 and companion c03:12 repeat it. The abstract and README:34 say "any fixed normalized model whose recognition laws are the agent laws", while the abstract's first paragraph admits interactions that depend on the current laws, for which (b) is not proved.

Counterexamples to the broad statement. Absorption (s7): since E >= 0, take P = Unif(K_i) (x) Unif(K_j) (x) pi_theta with a countable latent theta, pi_theta(n) = 2^-(n+1), and the recognition rule Q(x) = q_i (x) q_j (x) Q''(x), where Q''(x) = (1 - t) pi_theta + t (point mass at n*) is tuned so that KL(Q''||pi_theta) = E(x) - KL(q_i||Unif) - KL(q_j||Unif) + 2 log 2 >= 0. Then F(Q(x)) = E(x) + 2 log 2 on the whole interior (direct summation: residual 6.9e-15 over logits in [-25, 25]). The same device turns any potential bounded below into a free energy of a fixed normalized countable model with the agent laws as recognition factors, and, with a finite auxiliary alphabet, any potential on any set where it is bounded. A structural version (s3(c)) keeps the dependence on the receiver inside the model and lets the auxiliary recognition read only the source law. Two agents, binary alphabet, identity channel j -> i, fixed row beta = 0.6, so E = KL(q_i||G_i) + KL(q_j||G_j) + beta KL(q_i||q_j) + const, exactly the setting of (a). Model: y_j ~ G_j; an integer latent delta with prior c/(1 + delta^2); a natural-number latent theta with prior 2^-(theta+1); and y_i given delta with logit a' logit G_i(1) + b' delta, where a' = 1/(1+beta) and b' = beta/(1+beta). Recognition rule: Q(x) = q_i (x) q_j (x) Q'(x) (x) Q''(x), where Q'(x) puts mass 1 - eps on delta = 0 and eps on delta = sign(lambda) M, so that its mean is lambda = logit q_j(1), with M = ceil(lambda^2 + |lambda| + 2) and eps = |lambda|/M; and Q''(x) = (1 - t) prior + t (point mass at n*), with t chosen so that KL(Q''||prior) = (U' - L(x))/(1 + beta).
Algebra gives (1+beta) F(Q(x)) = KL(q_i||G_i) + beta KL(q_i||q_j) + L(q_j) + (1+beta) KL(Q''||prior): the dependence on q_i is matched exactly, and L depends on q_j only and is bounded above by U = beta max(log 1/h, log 1/(1-h)) + (1+beta)[2 log(1/c) + sup_M log(2M^2)/sqrt(M) + log 2] = 8.213, because KL(Q'||prior) <= 2 log(1/c) + eps log(1 + M^2), the term b' log(1 - x) + E_{Q'} log N(delta) is at most log 2, and the source self term is bounded (here h = G_j(1) and N is the normalizer of y_i given delta). Direct summation over the support gives max |(1+beta) F(Q(x)) - E(x) - U'| = 7.1e-15 at 9 x 241 points with logit q_i in [-20, 20] and logit q_j in [-30, 30], with U' = 9.213. So E = (1+beta) F(Q(x)) - U' on the whole interior, for a fixed normalized countable model in which q_i and q_j are the recognition laws of the agents' content variables and the auxiliary factors depend on q_j alone. A finite truncation (delta in {-50..50}, theta in {0..60}) is exact on the open set |logit q_j| <= 6 (s3(d), residual 1.8e-15), so (a) fails on every open set with compact closure: its finiteness and whole-interior hypotheses are both essential.

What is proved: (a) for finite models, every recognition rule, the whole interior; (b) for recognition exactly the product of the agent laws with fixed channels, any open set. Not proved: the abstract's statement for law-dependent channels on non-finite models, and the profile version in which every non-agent recognition factor is free-energy optimal (the construction uses non-optimal auxiliary factors, so it does not refute that version). Consistency with 2026-10-22 C1: thm:dyn-registered uses a point recognition of the configuration on a finite lattice, so neither (a) nor (b) applies, and its identity carries the configuration-dependent term C_n(x); there is no conflict.
**Fix:** Replace every cited sentence by the two proved statements: "no finite normalized model, with any recognition rule, reproduces E on the interior; no normalized model whose recognition is exactly the product of the agent laws reproduces E on any open set when the channels are fixed." Add a remark with the two constructions above, showing that countable latent variables, or auxiliary latent factors whose recognition depends on the configuration (even on the source law alone), allow an exact identification. Either prove (b) for law-dependent channels or restrict the abstract and README to fixed channels. State the profile version as an open problem.
**Falsifies:** An error in s3 (Q(x) not a probability law, or the directly summed KL not constant in x); or a rewording of every cited sentence to "whose recognition is exactly the product of the agent laws" with fixed channels, which would leave only the abstract's law-dependent case.

### D05-OBJ-02: "Per-agent dynamics generally has no global Lyapunov functional" rests on an example that has one
**Category:** inflation
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** companion/c03_dynamics.tex:10; AUTHORING.md:38 (the main-text statement prop:dyn-nonreciprocal, chapters/03_dynamics.tex:359-370, is correct)
**Evidence:** The companion says "Such non-reciprocal forces need have no global Lyapunov function: on a directed ring of three Bernoulli agents ... the linearized motion spirals, with complex eigenvalues that no gradient flow can have." Complex eigenvalues exclude a gradient structure, which is what prop:dyn-nonreciprocal states and proves; they do not exclude a Lyapunov function. For eq:dyn-three-cycle the matrix A = (-(1+w) I + w S)/(1+w) is normal and A + A^T has eigenvalues -2/(1+w) and -(2 + 3w)/(1+w) twice (s4 at w = 2: -0.667, -2.667, -2.667), so V(l) = |l - l*|^2 is a strict global Lyapunov function (its largest increment along a computed trajectory is -2.4e-8). On the whole class of per-agent belief dynamics with fixed rows, self weight at least b > 0 and fixed channels without null columns, a strict global Lyapunov function exists (D05-OBJ-12), so AUTHORING's "generally" is false there.

An instance of def:dyn-per-agent-flow with no Lyapunov function does exist, but it needs other ingredients (s4, s6): three Bernoulli agents, evaluators Ber(1/2), source sets {self, i+1} with priors (0.2, 0.8), rows evolving at T = 1, and a constant-in-input law-dependent channel whose compared law is Ber(sigma(s_i g tanh l_{i+1})) with signs s = (+, +, -) and g = 4 (admissible under chapters/03_dynamics.tex:26 and the hypotheses of thm:dyn-finite-flow). The configuration l = 0 with rows at the prior is the only equilibrium (l_1 > 0 forces l_3 < 0, then l_2 < 0, then l_1 < 0), orbits are bounded (|dl_i/dt + l_i| <= g), and the Jacobian has eigenvalues 0.6 +- 2.771i, -4.2 and -1 three times (predicted -1 + c/2 +- i c sqrt(3)/2 and -1 - c with c = pi_peer g = 3.2). A point on the two-dimensional unstable manifold has alpha-limit {0}; a continuous strict Lyapunov function V would, by LaSalle's invariance principle, force its omega-limit to be {0} as well, and then V(0) > V(x) > V(0). Numerically the orbit settles on a limit cycle of period 3.497594 (spread 4e-12 over successive cycles, the same from two initial conditions). With the same data, the Fisher gradient flow of F_tot = sum_i F_i converges to l = 0 and dissipates F_tot (largest increment 3.6e-16), so the two pictures share the equilibrium and give it opposite stability.
**Fix:** Replace the companion sentence by "need not be gradient flows: on a directed ring ... complex eigenvalues that no gradient flow can have"; write "need not have a global Lyapunov functional" in AUTHORING; add the law-dependent ring as the example of absence and the contraction proposition of D05-OBJ-12 as the positive case.
**Falsifies:** A continuous function strictly decreasing along nonconstant orbits of the s4 example (excluded by the unique unstable equilibrium with bounded orbits), or a reading of the companion sentence that does not infer the absence of a Lyapunov function from complex eigenvalues.

### D05-OBJ-03: The record-interrupted dynamics is not yet a well-defined process

**Location:** chapters/03_dynamics.tex:421-424 (def:dyn-record-interrupted); uses at chapters/04_prediction.tex:103, chapters/07_smooth_closure.tex:294, chapters/10_boundary_closure.tex:172, :223, :249-252
**Evidence:** The definition declares record times and measurable maps A_o and "follows the reciprocal Fisher flow on each interval". Four ingredients are missing. (i) Existence on each interval: thm:dyn-finite-flow and prop:dyn-chart-flow give maximal local solutions, and global existence is proved only for fixed channels without null columns (prop:dyn-global-flow); with law-dependent channels the flow can leave the interior before the next record. **Falsifies:** A passage of chapter 3 or app:records that defines the record law of this composite and states existence and invariance of its domain.

### D05-OBJ-04: Two claims about records at ch3:424-426 are imprecise, and one is false
**Category:** error
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/03_dynamics.tex:424-426
**Evidence:** (a) "Relative to a fixed normalized model, the reciprocal potential therefore gives up ... one objective that spans both interaction and data and is monotone across records." In the free-energy pictures the objective changes at every record: at the current recognition, F_{k+1}(Q) - F_k(Q) = E_Q[-log l(o_{k+1} | .)], which is positive for discrete records (s5(d), four records on a five-point model: +1.430, +1.064, +0.447, +0.583). What is monotone is the optimized value -log P(o_{1:k}) (1.069, 2.037, 2.468, 2.950; nondecreasing for discrete records, not in general for densities), and each free energy along its own descent between records. No functional is monotone along the composite of descent and records in any of the three pictures; when an assimilation moves the state off an attractor and the flow returns to it, no strict Lyapunov function of the composite can exist. (b) "Its stationary set does not depend on records, so after an assimilation event the flow relaxes back toward record-independent states. A persistent record dependence must be declared." The stationary set is record-independent; which stationary point is reached is not. In s5(c) one agent with E = KL(q (x) s || R), R = 0.49 on the diagonal and 0.01 off it, has two minima, q(1) = s(1) = 0.976 and q(1) = s(1) = 0.024 (E = 0.6692 at both). One record in each sector with likelihood (0.99, 0.01) moves (0.976, 0.976) to (0.291, 0.291), raising E by 0.444, and the reciprocal flow then converges to the other minimum and stays there. A record thus has a persistent effect through basin selection with no declared record dependence. (A record in one sector only is followed by a return to the original minimum, so the effect depends on the record.)
**Fix:** Replace (a) by the precise statements above. Replace (b) by "the stationary set is record-independent; when the potential has several stable stationary points, records select among them, and the selection persists."
**Falsifies:** A definition of "monotone across records" under which one functional is monotone along descent intervals and record arrivals together; or a hypothesis that restricts the remark to potentials with a unique stationary point.

### D05-OBJ-07: op:dyn-three-pictures is ill-posed as written, and its first question has a short negative answer for fixed channels
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/03_dynamics.tex:437-438 (op:dyn-three-pictures); AUTHORING.md:41
**Evidence:** "Per-agent models ... are conditionals of one normalized joint" mixes types: conditionals of a joint are functions of the peers' sample values, while P_i is a function of the peers' laws. The condition that makes "the per-agent dynamics coordinate descent of a global free energy as in prop:dyn-global-local" is that, on an open set of interior configurations, each F_i agrees with a mean-field free energy F_glob as a function of agent i's block up to block-constant terms. For fixed channels this fails whenever some nonself channel transmits information. The per-agent force on q_i contains -beta_ij log(q_j K_ij)(k), whereas the block force of F_glob is multi-affine in q_j (prop:dyn-global-local). Affinity in q_j of log(q_j K)(k) - log(q_j K)(k') on an open set makes the ratio of two affine functions of q_j equal to the exponential of an affine function; along any line this forces the exponent to be constant, so the two columns of K are proportional, and with row sums one all rows of K are equal. For fixed channels the answer is therefore "only when every peer is ignored", and the same reduction applies to the species question. The second question lacks an identification of configurations and weights. s5(b), two agents with unit variances and references a, b: with mutual edges the stationary gaps are (a - b)/(1 + 4 beta) for the reciprocal potential, (1 - beta)(a - b)/(1 + beta) for the mixture-source per-agent free energies and (a - b)/(1 + 2 tau) for a global model with one agreement record of precision tau, which agree under weight maps; with one directed edge 2 -> 1 the reciprocal flow moves the source to (a beta + b beta + b)/(2 beta + 1) while the per-agent source stays at b, so no weight map identifies them. The family named in the open problem has common stationary points only for mutual edges with matched weights.
**Fix:** Restate the first question as above and record the fixed-channel answer; pose the law-dependent case (a candidate: compared laws log-affine in the source law with a normalizer independent of it, reciprocated with transposed couplings and equal fixed rows, which makes the per-agent game an exact potential game whose potential is the mean-field free energy of a pairwise Markov random field); make "same stationary points" explicit by naming the map between configuration spaces and parameters; record the directed-edge separation.
**Falsifies:** A fixed information-transmitting channel for which the per-agent forces equal the block forces of a mean-field global free energy on an open set.

### D05-OBJ-10: Two hypotheses are missing from prop:dyn-no-global-vfe
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/03_dynamics.tex:375-385
**Evidence:** (a) needs E finite at interior configurations ("Then E is finite along the path" is asserted without a hypothesis). ## Extensions and improvements

The most useful next theorem is the version of the no-go that the narrative needs. D05-OBJ-01 shows that "some fixed normalized model in which the agent laws are recognition laws" is too weak a notion: an auxiliary latent factor whose recognition is slaved to the configuration absorbs any potential that is bounded below when the factor is countable, and any potential bounded on a given set when it is finite. The natural notion is the profile free energy, F~(x) = min of F(Q; o) over all recognition laws whose factors on the agents' content variables are the agent laws. Conjecture: for fixed channels, E is not a F~ + b on any open set, for any normalized model with countable auxiliary variables. Route: at fixed auxiliary factors F is (entropy of q_j) plus an affine function of q_j, so F~ restricted to q_j is entropy plus a concave function; for a single finite auxiliary block the concave part is -log sum_alpha exp(phi_alpha(q)) with phi_alpha multi-affine, whose third mixed derivative d_{q_i} d^2_{q_j} is a bounded cumulant expression, while that of E grows like q_j^{-2} near the boundary. Turning this into a proof needs control of the cumulant terms for countable auxiliary alphabets and of the entropy-coefficient freedom a > 1. Difficulty: medium.

The first question of op:dyn-three-pictures for law-dependent channels reduces to an exact-potential-game condition. The per-agent field is the Fisher gradient of a single function Phi for the product metric exactly when d_i Phi = d_i F_i for every agent, whose integrability condition is the symmetry d_j d_i F_i = (d_i d_j F_j)^T of the cross second derivatives (the multidimensional form of Monderer and Shapley 1996, Theorem 4.5, which I cite from secondary statements). For divergence couplings this fails off the diagonal (D05-OBJ-07, D05-OBJ-08 (iii)); for compared laws log-affine in the source law, nu_ij proportional to exp(sum_v q_j(v) f_ij(v, .)) with a normalizer independent of q_j, it holds when every edge is reciprocated with the transposed coupling f_ji(k, v) = f_ij(v, k) and equal fixed rows, and Phi is then the mean-field free energy of the pairwise Markov random field with these couplings. A full characterization (including evolving rows) is a well-posed medium-difficulty problem, and it would also settle the species question of the open problem.

The per-agent ring of D05-OBJ-02 deserves a proposition. With rows fixed at (pi_self, pi_peer), the law system dl_i/dt = -l_i + pi_peer s_i g tanh(l_{i+1}) is a three-dimensional cyclic feedback system with negative loop sign and a unique equilibrium, unstable exactly when pi_peer g > 2; the Poincare-Bendixson theorem for monotone cyclic feedback systems (Mallet-Paret and Smith 1990, cited from memory) then gives a nonconstant periodic orbit. With rows evolving, the absence of a strict Lyapunov function is already proved in D05-OBJ-02 and the periodic orbit is numerical. This matters for the program: the 2026-10-22 review (E7) recorded that exact inheritance of a Fisher gradient flow cannot create oscillation or inertia, so the per-agent picture with law-dependent channels is the first place in 2.0 where nonreciprocal oscillation is available as a mechanism.

Records can select among the minima of a multistable reciprocal potential (D05-OBJ-04 (b)), which gives the reciprocal picture a persistent, record-driven memory without the evidence-augmented potential. On finite tables with finitely many nondegenerate minima, the record-interrupted dynamics of D05-OBJ-03 with records drawn from a declared kernel induces, in the limit of long intervals between records, a Markov chain on basins whose transition probabilities are the record probabilities of crossing each separatrix after assimilation. Computing that chain for the bistable example of s5(c) is elementary; proving the long-interval limit uses the exponential convergence of prop:dyn-global-flow inside each basin. Difficulty: low on finite tables. Separately, ch3:435 should state which constructions survive the evidence-augmented potential, starting with the chapter 10 result of 2026-10-22 X5-IMPL-14.

Finally, the contraction proposition of D05-OBJ-12 is cheap and gives the per-agent picture its first global convergence theorem; the same oscillation argument with a small-gain bound on the evaluator coupling should extend it to coupled belief and model sectors, and its Gaussian analogue answers part of it2:open-convergence.
