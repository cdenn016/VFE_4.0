### D14-BND-01: The null flow reproduces the entire inherited hierarchy; the exact compression does not depend on the peer interaction

**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/10_boundary_closure.tex:4 ("the null flow of \cref{inh:null-flow} has no selected membership history to reproduce"); thm:boundary-tree-flow (73-126), thm:boundary-tree-inheritance (222-250); chapters/06_outlook.tex:12; companion/c10_boundary.tex:4
**Evidence:** The null flow Φ^0 of inh:null-flow deletes every peer cost D_ij (j ≠ i) and keeps the evaluator terms and the row entropies. Its potential Σ_i KL(q_i⊗s_i ∥ R) + Σ_i [T_q KL(β_i∥π_i) + T_s KL(γ_i∥π_i)] is invariant under the group 𝒮 of sec:boundary-symmetry by the same three facts used in the proof of lem:boundary-equivariance (entropies are invariant under reference-preserving maps, the evaluator reads only base marginals, the permutation preserves priors), and the Fisher metric is unchanged. Under Φ^0 every contrast mode decays at rate exactly 1 (measured 1.00003, 1.00002, 1.00001, `s10_dirichlet_nullrate.out`), against 3 + 2/(N − 1) = 3.28571 with the peer terms (measured 3.28564 to 3.28571). The admitted interface is, by the chapter's own description at line 252, the class of probes invariant under the compression group, so interface and symmetry are matched by construction.
**Fix:** Replace the null-flow sentence at line 4 by: "The null flow of \cref{inh:null-flow} preserves the same family, the same descriptors and the same descended interface, with profile equation ḟ = −f(log f + V^q − c_q); the exact hierarchy is a property of the prepared family and its symmetry, not of the interaction, which enters through D_k, the incoming term, and the decay rate (1 without peer terms, 3 + 2/(N − 1) with them)." ### D14-BND-02: Global convergence of the contrast (finite base) and its non-vanishing at finite times follow from the chapter's own lemmas
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/10_boundary_closure.tex:292 ("global convergence of the contrast to zero, and whether contrast can vanish at finite time, are not established here"); prop:boundary-contrast-decay (277-290); lem:boundary-global (130-146)
**Evidence:** (a) Finite-time vanishing. The u-independent set I is a closed linear subspace of the log chart, invariant by part (i) of the proposition, and the field is locally Lipschitz, so solutions are unique backward as well as forward. If f(t*) ∈ I, the solution in I through f(t*) coincides with the actual solution near t* in both time directions, so the set of t ∈ [0, t*] with f(t) ∈ I is nonempty, closed and open, and f(0) ∈ I. This is the argument of inh:smooth-no-finite-entry (05_inheritance.tex:983-1001), which the chapter can cite. A profile that depends on u at t = 0 depends on u at every finite time. (b) Global convergence on a finite base. By lem:boundary-global the trajectory stays in a compact subset of the open finite-dimensional state space, and F decreases at the rate of the summed Fisher variances, which vanish exactly at equilibria; LaSalle's principle puts the ω-limit set inside the equilibria. At an equilibrium h^q = c_q, so f is a critical point, hence a global minimizer, of the convex map f ↦ F(f, g, b) on normalized profiles (∫ f log f is convex, D_k is convex by the pair-law form at line 155, the evaluator term is linear in f). Averaging f over u at fixed r leaves f_0 and the evaluator term unchanged and lowers ∫ f log f + Σ_k b_k D_k strictly unless f is already u-independent (part iii). So every equilibrium has u-independent f and g, hence D_k = 0 and b = π, and on a finite base every solution has contrast tending to zero and rows tending to π; the Łojasiewicz argument of prop:dyn-global-flow then gives convergence to one equilibrium. Numerics (`s5_decay_convexity.out`, part d): from initial contrasts sup|log f − log f_0| of 4.19, 5.67 and 6.67 the contrast is 5e-8, 4e-7 and 1e-5 at t = 5, below 3e-12 at t = 10 and 0 at t = 20, with F monotone and no violation of eq:boundary-global-bound. Convexity of D_k: the minimum second central difference over 300 random directions is 0.067 to 0.23 (part c).
**Fix:** Replace the sentence at line 292 by a corollary: on a finite base every solution converges to a u-independent equilibrium with class-prior rows, and u-dependence is never lost at a finite time (citing inh:smooth-no-finite-entry). Keep the general-base convergence as open, because bounded sets of L^∞ are not compact.
**Falsifies:** A u-dependent equilibrium of the profile flow on a finite base, or a solution that is u-independent at a finite time from u-dependent data.

### D14-BND-07: The ergodicity condition in prop:boundary-fixed-family is stated too weakly
**Category:** error
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/10_boundary_closure.tex:184, prop:boundary-fixed-family ("rotations of the arcs through rationally independent irrational fractions of their length. This map is ergodic on each cell")
**Evidence:** A rotation of the torus T^n by (c_1, ..., c_n) is ergodic for Haar measure exactly when 1, c_1, ..., c_n are linearly independent over Q (the Kronecker–Weyl criterion; Walters 1982 states it in chapter 1, theorem number not checked). Rational independence of the c_v alone does not suffice: c_1 = √2 − 1 and c_2 = 2 − √2 are irrational and linearly independent over Q, but c_1 + c_2 = 1, so the character exp(2πi(x_1 + x_2)) is invariant and the product rotation of two arcs is not ergodic. Every cell has N − 1 ≥ 3 arcs once d ≥ 2, so the case arises. The conclusion of the proposition holds once the condition is corrected; the 4-point grid check (`s2_symmetry.out`, part b: dim Fix(H_α) = 4 = 2^d, equal to the lift image) uses a transitive cyclic shift on each arc and is consistent with the corrected statement.
**Fix:** "through fractions c_v of their length such that 1 and the c_v are linearly independent over Q", with a theorem locator in Walters.
**Falsifies:** Not applicable; the counterexample is explicit and the criterion is standard.

## Extensions and improvements

The convexity argument of D14-BND-02 does not depend on the particular rows, so it gives a structural statement worth recording. With identity channels and 𝒮-invariant local terms (base-only evaluators and references), every equilibrium of the profile flow is u-independent for every positive combination of the class costs, because ∫ f log f + Σ_k b_k D_k is convex and strictly reduced by averaging over u at fixed base. Persistent hidden contrast therefore needs a local term that reads the node coordinates, for example a reference density ρ(r, w)ψ(u(i, θ)) with a fixed nonconstant profile ψ. Such a term is H_α-covariant, so the family stays invariant and its equilibria become u-dependent (for the null flow, f ∝ ψ(u)e^(−V^q)), but it is not invariant under phase rotations: the phases stop being gauge and the phase quotient of thm:boundary-tree-inheritance is lost. It is also shared by the null flow, which keeps the same kind of u-dependent equilibrium. Within identity channels, then, persistent hidden contrast is incompatible with exact phase compression, and neither depends on the interaction. A construction that wants persistent, interaction-dependent compression has to use non-identity channels whose peer costs are not convex along the family, for example the selective channels of the 2026-10-21 research record. The identity-channel proposition is low effort; the selective-channel construction is open and, in my estimate, hard.

prop:boundary-joint-spectrum is stronger than descent through 𝒮, and the chapter can use that. The fine field at ξ depends only on r, on the value vector at ξ and on spectrum-determined scalars, so the flow commutes with pullback by every fibered measure-preserving map T, invertible or not. For a circle factor and T(θ) = 2θ, the configurations q and q∘T have equal joint spectra, but when ξ ↦ (q_i(ξ))_i is injective no bimeasurable reference-preserving map relates them. The root-level lumping is therefore a quotient by a semigroup of factor maps. This yields an interface hierarchy with a sharp boundary: outputs that read only base coordinates descend to the joint spectrum for every identity-channel configuration, including the heterogeneous two-block states of D14-BND-03 and -04, whereas full-sample fresh outputs need the 𝒮-symmetry. A corollary stating base-only root responses and base-only broadcast records for arbitrary heterogeneous families would cost a paragraph and would isolate exactly what the tree symmetry adds.

For op:boundary-minimality, the d = 1 computation of D14-BND-05 becomes a proof once the Jacobian is evaluated exactly at one rational point; real-analyticity then gives a nonzero determinant on an open dense subset of the connected descriptor domain. At d = 2 with a two-point base the unordered tree has 7 coordinates per sector. The root response supplies one scalar per derivative order, so one needs jets to order 6 or adaptive base-only records used as interventions (each record multiplies f by L(o | r), and the post-record jets add new rows to the Jacobian). An exact rank computation at one rational point with two interventions is a moderate symbolic task. Global orbit separation (not only local injectivity) is a question about separating invariants of the iterated wreath product C_2 ≀ ⋯ ≀ C_2 acting on R^(2^d) by permutations, combined with the flow; I did not find a primary source that settles it.

For the general-base form of D14-BND-02, the profile flow at each base point is a finite system driven by the scalar inputs c_q(t), b(t) and the base function V^q(t, ·). These inputs converge because they are functionals of quantities controlled by the decreasing energy and the bounds of lem:boundary-global. The theory of asymptotically autonomous systems (Markus; Thieme) should then carry the finite-base LaSalle argument over pointwise in r, uniformly through the bounds of lem:boundary-global. Difficulty: medium; the step to check is uniform convergence of V^q(t, ·) in L^∞(A).

For the conjugacy conjecture of D14-BND-04, a local version near the symmetric family is a finite computation at d = 2. Parametrize the conjugacy set near f^L = f^R, quotient by the tangent directions of the 𝒮-saturation, and compute the first-order normal velocity of the conjugacy defect along the remaining directions. If that linear map has trivial kernel, no flow-invariant subset of the conjugacy set passes through the symmetric family transversally to its 𝒮-saturation. This is low to moderate effort with the scripts in `scripts/D14-BND/`, which already compute exact fine velocities.
