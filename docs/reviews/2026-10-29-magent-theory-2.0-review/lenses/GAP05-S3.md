### GAP05-S3-01: Source-role stationarity through a code kernel is an EM fixed point, not the displayed moment match
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/18_link_dynamics.tex:300 (remark after `it2:certainty`) and :638 (`it2:open-general`); Item_2_link_dynamics.tex:277 and :294; companion/c18_link_dynamics.tex:180
**Evidence:** The remark reads: "For a regular exponential-family source $q_\eta$ with sufficient statistic $t$, differentiation gives $\partial_\eta\KL(p\Vert q_\eta)=\E_{q_\eta}t-\E_pt$ whenever the moments and derivatives exist. Thus source-role stationarity is a moment-matching condition. The Gaussian covariance formula is its second-moment instance". The displayed identity is correct; I confirmed it for a categorical source in logit coordinates and a von Mises source in natural coordinates (s6 A, residuals 1.1e-16 and 1.1e-16). But in the potential the source law is never the compared law. `it2:law-cost` compares $A_e$ with $B_e=q_j\mathcal K_e^{\mathrm{src}}$, and `it2:potential` compares with $m_e^{\mathrm{src}}=\mathcal N(\bar T_e\mu_j,\bar T_e\Sigma_j\bar T_e^T+R_e)$. For $q_\eta$ in a regular exponential family and a kernel with positive message densities, $\partial_\eta\log(q_\eta\mathcal K)(h)=\E_{q_\eta}[t\mid h]-\nabla A(\eta)$, so (my derivation)
$\partial_\eta\KL(p\Vert q_\eta\mathcal K)=\E_{q_\eta}t-\E_{\tilde p_\eta}t$, with $\tilde p_\eta=\int p(dh)\,q_\eta(\cdot\mid h)$.
Stationarity is therefore the fixed-point condition of the EM M-step for exponential families (Dempster, Laird and Rubin 1977, section 2, recalled and not reopened): the source matches, in its sufficient statistics, its own posterior reconstruction of the receiver's message law. The displayed formula is the identity-kernel case.

Two non-Gaussian checks (s6 B, B2). For a categorical source seen through an invertible noisy $4\times4$ code, the EM form matches the complex-step gradient to 2.4e-17, while the displayed formula with $p$ read on the content alphabet misses by 8.3e-2. At the stationary source ($\KL=3.1\times10^{-17}$, recovering the generating law to 1.4e-8), $|\E_qt-\E_{\tilde p}t|=4.8\times10^{-9}$ while $|\E_qt-\E_pt|=0.045$, and the naive match $q=p$ leaves $\KL(p\Vert p\mathcal K)=0.0043$. For a von Mises source through von Mises code noise, where the compared family is not exponential, the EM form matches to 8.3e-17, the message-space moment match misses by 0.198 and the content-space match by 0.385. For the Gaussian tangent potential, `it2:source-covariance` agrees with a complex-step gradient to 2.8e-16 and with the EM form $\frac12\Sigma^{-1}(\Sigma-\E_{\tilde p}(x-\mu)(x-\mu)^T)\Sigma^{-1}$ to 4.4e-16, where $\E_{\tilde p}(x-\mu)(x-\mu)^T=\Sigma-O\bar T\Sigma+OCO^T$ and $O=\Sigma\bar T^TS^{-1}$ (s6 C). It reduces to the message-space equality $S_{j,e}=C$ only because the linear-Gaussian code maps the Gaussian family onto an open set of Gaussian message laws and $\bar T_e$ has full row rank. The Gaussian covariance formula is thus an instance of the EM form, and the remark presents a consequence of linear-Gaussian closure as the general source-role condition, which `AUTHORING.md`:29 excludes. The open problem `it2:open-general` (18:638) asks how the Bayesian effect "compares with the source-role moment matching of the reciprocal potential", so it inherits the description. The law $\tilde p_\eta$ is the noisy reconstruction of `it1:noisy` (17:75) with the source's own law as decoder prior.
**Fix:** Replace the first two sentences by: "For a source $q_\eta$ in a regular exponential family with statistic $t$, compared through a fixed kernel $\mathcal K$, $\partial_\eta\KL(p\Vert q_\eta\mathcal K)=\E_{q_\eta}t-\E_{\tilde p_\eta}t$ with $\tilde p_\eta=\int p(dh)\,q_\eta(\cdot\mid h)$, whenever the moments exist and differentiation under the integral is justified. Source-role stationarity is the EM fixed-point condition $\E_{q_\eta}t=\E_{\tilde p_\eta}t$; for an identity kernel it is the moment match $\E_{q_\eta}t=\E_pt$. The Gaussian covariance formula is its second-moment instance at fixed mean." Add the $\beta$-weighted pooled form over the outgoing edges, and make the same change at Item 2:277, c18:180 and in the wording of 18:638.
**Falsifies:** A hypothesis in chapter 18 that restricts the remark to identity source kernels, or one that declares $\{q_\eta\mathcal K^{\mathrm{src}}\}$ a regular exponential family with statistic $t$; I found neither. Scripts s6 B and B2 exhibit two kernels for which the displayed identity is not the derivative.

### GAP05-S3-02: The pointwise interpretation gauge is a property of a dictionary group transitive on nonzero contents; it fails for compact linear groups with injective codes
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/18_link_dynamics.tex:59-61 (`it2:gauge-general`) and :62-76 (`it2:gauge` (i)); Item_2_link_dynamics.tex:75-76; chapters/17_communication_links.tex:7 (any $G\subseteq\GL(V)$); chapters/16_link_base_transport.tex:315 (an $SO(3)$ example)
**Evidence:** The remark is correct under its hypothesis $G\supseteq\GL^+(K,\R)$. In s3 (a) I built $\bar\theta_e'=sR_e$, a scaling times a rotation in $\operatorname{span}(v_e,y_i')$, for 200 random configurations at $K=2,3$ with random codes of rank 1 to $K$: $|\bar\theta_e'v_e-y_i'|\leq3.0\times10^{-13}$, every $\bar\theta_e'$ has a real logarithm (so it lies in the exponential image, as D24-LDYN also noted), the coded discrepancies agree to 4.7e-13, and the Laplace and $t_3$ record densities agree to 5.4e-13 relative. The only property the argument uses is transitivity of $G$ on $\R^K\setminus\{0\}$, which $\R_{>0}\times SO(K)$ and $SL(K)$ also have for $K\geq2$ (s3 (c), 9.5e-14 and 2.5e-14).

Nonlinear action. With contents in $S^1$, $SO(2)$ acting by rotation and von Mises records on $y_i-w_e-y_j$, the choice $w_e'=w_e+\delta y_i-\delta y_j$ preserves every record law for every displacement (1000 random cases, 5.3e-15), with no exceptional set. At the law level, with von Mises contents and dictionary laws, s2 gives $\partial C_e/\partial\mu_0=-\partial C_e/\partial\mu_1=-\partial C_e/\partial\alpha_e=0.162284769479$, so the edge cost depends on the three means only through $\mu_0-\alpha_e-\mu_1$.

Compact linear action. Take $G=SO(K)$ acting on $\R^K$ and put $v_e=\bar\theta_e^{-1}y_i+\delta y_j$. A displacement is absorbable on edge $e$ exactly when $\bar T_ev_e\in\bar T_e(|y_i'|S^{K-1})$. For a full-row-rank code of rank $r<K$ that set is the filled ellipsoid, so the condition is $|P_{\operatorname{row}\bar T_e}v_e|\leq|y_i'|$, an inequality; for an injective code it is the equality $|v_e|=|y_i'|$ (my derivation, via $x=\bar T^+u+z$ with $z\in\ker\bar T$). In s3 (c), with $K=2$, an injective code absorbs 0 of 400 random displacements, with median minimal mismatch 0.527, equal to $||y_i'|-|v_e||$ by brute force over all rotations; the rank-one code $(1,0)$ absorbs 305 of 400. In the tangent model with $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{so}(2)$ (s3 (d)), the belief projection of $\mathcal G$ has dimension 3 of 4 for $\bar T=I_2$ at either operating content and for $\bar T=(1,0)$, $c=e_1$, and dimension 4 of 4 for $\bar T=(1,0)$, $c=(1,1)/\sqrt2$; with $\mathfrak{gl}(2)$ it is always 4 of 4. Since $\mathfrak{so}(K)c=c^\perp$, the surjectivity in `it2:gauge` (i) holds for $\mathfrak{so}(K)$ exactly when $\bar T_e(c_i^\perp)=H_e$ on every edge.

For compact dictionary groups the records therefore separate belief from meaning in part: with injective codes a displacement preserves the record laws only if $|y_i'|=|\bar\theta_e^{-1}y_i+\delta y_j|$ on every edge, one scalar constraint per edge. The remark says "The interdependence of belief and meaning is therefore not a feature of the Gaussian tangent model: it holds pointwise, without linearization, for every record kernel of this form." That moves the feature from the noise family to the group. Nothing is false, because the hypothesis is stated, but chapter 17 admits any $G\subseteq\GL(V)$, chapter 16 works an $SO(3)$ example, and `VFE_4.0/docs/model-contract.md`:84, which admits only $\GL_\R$ today, describes how further groups would be added.
**Fix:** State the remark for any $G$ acting transitively on $\R^K\setminus\{0\}$, with $\GL^+(K)$, $\R_{>0}\times SO(K)$ and $SL(K)$ as examples. Add the compact counterpart: for $G=SO(K)$ a displacement is absorbable on $e$ exactly when $|P_{\operatorname{row}\bar T_e}(\bar\theta_e^{-1}y_i+\delta y_j)|\leq|y_i'|$ (equality for injective codes), and in the tangent model `it2:gauge` (i) holds for $\mathfrak{so}(K)$ exactly when $\bar T_e(c_i^\perp)=H_e$ on every edge. Say that the interdependence is a property of the dictionary group's action, as D22-COMM-03 recommends for identifiability.
**Falsifies:** A rotation in $SO(2)$ that preserves the record law of an injective-code edge for a displacement with $|y_i'|\neq|\bar\theta^{-1}y_i+\delta y_j|$; the brute-force minimization over rotations in s3 (c) found none.

### GAP05-S3-03: The Gaussian parent formula of it3:rigid presupposes Lebesgue reference measure, and the bridge-normalizer hypothesis read literally fails for its own Gaussian instance
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/19_curvature_hierarchy.tex:193-198 (hypotheses of `it3:rigid`), :205-210 (`it3:parent-gaussian`), :226; Item_3_curvature_hierarchy.tex:205-222 and :238; companion/c19_curvature_hierarchy.tex:111-124
**Evidence:** (a) The general-law part takes "a common sigma-finite reference measure $\mu$" and self densities with respect to $\mu$; the Gaussian part then asserts $P_A'=P_A+\sum_iP_i$ and $P_A'm_A'=P_Am_A+\sum_iP_im_i$ "For the Gaussian tangent family" without naming $\mu$. If $\mu=g\,dz$, the restricted parent law is proportional to $n_A\prod_in_i\,g^{-|I|}dz$, where the $n$ are the Lebesgue Gaussian densities, so the formula holds for all self parameters exactly when $g$ is constant. In s4 B1 ($|I|=2$, $P_A=0.5$, $P_1=P_2=1.5$), Lebesgue measure gives precision 3.5 and mean 0.4857, as the formula says; $\mu=\mathcal N(0,1)$ gives precision $1.5=P_A'-|I|$ and mean 1.1333; $\mu=e^{0.7z}dz$ gives precision 3.5 and mean 0.0857, shifted by $-|I|b/P_A'=-0.4$. In s4 B2 ($P_A=P_1=P_2=0.5$), $Z_A=1.489\times10^{-2}$ under Lebesgue measure, while under $\mu=\mathcal N(0,1)$ the base-10 logarithm of the box integral is 33.4, 133.9 and 530.5 at half-widths 10, 20 and 40, so $Z_A=\infty$ and the hypothesis `it3:rigid-normalizer` fails for the same self laws. D26-CURV-08 explains why the $\tau$-limit selects Lebesgue measure and D26-CURV-09 records the factor $h^{-|I|}$ under $\mu\mapsto h\mu$, but neither puts the hypothesis into the statement. (b) "Assume that the remaining bridge factors have a finite positive normalizer against this product measure": the product measure just named is $\bigotimes_A\mu\otimes\pi_b$. Against it a Gaussian bridge factor $\exp(-(o-(z_A-z_B-\omega c))^2/2R)$ has infinite integral, since it is constant along $z_A-z_B$; s4 B3 gives 15.96, 33.71, 69.20 and 140.19 on half-widths 5, 10, 20 and 40, growing at $\sqrt{2\pi R}=1.77$ per unit length. With the parent self laws included the integral is 0.3731. The proof's sentence "the assumed remaining normalizer makes the entire parent law a probability law" needs the second reading. (c) The proposition uses $\ell_e(o_e\mid\cdot)$ as a function on $H_e$ without stating that it is Borel. The diagonal evaluation is measurable automatically (the diagonal map is Borel), and changing versions of the self densities changes $p_A'$ only on $\mu$-null sets.
**Fix:** Write "For the Gaussian tangent family, with $\mu$ Lebesgue measure on $\R^K$," and add that a reference $g\,dz$ multiplies the parent density by $g^{-|I|}$. Write the bridge hypothesis as $0<\int\prod_{\mathrm{bridges}}\ell_e\prod_Ap_A'(z_A)\mu(dz_A)\,\pi_b(d\theta)<\infty$. Require $\ell_e(o_e\mid\cdot)$ to be Borel on $H_e$.
**Falsifies:** A convention fixing $\mu$ as Lebesgue measure for the Gaussian family of chapter 19 (19:10 declares self laws, not a reference measure), or an intended reading of "this product measure" that includes the parent self densities and is stated somewhere in chapter 19 or Item 3.

### GAP05-S3-04: it3:general-induced leaves its measure-theoretic hypotheses implicit, and the posterior at one registered record depends on the density version
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/19_curvature_hierarchy.tex:49-61 (`it3:general-induced`); Item_3_curvature_hierarchy.tex:61-72; companion/c19_curvature_hierarchy.tex:9-15
**Evidence:** The formula $\pi(d\omega\mid o)=Z(\omega)\pi(d\omega)/\int Z\pi$ needs (H1) a sigma-finite measure $\lambda$ on the record space dominating the record law for every $(y,\omega)$, implicit in "density"; (H2) a jointly measurable version of $\ell(o\mid y,\omega)$, so that $Z$ is measurable by Tonelli (such a version exists for countably generated sigma-algebras by Doob's measurable Radon-Nikodym theorem; locator not checked); (H3) prior independence of contents and dictionaries, a joint prior $p\otimes\pi$, which the remark does not state ("let $p(dy)$ be a content law"); and (H4) $0<\int Z\pi<\infty$, which it states. Under (H1) to (H4) the formula is Bayes' theorem for a dominated model, valid for marginal-almost every $o$. At one fixed registered $o$ it defines a version, and the remark's "chosen density" is that choice. The choice is not inert. **Fix:** Insert "Let $\lambda$ be a sigma-finite measure on the record space dominating every record law, let $\ell$ be a jointly measurable version of the density, and let contents and dictionaries be independent a priori. Then for $\lambda$-almost every $o$ with $0<\int Z\pi<\infty$, ..." and add "At a registered record the induced action is defined for a fixed version, canonically the version continuous in $o$ when it exists, as in \cref{it3:induced}."
**Falsifies:** A standing convention in chapter 19 that fixes continuous versions and product priors for the general remark. Product priors appear in the Gaussian model (19:10-25), but no convention is stated for the remark.

### GAP05-S3-05: The C^1 hypothesis of it1:location can be weakened to a square-root density in H^1, which covers Laplace noise
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/17_communication_links.tex:160-165 (`it1:location`, `it1:fisher-location`); Item_1_communication_links.tex:181-186; companion/c17_communication_links.tex:126
**Evidence:** The remark asserts part (ii) of `it1:identifiability` for location noise with "a continuously differentiable density $f$ with finite Fisher information", computing the information from the score without writing out differentiability in quadratic mean (DQM). I reconstructed DQM as follows. If $g=\sqrt f\in H^1(\R^r)$, then $\Vert g(\cdot-a)-g+a\cdot\nabla g\Vert_{L^2}=o(|a|)$ by Plancherel and dominated convergence, because $|e^{-\mathrm i a\cdot\xi}-1+\mathrm i a\cdot\xi|^2\leq4|a|^2|\xi|^2$ and $|\xi|\hat g\in L^2$; so the location family is DQM with information $J_f=4\int\nabla g\nabla g^T$. Under the remark's hypothesis, $g_\varepsilon=\sqrt{f+\varepsilon^2}-\varepsilon$ is $C^1$, $g_\varepsilon\to g$ in $L^2$, and $\nabla g_\varepsilon\to\nabla f/(2\sqrt f)\mathbf 1_{f>0}$ in $L^2$ by dominated convergence, using $\nabla f=0$ on $\{f=0\}$; hence $g\in H^1$. Averaging over referents keeps DQM: the shift is $a(\bar y,\delta)=-\bar T\delta\bar\theta^{-1}\bar y+O(|\delta|^2|\bar y|)$, the remainder ratio is bounded, and the finite second moment $M$ of 17:133 gives dominated convergence. The information is then $\E_\rho[(\bar T\delta\bar\theta^{-1}\bar y)^TJ_f(\bar T\delta\bar\theta^{-1}\bar y)]$, which is `it1:fisher-location`. So the remark is correct, and its $C^1$ clause is stronger than needed: the Laplace density is not $C^1$, but $\sqrt f$ is Lipschitz with $J_f=1$. Numerics (s5 C), with $H^2(t)=\int(\sqrt{f(x-t)}-\sqrt{f(x)})^2dx$, so that DQM gives $4H^2(t)/t^2\to J_f$: at $t=10^{-3}$ the ratio is 2.99895 for $x^2\varphi(x)$ ($C^1$ with a zero, $J_f=3$), 0.75000 for $t_5$ ($J_f=3/4$), 0.99967 for Laplace ($J_f=1$), and 3800 $=4/t$ for uniform noise, which is not DQM. For the referent-averaged experiment ($K=2$, $r=1$, four referents, s5 C2), at $t=10^{-4}$ the values are 68.2228 against 68.2504 ($x^2\varphi$), 17.0600 against 17.0626 ($t_5$) and 22.7413 against 22.7501 (Laplace), and for $\bar T\delta_0=0$ the location shift vanishes identically (2.2e-16). For the two-dimensional density proportional to $|u|^2e^{-|u|^2/2}$, quadrature gives $J_f=I$ (0.9999), $\operatorname{Cov}=2I$, eigenvalues 0.5 for $J_f-\operatorname{Cov}^{-1}$, and $4H^2/t^2=1.00001$ against $a^TJ_fa/t^2=0.99994$ (s5 C3). The one-dimensional statement is van der Vaart 1998, Example 7.8 with Lemma 7.6 (located through secondary sources; I did not open the book).
**Fix:** Replace "a continuously differentiable density $f$ with finite Fisher information" by "a density $f$ with $\sqrt f\in H^1(\R^r)$, equivalently with finite Fisher information $J_f=4\int\nabla\sqrt f\,\nabla\sqrt f^T$ (for example a $C^1$ density with finite $J_f$, or the Laplace density)", and add one sentence that the referent-averaged model is then differentiable in quadratic mean because $M$ is finite.
**Falsifies:** A density with $\sqrt f\in H^1(\R^r)$ whose location family is not DQM; the Plancherel argument excludes it. The Hellinger check is numerical support only.

## Answers to the focus questions

1. Non-Gaussian instances of `it2:law-cost-variation` and the own-block first variation. I built two. In s1, three agents have categorical contents, dictionaries in $\mathbb Z_3$ act by cyclic shift (a nonlinear action), the code is a noisy $3\times3$ kernel, and the joint dictionary prior couples a two-cycle. In s2, contents, messages and dictionaries are von Mises laws on $S^1$, $SO(2)$ acts by rotation, code noise is von Mises, the references are cardioid-type and the prior couples a two-cycle. The cost variation holds to 5.5e-16 and 5.6e-17; the own-block first variation $\log r_e-\E_{r_{-e}}\log\pi_\omega+\beta_e\Delta_e$ holds to 7.2e-16 and at most 1.4e-16. Replacing the joint prior by the product of its marginals changes the derivative by 1.26 (s1) and by 0.047 and 0.064 (s2), so the clause "retains the prior's correlations" is load-bearing. The row-gap identities hold to 5.2e-17 and 8.9e-16, and replicator dissipation of a dictionary law to 3.6e-16. The dominated-chart hypotheses hold in both instances: positive finite tables in s1; positive smooth densities on a compact space with parameter derivatives bounded uniformly near the base point in s2, so dominated convergence applies (quadrature at $N=256$ against $N=512$ agrees to 3.9e-16). They are not vacuous: in s7 the smooth positive family $B_\eta\propto e^{-h^\eta}$ and a receiver law with $\E_Ah<\infty=\E_A[h\log h]$ give $\KL(A\Vert B_\eta)$ finite at $\eta=1$, infinite for $\eta>1$, and not differentiable at 1. The Fisher-tensor clause is also needed: the polar chart of the von Mises family has $g_{\alpha\alpha}=kA_1(k)\to0$ at the uniform law (1.25e-5 at $k=0.005$), while the natural chart has Fisher matrix $I/2$ there (s2 (c)). Measurability of $\omega\mapsto\mathcal K^{\mathrm{rec}}(\omega)$ is D24-LDYN-06 and is not re-raised. No defect.

2. `it2:gauge-general` under a nonlinear dictionary action. For $SO(2)$ acting on circle-valued contents with von Mises records, the gauge holds for every displacement, and at the law level the edge cost depends only on $\mu_0-\alpha_e-\mu_1$ (s2, s3 (b)). For $\GL^+(K)$ the construction is exact and stays in the exponential image (s3 (a)). For $SO(2)$ acting linearly on $\R^2$ the conclusion fails with injective codes and becomes an inequality with lossy codes; the tangent version for $\mathfrak{so}(K)$ needs $\bar T_e(c_i^\perp)=H_e$. Finding GAP05-S3-02.

3. Hypotheses of `it3:general-induced` and the general-law `it3:rigid`. Dominating record measure, joint measurability and prior independence are implicit in `it3:general-induced`, and the posterior at one registered record depends on the version (GAP05-S3-04). In `it3:rigid` the diagonal evaluation is measurable automatically; measurability of $\ell_e(o_e\mid\cdot)$ is unstated; the normalizers are stated, but the Gaussian formula needs Lebesgue reference measure, which also decides whether $Z_A$ is finite, and the bridge normalizer must be taken against the parent self laws (GAP05-S3-03). The bridge prior under a coupled dictionary prior is D26-CURV-09.

4. Side conditions of `it1:noisy` and DQM in `it1:location`. The side conditions are written out in the checked table: regular posteriors exist because the content and message spaces are standard Borel; $\KL(q\Vert p)<\infty$ gives $q\ll p$, hence $q\otimes\mathcal K\ll p\otimes\mathcal K$ and $q\mathcal K\ll p\mathcal K$, which is what makes $\hat q$ independent of the version of $p^h$; s5 B shows $\hat q$ is version dependent when $q\mathcal K\not\ll p\mathcal K$. DQM is written out in GAP05-S3-05, where I also show the $C^1$ hypothesis can be weakened.

5. Moment matching on a non-Gaussian exponential family, and Gaussian features presented as theory. The displayed identity holds for identity kernels (categorical and von Mises, s6 A), but source-role stationarity through a code kernel is an EM fixed point (GAP05-S3-01). That remark is the one place in my scope where a consequence of linear-Gaussian closure is presented as the general property. Elsewhere in chapters 17 to 19: 17:125 ("the formal content of the statement that the receiver internalizes the message") presents the Gaussian lift direction $\Sigma_pA^T$ as general, already filed as D28-ITEMS-17 and still present in the chapter; companion c18:83 states exact factor means without the Gaussian caveat (XC3-SCOPE-05); `it3:parent-gaussian` relies silently on Lebesgue measure (GAP05-S3-03); `it2:gauge-general` trades the Gaussian feature for a group-transitivity feature (GAP05-S3-02). The other Gaussian features are flagged where used (17:103, 18:101, 18:125, 18:205, 18:283, 18:287, 19:180, 19:226, 19:265). As a check of the caveat in `it2:certainty` (i), a Laplace prior with Gaussian record noise gives exact posterior variances 0.170, 0.228, 0.250 and 0.250 at records 0, 1, 3 and 6 (s6 D): the record moves the uncertainty, as 18:300 says general posteriors may.

## Extensions and improvements

A general-law source-role proposition is the most useful addition in this scope, because it replaces the remark of GAP05-S3-01 by a statement the coupled dynamics can use. For a source $j$ with outgoing edges $e$ of weight $\beta_e$, the source-role part of $\mathcal E_T$ in $q_j$ is $\sum_e\beta_e\E_{r_e}\KL(A_e(\omega_e)\Vert q_j\mathcal K_e^{\mathrm{src}})$, which equals a $q_j$-free term minus $\sum_e\beta_e\int\bar A_e\log(q_j\mathcal K_e^{\mathrm{src}})$ with $\bar A_e=\int A_e(\omega)r_e(d\omega)$. For $q_j=q_\eta$ in a regular exponential family the force in natural coordinates is $\sum_e\beta_e(\E_{q_\eta}t-\E_{\tilde p_e}t)$ with $\tilde p_e=\int\bar A_e(dh)\,q_\eta(\cdot\mid h)$, the source's own `it1:noisy` reconstruction of what receiver $e$ predicts. Source-role stationarity is then the pooled EM condition $\E_{q_\eta}t=\sum_e\beta_e\E_{\tilde p_e}t/\sum_e\beta_e$, and the Fisher flow $\dot\eta=-\operatorname{Cov}_{q_\eta}(t)^{-1}\sum_e\beta_e(\E_{q_\eta}t-\E_{\tilde p_e}t)$ is a continuous-time natural-gradient EM. Amari (1995, Neural Networks 8) describes EM through alternating e- and m-projections; I recall this and did not reopen it. Two consequences deserve statements. The arithmetic pool of `prop:dyn-compensation` is the identity-kernel case only. With a non-injective code, the minimizers of $\KL(p\Vert q\mathcal K)$ over $q$ form a set on which only the code-visible part of $q$ is fixed, which is the general-law form of the observation at 03:134 that only the message marginal of the source moves. Difficulty: low for the identity and the pooled condition; moderate for uniqueness and stability of the joint flow, since EM fixed points need not be unique.

The compact-group counterpart of `it2:gauge-general` (GAP05-S3-02) is one proposition. For a linear action of $G$ on $\R^K$, a displacement is absorbable on edge $e$ exactly when $\bar T_ev_e\in\bar T_e(G\cdot y_i')$, with $v_e=\bar\theta_e^{-1}y_i+\delta y_j$. For a transitive $G$ the orbit is $\R^K\setminus\{0\}$. For $SO(K)$ it is the sphere of radius $|y_i'|$, whose image under a rank-$r$ code is the filled ellipsoid $\{u:u^T(\bar T\bar T^T)^{-1}u\leq|y_i'|^2\}$ for $r<K$ and its boundary for $r=K$. In the tangent model the condition becomes $\bar T_e(\delta y_i-\delta y_j)\in\bar T_e(\mathfrak g c_i)$, with $\mathfrak{so}(K)c_i=c_i^\perp$. With D22-COMM-03 (compact stabilizers are small), this would tell the program how much of the belief-meaning confound survives for $SO(3)$ frames: with injective codes, one scalar per edge is fixed by the records alone. Difficulty: low.

A general-law soft-to-rigid theorem is within reach and would settle part of `it3:open-general`. Suppose the self densities are bounded and continuous almost everywhere, the cross-scale factor is a Lebesgue-normalized approximate identity $k_\tau$, and the internal and bridge factors are bounded and continuous. Then $p_i*k_\tau\to p_i$ almost everywhere with $|p_i*k_\tau|\leq\Vert p_i\Vert_\infty$, so $p_A\prod_i(p_i*k_\tau)\to p_A\prod_ip_i$ in $L^1$ by dominated convergence, the normalizers converge to $Z_A$, and the normalized soft parent law converges in total variation to the rigid restriction with $\mu$ Lebesgue. s4 C supports this for a Gumbel parent with Laplace and $t_5$ children: the total variation distance is 4.2e-2, 1.7e-2, 5.8e-3, 1.7e-3 and 4.9e-4 at $\tau=4,16,64,256,1024$, with successive ratios 2.5, 2.9, 3.3 and 3.6 approaching 4, that is $O(\tau^{-1})$. When $Z_A=\infty$, as for two copies of $\frac13z^{-2/3}$, I expect the soft normalizers to grow like $\tau^{1/6}$ and the normalized soft laws to collapse weakly onto the singular point; I have not computed this. This is the reference-measure selection D26-CURV proposed, now with a non-Gaussian example. Difficulty: low for convergence, moderate for the rate under weak regularity.

Two smaller improvements. In `it3:general-induced`, when $o\mapsto\ell(o\mid y,\omega)$ is continuous with a locally integrable envelope, $Z_o(\omega)$ is continuous in $o$, and the posterior at the registered record is the limit of posteriors given shrinking neighborhoods of $o$. Stating this makes the continuous version the canonical one and removes the version dependence of GAP05-S3-04. In `it1:location`, the weakened hypothesis $\sqrt f\in H^1$ also gives DQM of the latent-label mixture in `it1:routed` (ii), because $|\sqrt{p_\delta}-\sqrt{p_0}|\leq\sqrt{\beta}\,|\sqrt{f_\delta}-\sqrt{f_0}|$ when only one component of weight $\beta$ moves; the missing-information inequality then transfers to every such noise law.
