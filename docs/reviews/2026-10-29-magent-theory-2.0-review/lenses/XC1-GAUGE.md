**Category:** inflation
**Severity:** high
**Location:** `frontmatter.tex:11` ("An angular extension admits a physically noninjective parent"); `chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1397`; `chapters/13_per_agent_inheritance.tex:5, 297` (section "A physical quotient with inherited records"), `:299` (protocol), `:302` (pa:phase-parent, "physically noninjective"), `:309`, `:316`, `:326`, `:396`; `chapters/14_collective_local_parent.tex:5, 41` ("The common physical rotation of psi is a separate nongauge action"), `:82` (section "Collective rather than primitive physical loss"), `:84` (protocol), `:109`, `:139`, `:159`, `:279` (lp:causal-coherence), `:285`, `:378`; `chapters/06_outlook.tex:14`; `companion/c14_collective_local_parent.tex:5, 7, 181`. **Evidence:** Chapter 1 fixes the vocabulary: "A distinction is called gauge relative to such an algebra when no element of the algebra separates the configurations. When only an external protocol separates them, the text names that protocol rather than calling the distinction physical without qualification" (ch1:87), where the algebra consists of "the rows, channels, and record kernels of the agents, together with any declared external response protocol". Chapter 10 applies it: phases resolved "only by the protocols excluded" are gauge (ch10:191). Chapter 12 applies it: "no active rotation is resolved within the population, and the words gauge and physical have no content here without the response protocol" (ch12:416). Chapter 17 applies it to code stabilizers (ch17:157). Chapters 13 and 14 do the opposite. In chapter 13 the hidden phase theta_i enters no cost, row, velocity, channel or likelihood ("All full and null costs and retained velocities are independent of theta_i", ch13:304), and the protocol excludes it from the outset ("Hidden absolute-angle queries are excluded at all stages", ch13:299). The only separating device is the child-level law diagnostic E_q exp(i Psi) (ch13:305-309), which the text says "is not a future boundary response in the fixed protocol" (ch13:316). Relative to every algebra the chapter declares, theta_i is therefore gauge in the sense of chapter 1, yet the retention is called "physically noninjective" in the proposition, the section title, the abstract and the summary at ch5:1397. Chapter 14 is the same: "No absolute psi response is admitted at any stage" (ch14:84), the admitted relative-angle queries are invariant under the common psi rotation, and the only separating device is "the excluded child absolute-angle diagnostic" (ch14:159); the common psi rotation is nevertheless "a separate nongauge action" and the loss "one common physical orientation" (ch14:41, :279, outlook:14). **Fix:** Keep the chapter 1 meaning and requalify chapters 13 and 14: "noninjective on a phase that is gauge relative to the population and the admitted protocol, distinguished only by the child-level law diagnostic \cref{pa:hidden-diagnostic}", and likewise for the common psi angle of chapter 14. Alternatively introduce a separate word in chapter 1 for the active-versus-passive distinction, declare in chapters 13 and 14 an algebra that contains child-level law diagnostics, and apply the same algebra to chapters 10 and 12 so that the abstract compares like with like. **Falsifies:** An element of the declared algebra of chapter 13 or 14 (a row, channel, evaluator, record kernel, or admitted protocol query) whose law changes under the hidden phase theta_i, respectively under a common rotation of every psi; or a declaration in chapter 1 or chapter 13 that child-level law diagnostics belong to the observable algebra used to judge "gauge".

### XC1-GAUGE-02: The reference-consistency hypothesis that replaced per-agent covariance as "the substantive symmetry requirement" is satisfied automatically once non-invariant ingredients are re-expressed as supplied data
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** `chapters/01_foundations.tex:85`; `chapters/02_interactions.tex:57-72` (hyp:int-reference-consistency, eq:int-kernel-reference), `:136`; `companion/c02_links.tex:39`; `AUTHORING.md:53`; uses at `chapters/17_communication_links.tex:30`, `chapters/19_curvature_hierarchy.tex:25`, `chapters/13_per_agent_inheritance.tex:326`, `chapters/14_collective_local_parent.tex:41`, `chapters/16_link_base_transport.tex:113`; contrast `chapters/15_contextual_coupling.tex:88, 483, 648`.
**Evidence:** The remediation of 2026-10-22 H6 and X1-GAUGE-01 correctly states that per-agent covariance is dressing (ch1:85, ch2:57-63) and moves the content to the common reference change: "A substantive symmetry requirement is a statement about the reference rules themselves, such as equivariance of an interaction under a common change of reference" (ch1:85); "The substantive symmetry requirement concerns the common reference change" (ch2:63). The hypothesis, however, reads "for every common reference change h_a in the declared structure group, re-expressing all reference laws and supplied interface data leaves the displayed channel unchanged" (ch2:66). Any rule built covariantly from its inputs passes once each non-invariant ingredient is declared as supplied data and re-expressed; this is the dressing mechanism of ch1:85 applied to the reference point sigma. `check_ch2_links.py` (d): the rule L = mu_i mu_j^T fails eq:int-reference-equivariance under a non-orthogonal h (residual 0.606); the same rule written with a declared metric M = I that is re-expressed as h^T M h passes (2.2e-16). The text satisfies the hypothesis in exactly this way where it invokes it: "Re-expressing these constitutive data is the reference-consistency requirement" for codes (ch17:30), "Coordinate changes carry this metric with the other constitutive data" for the Frobenius anchor (ch19:25), and the marked frames of chapters 13 and 14 rotate the evaluator direction e_1 and the record embeddings with the frame (ch13:326, ch14:41). In these places the common reference change is not a symmetry of the model with its data held fixed, since fixed codes, a fixed lab direction and a fixed Frobenius anchor each single out frames; the hypothesis certifies bookkeeping, not symmetry.
Chapter 15 makes the correct distinction ("Passive covariance does not imply active symmetry of arbitrarily fixed references", ch15:88; "passive coordinate covariance cannot replace the missing active symmetry assumption", ch15:648), and substantive cases exist: the moment family uses Sigma_j^{-1} from the law and passes with no supplied data (script (d), 4.4e-16), and the identity references of chapter 11 and the Haar channels of chapter 12 are invariant under the declared SO(2) and S^1 with data fixed. Two statements about the hypothesis are also unsupported. Chapter 2 promises that "Every result below whose conclusion concerns bundle data ... assumes reference consistency and says so" (ch2:72), and the companion says that "the main text imposes it as an explicit standing hypothesis wherever a conclusion should not depend on the choice of reference frame" (c02:39). Grep finds the label hyp:int-reference-consistency only at its definition and at ch2:136, and only two later prose mentions (ch16:113, ch17:30).
**Fix:** Split the notion in chapter 2. (a) Naturality: the rule is built equivariantly from the laws and a declared finite list of supplied data whose transformation laws are fixed in advance; this is automatic for every construction in the text and excludes only hidden structure. (b) Active G-equivariance: the rule, references, evaluators and every supplied datum are held fixed and the dynamics commutes with the common G action; this is the substantive symmetry, met by the moment family and by the data of chapters 11 and 12, and not by fixed codes, lab directions or Frobenius anchors. State which notion each result uses, correct ch2:72 and c02:39, and add after ch1:85 that a background datum carried with the reference reduces the effective reference group to its stabilizer (O(K) for a Frobenius form), which is the artificial-symmetry diagnosis (criterion C1 of Francois 2019) that ch1:85 already applies to the frames.
**Falsifies:** A rule in the text that fails hyp:int-reference-consistency even after its non-invariant ingredients are declared as re-expressed supplied data; or a restriction in chapter 2 on admissible supplied interface data that excludes background metrics, fixed codes and fixed lab directions.

## Extensions and improvements

A covariant tangent learning model is available at low cost and would remove XC1-GAUGE-04. Replace the anchor of each edge e: j -> i by the covariance-dressed form sigma^{-2} Tr(omega_e^T Sigma_i^{-1} omega_e Sigma_i), that is, the Gram matrix sigma^{-2}(Sigma_i^{-1} kron Sigma_i) on vec(omega_e), and each loop form W_gamma by the same construction at the base agent of gamma. Every statement of it1:learning, it1:complementary and it1:unreachable then holds verbatim with the Frobenius inner product replaced by the dressed one, the it1:pair formula becomes D = ||P^Sigma_cap(omega_01 + omega_10)||^2_Sigma with the Sigma-orthogonal projector, and the limits are covariant under the whole of GL(K) (script (iv): 2.1e-15). In the coupled flow of chapter 18 the anchor then depends on the content covariances, which adds a covariance force through the anchor to the G_Sigma equation of it2:coupled-derivatives; the static results are a direct substitution, while the coupled global-existence argument needs a new coercivity bound in place of it2:coupled-coercivity. I expect it from Tr(X^T Sigma^{-1} X Sigma) >= ||X||_F^2/cond(Sigma) once the content covariances stay spectrally bounded, which the self terms give, but I have not checked the argument.

The structure group can be derived rather than declared, which would settle XC1-GAUGE-03, XC1-GAUGE-05 and the tie of chapters 11 and 12 in one statement. Let G_act be the group of exact active symmetries of all fixed data of a construction. A monotonicity lemma is immediate from geo:collective: for groups G contained in G' acting isometrically on the same collective family, h^min_{G'} <= h^min_G pointwise, with equality in direction X exactly when the residual r^G_X is Fisher-orthogonal to the orbit tangents of G'. Applied to chapter 14 with G' = S^1_marked x S^1_psi, the difference is lambda^2 I_phys plus the cross term of lp:path-memory-polynomial, which is the entire content of the obstructions. Applied to chapters 11 and 12 with G' the untied group, the difference is I1 I2/(I1 + I2) d chi^2, and the curvature appears because the tied connection -I1/(I1 + I2) d chi depends on the context through the inertia ratio, whereas the untied connection -d chi does not. The program would then report the geometry for G_act and justify a smaller group only by data that break G_act down to it, as the evaluator N(m, I) of the chapter 15 witness already does for the diagonal belief and model rotation (ch15:304-318).

The connection can be given an operational role without a kinetic term by a shared-sample transport record, which answers the absence of the Hopf witness noted under focus question 6. For a contextual loop based at c_0 made of two declared paths gamma_1, gamma_2 to c_1, draw one referent Y from a declared law nu at c_0, carry it by the path transports of ibt:path, and register o with a normalized kernel k(o | T_{gamma_1} Y, T_{gamma_2} Y) that is invariant under the diagonal action at c_1. Its law is a function of the joint law of (Y, HY) with H = T_{gamma_2}^{-1} T_{gamma_1}, and by prop:loop-blindness (ii) it separates holonomies up to nu-null sets, including holonomies that fix nu. On the SO(3) example of ibt:sec:alignment, a small rectangle of sides dx, dy has H = exp(-F_xy dx dy) + o(dx dy) with F_xy = 2 w_beta sin x/(w_x + w_beta)^2 e_3-hat, so a kernel such as k(1 | u, v) = 1/2 + epsilon tanh(||u - v||^2) with 0 < epsilon < 1/2, where ||u - v|| = ||(H - I)Y|| for orthogonal transports, has success probability 1/2 + epsilon Tr(Omega^T Omega Sigma_Y) + o((dx dy)^2) with Omega = F_xy dx dy for an isotropic Gaussian referent of covariance Sigma_Y. The dependence is quadratic in the area because diagonal SO(3) invariants of a pair see only |u|, |v| and u.v, and u.v is blind to the skew first-order term; a first-order (oriented) curvature record needs a third marked vector. A law-level comparison would not see this holonomy at all, and an agent registering the record would carry information about the curvature. Normalization and invariance are elementary; whether agents act on such records is the open part, and the construction would be the first place in the text where the base connection changes an internal record.
