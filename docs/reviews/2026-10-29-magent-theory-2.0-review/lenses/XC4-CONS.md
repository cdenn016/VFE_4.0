I read in full `AUTHORING.md`, `README.md`, the three drivers, `preamble.tex`, `magent_report.sty`, `frontmatter.tex` (1-24), `chapters/06_outlook.tex` (1-16), `appendices/notation.tex` (1-70), `companion/glossary.tex` (1-43), the three TikZ figures, the compile summary and the three build logs at every warning, and these passages for narrative comparison: `03_dynamics.tex` 36-92, 155-180, 210-221, 319-352, 372-397; `05_inheritance.tex` 1010-1030, 1281-1399; `06_fusion.tex` 645-707; `11_conditional_formation.tex` 6, 388-394; `12_open_formation.tex` 4, 43, 129-131, 247-252, 524-528; `13_per_agent_inheritance.tex` 5, 25-44, 394-398; `15_contextual_coupling.tex` 586-594, 650-668, 957-964; `16_link_base_transport.tex` 352-364; `18_link_dynamics.tex` 207-262, 619-624; `companion/c03_dynamics.tex` 12, `c12` 5-9, `c13` 3-24, `c14` 3-8, `c15` 160-180, `c18` 326-412. Every line of every `.tex` file of the seven documents (main, supplement, companion, Items 1, 2, 3, 7) and all four `.bib` files was scanned by the scripts in `scripts/XC4-CONS/` for labels, references, citations, symbols, spacing macros, lists, rules, display punctuation, headings and chapter titles; I read the context window of 341 references (all 259 cross-file references of the main manuscript and 82 sampled same-file ones), every hit of the symbol census for G, T, K, \mathcal E, nu and D, and a word-level diff of every result shared between Items 1 to 3 and chapters 17 to 19. I did not read the proofs of chapters 1 to 19 line by line for mathematical correctness; that belongs to the domain lenses. Verdict: the three documents build cleanly and their reference graph is sound (no undefined or duplicate label in any of the seven documents, and each of 341 checked references points to an existing object of the intended kind; six cross-chapter sentences misquote their targets, XC4-CONS-09), but the set does not say the same thing everywhere. The abstract, outlook, companion and glossary state the reciprocal-potential obstruction without the hypotheses of `prop:dyn-no-global-vfe`, and the unqualified statement fails in a degenerate constant-channel case; three places state as proved a null-flow reproduction that chapter 12 itself calls unproved numerics of a limit field; the outlook turns a class-specific common-clock proposition into a necessity claim; and the working documents keep corrected-away three-objective conflations and cite the main manuscript by chapter numbers that are all off by one. Notation drift is extensive but local, the notation appendix and glossary stop at the chapter-12 material, and the bibliography has no duplicate keys but carries a malformed author field, case-folded proper names and seven stale records from the 2026-10-22 review.

## Findings

### XC4-CONS-01: The abstract, outlook, companion and glossary state the no-global-free-energy result without its hypotheses, and the unqualified statement is false for constant channels
**Category:** inflation
**Severity:** high
**Location:** frontmatter.tex:5; chapters/06_outlook.tex:6; companion/c03_dynamics.tex:12; companion/glossary.tex:20 (also README.md, AUTHORING.md); against chapters/03_dynamics.tex:375-385, prop:dyn-no-global-vfe
**Evidence:** The abstract says "we prove that it is not the free energy of any fixed normalized model whose recognition laws are the agent laws"; the outlook, c03:12 ("this function is not the free energy of any fixed normalized model in which the agent laws are the beliefs") and glossary:20 repeat it without qualification. The proposition proves something narrower. Its preamble holds "the rows of eq:dyn-potential fixed and positive" and assumes that "agent i attends to a source j≠i through an identity channel"; part (a) further restricts to finite K_i=K_j and a model "over finitely many finite-valued variables", and part (b) to fixed channels with the agent laws as product recognition on an open set. Both hypotheses are load-bearing. Part (a) uses E ≥ β_ij KL(q_i||q_j) → ∞ against a bounded F; with a positive non-identity channel on finite alphabets D_ij ≤ -log min ν is bounded and the argument gives nothing. Part (b) uses a nonzero mixed third derivative; I checked (`obstruction_extension.py`) that for a fixed Markov matrix K the derivative is β_ij Σ_y w(y)(vK)(y)²/(q_jK)(y)² (finite-difference relative error 1.2e-7), which vanishes identically when K is constant in its input. In that case the unqualified claim fails: with fixed rows, source sets excluding self, and compared laws C_ij that are fixed positive laws, every entropy coefficient equals 1+Σ_{j≠i}β_ij = 2, and E = 2F(⊗_k q_k⊗s_k; o) + b for the fixed normalized joint Π ∝ exp(Φ/2), Φ = Σ_i [log R_i + Σ_j β_ij log C_ij + Σ_l γ_il log C^s_il]. `obstruction_scope.py` gives a spread of E-2F of 4.4e-15 over 25 random three-agent configurations (1.23 for identity channels). The Gibbs-reduced potential used in chapters 8, 9, 11 and 12 (rows not fixed) and the law-dependent non-identity channels of chapters 11 and 12 are not covered by the proof either.
**Fix:** Qualify the four statements: "with fixed positive rows and an identity channel on some edge, the reciprocal potential is not, on finite alphabets, a positive multiple of the free energy of any fixed normalized finite model whose recognition rule assigns the agent laws, and for fixed channels it is not one with product recognition by the agent laws on an open set." Better, upgrade (b) as sketched under Extensions: for fixed rows, fixed peer channels, identity self channels and product recognition on finite tables, E = aF + b on an open set holds exactly when every attended peer channel is constant in its input and the entropy coefficients agree, so the obstruction is equivalent to the presence of one input-dependent peer channel.
**Falsifies:** A proof of the unqualified statement that covers constant channels (impossible by the identity above) or a reading of "the reciprocal law potential" in the abstract that excludes channels constant in their input and is stated somewhere in the text.

### XC4-CONS-02: The abstract, the outlook and the formation-standard section state as proved a null-flow reproduction that chapter 12 calls unproved numerics of a limit field
**Category:** consistency
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** frontmatter.tex:9; chapters/06_outlook.tex:10; chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1394; against chapters/12_open_formation.tex:4 and 248-249 (open:null-control) and companion/c12_open_formation.tex:7
**Evidence:** Abstract: "in the proof regime the null flow reproduces its membership history". Outlook: "in the regime of its proof the null flow reproduces its membership history". 05:1394: "in the regime of its proof the null flow reproduces its membership history, so its theorem does not establish (F5)". ### XC4-CONS-03: The outlook turns the shared-target common-clock proposition into a necessity claim for every reciprocal architecture
**Category:** inflation
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/06_outlook.tex:16; against chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1329-1336 (inh:common-clock) and chapters/11_conditional_formation.tex:6
**Evidence:** Outlook: "Under the reciprocal potential, interaction-sensitive selection requires receiver-dependent targets, since shared references with additive moment channels make every pairwise law comparison relax on a common clock." inh:common-clock assumes finite positive tables, a common model-independent evaluator G_0, belief source sets that exclude self, and every belief channel equal to a smooth law C[M] of the additive moment M, constant in its input; it shows that log-ratio contrasts then relax at rate 2 (rate 1 under the null). It says nothing about input-dependent channels, self-including source sets or non-additive moments. The shared-target, selective-channel architecture of chapter 12 (r_i=G_i=1, bit-preserving channels, 12:12) lies outside the proposition, and whether its selection is interaction-sensitive is exactly the unsettled (F5) question. Chapter 11 states the scope correctly ("every architecture with receiver-independent targets and additive moment channels"). The outlook does not cite inh:common-clock, whose label is never referenced.
**Fix:** "With shared targets and additive moment channels every pairwise comparison relaxes on a common clock (\cref{inh:common-clock}); interaction-sensitive selection under the reciprocal potential therefore needs receiver-dependent targets or channels outside that class."
**Falsifies:** A proposition in the text showing that every shared-target reciprocal architecture, including input-dependent channels, has a coupling-independent membership order.

## Extensions and improvements

The obstruction of XC4-CONS-01 admits a short characterization that would replace the qualified sentence with an exact statement. Work on finite positive tables with fixed positive rows, fixed peer channels, identity self channels (the default of chapter 3) and product recognition Q = ⊗_k q_k⊗s_k. Every free energy F(Q;o) of a fixed normalized model is a sum of factor entropies with unit coefficients plus the expectation of a fixed function under Q, which is affine in each factor, so all of its mixed third derivatives across two different factors vanish. In E the only term containing both q_i and q_j with two derivatives in q_j is β_ij KL(q_i||q_jK_ij), whose mixed derivative once in q_i and twice in q_j is β_ij Σ_y w(y)(vK_ij)(y)²/(q_jK_ij)(y)²; it is nonzero for some mass-preserving w, v exactly when vK_ij ≠ 0 for some mass-preserving v, that is, when K_ij is not constant in its input (the reverse term β_ji KL(q_j||q_iK_ji) contributes nothing to this derivative). If every attended peer channel is constant in its input, E is a sum of factor entropies with coefficients c_i^q = 1+Σ_{j≠i}β_ij and c_i^s = 1+Σ_{l≠i}γ_il plus a fixed expectation, and E = aF + b holds precisely when all these coefficients equal a. So, under these hypotheses, the reciprocal potential is a positive multiple of a fixed product-recognition free energy on an open set if and only if no attended peer channel depends on its input and the entropy coefficients agree. This is a low-cost proposition (the two directions above are the whole proof once interior regularity is stated; `obstruction_extension.py` and `obstruction_scope.py` support both directions numerically), it sharpens prop:dyn-no-global-vfe(b) from identity channels to every input-dependent channel, and it shows that the obstruction is a statement about interaction rather than about the divergence. Nonidentity self channels, law-dependent channels, variable rows and the Gibbs-reduced potential are not covered and should be posed as open in chapter 3.

A shared notation layer would remove most of XC4-CONS-06 to -08 at once. Define in `preamble.tex` macros for the reserved objects (evaluator, structure group, row temperature, time horizon, sending map, code, dictionary, potential, local term, reduced potential, belief and model rows) and generate both the notation appendix and the companion glossary from one table that lists, for each symbol, the chapter that defines it. The collisions that occur inside a single chapter (G in chapters 12 and 14, T in chapter 15 and c15, K and \mathcal E in chapter 18, r_e in c18) should be fixed first, because a reader cannot resolve them from context.

For the working documents, the cleanest repair is to decide their status explicitly. Either they are archival sources whose supported content now lives in chapters 17 to 19, in which case Items 1 and 2 should carry the corrected chapter text (XC4-CONS-04) and Item 7 should restate the formation standard and the definitions it uses so that it stands alone (XC4-CONS-05), or they are retired from the directory. In either case AUTHORING should say which of its rules apply to working documents, since its no-citation rule and the working documents' purpose currently conflict.

The null-flow statement of XC4-CONS-02 can be turned into a proposition with moderate effort. The null limit is explicit (u(t) = e^{-t}u(0), rows a Gaussian kernel of bandwidth e^t on u(0)), so its event sequence is computable in closed form from the initial data, and open:persistence already compares two fields on a tube. Proving that the full limit field and the null limit have the same partition sequence on the proof's initial set needs explicit, rigorously bounded crossing margins (interval arithmetic or analytic bounds on the four event times), and then the same persistence argument transfers the statement to small h. That would make the abstract's sentence true as written.
