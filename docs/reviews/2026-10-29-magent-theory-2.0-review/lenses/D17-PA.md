### D17-PA-02: The nonflat geometry is prepared in the initial section; it is present at t = 0, at zero coupling and under the null flow
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/13_per_agent_inheritance.tex:352-392 (pa:nonflat-window, pa:nonflat-limit); companion/c13_per_agent_inheritance.tex:233-254; frontmatter.tex:11; AUTHORING.md:77
**Evidence:** Context enters only through the prepared A_1(0) = a e(χ). In the full flow dA_i/dt = -A_i + εe(ω_i) has no peer term (the rows sum to one), so the marked sector relaxes independently of the other agents. At ε = 0 the section is A_i(t) = e^{-t}A_i(0), and the connection -I(e^{-t}a)/(I(e^{-t}a)+B(t)) tends to -a^2/(a^2+D), D = Σ_{i≠1}|A_i(0)|^2, with curvature tending to the nonzero constant -2aD/(a^2+D)^2. Recomputed by quadrature for a = 0.04, d = 0.03, d_3 = 0.025 (geometry0.out, geometry_null.out): F = -12.4903 at t = 0, -12.4925 at t = 1, -12.4928 at t = 5 and at t = 20. The prescribed null (pa:angular-null at ε = 0, complete self cost) gives -12.4903, -12.4924, -12.4959 and -12.4967 at the same times, with a rank-two tensor that decays more slowly. The curvature therefore exists before any selection, with no coupling and no records, and under the null; only the tensor decays (h_χχ = 5.3e-05 at t = 1 and 1.8e-08 at t = 5 in the full flow). The heading "A common nonflat grouping window" and the phrase "post-selection window" tie nonflatness to grouping only through the choice of time interval. The chapter calls the connection a diagnostic (13:350) but not prepared, and it gives no null comparison for the geometry. This is 2026-10-22 T5 ("The contextual geometry is prepared and decays") recurring.

**Fix:** After pa:nonflat-window, state that the curvature is present at t = 0, at ε = 0 and along the null flow with the same limit, that its value is fixed by the prepared section, and that the window is a choice of interval containing the entrance time. Give the geometry the same null comparison as selection, or make the marked sector interact (see Extensions) before calling it a geometry of the group.
**Falsifies:** A computation in which the curvature of the null section, or of the full section at t = 0, vanishes on the patch of pa:nonflat-window.

### D17-PA-03: The flow studied is not the per-agent dynamics defined in chapter 3, and its circle version has no declared chart
**Category:** consistency
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/13_per_agent_inheritance.tex:9 ("Gibbs-reduced convention"), :60-86 (pa:four-transfer), :214-237 (pa:angular-flow); chapters/03_dynamics.tex:354-357 (def:dyn-per-agent-flow), :223-232 (def:dyn-chart-flow, prop:dyn-chart-flow); chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1380 (inh:null-flow); AUTHORING.md, regularity rule
**Evidence:** def:dyn-per-agent-flow is the Fisher flow on finite positive tables of all u^a, rows β_i and γ_i included, at unit mobility. Chapter 13 instead minimizes the rows exactly at every state and differentiates the reduced per-agent free energy. The two fields share equilibria but not transients, and prop:dyn-morse, which relates full and reduced flows for E, uses the gradient structure that the per-agent dynamics lacks (prop:dyn-nonreciprocal). inh:null-flow defines the null for "the per-agent dynamics of def:dyn-per-agent-flow", so every null used in chapter 13 is the null of a variant that is not defined. The angular construction runs on {-1,1} x S^1 x S^1, outside finite tables; def:dyn-chart-flow and prop:dyn-chart-flow are stated for the reciprocal potential, and pa:angular-flow asserts that "the full own-law Fisher field preserves" the family with no chart, Lipschitz hypothesis or uniqueness statement for the density-level per-agent flow. Without uniqueness, preservation is shown only for the family solution. The missing hypothesis is easy here: G_i, μ_j ⊗ H ⊗ H and ℓ have bounded log densities and Gibbs rows are smooth in bounded costs, so the argument of prop:dyn-chart-flow applies. The mismatch does not hide a failure in the cases I tested: with dynamic rows started at the priors and integrated in log-row coordinates, pa:persistent-grouping holds in 24/24 trials at ε in {0.05, 0.3}, and the null never forms the full block (bit_dynamic_rows.out). This is the per-agent counterpart of 2026-10-22 L03-DYN-03, which remediation resolved for E only.
**Fix:** Define in sec:dyn-per-agent the Gibbs-reduced per-agent dynamics on finite tables and in bounded log-density charts (first variation Σ_j β_ij (log q_i - log κ_ij) - log ℓ_i, Lipschitz on bounded sets under bounded log densities), extend inh:null-flow to it, and state in pa:angular-flow that the chart flow is well posed, so that the family solution is the solution. Optionally add the dynamic-row version of pa:persistent-grouping; its ε -> 0 limit only adds a row block relaxing at rate one to π.
**Falsifies:** A definition in chapter 3 of the reduced per-agent flow and of its chart version that chapter 13 cites.

## Extensions and improvements

Persistent four-state grouping on a full open set (D17-PA-04). On the invariant product-content family the full flow reduces to du_i/dt = -u_i + β_ij u_j + β_ii J tanh v_i + H^q_i and dv_i/dt = -v_i + γ_ij v_j + β_ii J tanh u_i + H^s_i. Persistence of the selected pair needs only a forward-invariant box [δ, U]^4 inside the positive orthant, not convergence. On the face u_i = δ the field is at least -δ + (1 - β_ii)δ + β_ii J tanh δ - h ≈ β_ii (J - 1)δ - h, which is positive for δ > h/(β_ii (J - 1)); this is where J > 1 enters. On u_i = U it is at most -β_ii U + β_ii J + h < 0 for U > J + h/β_ii. The v faces need β_ii J > γ_ii, which holds near the uniform table (3/4 > 1/2) and must be checked with bounds on the Gibbs rows over the box. Entry into the box at a finite time follows from the existing finite-window argument; extension from the invariant family to all positive tables needs the same box in the eight conditional means, where the peer term mixes conditionals through W with factor κ < 1. Difficulty: moderate, mostly bookkeeping of row bounds; validated numerics would close it quickly. The result would be persistent interaction-dependent grouping on a full open set of positive laws, which the angular construction does not give.

Interaction-dependent geometry (D17-PA-02, D17-PA-10 (a)). Replace the Haar resampling of the marked circle by a von Mises output δ_z ⊗ vM(κ_ch, α_j) ⊗ H, α_j the mean direction of the sender. The family stays exactly invariant, the own-law field becomes dA_i/dt = -A_i + Σ_{j≠i} β_ij κ_ch e(α_j) + εe(ω_i), and the rows acquire a marked cost. The contextual momentum j(X) then depends on how agent 1 drags its peers, and the null keeps the decoupled prepared section, so a statement of the form "the curvature under Φ differs from the curvature under Φ^0 on a compact patch" becomes a meaningful interaction test for geometry. A first computation is available at ε = 0 to first order in κ_ch: the peers acquire contextual tangents proportional to κ_ch ∂_χ e(α_1), which change both j and K. Difficulty: low for the field, moderate for the estimate.

A selection-dependent interface (O1 of the derivation packet; D17-PA-10 (b)). Let the hidden output be vM(w(L_j) B_j) with a smooth gate w that vanishes identically for L_j ≥ ε/4 and is positive for L_j < ε/8. Before the handoff only the negative agent transmits its hidden phase, θ enters rows and the hidden equations, and the phase quotient fails the fiber test; on the attracting ball of pa:angular-grouping every L_j is near (15/7, 15/7, 5/7)ε, so the gate is closed there, the field is θ-independent and the quotient is exact on the post-handoff invariant set. This would turn "the phase quotient is available before any group is selected" into a quotient that exists only after the interaction has moved agent 3 across the gate. It is still a declared gate, as in chapter 8, so it addresses availability, not derivation, of the interface; the honest difficulty is moderate, and a smooth flat gate is a constitutive choice that the text would have to state as such.

Dynamic rows (D17-PA-03). The ε -> 0 limit of def:dyn-per-agent-flow for the Bernoulli and angular constructions adds the row block dβ_i/dt = -β_i(h - E h), h_j = D_ij + log(β_ij/π_ij) + 1, whose costs are O(ε^2); at ε = 0 it relaxes to π at rate one and decouples from u. The implicit-function and contraction arguments therefore go through with an enlarged state, and my 24/24 numerical trials agree. Adding this as a remark would make the per-agent results hold for the dynamics that chapter 3 actually defines. Difficulty: low.

Connection retention on all sections (D17-PA-05). Stating pa:geometry-inheritance as connection preservation on every regular section plus tensor loss Σ_i I(b_i)(dθ_i)^2 is a one-line consequence of geo:retained-connection and geo:minimal-contraction and quantifies exactly what the phase quotient loses geometrically.
