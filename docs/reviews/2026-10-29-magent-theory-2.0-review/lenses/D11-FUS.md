### D11-FUS-01: Interaction necessity holds only through the exact-equality selector; the explicit membership sequence is a coupling-independent dendrogram of the initial data
**Category:** inflation
**Severity:** high
**Location:** chapters/06_fusion.tex:660-669 and 687-692 (`sec:fus-status`, clause (F5) and the closing classification "the interaction is necessary for the grouping"); frontmatter.tex:9 ("finite-time fusion whose grouping requires the interaction"); README.md:36 ("meets interaction necessity"); chapters/06_outlook.tex:10; chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1394; companion/c05_inheritance.tex:264-266; companion/c07_fusion.tex:117 ("Switching the interaction off is the decisive control ... The interaction is therefore necessary for the grouping") and 119 ("The decisive difference is the role of the interaction ... here switching it off removes every event").
**Evidence:** (a) The verdict is automatic. `sec:fus-status` argues that at lambda = 0 the flow d mu_i/dt = -A mu_i is invertible, so distinct agents never coincide and H^0 has no event. The argument uses only that the null flow is backward unique and treats agents with equal data alike, which is the mechanism of `inh:smooth-no-finite-entry`. Every construction that pairs a finite-time sticking interaction with an exact-equality selector therefore passes (F5) in this form. What the clause records here is that the selector demands exact coincidence, not that the interaction decides which blocks form.

(b) The explicit membership sequence does not depend on the coupling. Theorem (reconstructed and proved here). On the common-offset class `fus:admitted-class`, with arbitrary multiplicities, define the normalized centroid linkage (NCL) of the initial offsets u_i^0: starting from singletons, repeatedly merge the adjacent current blocks I < J that minimize r_IJ = (c_J - c_I)/(n_I + n_J), where c_I is the n-weighted mean of the initial offsets in I. Then the membership history along Phi consists exactly of the NCL merges, at the times t_k = kappa^{-1} log(1 + kappa r_k/lambda). Proof. In the tau frame of `fus:time-change`, order is preserved and contact is sticky (`fus:sticking`). A current block G is a set of consecutive agents that no outside agent has touched, since an outside agent touching a member would have joined its block. So at every earlier tau the signs between members of G and outsiders are single valued and constant, the internal terms cancel by antisymmetry, and the weighted centroid of G moves at the constant velocity lambda(n_right(G) - n_left(G)) from tau = 0. Two adjacent current blocks G, H therefore approach at the constant relative speed lambda(n_G + n_H) from tau = 0 and meet at tau = (c_H(0) - c_G(0))/(lambda(n_G + n_H)) unless an earlier event involves one of them; the next event is the adjacent pair with the smallest such value. Induction on the number of blocks finishes the proof. Consequences: on the whole class the partition sequence P is the same for every lambda > 0 and every a > c > 0; only the event times depend on them. `fus:open-gap-condition` is the NCL condition 3 g_3 < g_1 + 2 g_2 (for g_1 <= g_3), that is 2D > 0, and tau_final = (2 g_2 + g_1 + g_3)/(8 lambda) is the root height divided by lambda.

Numerical support (`d11_events.py`, exact event stepping in the original time t, independent of the tau change): over 3000 random configurations with r = 2 to 8 agents and multiplicities 1 to 3, the sequence differs from NCL in 0 cases, changes between lambda in {0.01, 0.3, 5} in 0 cases, and the event times match kappa^{-1} log(1 + kappa r/lambda) to 3.2e-14. On the four-agent admitted class (D > 0) the sequence equals the single-linkage sequence of the initial gaps in 655 of 655 random cases.

(c) Thresholding the selector removes the verdict. Along Phi^0 on the class, every pairwise difference is e^{-kappa t} times its initial value (the differences lie on the (1,1) eigendirection of A, eigenvalue a - c). A threshold version of the same selector, which joins adjacent current groups when their normalized centroid distance reaches delta > 0, therefore reproduces along Phi^0 the NCL sequence for every delta below the smallest initial NCL height. Along Phi it reproduces the equality-selector sequence for delta below a datum-dependent bound. With the same thresholded selector on both flows, P = P^0 on the whole admitted class: (F5) fails for every small delta > 0 and holds only at delta = 0. On four agents the same holds with single linkage. Near the class the (1,-1) components of the differences decay at rate a + c rather than a - c, so I expect the same conclusion on the neighborhood of `fus:open-two-stage` (reasoned, not tested).

(d) The companion's contrast with the Wishart construction does not hold up. There the schedule is the centroid dendrogram of the initial shapes (2026-10-22 X4-PRIOR-01: UPGMC, with coupling-independent times); here it is the normalized-centroid dendrogram of the initial offsets, with coupling-dependent times. In neither construction does the partition sequence depend on the coupling strength. The only difference at zero coupling is that the equality selector never fires while the Wishart threshold selector does. The 2026-10-22 panel recorded the weak pass (2026-10-22 X2-FRAME-09) and the transient parents (2026-10-22 L05-FUS-01), but not the lambda-independence of P.

(e) Two technical points. Clause (F5) concerns P, while the text proves H != H^0 (06:665); P != P^0 also holds, since P has at least one event. `inh:null-flow` is defined for potentials of the form `eq:dyn-potential`, so its use for the nonsmooth potential (delete the pair terms, lambda = 0) is an extension by analogy and should be declared.
**Fix:** In `sec:fus-status` replace "(F5) holds" by: "(F5) holds in the formal sense for the exact-equality selector. No backward-unique null flow produces an attained equality, so this verdict holds for every finite-time sticking interaction and measures the exactness of the selector. On the common-offset family the partition sequence is the normalized-centroid-linkage dendrogram of the initial offsets and is the same for every lambda > 0; a null flow read by any threshold version of the selector reproduces it. The interaction determines the event times and the exactness of coincidence, not which blocks form." Add the NCL theorem after `fus:sticking` (see D11-FUS-06). Replace the abstract clause by "A nonsmooth extension gives finite-time fusion with exact inheritance; its explicit coalescence order is a coupling-independent linkage of the initial data, and fused parents compress nothing." In README.md:36, 06_outlook.tex:10, 05_inheritance.tex:1394 and c05_inheritance.tex:264 replace "meets interaction necessity" by "meets the interaction-necessity clause only through its exact-equality selector". In c07:117-119 delete "decisive control" and rewrite the Wishart contrast as in (d). If an interaction-dependent membership is wanted, it has to come from off-family data (D11-FUS-07).
**Falsifies:** A common-offset configuration whose membership sequence changes with lambda > 0 or differs from NCL (the proof excludes it and 3000 tests found none); or an explicit reading of (F5) that forbids relaxing the selector to any threshold and under which "grouping requires the interaction" means only that exact coincidence requires the interaction.

### D11-FUS-02: The companion's per-agent subdifferential inclusion has non-unique solutions and is not the Filippov regularization
**Category:** error
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** companion/c07_fusion.tex:64 ("the evolution is n_I d mu_I/dt in -partial_{mu_I} E ... this inclusion is the same as the Filippov regularization"), 66 ("For every initial configuration there is exactly one solution"); secondarily chapters/06_fusion.tex:371-372 and 383 (the u- and z-inclusions displayed with per-agent Sign sets).
**Evidence:** The main inclusion `fus:inclusion` uses the joint subdifferential of F, whose pair part is the set of sums lambda n_i n_j eps_ij (e_i - e_j) with antisymmetric selections eps_ji = -eps_ij (06:221). The companion's partial subdifferentials let each agent choose its own selection. For two coincident unit agents, the joint subdifferential of lambda|x_1 - x_2| is the antidiagonal segment {lambda(s, -s): |s| <= 1}, while the product of the partial subdifferentials is the square [-lambda, lambda]^2. The per-agent inclusion is then satisfied by every curve mu_1 = mu_2 = w with dw/dt = -A w - lambda theta, theta in [-1,1]^2 constant. `d11_partial_inclusion.py` verifies the inclusion along three such curves from w(0) = (1, -0.5) (selection residual below 1e-9); they reach w(2) = (0.129, 0.055), (-0.155, -0.040) and (0.082, 0.292). Only theta = 0 satisfies the joint inclusion. The limiting gradients at coincidence are plus or minus lambda(1, -1), so the Filippov set is the segment, which is the joint subdifferential, not the product. The monotonicity argument at c07:66 applies to the joint operator only; the per-agent operator is not monotone. In the main text the displayed u- and z-inclusions inherit antisymmetry from `fus:coordinate-flow`, and the proof of `fus:sticking` verifies the full inclusion, so no main-text result is affected; read alone, those displays have the same defect.
**Fix:** c07:64: "With unit Fisher mobility the evolution is W d mu/dt in -partial E, the subdifferential of E as a function of the whole configuration; equivalently every pair uses one antisymmetric selection eps_JI = -eps_IJ in Sign(mu_I - mu_J)." Add the same convention to the displays at 06:371-372 and 383.
**Falsifies:** A reading of partial_{mu_I} E as the mu_I-component of the joint subdifferential. The companion's own gloss ("the interval [-1,1] at zero" for each agent's subgradient, c07:64) describes the per-agent object.

### D11-FUS-03: Block averages of separated, unsynchronized blocks do close; the stated reason that only synchronized blocks inherit is false
**Category:** error
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/06_fusion.tex:542-546 ("for a partition into unsynchronized blocks the block average does not determine the external sign forces ... the selected partition is therefore distinguished by inheritance itself"), 622-625; companion/c07_fusion.tex:99 ("an average of separated children is not claimed to close their dynamics").
**Evidence:** Call a partition separated if, in each sector and for every pair of blocks, all means of one block lie strictly below all means of the other. Then every external sign eps_ij^alpha with i in I and j in J is single valued and constant, and the n-weighted average of `fus:coordinate-flow` over I satisfies d/dt mean_I^alpha = -a mean_I^alpha + c mean_I^{alpha'} - lambda sum_J n_J sign(mean_I^alpha - mean_J^alpha), the coarse equation; the internal terms cancel by antisymmetry. The weighted-average retention therefore intertwines the flow on the separated set until the first interleaving. In the four-agent example the pairs A and B stay separated until t_2, so the partition {A, B} is dynamically inherited by averaging from t = 0, before either pair synchronizes, and for all later times. The proximal integrator confirms the discrete version exactly: from a perturbed four-agent datum, the block averages of A and B follow the coarse two-parent flow with maximal deviation 7.1e-14 on [0, 1] while all four agents are distinct (`d11_prox.py open`). The parent law also descends for unsynchronized unit children: the geometric mean of independent LN(mu_j, 1) samples is LN(mean, 1/n). What fails for unsynchronized blocks is forward invariance of the retained structure (separation is not invariant in general) and child-level response inheritance: the children's log samples given the retained log mean are N(Zbar 1 + (mu - mean 1), I - 11^T/n), which depends on the discarded deviations.
**Fix:** Replace 06:542-546 by: "For blocks that are separated in both sectors the weighted average closes the dynamics until two blocks interleave. Inheritance of the declared responses, fresh child messages and the joint reconstruction, holds only on synchronized strata, which are also the only invariant ones. The selected partition is distinguished by response inheritance and invariance, not by the averaged dynamics." Correct c07:99 in the same way.
**Falsifies:** A separated, unsynchronized partition whose block averages leave the coarse trajectory before any interleaving; the derivation and the 7e-14 check exclude it.

## Extensions and improvements

The most useful single addition is a closed-form coalescence theorem for the common-offset family. On that class, with arbitrary multiplicities, the tau-frame solution is the l1 convex-clustering path at parameter lambda tau, and its merges are the normalized-centroid-linkage merges r_IJ = (c_J - c_I)/(n_I + n_J) computed from the initial offsets, at the original times t_k = kappa^{-1} log(1 + kappa r_k/lambda). The proof is the centroid argument of D11-FUS-01(b): a current block has never been touched from outside, so its weighted centroid has moved at the constant velocity lambda(n_right - n_left) since tau = 0, and adjacent current blocks meet at the time their initial centroid gap divided by lambda(n_I + n_J) prescribes. This replaces the case analysis behind `fus:two-stages`, `fus:open-gap-condition`, tau_final and `fus:dyadic` by one statement, it makes the coupling-independence of the partition sequence explicit, and it identifies what is new in the chapter (the statistical inheritance) and what is the classical Clusterpath geometry.

A threshold-stable interaction-dependence result is available off the family through sector decoupling (D11-FUS-07). Rescale time by s = lambda t. The rescaled field is the sum of the two independent sector sticky fields and terms of order 1/lambda from the trap and the cross coupling, and the W-metric contraction of `fus:contraction` gives uniform convergence on bounded s-intervals to the decoupled sector flows as lambda grows. Each limiting sector flow is the one-dimensional sticky flow, so its merge heights are sector NCL heights, and a pair synchronizes fully at the larger of its two sector heights. Wherever these heights are strictly ordered the full-synchronization order is stable on an open set, while the null flow read by any threshold selector orders pairs by the averaged offsets (q + s)/2. A four-agent datum whose sector-wise trees produce the two outer pairs first while the averaged tree does not would give the chapter an (F5) that survives every threshold, which the explicit family cannot provide. The required estimates are short; the search for the four-agent datum is a finite computation of sector NCL trees.

Persistence does not require giving up convexity (D11-FUS-08). With agent-specific single-well references the potential stays (a - c)-strongly convex, the well-posedness proof is unchanged, synchronized blocks inherit the averaged reference by the parallel-axis identity, and the long-time configuration is the convex-clustering minimizer of the references, which has several blocks for intermediate lambda. The same strong convexity makes the final partition independent of the initial data, so the strong form of (F5) and a final-stage (F6b) are impossible for any strongly convex potential. This is a precise statement of what the chapter's remark gestures at: a history-dependent final partition needs several minima, whereas persistence needs only heterogeneous minima. Finite-time arrival of the continuous flow at the final partition should follow from a strict-complementarity condition at the minimizer; I did not check a primary source for that identification property.

Two low-cost sharpenings of `fus:prox`. The proximal objective is (kappa + 1/h)-strongly convex in the W metric, so comparing its value at mu^k with its minimum gives F(mu^{k+1}) + (1/h + kappa/2)||mu^{k+1} - mu^k||_W^2 <= F(mu^k), stronger than the stated (2h)^{-1}. And the step can be computed exactly: alternating over the two sectors (the coupling -c mu^q mu^s is smooth and the nonsmooth part separates by sector), each sector subproblem preserves the order of its targets because the quadratic weights are proportional to the multiplicities, the pair penalty is linear on the corresponding monotone cone, and the subproblem is a weighted isotonic regression solved by pool-adjacent-violators, with sweep contraction factor c/(a + 1/h). This is the integrator used for every off-family computation in this report, and it creates exact ties as `fus:prox` requires.
