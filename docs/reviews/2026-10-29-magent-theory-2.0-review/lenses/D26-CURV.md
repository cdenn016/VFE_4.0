# D26-CURV: Curvature costs and supplied hierarchies

I read in full chapters/19_curvature_hierarchy.tex (lines 1 to 268), companion/c19_curvature_hierarchy.tex (1 to 132) and Item_3_curvature_hierarchy.tex (1 to 288); a difflib comparison shows the working-document body is identical to chapter 19 apart from chapter-level sectioning, the integration note, the bibliography block and the opening paragraph at chapter line 5. I also read the 2026-10-26 integration record, future_TODO.md item 3, the 2026-10-23 gauge and variational audits with their check output, item3_checks/checks.py and its output, the parts of chapters 17 and 18 that chapter 19 invokes (it2:model, it2:per-agent-model, it2:widening, it2:gauge, it2:potential, it2:potential-forces, it1:identifiability, it1:dressed-defect, chapter 17 lines 252 to 280 and 462), inh:recursive-formation, the abstract, the outlook, the README and AUTHORING entries for items 1 to 3, and the primary texts of Hansen and Ghrist 2021 (archived arXiv v1, all 23 pages) and 2019 (arXiv 1808.01513, sections 2 and 3). Verdict: I found no false identity or proposition in the scope; every formula I recomputed holds as a statement about the declared Gaussian tangent model, and the 2026-10-23 graph, normalization and observability repairs are in place. The main weakness is the regime: the cohomological part is defined by a weak-anchor limit in which the tangent approximation that defines the model fails, and in the exact Gaussian-content record the omitted log-determinant then dominates the dictionary Hessian by a factor of order 1/ε and charges directions the tangent cost cannot see, while a small-noise limit at fixed anchors yields the same cohomological form consistently. The H^1 citation points to a paper that does not define H^1, it3:flat-wall needs identity dictionaries on its connecting paths, and several cheap strengthenings are available.

Scripts and outputs are in docs/reviews/2026-10-29-magent-theory-2.0-review/scripts/D26-CURV/ (d26_common.py helpers; d26_induced, d26_cycles, d26_stationary, d26_hierarchy, d26_background, d26_recognition, d26_exact_det, each with a _output.txt). All ran single-threaded in under 30 s. Numerical agreement supports the identities at the tested instances; it does not prove them, and where I state a proof I say so.

## Findings

### D26-CURV-01: The weak-anchor limit that defines the cohomological part lies outside the tangent model, and the omitted determinant dominates there
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** high
**Location:** chapters/19_curvature_hierarchy.tex:25 (tangent approximation), :77-90 (it3:induced, it3:projection, it3:quotient), :122-124, :127 (it3:observation), :259-262 (it3:open-fluctuation); companion/c19_curvature_hierarchy.tex:39, :43-50, :66; Item_3_curvature_hierarchy.tex:89-102.
**Evidence:** Line 25 says the tangent record "drops the product ω_e(y_i−c_i) in T̄_e(e^{−ω_e}y_i−y_j)". The theorem then takes P = εP̄, ε↓0. In that limit the common mode y_i = u for all i (the kernel of δ for injective codes on a connected graph) is constrained by no record, so its posterior precision is εN^T P̄ N with N = (1⊗I_K)/√|V|. The posterior spread of y_i − c_i is then of order ε^{−1/2} and the dropped product is of order ‖ω‖ε^{−1/2}, not small. Restoring it while keeping Gaussian contents (records T̄_e(e^{−ω_e}y_i − y_j) + ε_e, linear in y, the model class of it3:open-fluctuation) makes the content precision J(ω) = εP̄ + L(ω)^T R^{−1} L(ω) dictionary dependent. A Schur complement on the K-dimensional zero mode gives ½ log det J(ω) = const + ½ log det(εN^T P̄ N + G(ω)) + O(‖ω‖), with G(ω)_{uu'} = (𝒱_u ω)^T Π_H (𝒱_{u'} ω)/|V| and (𝒱_u ω)_e = T̄_e ω_e u, so its curvature at ω = 0 is tr((N^T P̄ N)^{−1} G)/ε. d26_exact_det.py (K = 2, three agents, four edges, injective codes): along ω_e = E_22 on every edge, where ωc = 0 for c = e_1 and the tangent weak-anchor curvature (𝒱ω)^T Π_H 𝒱ω is exactly 0, the curvature of ½ log det J at 0 is 120.3, 1193.8, 11928.3 at ε = 1e-2, 1e-3, 1e-4, against the prediction 119.3, 1192.8, 11928.0. Along a random direction it is 26427 at ε = 1e-4, against a tangent curvature of 8.63. At finite displacement t = 10√ε the exact increase approaches the predicted log-determinant ratio (2.25 against 2.39 at ε = 1e-4 along E_22; 4.62 against 4.58 along the random direction). So in the limit the theorem uses, the leading dictionary dependence of the evidence of the record that the model linearizes is a record-independent log-determinant of width √ε. It penalizes every non-absorbable first-order holonomy on every content direction, and the cohomological quadratic part is smaller by a factor of order ε. it3:observation and the companion section "Invisibility belongs to an experiment" correctly attach blindness to one operating content, but the one-content tangent experiment is exactly the one that stops describing the record when ε is small.

A consistent regime exists. With R = ρR̄ and fixed anchors P = κP̄, C_P^{−1} = ρ^{−1}Π_H(R̄) + R̄^{−1/2}(AA^T)^+ R̄^{−1/2} + O(ρ) with A = R̄^{−1/2}δP^{−1/2}, so ρ𝒱^T C_P^{−1}𝒱 tends to 𝒱^T Π_H(R̄)𝒱 (8.6279 at ρ = 1e-4 against the limit 8.6277, κ = 1). The ratio of determinant curvature to tangent curvature is 0.31, 0.031, 0.0031 for κ = 1, 10, 100, of the order of the common-mode prior variance divided by |c|², which is small exactly where dropping ω_e(y_i−c_i) is justified. Convergence needs ρκ small (at κ = 100, ρ = 1e-2 the value is 9.73), so the window is noise variance ≪ content prior variance ≪ |c|².
**Fix:** State the cohomological part as the leading term of the record precision in the small-noise limit R = ρR̄, ρ↓0, at fixed anchors in the window ρ ≪ (content prior variance) ≪ |c|² (the same proof with the two terms of C_P exchanged). Keep the weak-anchor limit only as a statement inside the declared tangent model, and add to the remark at line 122 and to it3:open-fluctuation that at weak anchors the omitted determinant ½ log det(εN^T P̄ N + G(ω)) dominates the tangent quadratic and charges the directions with ωc = 0.
**Falsifies:** The finding is wrong if the curvature of ½ log det J(ω) at ω = 0 in the exact-record model stays bounded as ε↓0, or if the common mode acquires a record-determined posterior precision in the weak-anchor limit. Neither holds in the computation above. The identity inside the tangent model is not in dispute.

### D26-CURV-02: The twisted code sheaf governs the exact Gaussian-content evidence away from the identity background, which answers part of it3:open-fluctuation
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** high
**Location:** chapters/19_curvature_hierarchy.tex:62-97 (it3:induced), :122-124, :259-262 (it3:open-fluctuation); companion/c19_curvature_hierarchy.tex:39.
**Evidence:** For fixed dictionaries θ the exact record o_e = T̄_e(θ_e^{−1}y_i − y_j) + ε_e is linear in y, so the proof of it3:induced applies verbatim with the twisted coboundary (δ^θ y)_e = T̄_e(θ_e^{−1}y_i − y_j), the coboundary of the sheaf with restriction maps T̄_eθ_e^{−1} and T̄_e: −log Z(θ) = ½ log det(P + δ^{θT}R^{−1}δ^θ) + ½‖o − δ^θ m‖²_{(R + δ^θ P^{−1}δ^{θT})^{−1}} + const. I checked two consequences in d26_background.py. (i) Linearizing at a background θ^0 instead of I, with injective codes and nonzero operating contents, the weak-anchor penalty on reinterpretations has rank K(|E|−|V|) + h^0(θ^0), where h^0 = dim H^0 of the twisted sheaf (Euler characteristic). On the directed three-ring with K = 2 the rank is 2 at the identity and at a pure-gauge background, 0 for a holonomy rotation by 0.7, 1 for diag(1, 2) and 1 for the unipotent I + E_12; on a five-edge graph it is 6 at the identity and 4 at a generic nonflat background. The cohomological quadratic part is therefore largest at flat backgrounds and can vanish on a curved loop: it is a Hessian at flatness, not a cost that grows with curvature. (ii) With P = εP̄, ½ log det J(θ) = (h^0(θ)/2) log ε + O(1); the measured slopes in log ε for the five ring backgrounds are 1.0, 1.0, 0.0001, 0.5, 0.5 against h^0/2 = 1, 1, 0, 0.5, 0.5. For injective codes on a connected graph h^0 = K exactly when the dictionaries are pure gauge in the signed sense, θ_e = g_i g_j^{−1} on every edge. That condition forces θ_{e'} = θ_e^{−1} on attending pairs and also sees the signed cycles of a DAG that no directed walk detects.
**Fix:** Add a proposition. At fixed θ the exact evidence is the displayed formula. As ε↓0 it equals (h^0(θ)/2) log ε + ½ log det(Z_θ^T P̄ Z_θ) + ½ log pdet(δ^{θT}R^{−1}δ^θ) + ½ o^T Π_H(θ) o + O(ε), with Z_θ an orthonormal basis of ker δ^θ; near the identity background the crossover is the ½ log det(εN^T P̄ N + G(ω)) of D26-CURV-01. Difficulty: the fixed-θ formula and the leading asymptotics take about a page; uniformity of the expansion across jumps of h^0, non-injective codes, and the behavior of the pseudo-determinant term for noncompact holonomy are the moderate part.
**Falsifies:** A configuration with injective codes whose weak-anchor rank or log ε slope differs from K(|E|−|V|) + h^0 or h^0/2.

### D26-CURV-03: The H^1 attribution cites a paper that defines only H^0; the quotient-norm identity is the discrete Hodge theorem of Hansen and Ghrist 2019
**Category:** citation
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/19_curvature_hierarchy.tex:33 and :88 (it3:quotient); companion/c19_curvature_hierarchy.tex:52; Item_3_curvature_hierarchy.tex:45; item1_refs.bib:1-9 (HansenGhrist2021).
**Evidence:** The archived primary text of Hansen and Ghrist, Opinion dynamics on discourse sheaves (arXiv 2005.12798 v1, Research/sources/pdfs/HansenGhrist2021Discourse.pdf), sections 2.1 to 2.3, pages 3 to 6, defines stalks, restriction maps, C^0, C^1, δ (eq. 2.3), H^0 = ker δ (eq. 2.4), local sections, relative H^0, and the Hodge theorem H^0 = ker L_F (Theorem 2.2). It never defines H^1, and its conclusion (page 22) says "much of the language and techniques of sheaf theory and sheaf cohomology have been excised from this paper". The cited statement "Its first cohomology ... is H^1 = H/im δ" and the identity (𝒱ω)^T Π_H 𝒱ω = min_a ‖𝒱ω − δa‖²_{R^{−1}}, read as the squared norm of the harmonic representative, are section 2.2.2 (cochain complex, H^k) and Theorem 3.1 (H^k = ker δ_k / im δ_{k−1} ≅ ker Δ_k, with C^k = ℋ^k ⊕ im δ_{k−1} ⊕ im δ_k^*) of Hansen and Ghrist, Toward a spectral theory of cellular sheaves (arXiv 1808.01513; J. With the R^{−1} weight, Π_H = R^{−1}P_h, where P_h is the R^{−1}-orthogonal projection onto ℋ^1 = ker(δ^T R^{−1}); I checked Π_H δ = 0 to 5.0e-15 and the min identity to 1.7e-13 (d26_induced.py). **Fix:** Cite Hansen and Ghrist 2019, section 2.2.2 for H^1 and Theorem 3.1 for the harmonic representative; keep HansenGhrist2021 for discourse sheaves and H^0; state that it3:quotient is the R^{−1}-weighted discrete Hodge decomposition in degree one (weighted least squares).
**Falsifies:** A definition of H^1 or of the degree-one Hodge decomposition in the published SIAM version of the 2021 paper (only the arXiv v1 was checked).

### D26-CURV-04: At stationarity the exact loop product, not only its linearization, fixes the orthogonal complement of c
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/19_curvature_hierarchy.tex:166-180 (it3:dissonance, it3:holonomy-gain and the following paragraph); companion/c19_curvature_hierarchy.tex:88-97.
**Evidence:** With a common c ≠ 0 every stationary dictionary is ω_e = v_e c^T (it3:rank-one). The set 𝔞_c = {vc^T} is a Lie subalgebra, [vc^T, wc^T] = ((c·w)v − (c·v)w)c^T. Since (vc^T)^n = (c·v)^{n−1} vc^T, exp(vc^T) = I + φ(c·v) vc^T with φ(s) = (e^s − 1)/s and determinant e^{c·v} > 0, and (I + uc^T)(I + wc^T) = I + (w + u(1 + c·w))c^T. Hence every stationary ordered loop product lies in G_c = {I + uc^T : 1 + c·u > 0}, the identity component of the pointwise stabilizer of c^⊥, onto which exp maps 𝔞_c bijectively (c·v = log(1 + c·u)). So H_γ = I + h_γ c^T exactly, and H_γ − I annihilates c^⊥ for the full ordered product. d26_stationary.py: for x ⊥ c, the norm of (H − I)x is 1.1e-16, 7.1e-17 and 1.2e-16 on three loops whose H − I has entries up to 0.88, 0.99 and 3.37. With equal gains the generator sum vanishes to 1e-16 while the largest entry of H − I is 0.0511, 0.0136, 0.00353, 0.000898 as the self-mean scale halves (ratio approaching 4), so only the first-order sum cancels; the second-order remainder still has the form h c^T.
**Fix:** Replace "the linearized loop map annihilates c^⊥" by the exact statement H_γ = I + h_γ c^T, with h updated along the walk by h ← h + u_e(1 + c·h) and u_e = φ(c·v_e)v_e, and note that the residual dissonance is the single vector h_γ (H_γ c − c = |c|² h_γ).
**Falsifies:** A stationary state with common nonzero c whose exact ordered product moves a vector orthogonal to c.

### D26-CURV-05: The rank statement is dim H^1, the injective-code clause excludes selective codes, and cycle spanning has an exact criterion
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/19_curvature_hierarchy.tex:91-93, :105, :112-120; companion/c19_curvature_hierarchy.tex:54; compare chapters/18_link_dynamics.tex:15.
**Evidence:** (i) The proof of the rank K(|E|−|V|+1) (:105) uses injectivity, connectivity and c ≠ 0, not cycle spanning. For full-row-rank codes, the class of it2:model, im 𝒱 = H and the rank is dim H^1 = Σn_e − rank δ = Σn_e − K|V| + dim H^0; d26_induced.py gives ranks 10, 7, 6 against dim H^1 = 10, 7, 6 for random widths, where the injective formula would give 12. (ii) Chapter 18 requires codes of full row rank n_e ≤ K, so inside that class "every code is injective" means square invertible codes, and the directed-holonomy clause excludes the selective codes that motivate chapter 17. For a selective code the zero set is larger: K = 2, T̄ = (1, 0) on both edges of a two-cycle, c = e_1 and ω_e = ω_{e'} = E_21 give (Bω)_γ c = 2e_2 ≠ 0 with 𝒱ω = 0. (iii) With all directed closed walks admitted, they span the cycle space if and only if the condensation multigraph (strong components contracted, inter-component edges kept with multiplicity) is a forest. Proof: directed cycles lie inside strong components; each component is strongly connected, so its directed cycles span its cycle space (Gleiss, Leydold and Stadler, Discuss. Math. Graph Theory 23 (2003) 241-260, doi 10.7151/dmgt.1200, abstract checked); and dim Z_1(G) − Σ_S dim Z_1(S) is the cycle rank of the condensation. The criterion agreed with a direct rank computation on 3000 of 3000 random digraphs (d26_cycles.py). Two counterexamples have directed cycles that do not span: {1↔2, 2→3, 1→3}, residual 0.4 with the only directed cycle summing to zero; and the bidirected triangle with only its two-cycles declared (the minimal loop family of chapters 17 and 18), all declared sums zero and residual 6.
**Fix:** State the rank as dim H^1 of the code sheaf with the injective formula as a corollary, attach cycle spanning only to the directed-walk clause, say that injective codes are square in the chapter-18 class, and add the condensation criterion with the citation for the strongly connected case.
**Falsifies:** A digraph for which the criterion and the rank of the directed-cycle chains disagree.

### D26-CURV-06: it3:flat-wall needs identity dictionaries on the connecting paths, and its pairing hypothesis can be replaced by an exact one
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/19_curvature_hierarchy.tex:228-242 (it3:flat-wall), :244; companion/c19_curvature_hierarchy.tex:128-130; it3:hierarchy at :37-44.
**Evidence:** (i) The hypothesis allows cross-scale edges "only when they supply the required directed paths" (:229) but assumes identity only for internal dictionaries, while the holonomy θ̄_g θ̄_e (:231) needs identity on every edge of the paths. Counterexample in the scalar positive group: I = {a_1, a_2}, J = {b}, bridges e: b → a_1 with θ_e = 3 and g: a_2 → b, and a path a_1 → A → a_2 through a parent with dictionaries 2 and 0.25. Then θ_g = 2/3 makes the only wall walk flat (product 1) while θ_g θ_e = 2, so no common θ_D exists (d26_hierarchy.py). The declared hierarchy has only parent-to-child factors, so such paths need child-to-parent edges outside the declared data; in it3:rigid the cross-scale dictionaries are pinned, so the rigid case is unaffected. (ii) The all-pairs path hypothesis is sufficient but not necessary. The conclusion holds if and only if the bipartite pairing graph (vertices the bridges, edges the pairs (e, g) closed by an admissible walk) is connected with both directions nonempty. Example: I with the single internal edge a_1 → a_2, J with b_2 → b_1, bridges e_1: b_1 → a_1, e_2: b_2 → a_2, g_1: a_2 → b_2, g_2: a_1 → b_1. Three of the four pairs close, the pairing graph is connected, and flatness forces θ_{e_2} = θ_{e_1} although (e_2, g_2) closes no walk. On a disconnected pairing graph one assigns a different θ_D per component, as in the line-244 example.
**Fix:** Require identity dictionaries on every edge of the connecting paths (or delete the cross-scale allowance) and state the equivalence under connectivity of the pairing graph.
**Falsifies:** A standing convention, which I did not find, that every edge used in the connecting paths carries the identity; it would make (i) vacuous.

### D26-CURV-09: The general-law rigid restriction does not say which bridge prior survives when the dictionary prior couples pinned and free dictionaries
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/19_curvature_hierarchy.tex:193-203 (it3:rigid), :25 (λ = 0 stated in the Gaussian setting); chapters/18_link_dynamics.tex:20 (anchored loop prior).
**Evidence:** it3:rigid fixes internal and cross-scale dictionaries at I and uses "the declared bridge-dictionary prior" (:198). In the link models of chapters 17 and 18 the dictionary prior is the anchored loop prior, which couples dictionaries around loops that contain internal, cross-scale and bridge edges. Pinning then requires restricting the joint prior density at the pinned values, a second evaluation on a null set with its own normalizer. For the Gaussian loop prior the restriction leaves wall terms (λ/2)‖Σ_{bridges in γ} ω_e‖²_{W_γ}, which couple bridge dictionaries and are not mentioned. The text does not say whether λ = 0 at :25 is a standing hypothesis for the general-law proposition. The dependence on the reference measure (μ ↦ hμ multiplies the restricted parent density by h^{−|I|}) is acknowledged at :226.
**Fix:** Assume the dictionary prior factorizes between bridge and pinned dictionaries, or define the bridge prior as the normalized restriction of the joint prior density at the pinned values with a finite positive normalizer, and record the induced wall couplings.
**Falsifies:** A standing λ = 0 hypothesis covering it3:rigid.

## Extensions and improvements

The exact Gaussian-content evidence is the most valuable next theorem in this scope, because it answers the question that future_TODO item 3 poses (is curvature penalized, and by what) in the simplest model that keeps the dropped product. For fixed dictionaries θ the records T̄_e(θ_e^{−1}y_i − y_j) + ε_e are linear in y, so −log Z(θ) = ½ log det(P + δ^{θT} R^{−1} δ^θ) + ½‖o − δ^θ m‖²_{(R + δ^θ P^{−1} δ^{θT})^{−1}} + const is exact (D26-CURV-02). The proposition to prove is the weak-anchor expansion (h^0(θ)/2) log ε + ½ log det(Z_θ^T P̄ Z_θ) + ½ log pdet(δ^{θT} R^{−1} δ^θ) + ½ o^T Π_H(θ) o + O(ε), together with the uniform crossover ½ log det(εN^T P̄ N + G(ω)) near flat backgrounds (D26-CURV-01). It shows that the evidence prefers signed-flat dictionaries by a record-independent factor ε^{−(K − h^0)/2}, that this preference sees every content direction, and that it enforces θ_{e'} = θ_e^{−1} on attending pairs at leading order. The last point bears on the standing decision in future_TODO that inverses are not imposed: the evidence, rather than the convention, would supply them. Difficulty: the fixed-θ identity and the leading term are short; uniformity across strata of h^0 and the noncompact behavior of the pseudo-determinant need a careful perturbation argument. For orthogonal dictionaries a diamagnetic-type inequality for connection Laplacians on graphs may show that the determinant is minimized at flat connections for every ε; I did not check a primary source for that inequality.

The small-noise version of it3:induced is the consistent statement of the cohomological part. With R = ρR̄ and fixed anchors, C_P^{−1} = ρ^{−1}Π_H(R̄) + R̄^{−1/2}(AA^T)^+ R̄^{−1/2} + O(ρ), so ρ times the record precision tends to 𝒱^T Π_H(R̄) 𝒱 at rate O(ρ) while the omitted determinant stays subleading whenever the common-mode prior variance is small compared with |c|². The proof is the one in the chapter with the roles of the two terms of C_P exchanged; the theorem should carry the window explicitly.

The exact stationary structure of D26-CURV-04 suggests a sharper corollary: with common c the stationary loop products form a subset of the group G_c ≅ {I + uc^T}, so the stationary dissonance of every loop is one vector h_γ, and two loop products commute exactly when h_1(c·h_2) = h_2(c·h_1), that is, when the h vectors are parallel or both orthogonal to c (the abelian normal subgroup of transvections). A direct computation of h_γ to second order in the gains would quantify the residual that equal gains leave. A three-objective map of the induced cost is also cheap. By it2:potential-forces (i), minimizing the reciprocal potential over content means at fixed covariances produces the same cohomological quadratic part with R_e replaced by S_{j,e}/β_e and o = 0, while a per-agent model integrating only its own content couples just the dictionaries of its incoming star, a relative cohomology with the senders clamped and no cycle term. Stating this would show which objectives carry a curvature cost at all.

For general laws, the reference measure of it3:rigid can be derived rather than declared: if the cross-scale factor is a normalized kernel k_τ(z, y_A)μ(dz) converging weakly to the Dirac mass at y_A, the soft parent marginal converges to the rigid restriction with that μ under bounded continuous densities and an integrable product (dominated convergence). This addresses the soft-to-rigid part of it3:open-general and explains why the Gaussian case selects Lebesgue measure (D26-CURV-08).
