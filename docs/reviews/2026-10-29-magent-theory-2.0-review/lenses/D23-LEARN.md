Anal. 30 (2011) 20-36 (bibliographic data taken from reference [134] of Gao et al.; not verified independently in this session because the Exa account had no credits). The tangent statement `it1:coboundaries` (ker B equals the coboundaries when the cycle chains span) is the vanishing of graph first cohomology with Lie-algebra coefficients on the spanned cycles, the gradient part of the combinatorial Hodge decomposition of edge flows (Jiang, Lim, Yao and Ye, Math. Program. 127 (2011) 203-244; Lim, SIAM Rev. 62 (2020) 685-715; neither verified in this session), and linearizing relative group elements on a graph is Govindu's Lie-algebraic averaging (CVPR 2004; not verified in this session). Forum 30 (2011) 1481-1491; Huang and Guibas, Comput. Graph. Forum 32 (2013) 177-186; data from references [119] and [82] of Gao et al.). Lines 277-500 cite none of these; the chapter's only synchronization citations, SingerWu2012 and BandeiraSingerSpielman2013, sit at line 246 on the frustration corollary. ### D23-LEARN-03: The clause "strictly smaller on the orthogonal complement" in it1:learning(iii) fails at λ = 0 and fails against the prior covariance
**Category:** error
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/17_communication_links.tex:332 (`it1:learning`(iii))
**Evidence:** The statement reads "It equals the prior covariance on ker L_N = Z^T(N∩ker B), is strictly smaller on the orthogonal complement, and tends to zero there as λ→∞." The proof computes eigenvalues σ²/(1+λσ²μ), so the intended comparison is with the anchor variance σ², and strictness needs λ > 0. The model admits λ = 0 (line 292), where the limiting covariance is σ²I and nothing is strictly smaller (check_learning.py, L3: largest eigenvalue 1.3000 = σ² at λ = 0). Read as a comparison with the prior covariance of the invisible coordinates, Z^TΛ_0^{-1}Z, the clause is false for λ > 0 as well. Counterexample: the reciprocal pair in K = 2 with both codes (1,0) and σ² = λ = 1. The complement of ker L_N is two-dimensional, the limiting covariance there has eigenvalues 1/3 and 1/3, and the prior marginal minus the limit has eigenvalues 0 and 0 (check_learning.py, L3). The reason is that B^TWB maps the symmetric invisible directions back into N, so D = Y^TΛ_0Z vanishes on them and conditioning on the identified coordinates does not lower their variance; the reduction from σ² to 1/3 is already present in the prior.
**Fix:** "For λ > 0 it is strictly smaller than σ² on the orthogonal complement, with eigenvalues σ²/(1+λσ²μ), μ > 0; it never exceeds the prior marginal Z^TΛ_0^{-1}Z and can equal it."
**Falsifies:** Reading "prior covariance" as the anchor covariance σ²I together with an added hypothesis λ > 0; under that reading the clause is true.

### D23-LEARN-04: The complete-exchange recovery claim cites the isotropic-anchor shrinkage formula without its hypothesis; it and it1:complementary(ii) hold for every positive-definite anchor

When N∩ker B = 0, L_N is positive definite and S = Z^TΠ_0Z + λL_N, so S^{-1} → 0 at rate 1/λ; for Bω* = 0 the limit error is Z^T(ω_∞ - ω*) = -S^{-1}Z^TΠ_0ω* → 0. ### D23-LEARN-08: Extension: detectable dissonance as the exponent of a test of flatness, and the constraint-first limit
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/17_communication_links.tex:376-396 (`it1:detectable`, `it1:complementary`), :451, :489
**Evidence:** 𝒟(ω*) = 0 exactly when (ω* + N) ∩ ker B is nonempty, that is, when some flat configuration reproduces every message law. Then the flat hypothesis H_0: ω ∈ ker B cannot be rejected by any number of messages; otherwise the per-exchange KL from the truth to the best flat fit is δ_F(ω*)² = ½ min over ω ∈ ker B of (ω - ω*)^T F_κ (ω - ω*) > 0. Numerically (check_unreachable.py, U4, which prints the Fisher-weighted squared distance 2δ_F²): a generic truth has 𝒟 = 30.67 and 2δ_F² = 2.33; a truth with Bω* ∈ B(N) has 𝒟 = 1e-28 and 2δ_F² = 5e-14. Both 𝒟 and δ_F² are positive-definite quadratic forms on X/(N + ker B), hence equivalent with constants given by extreme generalized eigenvalues, but they are different numbers: 𝒟 uses the loop metric W, the detection rate uses the Fisher metric. The chapter's statement "messages determine it" (line 387) is the qualitative half of this.
**Fix:** Add a proposition. In the tangent model with observed labels, the generalized log-likelihood ratio for H_0: Bω = 0 satisfies n^{-1} log LR_n → δ_F(ω*)² almost surely (both maximized log likelihoods are Gaussian least squares, and the strong law applies as in `it1:learning`(ii)); δ_F(ω*) = 0 if and only if 𝒟(ω*) = 0; and c_1 𝒟 ≤ δ_F² ≤ c_2 𝒟 on X/(N + ker B). Add to `it1:open-learning` that the constraint-first posterior concentrates on the F_κ-projection of ω* onto ker B, ties in ker B ∩ N being broken by the anchor, which is the misspecified-model limit of Berk (1966), already cited at line 275. Difficulty: low, about one page.
**Falsifies:** A truth with 𝒟(ω*) = 0 and positive Fisher excess, or the converse; the argument above excludes both.

## Extensions and improvements

The most useful next theorem is the detection statement of D23-LEARN-08. The chapter proves that 𝒟(ω*) is a function of the identified coordinates; the missing half is a rate. In the tangent model the flat hypothesis is rejectable exactly when 𝒟(ω*) > 0, and the generalized log-likelihood ratio grows like n δ_F(ω*)², with δ_F² equal to one half of the minimal squared F_κ-distance from ker B to ω*. Because 𝒟 and δ_F² are positive-definite forms on the same quotient X/(N + ker B), the theorem would come with explicit equivalence constants, and it would separate the loop metric W, which shapes the prior, from the Fisher metric, which governs evidence. The same calculation gives the constraint-first limit for a dissonant truth (the F_κ-projection onto ker B, distorting identified coordinates, as computed in check_learning.py OL), which should be recorded in `it1:open-learning` because the chapter's exact nonlinear statements are of constraint form.

Part of the fourth question of `it1:open-learning` is already answerable for general laws. In the tangent linear message model with any location noise law and any prior, statement (i) of `it1:learning` says the messages are conditionally independent of ω given Y^Tω, so for every n the posterior conditional law of the invisible coordinates given the identified ones equals the prior conditional law; this is the classical non-identifiability principle (Dawid, J. R. Stat. Soc. B 41 (1979) 1-31; Poirier, Econometric Theory 14 (1998) 483-509; neither verified in this session). Whenever the identified coordinates concentrate at Y^Tω*, the limit law is the prior disintegration at that value, Gaussian or not. For the nonlinear grounded model in an exponential chart the identified map is Φ(ω) = (T̄_e e^{-ω_e})_e, and the limit on the fiber Φ^{-1}(Φ(ω*)) is the disintegration of the prior along Φ, whose density relative to surface measure on the fiber is proportional to π(ω)/J_Φ(ω) by the coarea formula, with J_Φ the normal Jacobian. In the tangent model J_Φ is constant, which is why the limit is the prior conditioned on the coset; in a nonlinear chart the Jacobian is a correction that a "prior restricted to the fiber" statement would omit. Difficulty: moderate; the work is posterior consistency of the identified coordinates and regularity of Φ near the fiber (on the group side each Stab(T̄_e) is an open subset of an affine space, so the fiber is smooth).

The general-anchor λ → ∞ limit (D23-LEARN-04) costs half a page: z_λ tends to the Π_0-minimal point of {z : BZz = -(I-Q)Bω*} at rate 1/λ, with the closed form given in that finding, and every recovery and loop-energy statement of `it1:learning`(iv)-(v), `it1:complementary`(ii) and `it1:complete-exchange` then holds for every positive-definite anchor. Together with the arborescence lemma of D23-LEARN-10, `it1:exact-flat` extends from the complete exchange to every strongly connected graph: with the arborescence loop family, the configurations reproducing every message law with identity holonomy on the loops are exactly g_iu_iu_j^{-1}g_j^{-1} with T̄'_e(u_i - u_j) = 0, and uniqueness holds exactly when the code sheaf of the T̄'_e has only constant sections. Difficulty: low.

The bridge to PIFB2 is exact at second order. PIFB2's generator prior (pifb2/05_section.tex:30-51) is an anchored Gaussian times exp(-Σ c_p 𝔔_p) with the metric defect 𝔔_p = ½‖I - ρ(Θ_p)‖²_G; with fiber metric G^fib = Σ^{-1} this is the dressed defect Q(H;Σ) of `it1:dressed-defect`, and Q(e^{εX_k}⋯e^{εX_1};Σ)/ε² tends to ½‖Σ^{-1/2}(ΣX)Σ^{1/2}‖²_F, the dressed tangent form of line 307 with W_γ = Σ^{-1} ⊗ Σ (check_pifb2_bridge.py (a): 11.7937 at ε = 1e-3 against 11.7991). So the tangent model is the quadratic approximation of PIFB2's prior at the identity, and PIFB2's normalizer Z_loop = E_ν[exp(-A_loop)] becomes det(Π_0)^{1/2}det(Π_0 + λB^TWB)^{-1/2} in that approximation (check (b): Monte Carlo 0.56739 with 4e5 samples against 0.56796). For the nonlinear dressed two-cycle prior, a Monte Carlo estimate Z ≈ 0.211 lies inside the Jensen bounds [3e-4, 1] (check (c)), which are valid but loose; a sharper normalizer bound, or the observation that Z cancels from every posterior at fixed λ and Σ, is what `it1:open-learning` needs to state.

A conformance check against the implementation is cheap and was not run: `VFE_4.0/vfe4/latent_links.py` implements a based mutual-loop cost ½‖I - e^{ω_a}e^{ω_b}‖²_F per primitive pair (doubled when channels are shared) next to a Gaussian generator KL to an isotropic anchor. In the small-generator regime with grounded data its optimized means should reproduce (I - Π_N)ω* and the factors λσ²μ/(1+λσ²μ) of `it1:shrinkage`, provided its loop multiplicity matches the convention requested in D23-LEARN-05.
