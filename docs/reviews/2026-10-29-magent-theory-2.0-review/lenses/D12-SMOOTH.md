## Findings

### D12-SMOOTH-01: The sufficient condition stated inside (H3) does not imply (H3); the G2-01 counterexample channel satisfies it
**Category:** error
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/07_smooth_closure.tex:107 (smooth:hypotheses, (H3), second sentence); also 07:67 ("differentiable in the laws in the sense required by smooth:hypotheses") and 07:109
**Evidence:** (H3) assumes that the public first variations are Lipschitz into L^infty on bounded chart sets, and then asserts that "channels with kernel densities whose logarithms are bounded on bounded subsets and which depend on the laws through a continuously differentiable map into L^infty with locally Lipschitz derivative" satisfy it. That is false. Take the channel of 2026-10-22 G2-01: Z = (0,1] with Lebesgue rho, b[mu_j](z1,z) = 1 + c(y_j) phi(z1) phi(z), phi = cos 2 pi z, y_j = int g dmu_j, g = log(1/z) (in L^1, not in L^infty), and c smooth with |c| <= 1/2. Then b lies in [1/2, 3/2], is normalized, and u_j -> y_j(u_j) = E_rho[g e^u]/E_rho[e^u] is C^infty on the chart L^infty_0(rho), because v -> E_rho[g v] is a bounded linear functional on L^infty with norm ||g||_1 = 1. Its derivative Dy[du] = int (g - y) du dmu_j is locally Lipschitz in operator norm, so b is C^infty into L^infty(rho x rho) with a locally Lipschitz derivative. Every requirement of the sentence holds. The first variation of D_ij = KL(mu_i || mu_j B[mu_j]) with respect to mu_j is, however, -int (dmu_i/dnu)(z) b(z2,z) dz - c1(y_j) x_j J g(z2), where c1 = dc/dy, x_j = int phi dmu_j and J = int (dmu_i/dnu) phi dz. It is unbounded in z2 whenever c1(y_j) x_j J != 0, so h_j is not in L^infty and the chart field is undefined, as 07:109 itself says of such a channel. Check `d12_h3_sufficient_condition.py`, on n-cell grids with exact cell averages of g: sup|h - E h| = 0.207, 0.318, 0.429, 0.540, 0.651 for n = 16, 64, 256, 1024, 4096, with fitted slope in log n equal to 0.08000 against the predicted |c1 x_j J| = 0.08000 and a complex-step versus closed-form residual of at most 4.4e-16. Over the same range, the L^1(mu_j) norm of h - E h (0.081), the chart operator norm of dy (1.015) and that of db (0.507) stay bounded. The missing ingredient is the one G2-01 had stated, "a jointly bounded first-variation kernel", which the remediation dropped. A linear functional on the chart tangent space L^infty_0 has an L^infty density with respect to mu exactly when it is continuous for the L^1(mu) norm of du, and C^1 smoothness in the L^infty norm does not give that. The case matters in practice: mean-dependent channels on an unbounded public space (g(z) = z) meet the stated condition and fail (H3).
**Fix:** Replace the sentence by: "as do channels with kernel densities b_ij[mu] whose logarithms are bounded on bounded subsets, provided mu -> b_ij[mu] is continuously differentiable into L^infty(rho_j x rho_i) with derivative represented by a bounded kernel, Db_ij[mu](dmu_k) = int beta_ijk[mu](z2,.,.) dmu_k(z2) with beta_ijk in L^infty(rho_k x rho_j x rho_i) locally Lipschitz in mu; equivalently, the derivative is continuous for the total-variation norm of dmu_k. Channels that depend on the laws through moments int g dmu qualify when g is bounded." Add one sentence at 07:109 noting that the counterexample satisfies the L^infty-smoothness condition, so that condition alone is not enough.
**Falsifies:** A proof that the displayed first variation of the G2 channel lies in L^infty at a state with c1(y_j) x_j J != 0, or a stated requirement of the (H3) sentence that the G2 channel violates.

### D12-SMOOTH-02: The introduction and the companion describe the identifications made by the spectrum as symmetries of the reference; equimeasurability is strictly weaker
**Category:** error
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/07_smooth_closure.tex:7 ("so it identifies distinct conditionals only when the reference has nontrivial symmetry"); companion/c08_smooth.tex:42 ("The compression is the symmetry of the reference, not something the dynamics creates"); 07:153 ("Noninjectivity requires ... or an atomless reference")
**Evidence:** Section 3 (07:153) states the correct finite criterion: on a finite alphabet the retention is injective iff no two distinct sets of atoms of A have equal mass. A coincidence of subset masses is not a symmetry of A. Counterexample (`d12_spectrum_symmetry.py` (a)): A = (0.3, 0.2, 0.1, 0.4). Its only A-preserving permutation is the identity, yet kappa = (0.45, 0.2, 0.15, 0.2) and kappa2 = (0.15, 0.2, 0.05, 0.6) are distinct and both have spectrum 0.4 delta_{log 1.5} + 0.2 delta_0 + 0.4 delta_{log 0.5}, because atoms {1,3} and {4} both have mass 0.4. The identification has dynamical content. In the receiver-reconstruction fine flow with kappa at one public value and kappa2 at the other, the public laws and rows at T = 0.5 coincide with those of the product configuration to 2.2e-16, against 5.6e-3 for a control with a different spectrum (part (b)). A = (1/2, 1/4, 1/4) behaves the same way (part (c)): its one nontrivial automorphism does not carry (c1, c2, c2) to (c2, c1, c1). On an atomless reference, take ([0,1], Lebesgue), l(v) = c(v - 1/2) - log(2 sinh(c/2)/c) and l2(v) = l(2v mod 1). They are equimeasurable, and since l is injective any S with l2 = l o S must be v -> 2v mod 1, which is not invertible, so no automorphism of the reference relates them. The necessity half of the finite criterion also holds, which the text implies but does not prove: from two disjoint equal-mass atom sets one builds an equal-spectrum pair (verified constructively for 200 random references in part (d)). On a reference with both atoms and an atomless part, noninjectivity comes from the atomless part, so "an atomless reference" should read "a reference with a nonzero atomless part". The 2026-10-22 remediation log (R-FUS, entry L05-FUS-03) records exactly this distinction, choosing "equimeasurability quotient" over "orbit of Aut(V,A)", but the second clause of 07:7 and c08:42 reintroduce the symmetry reading. This is the persisting part of 2026-10-22 L05-FUS-03.
**Fix:** At 07:7, replace the clause with "so it identifies two conditionals exactly when their log densities are equimeasurable under the reference; on a finite alphabet this requires two distinct sets of atoms of equal mass, which is weaker than a symmetry of the reference". At c08:42, replace the sentence with "The compression is the equimeasurability of log densities under the declared reference, not something the dynamics creates." At 07:153, write "or a reference with a nonzero atomless part" and add the one-line necessity construction.
**Falsifies:** A definition of "symmetry" in the chapter as equal-mass coincidence of atom sets (none appears), or an A-preserving bijection carrying kappa to kappa2 in the example (the permutation enumeration finds none).

### D12-SMOOTH-03: Chapter 7 calls joint-spectrum closure "not established" and a coupled exact completion "not available", while chapter 10 proves it and calls it the analogue of chapter 7

Chapter 10 itself says (10:202) "This is the identity-channel analogue of the conditional spectrum of ch:smooth-closure". In the language of chapter 7 that setting has private-transmitting channels (the identity channel reads every non-base coordinate of the sender), private data feed into every cost, row and public force, and the retained joint spectrum is noninjective wherever the reference allows equimeasurable rearrangements. At 07:321, cite prop:boundary-joint-spectrum and restate what remains open: a coupled architecture whose exact completion compresses beyond equimeasurable rearrangements of the private value vector, or one that forgets distinct public child states.

### D12-SMOOTH-04: The record-and-flow composite used by smooth:feedback is only partly defined (persisting part of 2026-10-22 H1)
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/07_smooth_closure.tex:288 and 291 (smooth:feedback: "Hence the marked state determines any finite sequence of these records, feedback updates, and original smooth-flow intervals"; "Actions selected from retained histories preserve this induction"), 07:294; chapters/03_dynamics.tex:421-424 (def:dyn-record-interrupted); appendices/record_process.tex:6-31
**Evidence:** 2026-10-22 H1 (with L03-DYN-01 and X5-IMPL-03) found that the composite of records and flow used by chapter 7 was never defined. The text now points (07:294) to def:dyn-record-interrupted, which fixes declared record times 0 < t_1 < t_2 < ... and declared measurable maps A_o: S -> S, and follows the flow between them. Three things the proposition uses are still missing. (a) The definition gives a path for a given record sequence but no record law, whereas the proposition is about laws ("the inherited record kernel gives the same next-record law"). The kernel construction of def:registered-event and prop:registered-history supplies laws in discrete time without the flow, and nothing assembles the two. A process "in the sense of Davis 1984" (03:424) needs a jump kernel, which here would be o ~ N(.) followed by the update. (b) Joint measurability of (x, o) -> A_o(x) is required by def:registered-event but not by def:dyn-record-interrupted, and the Ionescu-Tulcea product needs it. (c) smooth:wellposed gives only a maximal interval [0, T*) (07:215 says global existence is not given). The composite with declared t_1 is therefore undefined whenever T* < t_1, and the proposition does not restrict the record times. Adaptive choices ("Actions selected from retained histories") fall outside a definition with declared times. Each repair is routine, but as written the statement that the marked state "determines any finite sequence" concerns a process whose law is not defined.
**Fix:** Add to def:dyn-record-interrupted a normalized record kernel R(x, do) and a jointly measurable update A(x, o), and define the history law as the Ionescu-Tulcea product of J_k(x, dx2, do) = R(Phi_{t_k - t_{k-1}}(x), do) delta_{A(Phi(x), o)}(dx2), stopped at the first blow-up time. Allow t_k and the protocol at stage k to be measurable functions of the past records. Then state smooth:feedback as the intertwining (R_ret x id)_# J_k(x, .) = J_k^c(R_ret(x), .) on the event that every flow interval lies inside the maximal interval of smooth:wellposed, which is exactly what its proof verifies.
**Falsifies:** A definition elsewhere in 2.0 that composes a state-dependent record law with the continuous-time reciprocal flow and that chapter 7 could cite, or a global-existence result covering the channel classes of smooth:hypotheses.

### D12-SMOOTH-05: The joint spectrum closes for private-transmitting identity channels with law-dependent public channels: an exact completion in a coupled architecture
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/07_smooth_closure.tex:257 (smooth:channel-variants), 07:321 (smooth:formation-open)
**Evidence:** Sketch. Take finite public alphabets Z_k, a common private space (V, A), full channels K_ij((z1, v1), d(z, v)) = B_ij[mu](z1, dz) delta_{v1}(dv) with B_ij law-dependent and not the identity, and local references R_i^c x A x A^s. Write L(v) = (l_{k,z}(v))_{k,z}, the vector of all private log densities at one private point. Then nu_ij(z, v) = sum_{z1} mu_j(z1) e^{l_{j,z1}(v)} B_ij(z1, z) A(dv) is a function of L(v) and the public laws. Every cost D_ij, every receiver and source first variation, and every centering constant is either a function of L(v) or an A-integral of one, and the channel-derivative terms are functions of z times such integrals. Hence the fine conditional velocity at v is G(L(v); mu, sigma, Xi, rows) with Xi = L_# A. Xi satisfies the nonlinear continuity equation dXi/dt + div_x(G(x; mu, sigma, Xi, rows) Xi) = 0, and the public laws and rows have velocities that are functionals of (mu, sigma, Xi, rows). On a finite V the retained system is a finite ODE in the atom positions of Xi with fixed masses, so uniqueness is Picard-Lindeloef and two fine states with equal Xi have equal retained futures. Check `d12_joint_spectrum.py` (3 agents, public bit, V of 4 points with A = (0.3, 0.2, 0.1, 0.4), law-dependent public channels, Fisher rows). Configuration X2, which exchanges two log-density values for all agents at once, differs from X by 0.206 in the fine laws but has the same joint spectrum. At T = 0.5 its public laws and rows agree with those of X to 2.2e-16 and its joint spectrum is unchanged. X3 exchanges the values for agent 0 only, which keeps every per-agent spectrum but changes Xi; its public velocity at t = 0 differs from that of X by 4.5e-2 and its trajectory at T = 0.5 by 3.3e-3. Private data therefore feed back into the public flow, the per-agent spectra are not closed, and the joint spectrum is. Because Aut(V, A) is trivial for this A, the fiber X ~ X2 is not a symmetry orbit of the fine system. The compression is real but nongeneric on a finite generic reference, where it holds only on the invariant coincidence class l(1) = l(3). It is generic on an atomless reference. This extends prop:boundary-joint-spectrum from identity public channels to arbitrary regular public channels. Difficulty: low for finite Z and V; medium for general V, which needs a Dobrushin-type W_1 stability estimate for the nonlinear continuity equation with bounded supports; for infinite Z the vector becomes function-valued and the measurability is harder.
**Fix:** State the proposition after smooth:channel-variants for finite public alphabets, prove it as above, and add the marked version (the law of (L(v), T(v)) under A), which descends under T-measurable likelihoods by the argument of smooth:feedback. Then smooth:formation-open can say precisely that what remains open is compression of public child states or compression beyond equimeasurable rearrangement of L.
**Falsifies:** Two fine states with equal Xi, public laws and rows whose public trajectories differ in this architecture, or an integrand in the fine variation that depends on the private point other than through L(v).

### D12-SMOOTH-06: The CGF class is the minimal private input to the public flow, and (H1) is not needed to identify a spectrum from its Renyi curve
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/07_smooth_closure.tex:158 ("Under (H1) the spectrum has compact support, so Lambda_z on (0,1] determines xi_z"); 07:241-251 (smooth:cgf-completion)
**Evidence:** smooth:cgf-completion proves sufficiency of the class [z -> Lambda_z on (0,1] modulo functions of b]. The converse also holds. Suppose two private configurations with the same initial public laws and rows produce the same public trajectory on some [0, eps). The public velocities then agree, so H(., t) - H2(., t) = c(t) rho-a.e. for each t in [0, eps); one null set serves all t by continuity in t. With Delta_z = Lambda_z - Lambda2_z and H = a^2 d/da(Lambda/a), this gives d/da(Delta_z/a) = c(t(a))/a^2 for a in (e^{-eps}, 1]. Since Delta_z(1) = 0, Delta_z(a) = a K(a) with K independent of z. Real-analyticity of each Lambda_z on (0,1] then gives Delta_z - Delta_{z0} = 0 on all of (0,1], so the two configurations have the same class. The class is therefore exactly the private information that the public trajectory needs and reveals. Separately, the restriction to (H1) at 07:158 is unnecessary. For every kappa ~ A, int e^{br} xi(dr) = int (dkappa/dA)^b dA lies in (0,1] for b in (0,1) by Hoelder and is analytic in the strip 0 < Re b < 1. A law is determined by its moment generating function on any nondegenerate interval where it is finite (tilt and use the characteristic function), so Lambda on any subinterval of (0,1) determines xi. Check: the control in `d12_closure_flow.py` B2 (different classes at the two public values) separates the public trajectories by 1.1e-2, while equal classes agree to 3.1e-11.
**Fix:** Add "and conversely, equal public trajectories on any initial interval force equal classes" to smooth:cgf-completion with the three-line argument, and delete "Under (H1)" at 07:158, replacing it with the Hoelder statement.
**Falsifies:** Two configurations with different classes and identical public trajectories on an interval, which the analyticity argument excludes.

### D12-SMOOTH-07: Freezing the receiver conditional in the compared law does not change the force at the frozen state
**Category:** error
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/07_smooth_closure.tex:87 (proof of smooth:peer-cancellation-rule: "Holding kappa_i fixed only in the denominator would violate that identity and would define another force")
**Evidence:** Let F(q, k) = KL(q_i || (mu_j B) k) with k a frozen conditional. The derivative of F in k is -int (dq_i/dnu)(kappa_i/k) d(dk), which vanishes at k = kappa_i because each dk_z has total mass zero. The first variation with kappa_i frozen at its current value therefore equals the true one at that state. Check `d12_frozen_denominator.py` (3 agents, law-dependent channels, both sectors): the instantaneous Fisher velocities agree to 7.8e-16. The second variation along a tangent direction differs (7.054 against 10.780), and with the snapshot held while the law moves by 0.05 along that direction the velocities differ by 9.4e-2. The identity is violated away from the snapshot, as the sentence says, but "another force" holds only for a held (lagged) snapshot, not for the force at the state itself. Chapter 3 states this correctly for identity self channels (03_dynamics.tex:134: "Its first derivative still vanishes at equality, but its curvature and its force after displacement differ").
**Fix:** Replace the sentence by "Holding kappa_i fixed in the denominator leaves the force at the frozen state unchanged, but it violates the identity away from that state, changes the second variation, and, if the snapshot is held while the laws move, changes the force (compare 03_dynamics.tex:134)."
**Falsifies:** A state at which the complex-step first variations with live and frozen kappa_i differ.

### D12-SMOOTH-08: The finite-alphabet sufficiency sentence omits the positivity hypotheses of thm:dyn-finite-flow
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/07_smooth_closure.tex:100 ("On finite public alphabets with twice continuously differentiable channel entries it suffices that each private log density be bounded"); 07:195
**Evidence:** thm:dyn-finite-flow (03_dynamics.tex:156) also assumes strictly positive reference and evaluator entries and that every compared-law entry is positive. Without the latter, a normalized C^2 public channel with a zero column (for example one sending every z1 to one receiver value) gives D_ij = infinity for a positive receiver law, which lies outside the admissible domain of def:dyn-potential, and the force is undefined. With positivity, (H2) and (H3) hold automatically on finite public alphabets. Bounded log charts are then relatively compact subsets of the interior, where C^1 forces are Lipschitz, so the sentence becomes true, and part (iii) of smooth:wellposed gives the fine uniqueness for a general private space V.
**Fix:** Write "On finite public alphabets with strictly positive r_i^c and G_i^c, twice continuously differentiable channel entries, and positive compared laws on the interior, (H2) and (H3) hold, and it suffices that each private log density be bounded."
**Falsifies:** A finite-alphabet channel with zero compared-law entries for which the public chart field is still defined and locally Lipschitz.

## Extensions and improvements

The most valuable next step is the coupled closure of D12-SMOOTH-05, because it is the first exact completion in which private conditionals act on interactions. For finite public alphabets and a common private reference (V, A), with channels B_ij[mu](z1, dz) delta_{v1}(dv), the vector L(v) = (l_{k,z}(v))_{k,z} at a private point determines every integrand of the fine variation up to A-integrals, so the joint spectrum Xi = L_# A evolves by a nonlinear continuity equation whose velocity G(x; mu, sigma, Xi, rows) is explicit. On a finite V this is a finite ODE in the atom positions of Xi, and two fine states with equal Xi have equal futures; `d12_joint_spectrum.py` shows this with a reference that has no nontrivial automorphism. On a general V the step that remains is a stability estimate of Dobrushin type: for two solutions with bounded supports, W_1(Xi_t, Xi2_t) is bounded by C e^{Ct} W_1(Xi_0, Xi2_0), using that G is Lipschitz in x and in Xi (through integrals of bounded Lipschitz functions) under a uniform log-density bound. The marked version retains the law of (L(v), T(v)) under A and descends under T-measurable likelihoods by the computation of smooth:feedback. Three limits should be stated with it. On a generic finite reference the retention is injective, so it compresses only on invariant coincidence classes; compression is generic on atomless references. The public laws remain labeled, so nothing is aggregated. For an infinite public space the vector becomes function-valued and measurability needs care. Even so, the result replaces the statement "not available" at 07:321 by a precise open problem.

The second step is to make the analytic hypotheses correct and reusable. The sufficient condition in (H3) should require that the derivative of each channel kernel act continuously on total-variation tangents (D12-SMOOTH-01); with that change, identity channels, bounded-kernel channels and moment channels with bounded moment functions are covered, and mean-dependent channels on unbounded public spaces are excluded, as they must be. A single Banach-chart well-posedness proposition for the Fisher flow of def:dyn-potential, placed in chapter 3 and stated with (H2) and the corrected (H3), would let chapters 7, 9, 10 and 12 cite one result, as 2026-10-22 G2 recommended. The global public bounds of G2-04 (oscillation of the public log densities bounded uniformly in time for two-sided bounded kernels, and a max/min principle with energy-controlled centering for identity channels) would then upgrade smooth:equal-spectra and smooth:cgf-completion to statements on [0, infinity) in those classes, and would make the record composite of D12-SMOOTH-04 globally defined there.

The third step completes the minimality picture. With the converse of D12-SMOOTH-06, the class of z -> Lambda_z on (0,1] modulo functions of b is exactly the private information that the public trajectory needs and reveals, so it is the minimal completion for the public flow. The private sector itself admits an equally explicit description: each private log density moves along its own ray, l_z(t) = a l_z(0) - log Z_z(a), so the orbit of every conditional is the one-parameter exponential family exp(a l_z(0) - Lambda_z(a)) A based at the reference, and the spectrum at time t is an affine image of the initial one. The private sector therefore closes on finitely many numbers per public value whenever the initial log densities, modulo constants, are finitely parametrized. A linear span gives the exponential-family case of smooth:exponential-family, with spectra equal to the laws of theta_z . T under A. Stating it this way removes the need to treat that case as a separate remark and makes clear that finite parametrization is a property of the preparation, not of the dynamics.

The fourth step is a formal composite process. Writing the record-interrupted dynamics as the Ionescu-Tulcea product of flow-then-register kernels, as in the fix of D12-SMOOTH-04, would put chapter 7 on the same footing as the finite adaptive history statements of chapters 12 to 14. It would also make precise what "Actions selected from retained histories" means: measurable selection of the next record time and protocol from the retained history. The proof of smooth:feedback is then the induction on kernels that its current text sketches.
