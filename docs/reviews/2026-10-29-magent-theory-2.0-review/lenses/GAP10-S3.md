### GAP10-S3-01: The own-block contextual field is not well posed under the stated regularity hypotheses
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/15_contextual_coupling.tex:257-262 (own-block remark), :72 (the C^3 hypothesis), :220-221 (`ctx:f:thm:ode`); chapters/03_dynamics.tex:336-357 (`prop:dyn-per-agent`, `def:dyn-per-agent-flow`)
**Evidence:** The remark says that adding the contextual force to the own-block gradients "gives a locally well-posed vector field under the same local regularity assumptions". The assumptions on record are those of `ctx:f:thm:ode`: a smooth positive Fisher metric, "C^3 finite local potentials", and `ctx:f:ass:branch`. At 15:72 the C^3 hypothesis is placed on the sum ("Assume every term is finite and E_v is C^3"), not on individual terms, and not on the per-agent free energies of `prop:dyn-per-agent`, whose self costs carry the weights beta_ii and which contain a persistent record term that E_v does not contain (03:435). The own-block field needs the own-block gradient A_iv, as a function of all laws and rows at the context, to be locally Lipschitz. A C^3 sum does not give that, and no lens checked it (D19-CTXF lists it as not checked; XC3-SCOPE's Question 2 checked only the non-gradient statement).

Counterexample A, inside the chapter 15 structure (singleton source sets are allowed at 15:56 and 15:218; one context or the trivial group, so the branch hypothesis is vacuous). Take q_i = N(m_i, 1), R_1 = N(1, 1), R_2 = N(-2, 1), and law-dependent translation channels nu_12 = N(m_2 + sigma, 1), nu_21 = N(m_1 + sigma, 1) with sigma = |m_1|^(7/4). Then D_12 + D_21 = (m_1 - m_2)^2 + sigma^2 and E_v = (m_1 - 1)^2/2 + (m_2 + 2)^2/2 + (m_1 - m_2)^2 + |m_1|^(7/2), which is C^3: in s06 Part A the identity has sympy residual 0 on both sides of m_1 = 0, the right limits of the first three derivatives of |m|^(7/2) are 0, 0, 0, and sigma itself is C^1 but not C^2 (right limit of sigma'' is infinite). The receiver split F_1 = KL(q_1 || R_1) + D_12 has dF_1/dm_1 = (m_1 - 1) + (m_1 - m_2 - sigma)(1 - sigma') with sigma' = (7/4) sgn(m_1)|m_1|^(3/4). The own-block field vanishes at (0, -1); in the coordinates x = m_1, y = m_2 + 1 its components are -2x + y + sigma + (1 + x - y - sigma) sigma'(x) and -2y + x + sigma. Difference quotients of the first component at x = 1e-2, 1e-4, 1e-6, 1e-8 are 3.62, 15.5, 53.3 and 173, tracking (7/4)x^(-1/4). Non-uniqueness: on W = {0 < x <= delta, |y| <= x} the first component is at least x^(3/4) and W is forward invariant for small delta, so every solution from (x_0, 0) with small x_0 > 0 satisfies x(t) >= (t/4)^4 while in W; by Arzela-Ascoli a subsequence converges as x_0 -> 0+ to a solution from (0, -1) with the same lower bound, distinct from the constant solution. Numerically m_1(2) = 0.19749, 0.14183, 0.13726 for x_0 = 1e-4, 1e-8, 1e-12 (DOP853, rtol 1e-12). The reciprocal flow of E_v from the same point has a C^2 field and is unique.

Counterexample C, inside the chapter 3 per-agent structure (self sources, unit temperature, evolving rows, law-dependent evaluators and channels, all admitted at 15:56-72 and 03:320-334). With q_1 = N(m_1, 1), q_2 = N(0, 1) fixed, phi(m_1) = -sgn(m_1)|m_1|^(7/4), s_11 = s_12 = 1 + m_1^2 and s_22 = s_21 = 1, choose the evaluator and channel means so that the four Gaussian KL costs are c_11 = phi + s_11, D_12 = phi + s_12, c_22 = -phi + s_22 and D_21 = -phi + s_21 (each right side is positive near m_1 = 0, so each is a KL between unit-variance Gaussians). Because each row sums to one, F_1 + F_2 equals 2 + m_1^2 plus the row entropies, which is C^infinity (sympy residual 0 on both sides). Agent 1's own-block force is dF_1/dm_1 = phi' + 2 m_1 with phi' = -(7/4)|m_1|^(3/4), independent of the rows, and the row cost differences c_11 - D_12, c_22 - D_21 are smooth, so the row equations are Lipschitz. From m_1 = 0 both the constant solution and a solution with m_1(2) close to 0.094 exist (limits from x_0 = 1e-8, 1e-12: 0.09611, 0.09384). Since F_1 + F_2 is C^infinity, any regularity hypothesis placed on the total holds (03:357 identifies the total with a potential of reciprocal form; XC3-SCOPE-08 questions that identification, and the counterexample does not depend on it), while the per-agent field is not unique. The remark therefore transfers a regularity conclusion from the reciprocal potential to the per-agent free energies without proof, which is the transfer the three-objective rule excludes.
**Fix:** Replace "under the same local regularity assumptions" by "if each per-agent free energy F_iv, including its persistent record term, is C^2 jointly in all laws and interior rows of its context on the chosen chart (C^3 if a C^2 field is wanted), then ...". In `ctx:f:thm:ode`, note that C^2 local potentials already give a C^1 field, hence local uniqueness and C^1 dependence on initial data; the C^3 hypothesis is stronger than the proof uses.
**Falsifies:** A reading of 15:72 or of `ctx:f:thm:ode` that imposes C^3 on every term (the text says "every term is finite and E_v is C^3"), or a proof that per-agent free energies inherit joint C^2 regularity from a C^3 reciprocal potential; counterexample C excludes the latter.

### GAP10-S3-02: Infinitesimal identifiability does not supply an isometric target for noncompact groups; the free SL(K) link locus is not proper
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/16_link_base_transport.tex:286 ("Suppose Z is smooth and J_Z is injective on the region considered"; "a full-rank vector collection is not necessary when the group action on links is itself identifiable"), :288-304 (`ibt:alignment`), :306-311 ("Its invariant metric is an additional comparison choice"), :346-348; companion/c16_link_base_transport.tex:149-158
**Evidence:** `ibt:alignment` assumes a Riemannian target with an isometric G-action, so the proposition is correct. The surrounding text presents identifiability as what link-only alignment needs and the target metric as one more choice to be made. For a noncompact group that choice can be impossible even where every isotropy group is trivial. Take G = SL(2) acting by conjugation on pairs in SL(2)^2, D = diag(2, 1/2), z = (D, I + E_12) and z' = (D, I + E_21). Both have injective J (singular values 2.06, 2, 1.5; s04 part A). For t -> 0 put z_n = (D, [[1 + t^2, 1], [t^2, 1]]), which lies in SL(2)^2 and converges to z, and g_n = diag(t, 1/t). Then g_n z_n g_n^{-1} -> z', while g_n z g_n^{-1} = (D, I + t^2 E_12) -> (D, I) (distances 1.4e-6 at t = 1e-3). Suppose an invariant Riemannian metric existed on an invariant open set U containing z and z', for instance the open set on which J is injective, with distance d. Then d(g_n z, z') <= d(g_n z, g_n z_n) + d(g_n z_n, z') = d(z, z_n) + d(g_n z_n, z') -> 0, so g_n z -> z' in U and hence in the ambient space, contradicting g_n z -> (D, I) != z'. The obstruction is non-properness, not isotropy; D21-BT-02 treated only points whose stabilizer acts with noncompact image. The positive side is short. On link tuples irreducible over R the action of PGL(K) is proper: if z_n -> z and g_n z_n -> z' with z, z' irreducible, normalize ||g_n|| = 1 and pass to a limit g != 0 with g z = z' g (entrywise in the tuple); ker g is invariant under the tuple z, so g is invertible and the classes [g_n] converge. The same argument shows that orbits of irreducible tuples are closed in the ambient matrix space. Proper actions carry invariant Riemannian metrics (Palais, Ann. Math. 73 (1961) 295-323; locator not re-verified), and GAP10-S3-03 constructs a canonical one.
**Fix:** After `ibt:alignment` add: "For noncompact G, injectivity of J_Z does not provide the isometric target. An invariant Riemannian metric exists on any invariant open set on which the action is proper, for example on link tuples irreducible over R when G = SL(K); the free locus need not be proper." Qualify the sentence at 16:286 to identifiability on a proper stratum.
**Falsifies:** An SL(2)-invariant Riemannian metric on an invariant open subset of SL(2)^2 containing (D, I + E_12) and (D, I + E_21).

### GAP10-S3-03: A canonical invariant metric for SL(K) link targets, and a theorem: its alignment selector always preserves a fiber metric
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/16_link_base_transport.tex:286-311 (`ibt:alignment` and the example metric at 306-310), :222 (curvature restriction of the rectangular rule), :256-281 (`ibt:group`); D21-BT-01, D21-BT-02 and D21-BT script s05b
**Evidence:** Construction. For a link tuple z = (K_e) with edge weights w_e put f_z(P) = sum_e w_e Tr(P^{-1} K_e P K_e^T) on positive matrices modulo scale. Then f_{h^{-1} z h}(h^{-1} P h^{-T}) = f_z(P); along a symmetric-space geodesic P = L e^{t sigma} L^T the second derivative is sum_e w_e ||[sigma, B_e]||^2 with B_e = L^{-1} K_e L; on the absolutely irreducible locus (common commutant equal to the scalars) this is positive for every traceless symmetric sigma != 0, and the minimum is attained because the orbit is closed (GAP10-S3-02), since f_z(P) is the squared norm over the orbit. So the Kempf-Ness point S_z is unique up to scale, smooth by the implicit-function theorem, and equivariant, S_{h^{-1} z h} proportional to h^{-1} S_z h^{-T}. The metric G_z(dz, dz) = sum_e w_e Tr(S_z^{-1} dK_e S_z dK_e^T) is positive, invariant under GL(K) conjugation and independent of any reference inner product (it is scale invariant in S). s04 part B, exact Newton solver: equivariance residual 3.7e-16 (K = 2) and 2.4e-15 (K = 3), invariance 3.6e-15, moment map at the balanced representative 2.4e-15; along (A, E_12 + eps E_21) the condition number of S is 1/eps to four digits, so the metric blows up toward the reducible locus, as GAP10-S3-02 requires.

Theorem. With this self-balanced metric the solution of `ibt:eq:augmented-normal` satisfies dS + aS + S a^T = 0, so transports are isometries of S^{-1} and holonomy is compact (in SO(K) on loops in a balanced gauge). Proof: in a gauge with S = I along the base the moment map mu = sum_e w_e [B_e, B_e^T] vanishes identically. In the Frobenius product ad_xi is skew-adjoint for skew xi and ad_eta self-adjoint for symmetric eta, so 2<xi.z, eta.z> = <z, [eta, xi].z>, and <z, zeta.z> = Tr(zeta mu) = 0 for every symmetric zeta, in particular for the symmetric [eta, xi]; skew and symmetric orbit directions are orthogonal. Differentiating <z, eta.z> = 0 along the base gives <dz, eta.z> = 0. The symmetric part of the normal equation is therefore K_ss a_s = 0, and freeness makes K_ss positive, so a_s = 0: a is skew in the balanced gauge. s04 part C: the compatibility residual is 2.1e-10 and 8.4e-11 for the self-balanced metric and 2.2e-10 for the self-balanced metric with weights (1, 10), but 2.7e-2 and 1.6e-3 for the invariant metric with weights (1, 10) dressed by the Kempf-Ness point of weights (1, 1), whose loop holonomy has the real eigenvalues 1.000727 and 0.999274 (noncompact). Part F: on a loop where det[K_1, K_2] > 0 the self-balanced holonomy is a nontrivial rotation, eigenvalues 0.999977 +- 0.006763i with modulus 1 and isometry defect 1.5e-10. The rectangular rule is the case of the X target, whose Kempf-Ness point is H_X; this is why `ibt:eq:metric-compatibility` holds. The augmented metric at 16:306-310 dresses the link term by H_X, which is not the Kempf-Ness point of the combined norm, and that is the source of the hyperbolic holonomy in D21-BT s05b.

SL(2) pairs. Writing a balanced link as B = s I + lambda T + a J (T traceless symmetric), mu = 0 reduces to a_1 T_1 + a_2 T_2 = 0. So either both balanced links are symmetric, which forces det[K_1, K_2] >= 0, or their traceless symmetric parts are parallel, which forces det[K_1, K_2] = -4(lambda_1 a_2 - lambda_2 a_1)^2 <= 0; det[K_1, K_2] is a conjugation invariant. On the second branch the Frobenius metric is 2(ds^2 + dlambda^2 + da^2) + 2(sum_i lambda_i^2) dtheta^2 with theta the orbit angle and no cross terms, so the horizontal distribution {dtheta = 0} is integrable and the connection is flat. On the first branch the connection form is proportional to (lambda_1^2 dtheta_1 + lambda_2^2 dtheta_2)/(lambda_1^2 + lambda_2^2), the rotation-shape connection of a pair of symmetric matrices, with curvature d(lambda_2^2/(lambda_1^2 + lambda_2^2)) wedge d(theta_2 - theta_1), generically nonzero. s04 part E at 14 random fields: at the 9 fields on the first branch the balanced skew parts are at most 7.1e-16 and |F_12| ranges from 4.0e-3 to 7.0; at the 5 fields on the second branch the parallelism defect is at most 1.9e-16 and |F_12| <= 6.7e-6, the finite-difference floor.
**Fix:** Add after `ibt:alignment`: "For G = SL(K) or GL(K) acting by conjugation on link tuples, the Kempf-Ness point S_z gives a canonical invariant target metric on the absolutely irreducible locus. With it the selected connection preserves S^{-1}, so its holonomy is compact; noncompact holonomy requires a dressing that is not the Kempf-Ness point of the metric's own norm." Cite Kempf and Ness (Lecture Notes in Math. 732, 1979) and, for real groups, Richardson and Slodowy (J. London Math. Soc. 42, 1990); locators from memory, not re-verified. The proof above is self-contained and does not need them. Difficulty: low for the statement and proof; moderate for a holonomy classification at K >= 3.
**Falsifies:** An absolutely irreducible SL(2) pair field for which the self-balanced selector violates dS + aS + S a^T = 0 beyond discretization error, or a balanced SL(2) pair with det[K_1, K_2] < 0 whose traceless symmetric parts are not parallel.

### GAP10-S3-04: An isolated isotropy jump carries SU(2) holonomy (-1)^w; the selector cannot be continued across a jump of odd winding

**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/16_link_base_transport.tex:288-304 (`ibt:alignment`), :313-336 (the SO(3) example), :338-350 (`ibt:invisible` and remark); appendices/base_connection.tex:71-74 (`open:conn-derived`); D21-BT-04(c); 2026-10-22 L02-GEOM-03
**Evidence:** Take two links Q_1 = R_{e_1}(alpha), Q_2 = R_{u(c)}(beta) with the product bi-invariant metric, u(0) = e_1, and an isolated transversal zero at c = 0 of the component of u orthogonal to e_1; there the common stabilizer jumps from trivial to SO(2) and the locked form loses rank. By the reduction of D21-BT-07 the connection in that gauge is a^ad = -(w_beta/(w_alpha + w_beta)) e_3-hat dtheta, bounded when the inter-axis angle theta is Lipschitz. Consequences. For SO(3), if a gauge f continuous on the disk made f.a continuous at the jump, then phi = f h^{-1} would have bounded phi^{-1} dphi on the punctured disk, so phi would be null-homotopic on small circles and f would have class w mod 2 = 1 there, which contradicts continuity of f on the disk; the singularity is removable only by a gauge transformation that is discontinuous at the jump. Difficulty: low for the two-link SO(3)/SU(2) statement (the proof above); a classification for general compact G, more links and higher-codimension isotropy is open.
**Falsifies:** An isolated parallel-axis point of odd winding at which the SU(2) small-loop holonomy tends to +I.

### GAP10-S3-05: The global statement behind 16:160 is true; it can be stated as a proposition with a nontrivial realization and the Narasimhan-Ramanan converse

**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/16_link_base_transport.tex:160, :222 ("No universality claim for this particular real message rule is made"); companion/c16_link_base_transport.tex:64
**Evidence:** Proposition, proved here. In each stereographic chart X = g^{-1} f^T (f the coordinate frame, g = f^T f), Q_X = f, P_X = I - n n^T, a equals the Levi-Civita form g^{-1} f^T df, the two charts glue with a_S - (h^{-1} a_N h + h^{-1} dh) = 0 exactly on the overlap, F_uv = (4/(1 + u^2 + v^2)^2) [[0, 1], [-1, 0]], the Pfaffian density equals the area density, its integral is 4 pi, and the Euler number is 2, so E is not trivial; here r = 3 < K + n = 4. Math. 83 (1961) 563-572 and 85 (1963) 223-231; statement and locators from memory, not re-verified), which I expect to give the real orthogonal case by complexification and restriction to the real part, as sketched, but did not check against the source.

## Extensions and improvements

The own-block remark can be turned from a negative statement into a criterion. If at each context the per-agent free energies admit an exact potential Phi_v in the sense of Monderer and Shapley, that is d_{theta_i} F_iv = d_{theta_i} Phi_v for every agent (on a simply connected chart, equivalently symmetric mixed derivatives d_{theta_j} d_{theta_i} F_iv = (d_{theta_i} d_{theta_j} F_jv)^T), then the own-block contextual field is -grad_G(sum_v m_v Phi_v + lambda C_*) and dissipates that function at the rate of its squared metric norm, with the same branch caveats as `ctx:f:thm:ode`. The simplest instance is Gaussian location laws with identity channels and fixed symmetric peer rows, beta_ij = beta_ji, where Phi = sum_i beta_ii |m_i - rho_i|^2/2 + sum_{i<j} beta_ij |m_i - m_j|^2/2. Chapter 3 already cites the cross-derivative criterion (03:370); applying it at 15:257 costs a paragraph. Difficulty: low.

For noncompact structure groups the Kempf-Ness dressing of GAP10-S3-03 is a natural default comparison metric: it is defined by the link data alone, needs no reference inner product, and its selector has compact holonomy. Combined with D21-BT-01 this gives a holonomy dictionary for chapter 16: the rectangular rule and every self-balanced alignment are metric connections, while noncompact holonomy needs a deliberately unbalanced dressing such as the augmented H_X metric of 16:306-310. For SL(2) pairs the dictionary is complete: flat on det[K_1, K_2] < 0 and rotation-shape curvature on det[K_1, K_2] > 0. The next computations are the balanced sets of SL(3) pairs and of SL(2) triples, where the stratification by commutator invariants should again decide flatness; and the Gaussian law-dressed target of D21-BT's extensions, whose Kempf-Ness point is the covariance itself. Difficulty: moderate.

The Z_2 obstruction of GAP10-S3-04 gives `open:conn-derived` a concrete topological component. A generic two-link field over a two-dimensional base meets the parallel-axis set at isolated points (codimension two), each with winding w = +-1, so any continuation scheme must either allow Z_2 defects or restrict to a lifted group in which they appear as holonomy -1. A natural test is a spinor-valued sector transforming under SU(2) with the same links: its parallel transport around a jump flips sign, which is an observable for the agents' spinor data and a candidate holonomy-sensitive record of the kind requested by 2026-10-22 R9. Difficulty: low for the two-link statement, moderate for a classification by isotropy type.

The global message construction (GAP10-S3-05) shows that nothing in chapter 16 needs a trivial bundle: every rank-K bundle over an n-dimensional base is reached with r <= K + n message columns, and the classifying map c -> W(c) in Gr_K(R^r) is the Grassmannian sigma-model field of D21-BT's extensions. If label monodromy is allowed (agent labels permuted around a loop of contexts), the message bundle is flat with permutation holonomy and `ibt:eq:message-gamma` with a locally zero Gamma covers it, since permutation matrices are orthogonal. Difficulty: low.

The joint decomposition of GAP10-S3-07 sharpens a modeling choice the chapter makes implicitly: geometric inheritance is required for the history-averaged connection a(X) = -E <s, l_m>/E ||l_m||^2, not branch by branch. D18-LP-01's open residual (history-dependent germ-local phases under joint equality) is the question whether E_H[I^H((a_H - k)^2 - k^2)] = 0 can hold on a bump class; the identity is now checked numerically with history-dependent phases, so the remaining step is purely the variational argument sketched there.
