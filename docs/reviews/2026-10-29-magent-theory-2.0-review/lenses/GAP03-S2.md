### GAP03-S2-03: form:full-flow asserts uniqueness among "positive dominated regular solutions" without defining them; the clause is true for an explicit class, and its proof omits two steps

**Location:** chapters/11_conditional_formation.tex:111-113 ("These solutions are global and regular on the stated family"), :170-175 (uniqueness sentence), :197 (form:mean-field, "regular dominated family"); chapters/03_dynamics.tex:111, :221-232 (def:dyn-chart-flow, prop:dyn-chart-flow)

The declared chart framework does not cover this flow either: log(W_2(alpha, P)/lambda) is unbounded for every probability reference lambda as soon as (alpha, P) moves, so no L^infinity chart of def:dyn-chart-flow contains the Wishart curve, and prop:dyn-chart-flow does not apply. This is the regularity rule of AUTHORING (well-posedness and uniqueness of every flow beyond finite tables) applied to the chapter's base flow. A definition under which the clause holds, and its proof (focus question 2):

Definition. A regular solution on [0, T) with initial laws q_i(0) is a family of Lebesgue densities q_i(t, X) > 0 on S^{++}_2 such that (R1) each q_i(t) is a probability density with E|X|, E|log det X| and E|log q_i| finite; (R2) t -> M_q(t) is locally integrable; (R3) for almost every X, t -> log q_i(t, X) is absolutely continuous and d/dt log q_i = -h_i + E_{q_i} h_i for almost every t, where h_i = 2 log q_i - 2 c_0 log det X + Tr[(I + C_q(M_q(t))) X] and t -> E_{q_i} h_i is locally integrable.

Theorem. If q_i(0) = W_2(alpha_i(0), P_i(0)), every regular solution is the Wishart solution: q_i(t) = W_2(alpha_i(t), P_i(t)), where (alpha, P) is the unique solution of alpha_i' = -2(alpha_i - alpha_0), P_i' = -2 P_i + I + C_q(sum_j alpha_j P_j^{-1}). On the common-precision family this is the solution of form:full-flow, global in time.

Proof. Put C(t) = C_q(M_q(t)); it is locally integrable by (R2), since ||Lambda_q^{-1}|| <= 1. For almost every X, (R3) is a scalar linear equation, so log q_i(t, X) = e^{-2t} log q_i(0, X) + (1 - e^{-2t}) c_0 log det X - Tr(Chat(t) X) + k_i(t), with Chat(t) = integral_0^t e^{-2(t-s)} (I + C(s)) ds and k_i a function of t alone. With Wishart initial data this is det(X)^{a_i(t) - 3/2} e^{-Tr(Pi_i(t) X)} up to normalization, with a_i(t) = alpha_0 + e^{-2t}(alpha_i(0) - alpha_0) > 1/2 and Pi_i(t) = e^{-2t} P_i(0) + Chat(t). Step omitted in the manuscript (1): normalizability forces Pi_i(t) to be positive definite. If Pi had an eigenvalue pi_2 <= 0, rotate it to diag(pi_1, pi_2) and use the chart x_11 = u, x_12 = sqrt(u) z, x_22 = v + z^2 of form:addition; the v integral of v^{a - 3/2} e^{-pi_2 v} diverges. Hence q_i(t) = W_2(a_i(t), Pi_i(t)). Step omitted (2): closure. M_q(t) = sum_j a_j(t) Pi_j(t)^{-1} is then continuous, so C is continuous, Pi_i is continuously differentiable, and Pi_i' = -2 Pi_i + I + C_q(sum_j a_j Pi_j^{-1}). Thus (a, Pi) solves the closed finite-dimensional system, whose field is smooth on {a > 1/2, Pi positive definite}, and Picard-Lindelof uniqueness gives (a, Pi) = (alpha, P). Domination (integrable envelopes for differentiating E_q along the solution) is not needed for uniqueness; it is needed for the tangency computation and the energy identity. s2 checks symbolically that the integrating-factor formula solves the pointwise equation, that Wishart data stay Wishart with P(t) = e^{-2t} P(0) + Chat(t) and P' = -2P + I + C(t) (residuals 0), and that the v integral diverges for pi_2 <= 0.
**Fix:** Insert the definition and the two steps into the proof of form:full-flow, replace "positive dominated regular" at 11:173 and "regular dominated" at 11:197 by the defined class, and add at 03:221-223 that the Wishart chapter uses a finite-dimensional invariant family together with this uniqueness argument, not an L^infinity chart.
**Falsifies:** A regular solution in the sense of (R1) to (R3) with Wishart initial data that is not Wishart at some t > 0; the proof excludes it.

## Extensions and improvements

The integrating-factor argument of GAP03-S2-03 gives more than uniqueness. For arbitrary positive initial densities with finite first moments, every regular solution satisfies log q_i(t) = e^{-2t} log q_i(0) + (1 - e^{-2t}) c_0 log det X - Tr(Chat(t) X) + k_i(t), with one coefficient Chat common to all agents. Writing m_i(t, Chat) for the mean of the normalized density proportional to q_i(0)^{e^{-2t}} det(X)^{(1 - e^{-2t}) c_0} e^{-Tr(Chat X)}, the whole density flow of form:energy is the three-dimensional nonautonomous ODE Chat' = -2 Chat + I + C_q(sum_i m_i(t, Chat)), Chat(0) = 0. Since Lambda_q is at least I, I + C_q is at least (2 - kappa alpha_0) I, so for kappa alpha_0 <= 2 the coefficient stays positive semidefinite and integrability reduces to an envelope condition on q_i(0) (for instance q_i(0) bounded by a multiple of a Wishart density), and Lipschitz dependence of m_i on Chat follows by dominated convergence; for kappa alpha_0 > 2 an eigenvalue barrier like the one in form:full-flow is needed. This would make the invariance and common-clock statements of form:full-flow and form:mean-field statements about the full law space, with the Wishart family as the subfamily closed under the sum map, and it makes the explicit geometric-interpolation remark at 11:179 exact with a moving target. Difficulty: low for the reduction, moderate for global existence when kappa alpha_0 > 2.

form:parent holds on a larger invariant family than the one it is stated on. For a fixed partition, the family on which precisions are common within each block, but may differ across blocks, is invariant, because form:precision-difference keeps within-block differences at zero. The parents W_2(A_I, P_I) are exact, M = sum_I A_I P_I^{-1}, and each parent precision follows P_I' = -2 P_I + I + C_q, the velocity of each of its children; the proof of GAP03-S2-04 applies without change. In the four-agent selection this makes the first stage exact on a family with three additional precision coordinates per sector, and reduces form:approximate-open to one inexact join, the root join of two first-stage parents whose precisions differ by e^{-2 t_2}(P_L(0) - P_R(0)) at the join time t_2. That is the smallest concrete instance of the open problem, and D15-WISH's sketch of a fourth-order relative-entropy defect for moment matching applies to it directly. Difficulty: low for the exact part, moderate for the defect bound.

GAP03-S2-07 suggests stating the inheritance theorem at its natural generality. Let an exponential family on a convex cone closed under addition carry a declared common subfamily on which the law of an independent block sum is again in the family, with natural parameters given by an affine map L; take receiver-independent targets that read only the total mean moment, and parent targets equal to L applied to copies of the fine targets. Lemma A, Lemma B and the chain rule then give the exact same-time parent flow, type closure, and the response corollary. Instances are the Wishart family on SPD(n) for every n and the gamma family at common rate (shape additive), and Gaussian location families with common covariance (natural mean parameter averaged); Poisson laws are excluded (s7). Recording this would separate what the chapter needs from Wishart laws (a cone of matrix contents with a rotation action for form:geometry) from what it needs from exponential families (Bregman linearity of Fisher-Rao relative-entropy flows), and would tell later constructions which families can carry exact additive inheritance. Difficulty: low.

For the null-flow control, the cleanest repair is the one D15-WISH proposed: compare the partition sequence of the construction with that of the same construction at zero coupling, with all rows, self channels and the selector unchanged. Chapter 11 fails that test exactly, for every row convention, by form:kappa-independence (i) and (ii). If the null-flow clause is kept, GAP03-S2-01 shows that its convention must be declared, and GAP03-S2-02 that the post-handoff null must be declared too; with convention (b) the reversal and structural changes of s5 and s6 are a property of the self-row reweighting, which for this construction is present at zero coupling and therefore says nothing about interaction. A useful small theorem here: under (b), at kappa = 0 and P(0) = I, each agent obeys the autonomous equation b' = -(1 + beta(b)) b with beta(b) = 1/(1 + 3 e^{F(b)/T}) and F(b) = KL(W_2(alpha_0 + b, I) ‖ W_2(alpha_0, I)), so the null contraction is a common nonlinear map of each initial deviation; reversals and changed blocks occur exactly where this nonlinear map changes the order of centroid distances, which can be decided by comparing the null meeting times of adjacent pairs as functions of the initial deviations. Difficulty: low.
