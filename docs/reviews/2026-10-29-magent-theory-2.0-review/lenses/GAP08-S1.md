### GAP08-S1-01: The filter on general state spaces: statement, measurability of the terminal conditional law, and what fails off countable alphabets
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** supplements/s04_inheritance.tex:61-112 (`sinh:filter`, `sinh:filter-law`, `sinh:next-record`, `sinh:filter-update`, the Markov-process sentence at :95-97 and the zero-probability remark at :109-112); chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1012-1027 (memory route and information state); appendices/interacting_memory.tex:52
**Evidence:** `sinh:filter` is stated and proved for a finite chain, and the proof uses finiteness in three places: every retained history is an atom, so the conditional law is the ratio $f_{h_n}/f_{h_n}\mathbf 1$; the update is a ratio of vectors; and the remark at :109-112 relies on zero-probability histories being exactly the impossible ones. Chapter 5 invokes the construction for "a declared stochastic process" (05:1013-1015) and as an information state on "the simplex of laws on $\mathsf S$" (05:1021-1027), and the configuration spaces of the theory are standard Borel spaces of law tuples. I reconstructed the general statement and its proof.

Theorem. Let $\mathsf S,\mathsf Z$ be standard Borel, $T_n:\mathsf S\rightsquigarrow\mathsf S$ Markov kernels, $R:\mathsf S\to\mathsf Z$ Borel, and $X$ a Markov chain with $X_0\sim p_0$ and $P(X_{n+1}\in\cdot\mid X_{0:n})=T_n(X_n,\cdot)$; put $Z_n=R(X_n)$ and $H_n=(Z_0,\ldots,Z_n)$. Give $\Prob(\mathsf S)$ the $\sigma$-algebra generated by the evaluations $p\mapsto p(B)$, which makes it standard Borel (Kechris 1995, Section 17.E, from memory).
(a) There is a kernel $\pi_n:\mathsf Z^{n+1}\rightsquigarrow\mathsf S$ with $\pi_n(H_n,\cdot)=P(X_n\in\cdot\mid H_n)$ almost surely, unique up to null sets of the law of $H_n$; $\Pi_n=\pi_n(H_n,\cdot)$ is a $\sigma(H_n)$-measurable random element of $\Prob(\mathsf S)$.
(b) There are kernels $\mu_n:\Prob(\mathsf S)\rightsquigarrow\mathsf Z$ and $U_n:\Prob(\mathsf S)\times\mathsf Z\rightsquigarrow\mathsf S$ such that for every $p$ and bounded Borel $f$, $\int p(dx)\int T_n(x,dy)f(y,Ry)=\int\mu_n(p,dz)\int U_n(p,z,dy)f(y,z)$.
(c) $P(Z_{n+1}\in\cdot\mid H_n)=\mu_n(\Pi_n,\cdot)$ and $\Pi_{n+1}=U_n(\Pi_n,Z_{n+1},\cdot)$ almost surely.
(d) For every $k$ and Borel $C\subseteq\mathsf Z^k$, $P((Z_{n+1},\ldots,Z_{n+k})\in C\mid H_n)=\Phi_{n,k}(\Pi_n,C)$ almost surely, where $\Phi_{n,k}(p,\cdot)$ is the law of $(RX_{n+1},\ldots,RX_{n+k})$ when $X_n\sim p$.
(e) $(\Pi_n)$ is a Markov chain on $\Prob(\mathsf S)$ for the filtration $\sigma(H_n)$, with kernel $Q_n(p,B)=\int\mathbf 1_B(U_n(p,z,\cdot))\mu_n(p,dz)$.

Proof. (a) is the existence of regular conditional laws on standard Borel spaces; $\Pi_n$ is measurable because each evaluation $\pi_n(\cdot,B)\circ H_n$ is. (b) The map $p\mapsto\nu_p$, $\nu_p(D)=\int p(dx)\int T_n(x,dy)\mathbf 1_D(y,Ry)$, is a kernel from $\Prob(\mathsf S)$ to $\mathsf S\times\mathsf Z$ (measurable on product sets, then by a monotone-class argument), and the disintegration theorem for kernels into a Borel space gives $\mu_n$ and $U_n$. (c) For Borel $A\subseteq\mathsf Z^{n+1}$, $C\subseteq\mathsf Z$, $B\subseteq\mathsf S$, the Markov property and conditioning on $H_n$ give $P(H_n\in A,Z_{n+1}\in C,X_{n+1}\in B)=\E[\mathbf 1_A(H_n)\nu_{\Pi_n}(B\times C)]=\E[\mathbf 1_A(H_n)\int_C\mu_n(\Pi_n,dz)U_n(\Pi_n,z,B)]$. Taking $B=\mathsf S$ gives the next-record law; then the last expression equals $\E[\mathbf 1_A(H_n)\mathbf 1_C(Z_{n+1})U_n(\Pi_n,Z_{n+1},B)]$, and a $\pi$-$\lambda$ argument over the sets $A\times C$, which generate $\sigma(H_{n+1})$, together with a countable generator of the Borel sets of $\mathsf S$, gives the update. (d) The Markov property gives $P(\text{future}\in C\mid X_{0:n})=\Phi_{n,k}(\delta_{X_n},C)$; condition on $H_n$ and use linearity of $\Phi_{n,k}$ in $p$. (e) $\E[\varphi(\Pi_{n+1})\mid H_n]=\int\varphi(U_n(\Pi_n,z,\cdot))\mu_n(\Pi_n,dz)=Q_n\varphi(\Pi_n)$, which is a function of $\Pi_n$; the tower property gives the Markov property in the natural filtration as well.

Three consequences are not visible in the finite text. For a countable $\mathsf Z$ the standard Borel hypothesis on $\mathsf S$ can be dropped: $f_{(h,z)}(B)=\int f_h(dx)T_n(x,B\cap R^{-1}\{z\})$ is the supplement's recursion with measures in place of vectors, $U_n(p,z,\cdot)=(pT_n)(\cdot\cap R^{-1}\{z\})/(pT_n)(R^{-1}\{z\})$ where the denominator is positive, and "almost surely" becomes "at every history of positive probability". For an uncountable $\mathsf Z$ (for example a real-valued retained coordinate) every history has probability zero, the update is defined only for $\mu_n(p,\cdot)$-almost every $z$, and the remark at s04:109-112 that the construction "needs no arbitrary prediction on an impossible history" has no analogue: choices of versions on null sets are unavoidable. And the Markov property (e) holds only after a jointly measurable version $U_n$ has been fixed, the requirement that appendices/interacting_memory.tex:52 already states for its own law-valued states. The doubling-map example of D10-FORM-10 ($\mathsf Z=\{0,1\}$, infinite $\mathsf S$, a filter that never concentrates) is a countable-alphabet instance of this theorem. The finite proposition itself I confirmed exactly (s3 (a) to (c), below); the general statement is proved above and was not computed.
**Fix:** Add the theorem after `sinh:filter`, with the finite proposition as its instance, and replace 05:1016-1017 ("In a finite model it is a probability vector with an exact recursive update, derived in the supplement") by a sentence covering the countable case (the same formulas with measures) and the general case (a jointly measurable disintegration, with every identity holding almost surely). Cite Kallenberg 2021 for regular conditional laws and the kernel disintegration theorem (I did not check its theorem numbers) and Kechris 1995, Section 17.E. Difficulty: low.
**Falsifies:** A Markov chain on standard Borel spaces with a Borel retention for which the conditional law of $X_n$ given $H_n$ fails to determine, almost surely, the conditional law of the future retained path given $H_n$. The proof above excludes it.

### GAP08-S1-02: The general memory-route sentences omit the Markov hypothesis that the supplement uses
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1013-1015 ("For a declared stochastic process, another is to retain the conditional law of the hidden configuration given the accessible retained history. That conditional law determines future predictions."); companion/c05_inheritance.tex:150-154 ("under a declared stochastic process, the conditional law of hidden states ... That vector determines every future response")
**Evidence:** `sinh:filter` assumes a Markov chain (s04:63), and the proof uses the Markov property at every propagation step. Without it the sentence is false. Let $X_0,X_1$ be independent fair bits, $X_{n+1}=X_{n-1}$ for $n\geq1$, and retain everything, $R=\id$. The histories $(0,0)$ and $(1,0)$ each have probability $1/4$ and the same terminal conditional law $\delta_0$ of $X_1$, yet the next record $X_2$ is $0$ after the first and $1$ after the second (s3 (e), exact enumeration). Time-dependent kernels are harmless: the proof goes through with $T_n$ in place of $T$.
**Fix:** "For a declared Markov process (time-dependent transition kernels allowed), another is to retain the conditional law of the current configuration given the accessible retained history." Make the same change in the companion.
**Falsifies:** A convention, stated somewhere in the manuscript, that the hidden configuration of every declared process is a Markov state of that process. I found none in chapter 5 or the companion.

### GAP08-S1-03: The cue update of the structured family states a vacuous finiteness condition; the condition that can fail is positivity
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** low
**Location:** appendices/conditional_memory.tex:65 ("With cue densities $\mu_a(u)$, varying $q_X$ gives $q_X(u)\propto l(o\mid u)\exp(\sum_as_a\log\mu_a(u))$, whenever the normalizer is finite")
**Evidence:** By the weighted arithmetic-geometric mean inequality, $\prod_a\mu_a(u)^{s_a}\leq\sum_as_a\mu_a(u)$ pointwise for $s$ in the simplex, so the normalizer $N(s)=\int l(o\mid u)\prod_a\mu_a(u)^{s_a}du$ satisfies $N(s)\leq\sum_as_aZ_a(o)$, which is finite because $\hat\pi_aZ_a(o)\leq Z(o)<\infty$. Finiteness therefore always holds. What can fail is $N(s)>0$. If two labels with $s_a>0$ have cue densities with disjoint supports, the product vanishes identically, every $q_X$ has $\KL(q_X\Vert\mu_a)=\infty$ for one of them, and the structured free energy is $+\infty$ for every $q_X$; then no update exists. In general $N(s)>0$ holds exactly when some $q_X$ has finite structured free energy at that $s$: the candidate $q_X\propto l\prod_a\mu_a^{s_a}$ then has value $\KL(s\Vert\hat\pi)-\log N(s)$. s5 (c): on a four-point cue alphabet, $N=0.1698\leq\sum_as_aZ_a(o)=0.1768$; with $\mu_1,\mu_2$ supported on $\{0,1\}$ and $\{2,3\}$ and $s=(1/2,1/2)$, the weighted product is zero at every point. The label update of `cmem:product-label` assigns $s_a=0$ to every label whose cue law does not dominate $q_X$, so an alternating scheme started on such a face stays on it.
**Fix:** "whenever the normalizer is positive; it is always finite, being at most $\sum_as_aZ_a(o)$". Add that, for $s$ charging labels whose cue supports have no common part of positive $l(o\mid\cdot)$-weighted measure, the structured family has infinite free energy.
**Falsifies:** A point $s$ of the simplex with $N(s)=\infty$ while every $Z_a(o)$ is finite. The inequality excludes it.

### GAP08-S1-04: The restricted routing equivalence does not need a Gaussian receiver
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** low
**Location:** supplements/s03_variation.tex:35-64 (`prop:sup-row-equivalence`, `eq:sup-row-constant`, `eq:sup-covariance-difference`, and the setup at :35); AUTHORING.md:29
**Evidence:** The likelihood score $C_j$ depends on the receiver only through its mean $a$ and covariance $V$, because `prop:sup-residual-cost` holds for every joint law with finite second moments (exact check at a non-Gaussian seven-point law below). For any receiver law $p$ in the comparison space with finite second moments and finite differential entropy $H(p)$ and a Gaussian source $\mathcal N(b_j,W)$, $\KL(p\Vert\mathcal N(b_j,W))=-H(p)+c_W+\tfrac12[(a-b_j)^TW^{-1}(a-b_j)+\Tr(W^{-1}V)]$. With $R=TW$ this gives
$$C_j-\frac{\KL(p\Vert\mathcal N(b_j,W))}{T}=c_{TW}+\frac d{2T}+\frac{H(p)-c_W}{T},$$
which does not depend on $j$; for Gaussian $p$, $H(p)=\tfrac12\log\det(2\pi eV)$ and the display reduces to `eq:sup-row-constant`. For an affine receiver family $X=a+M\varepsilon$ with a fixed standardized $\varepsilon$ of finite entropy, $H(p)=H(\varepsilon)+\tfrac12\log\det V$, so `eq:sup-covariance-difference` holds verbatim. s7: receiver uniform on a parallelogram ($\varepsilon$ uniform on $[-\sqrt3,\sqrt3]^2$, $H(p)=\log12+\log|\det M|$), three Gaussian sources with a common $W$, $T=0.7$. The three values of $C_j-\KL_j/T$ agree with the predicted constant to $7.9\times10^{-31}$, the two row vectors agree to 12 digits, and the directional derivative along $dM$ equals $\Tr(V^{-1}dV)/(2T)=1.09422492401216$ for each source. The Gaussian constant of `eq:sup-row-constant` evaluated at the same $V$ is off by $(H(p)-H(\mathcal N(a,V)))/T=-0.504$, so the general statement is a different identity, not a relabeling. The hypotheses that are used: Gaussian sources with one common held covariance, $R=TW$, zero record, and zero visible cross covariance $A\Sigma_{ij}B^T=0$ (independence is sufficient). The zero record is needed: for $o\neq0$ the difference acquires $-(a-b_j)^TW^{-1}o/T$, which depends on $j$.
**Fix:** State the proposition for a receiver law with finite second moments and finite entropy, Gaussian sources $\mathcal N(b_j,W)$ with a common held $W$, $R=TW$, $o=0$ and $A\Sigma_{ij}B^T=0$, with the displayed constant; give `eq:sup-row-constant` as the Gaussian-receiver instance and note that `eq:sup-covariance-difference` holds for every affine receiver family. This is what AUTHORING.md:29 asks ("Where a general-law statement is available ... state it first and give the Gaussian case as its instance").
**Falsifies:** A receiver law with finite entropy for which $C_j-\KL_j/T$ depends on $j$ under these hypotheses. The two-line derivation excludes it.

### GAP08-S1-05: geo:moving-channel assumes a consequence of its other hypotheses, and its sign remark holds within one smooth family
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/02_geometry.tex:316-332 (`geo:moving-channel`, `geo:moving-score`, the remark at :332)
**Evidence:** (a) The hypothesis "differentiation of channel normalization gives $\E[b_\theta\mid Y]=0$" is implied by the others. Apply `geo:fisher-loss` to the joint experiment with the fixed projection $(y,\bar y)\mapsto y$: its output is the input family $P_\theta$, because every channel is normalized, and its score is $\E[\ell_\theta+b_\theta\mid Y]=\ell_\theta+\E[b_\theta\mid Y]$. The input family has score $\ell_\theta$ by hypothesis, and the $L^2$ derivative in `geo:dqm` is unique, so $\E[b_\theta\mid Y]=0$ almost surely. (b) The step "conditional centering of $b_\theta$ eliminates the mixed terms" is the chain rule `geo:joint-fisher` (Zamir 1998) with the input as conditioning variable; the proof can cite it. (c) In the linear-Gaussian moving channel $Y\sim\mathcal N(\theta,1)$, $\bar Y\mid Y\sim\mathcal N(\theta Y,1)$, the output is $\mathcal N(\theta^2,\theta^2+1)$ with $I(\bar Y)=\theta^2(4\theta^2+6)/(\theta^2+1)^2$, so $I(\bar Y)-I(Y)$ equals $-1$ at $\theta=0$, $3/2$ at $\theta=1$ and $139/50$ at $\theta=3$ (s4 (b); the formula of `geo:moving-score`, with the conditional variance of the quadratic score computed from the Gaussian law of $Y$ given $\bar Y$, agrees symbolically, residual 0). The sign of the change varies along one smooth channel family, which is a sharper form of "no fixed sign" than the contrast between fixed channels and the logistic example.
**Fix:** Drop the centering hypothesis or mark it as a consequence of joint and input differentiability; cite `geo:joint-fisher` in the proof; add the linear-Gaussian example.
**Falsifies:** A joint DQM family with score $\ell_\theta+b_\theta$, over an input DQM family with score $\ell_\theta$, for which $\E[b_\theta\mid Y]\neq0$ on a set of positive probability. Uniqueness of the $L^2$ derivative excludes it.

## Panel findings re-derived in this scope (not re-raised)

D06-PRED-04 (`cmem:free-energy` hypotheses). I derived the identity from `eq:agent-free-energy` and the chain rule, and re-derived the counterexample analytically. With $U\sim\mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)$ and $l(0\mid u)=1/(u\log^2u)$ on $(0,1/e]$, $e$ on $(1/e,1)$, the substitution $s=-\log u$ gives $Z(0)=1+(e-1)=e$ and $\int_{e^{-S}}^{1/e}l\log l\,du=\log S-2+2(\log S+1)/S\to\infty$; s5 (d) matches the closed form by quadrature at $S=10,10^3,10^6,10^{12}$ (0.96310211, 4.9235708, 11.81554, 25.631021) and gives $Z(0)=e$ to 20 digits. Hence $\KL(\Pi^0\Vert\mu)=\infty$, the separated form of $f_a$ in `cmem:gibbs` is $\infty-\infty$ at the posterior, and $\mathcal F(\Pi^0;0)=-1$ under `eq:agent-free-energy`. The finding stands as stated.

D02-LINKS-07 (s02:69, the DQM sentence). Independent quadrature at 40 digits (s6) for $p(x)=e^{-|x-1|^{1/2}}/4$: $\KL(\nu\Vert(1+\epsilon)_\#\nu)/|\epsilon|$ is $5.5\times10^{-3}$, $6.9\times10^{-4}$, $8.4\times10^{-5}$, $9.8\times10^{-6}$ for $|\epsilon|=10^{-2}$ to $10^{-5}$ (both signs), so the cost is differentiable at the diagonal with derivative 0, while the truncated scale Fisher information grows by $0.558$, $0.573$, $0.575$ per factor 100 in the cutoff, approaching $\log(100)/8=0.5756$. $\KL/\epsilon^2$ increases by $0.143$ per decade, a slope of about $1/16$ in $\log(1/\epsilon)$; I did not derive this rate analytically. The finding stands.

D10-FORM-10 (ch5:1028-1032). Consistent with GAP08-S1-01: on an infinite space the filter need not concentrate even when the history determines the initial state; the statement holds on finite spaces, where the consistent sets stabilize.

D04-DYN, wording note on s03:46 ("only when $A$ is invertible"). As an identity between functionals of all laws the condition is right: if $A$ is not injective, two receiver laws with equal $A$-images are separated by the peer divergence and not by the comparison-space divergence. For particular laws it is too strong: $q_i=\mathcal N(0,I_2)$, $A=(1\ 0)$, $q_j=\mathcal N(0,1)$, $B=1$ and the channel $y\mapsto\delta_y\otimes\mathcal N(0,1)$ make both divergences zero. I agree with the note and do not raise it.

## Extensions and improvements

The general filter of GAP08-S1-01 carries over the exact handoff class of `inh:intertwining` without change. Suppose $\Lambda:\mathsf Z\rightsquigarrow\mathsf S$ satisfies $\Lambda(z,R^{-1}\{z\})=1$ and $\Lambda T=T^c\Lambda$ with $T^c=\Lambda T\Phi_R$ on standard Borel spaces. If $\Pi_n=\Lambda(Z_n,\cdot)$, part (b) of the theorem applied to $p=\Lambda(z,\cdot)$ gives $\nu_p(dy\,dz')=T^c(z,dz')\Lambda(z',dy)$, so $\mu_n(p,\cdot)=T^c(z,\cdot)$ and $U_n(p,z',\cdot)=\Lambda(z',\cdot)$ for $T^c(z,\cdot)$-almost every $z'$. By induction the filter of every preparation $\nu\Lambda$ is $\Lambda(Z_n,\cdot)$ almost surely, and the retained process is Markov with kernel $T^c$. This is the Rogers and Pitman theorem in the form needed for the theory's configuration spaces, with the parent state a point of $\mathsf Z$ rather than a law. Difficulty: low.

The general form of the routing equivalence (GAP08-S1-04) gives an exact statement of how the likelihood picture and the divergence picture differ on this block. With rows $\beta$ summing to one, $\sum_j\beta_jC_j=\sum_j\beta_j\KL(p\Vert\mathcal N(b_j,W))/T+H(p)/T+\text{const}$ for every receiver law $p$ with finite entropy. The two receiver objectives therefore differ by the receiver entropy divided by the temperature, and the covariance-force difference of `eq:sup-covariance-difference` is the entropy force $T^{-1}dH(p)$, which is $\Tr(V^{-1}dV)/(2T)$ on every affine family and shape-dependent otherwise. D04-DYN's proposed bridge for `op:dyn-three-pictures` uses the Gaussian case of this identity; the display above removes the Gaussian hypothesis on the receiver, though not on the sources. It also states, in a form the three-objective rule of AUTHORING can cite, why a conclusion about stationary covariances cannot transfer between the two pictures even when their rows agree. Difficulty: low.

The remark after `geo:moving-channel` leaves the moving deterministic statistic schematic. For a smooth family of diffeomorphisms $f_\theta$ of $\R^d$ and a DQM family $P_\theta$ with positive $C^1$ densities $p_\theta$, put $w_\theta=(\partial_\theta f_\theta^{-1})\circ f_\theta$. The output $(f_\theta)_\#P_\theta$ has density $p_\theta(f_\theta^{-1}x)|\det Df_\theta^{-1}(x)|$, and its score at $x=f_\theta(y)$ is $\ell_\theta(y)+p_\theta(y)^{-1}\nabla\cdot(p_\theta w_\theta)(y)$, the continuity-equation term replacing $b_\theta$. I checked this by hand on the location map $f_\theta(y)=y+\theta$, which gives the score $x-\theta$ of $\mathcal N(\theta,1)$, and on the scale map $f_\theta(y)=e^\theta y$, which gives $y^2-1$, the score of $\mathcal N(0,e^{2\theta})$ in $\theta$. For a non-invertible smooth statistic one conditions this sum on the output, as in `geo:fisher-loss`. The regularity needed (integrability of the transport term, DQM of the output) has to be declared, in line with the AUTHORING regularity rule; this would make the comparison invoked at 14:335 a stated result. Difficulty: moderate.
