### D10-FORM-01: The compression clause depends on an undefined child-level probe class, and the manuscript applies it under incompatible readings
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** high
**Location:** chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1387-1388, `inh:recursive-formation` (F3), (F4); :686-712, `inh:inherited-parent` (B); :536-552 (response retention D_a); :522-532 (canonical-parent remark); chapters/09_public_compression.tex:251; chapters/11_conditional_formation.tex:393; chapters/12_open_formation.tex:526; companion/c05_inheritance.tex:216-223.
**Evidence:** (F3) requires, through (B) of `inh:inherited-parent`, that for every protocol a in the declared class A_I the joint kernel of the retained response D_a(O_a) and the next retained state factor through R_I on Ω_I; in particular x ↦ law_x(D_a(O_a)) is constant on every fiber of R_I in Ω_I. (F4) requires that "some admitted child-level probe distinguishes two configurations with the same retained value". The phrase "admitted child-level probe" is defined nowhere in chapter 5, the companion or the glossary, and its three possible readings give three different clauses.

(i) The probe is a member of A_I and "distinguishes" refers to its retained response. Then (B) forbids exactly what (F4) requires, and (F3) and (F4) cannot hold together for any construction.

(ii) The probe is in A_I and "distinguishes" refers to the unretained output O_a. Then (F4) needs D_a to discard the separating information, but D_a is not among the data that `inh:inherited-parent` declares "in this order" (A_I, P_I, Ω_I, R_I), so it may be chosen after R_I, and a constant D_a makes (B) vacuous for every protocol.

(iii) The probe belongs to a separate class of pre-handoff queries that the parent need not reproduce. Chapter 11 uses this reading ("the singleton responses available before the join distinguish points of its fiber", ch11:393), and so does chapter 12 ("a pre-handoff query distinguishes its fibers", ch12:526).

Chapter 9 uses reading (i): uniform child sampling "distinguishes configurations in one fiber; but that probe is not inherited by the parent, so (F3) fails for it, and if the probe class is reduced to responses computed from the phase law, (F4) fails instead" (ch09:251). Under reading (iii), the chapter 9 retention would pass (F4) with uniform child sampling as the pre-handoff probe and a phase-law response class for the parent, which is the pattern of chapters 11 and 12. The verdicts on chapters 9, 11 and 12 therefore do not come from one definition.

Under reading (iii) the parent protocol class is a narrowing of the child-level class at the handoff, and "with the class of admitted probes fixed before the retention is chosen" in (F3) has no mathematical content: for any noninjective retention R that passes (D) and (C), the class A_R of protocols whose retained responses factor through R satisfies (B), and A_R can be written down first. The canonical-parent remark (ch05:526-532) shows that the declared interface alone determines how much an exact parent may forget, so (F4) measures compression relative to a free parameter. The 2026-10-22 reassessment asks that probes not be narrowed "silently ... at handoff", and the 2026-10-23 Item 7 requirements ask to "Distinguish that diagnostic from future boundary probes required to descend"; the definition contains neither distinction. D10-FORM-03 shows the consequence.
**Fix:** Declare before selection two population-level classes, closed under relabeling and independent of the block selected: a child-level diagnostic class B and a parent protocol class A, each protocol with its response retention D_a. Define A_I as the members of A that read block I only through its declared channels. Require (B) for A_I, and replace the second part of (F4) by: there are b in B and x ≠ x' in the reachable post-handoff set with R_I(x) = R_I(x') and law_x(b) ≠ law_x'(b). State which A and B each chapter uses, and re-derive the verdicts of chapters 8, 9, 11 and 12 from that one definition. If compression should not be relative to a declared interface, fix A by a rule, for example the protocols generated by the agents' own declared channels and record kernels; that class contains single-child sampling.
**Falsifies:** A sentence in chapter 5 that defines "admitted child-level probe" and its relation to A_I and D_a, or a derivation of the chapter 9 and the chapter 11 and 12 verdicts from a single reading.

### D10-FORM-02: The counterfactual clause (F6b) has no declared class, its existential quantifier is met by degenerate handoffs, and the chapter 11 verdict depends on an unstated restriction
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** high
**Location:** chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1390 and 1392, `inh:recursive-formation` (F6), (F6b); :1377, 1394; chapters/11_conditional_formation.tex:296-310, 393; chapters/06_fusion.tex:680-684; D:/MultiAgentELBO_Theory/SPEC.md:233-249 (R14). Persists 2026-10-22 X4-PRIOR-01 and H2.
**Evidence:** (F6) requires that "this second-stage selection does not occur from every counterfactual handoff in the inherited class". "The inherited class" is defined nowhere in chapter 5, the companion or the glossary, and the clause is existential: one counterfactual handoff without the second stage suffices. With from-above threshold rules (the rule of `form:threshold-rule` and of `inh:scalar-additive`), a counterfactual handoff that places two parents within δ never produces their union: "two shapes initially closer than δ never reach δ from above and are never joined to each other" (ch11:308-310). Under the unrestricted reading every such construction meets (F6b). Chapter 11 reaches the opposite verdict by restricting the class: "the union also forms from every counterfactual handoff in which two parent scores are farther apart than δ; the counterfactual part of (F6) is not met either" (ch11:393). Chapter 6 uses another class, "every configuration of two first-stage parents, whatever their means" (ch06:681-683).

The earlier standard that X4-PRIOR-01 asked to restore was specific: R14 declares "a handoff class, fixed before the comparison, that contains every first-stage handoff and, with probability bounded below, the handoffs of histories without first-stage formation", a declared statistic, a uniformity condition for the supplied-scale verdict and a margin, and it records suppression of the second stage as first-stage dependence rather than recursion. (F6b) keeps none of these. `check_formation_clauses.py`, lines (F6b): in the anchored consensus of D10-FORM-03 every counterfactual parent gap above δ unites, while 50 grid handoffs with gap below δ and the sign of the limit never reach δ from above; the verdict flips with the class. The 2026-10-23 Item 7 audit asked for "a specified counterfactual handoff class" and recorded the equilibrium-on-boundary case; the definition was not updated.
**Fix:** Replace (F6b) by an R14-type clause. Declare before the comparison a counterfactual class C that contains every actual first-stage handoff from the admitted class and the states, at the same handoff times, of trajectories from a declared larger class on which the first-stage event has not occurred; exclude states outside the selector's domain condition, for example parents already within the threshold. In the deterministic case require that the set of handoffs in C from which the declared second-stage event does not occur have nonempty interior in C; in the stochastic case require a margin bounded below between the second-stage probability after first-stage formation and after counterfactual handoffs. State that "no newly tuned attraction" means the second-stage selector is the first-stage rule with the same constitutive data.
**Falsifies:** A definition of "the inherited class" in the text, or a proof that the chapter 6 and chapter 11 verdicts hold for every admissible choice of class.

### D10-FORM-08: "Interaction-sensitive selection ... requires receiver-dependent targets" does not follow from `inh:common-clock`
**Category:** inflation
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1371-1373; chapters/06_outlook.tex:16; companion/c05_inheritance.tex:240-252.
**Evidence:** `inh:common-clock` has four hypotheses: a shared model-independent evaluator, self excluded from the belief sources, constant-output channels C[M], and dependence on the laws through one additive moment. The negation of their conjunction is the disjunction of their negations, and the remark keeps one ("receiver-dependent targets, such as selective channels with nonconstant Gibbs rows"). `check_common_clock.py` (d): dropping any one hypothesis breaks the identity (k-spread of r_i − r_j: 1.18 with a self source and unequal self rows, 2.71 with heterogeneous evaluators, 1.51 with an input-dependent channel, 1.63 with a model-dependent evaluator and unequal model laws). Counterexample to the conclusion, (e): three binary agents with evaluator log-odds γ = (0, 0.75, 3), the receiver-independent target C[M] = Ber(σ(0.8M − 0.4)) with M = Σ_i q_i(1), self excluded. The log-odds differences obey the closed linear law dΔ/dt = −2Δ + Δγ along the full flow and dΔ/dt = −Δ + Δγ along the null (integration residual 6.3e−12), so full-flow gaps tend to Δγ/2 and null gaps to Δγ. With δ = 1/2 the pair rule joins {0,1} at t ≈ 1.22 along the full flow and never along the null, and the sequences differ in 20 of 20 perturbations of size 0.1. The target is the same law for every receiver. The outlook repeats the conclusion ("since shared references with additive moment channels make every pairwise law comparison relax on a common clock"), and the companion drops the shared-evaluator hypothesis in its paraphrase ("If every receiver compares itself with the same target law, and the channels see the population only through additive moments, then ... the logarithm of the ratio of their laws shrinks by the factor e^{−2t}, up to a constant"), which is false for heterogeneous evaluators: the log-ratio relaxes to ½ log(G_i/G_j), which depends on k.
**Fix:** "Interaction-sensitive selection under the reciprocal potential requires violating at least one hypothesis of `inh:common-clock`: heterogeneous or model-dependent evaluators, a self source with unequal self rows, input- or source-dependent channels, or law dependence beyond one additive moment. Selective channels with nonconstant Gibbs rows are one route." Note that heterogeneous evaluators give (F5) with membership supplied by the references, so the strong form fails. Update 06_outlook:16, and add the shared model-independent evaluator and self-exclusion to c05:240-245.
**Falsifies:** A reading of "receiver-dependent targets" that includes evaluators, references and self sources, stated in the text.

## Extensions and improvements

The most valuable change in this scope is to make the formation standard decidable. The clauses that fail to be well-posed share one cause: each quantifies over an object the construction is free to choose after seeing its own dynamics (the topology in (F1), the parent protocol class and response retentions in (F3) and (F4), the window in (F5), the counterfactual class in (F6b), and for the global objective the null itself). The replacement in D10-FORM-15 moves every such object to a declaration made before the dynamics is run and fixes the rest at theory level: openness in the product of open simplices or the L^∞ chart topology; population-level protocol and diagnostic classes closed under relabeling, with A_I induced from A; (F5) as "P_[0,τ](x) is not an initial segment of P⁰_[0,∞)(x)"; and an R14 counterfactual class containing the actual handoffs and the handoffs of first-stage-free trajectories, with an interior condition in the deterministic case. With these, every verdict in the classification paragraph becomes a statement that can be checked from the construction's declared data, and the anchored consensus of D10-FORM-03 becomes a two-stage selection failing (F6b), which is the intended outcome.

The second addition is the obstruction of D10-FORM-16. In scalar reciprocal pairwise dynamics, exact centroid closure of a block on an open set forces every cross-block pair force to be linear with a member-independent coefficient and the members' local forces to be affine with a common slope, while the within-block force stays free. The proof is two applications of Pexider's equation, and the numerical check separates linear from bounded-confidence cross forces (fiber residuals 1.8e−14 against 1.19). Together with `inh:common-clock` this locates the tension the formation program must resolve: selective coupling is what escapes the common clock, and exact additive compression forbids selective coupling across the block. The natural next statements are the version with normalized Gibbs rows, where each weight depends on a whole row and the Pexider argument must be applied to the row-normalized force, and the version for exponential-family agents with sum-closed natural parameters, which is the setting of L08-CND-10. A negative answer there would show that exact compression in the reciprocal model must come from symmetry quotients or from retentions that keep the cross-coupling data, which is what chapters 10 and 12 do.

Third, chapter 5 would gain from a short calibration suite placed beside the standard: the anchored consensus (D10-FORM-03), the heterogeneous-evaluator common-clock example (D10-FORM-08), and the Gaussian moment-projection counterexample (D10-FORM-17), each a finite closed-form computation that decides one clause or one inheritance test. The Gaussian moment-projection example also answers the question the focus brief raised: in the restricted Gaussian peer-KL flow the pooled moments are not an exact parent (pooled-variance velocities 0 and −19/16 on one fiber), whereas in linear location consensus they are (the spread obeys d/dt spread = −(22/3)·spread). Fourth, the statistical-partition results have one content-level input that the theory itself produces, the joint private-record posterior of the interacting-memory appendix; computing its finest independent partition as a function of the declared protocol would give the first selector in the manuscript whose input is not a declared threshold or a preparation ensemble.

Fifth, for the global free energy a null flow should be defined before any formation claim is made under that objective. The two candidates are the deletion of every likelihood factor whose scope meets two agents, which leaves independent single-agent models with their records, and the frozen-peer mean-field null, which keeps the factors but holds Q_{−i} at its initial value. They agree on product-factor models and differ whenever a coupling factor is not multiplicatively separable; the choice should be made in chapter 5 and justified by what the control is meant to isolate.
