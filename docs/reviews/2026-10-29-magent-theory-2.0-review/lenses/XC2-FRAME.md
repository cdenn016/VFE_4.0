### XC2-FRAME-01: Clause (F4) is read four ways; chapter 13's "physical compression" and the abstract's "physically noninjective parent" hold only under chapter 13's own reading
**Category:** consistency
**Severity:** high
**Location:** chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1387-1388 (inh:recursive-formation, (F3), (F4)), 686-712 (inh:inherited-parent), 527-532; chapters/08_smooth_erasure.tex:15, 261; chapters/09_public_compression.tex:251; chapters/11_conditional_formation.tex:393; chapters/12_open_formation.tex:416, 526; chapters/13_per_agent_inheritance.tex:221, 299, 301-316 (pa:phase-parent), 396; chapters/14_collective_local_parent.tex:5, 41, 84; frontmatter.tex:11; README.md:30; AUTHORING.md:45, 53, 75, 81; companion/c13_per_agent_inheritance.tex:184, 192; Item_7_formation_feedback.tex:254-256
**Evidence:** (F4) asks that "some admitted child-level probe distinguishes two configurations with the same retained value" (05:1388), while (F3) asks that every probe of the class "fixed before the retention" descend through it (05:1387, clause (B) of inh:inherited-parent at 699-701). If both clauses quantify over one class they cannot hold together, so (F4) needs a second probe class, and the definition never declares one or says when it is fixed. The chapters supply four different classes.

(a) Chapter 8 counts only the admitted response class, which reads public coordinates (08:15): "Compression, (F4), fails: the retention is non-injective only on private and gate coordinates that no admitted probe reads" (08:261). The verdict stands although the population's own peer channel reads the private mean through the gate before closure (08:15).

(b) Chapter 9 uses one class for both clauses and reports the resulting dilemma: "that probe is not inherited by the parent, so (F3) fails for it, and if the probe class is reduced to responses computed from the phase law, (F4) fails instead" (09:251).

(c) Chapters 11, 12 and Item 7 use probes admitted before the handoff: "the singleton responses available before the join distinguish points of its fiber, although the distinguished coordinate is inert" (11:393); "a pre-handoff query distinguishes its fibers" (12:526); "a receiver attending to one member through an identity channel as before the handoff" (it7:256).

(d) Chapter 13 uses "a permitted child-level distinction for assessing physical loss, but it is not a future boundary response in the fixed protocol. If it is admitted there, the quotient fails" (13:316).

Under (a), (b) and (c) the chapter 13 parent fails (F4). Its protocol excludes hidden absolute-angle queries "at all stages" (13:299), and the companion is explicit: "No hidden absolute-angle query is in this protocol, before or after a handoff" (c13:192). The erased phase enters no cost, row, channel, record or velocity and satisfies d(theta_i)/dt = 0 (pa:angular-ode, 13:221). Its only witness, pa:hidden-diagnostic, equals I_1(b_i)/I_0(b_i) exp(i theta_i), and the same equation gives db_i/dt = -b_i, so even the excluded witness decays like e^{-t}. Chapter 14 opens by conceding the point: "The phase parent of pa:phase-parent erases a distinction already invisible on each primitive port" (14:5). By the manuscript's own convention (AUTHORING.md:53, where "physical" requires a declared observable algebra that resolves the distinction; 12:416, "the words gauge and physical have no content here without the response protocol"), the chapter 13 loss is a gauge quotient relative to the declared algebra, the profile that chapter 10 calls "an equivariant symmetry reduction" (10:4: discarded data "invisible to the fine energy, the Fisher metric, and the admitted records" that also decay). AUTHORING.md:75 says the admitted interface "excludes the erased physical phase from the outset". The same equivocation recurs in chapter 14: its common orientation is "a separate nongauge action" (14:41), where "nongauge" means "not the marked structure group", while no admitted query resolves it at any stage (14:84, "No absolute psi response is admitted at any stage"); in AUTHORING's sense it is gauge. The reading also decides Item 7: under (a) or (b) its distinguishing probe is absent from the post-handoff routed class (it7:254), so Item 7 fails (F4) (XC2-FRAME-03). Status assigned: the intertwining statement of pa:phase-parent is D and correct as an intertwining; "physical compression" (13:396) and "physically noninjective parent" (frontmatter.tex:11) are S. The reading-dependence also carries forward the 2026-10-22 X2-FRAME-04 concern: 07_smooth_closure.tex:318 now defers the aggregation question to (F4), but (F4) under readings (c) and (d) is met by quotients of inert per-child coordinates (11:393, "inert"; 12:526 with open:all-partitions, an orientation symmetry available for every partition at every time), which is the "complete labeled tuple" case that 07:318 says leaves aggregation untouched.
**Fix:** Rewrite (F4): "A child-level probe class A^ch, declared together with A_I before any retention and containing only probes that some agent, channel, record, or declared external querier can apply before the handoff, contains a probe that separates two configurations in one fiber of R_I on the post-handoff invariant set." If persistence of the loss is intended, add "and the separation does not tend to zero along the post-handoff flow". Chapters 13 and 14 then fail (F4). Replace "physically noninjective parent" at frontmatter.tex:11, README.md:30 and inh:formation-open (05:1397) by "a parent that quotients a protocol-invisible hidden phase", and "nongauge" at 14:41 and AUTHORING.md:81 by "outside the marked structure group".
**Falsifies:** A query in chapter 13's declared protocol, at any stage, whose response law depends on theta_i; or a sentence in chapter 5 that declares the (F4) probe class and the order of its declaration, under which chapters 8, 9, 11, 12 and 13 all receive the verdicts they now report.

### XC2-FRAME-02: (F5) leaves the selector free; the chapter 13 verdict holds for thresholds in (1/8, 1/3) and fails for (1/3, 1) on identical dynamics

**Severity:** high
**Location:** chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1380 (inh:null-flow), 1386, 1389 ((F2), (F5)); chapters/13_per_agent_inheritance.tex:138-174 (pa:persistent-grouping, selector pa:selector at 158-163), 396; frontmatter.tex:11; chapters/06_outlook.tex:16; README.md:30; chapters/14_collective_local_parent.tex:80, 272-279 (lp:causal-coherence); Item_7_formation_feedback.tex:198-203, 258, 345, 376
**Evidence:** (F5) compares P and P^0 under "the same selector" (05:1380), but nothing requires the selector and its constants to be fixed before the two flows are computed, or the verdict to hold over a declared class of selectors. In pa:persistent-grouping the selector connects i and j when their log odds share a sign and min/max(|l|) > 1/4 (13:160). The proof computes the limit equilibrium u* = (15/7, 15/7, 5/7) (13:153) and observes "At u* the minimum ratio is 1/3" (13:163); the initial same-sign ratio is 1/8. Script `ch13_selector_threshold.py` integrates the exact log-odds equations pa:bit-flow and pa:bit-null (unit-temperature Gibbs rows, priors 1/5 and 2/5, records (1,1,-1), l(0) = epsilon (4, 1/2, -1), RK4 with dt = 0.02 to t = 100) for epsilon = 0.01 and 0.05. For c in {0.13, 0.20, 0.25, 0.30, 0.33} the full flow gives 1|2|3 -> 12|3 -> 123 and the null gives 1|2|3 -> 12|3, so P != P^0. For c in {0.34, 0.40, 0.50, 0.60, 0.75, 0.95} both give 1|2|3 -> 12|3, so P = P^0 and (F5) fails. The supremum over the full trajectory of agent 3's same-sign ratio is 0.3333 (epsilon = 0.01) and 0.3335 (epsilon = 0.05). In frontmatter.tex:11 and after 13:174 write "under the declared same-sign ratio selector with constant in (1/8, 1/3); the selector-free effect is that agent 3's public odds reverse sign against its record."
**Falsifies:** A threshold c > 1/3 for which the full flow of pa:bit-flow still selects the three-agent block from the theorem's open class, for example through a transient ratio above c that my fixed-step integration missed; or a clause of chapter 5 that fixes the selector class before the dynamics.

### XC2-FRAME-04: "Interaction-sensitive selection ... requires receiver-dependent targets" does not follow from inh:common-clock; a shared geometric-mean target reverses the first join
**Category:** inflation
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1371-1373 (after inh:common-clock); chapters/11_conditional_formation.tex:208 (after form:mean-field); chapters/06_outlook.tex:16; companion/c05_inheritance.tex:251-252
**Evidence:** inh:common-clock proves a common clock when every belief channel equals one law C[M] of the additive moment M = sum_i E_{q_i} T and the selector reads only pairwise log-ratios modulo constants. Four passages conclude that interaction-sensitive selection "therefore requires receiver-dependent targets" (05:1372; 11:208 "needs targets that depend on the receiver"; 06_outlook:16; c05:251 "needs receivers that weigh different sources differently"). The contrapositive of the proposition says only that some hypothesis must fail; a target that is the same for every receiver but depends on the population non-additively is not excluded. Counterexample (`shared_nonadditive_target.py`, `common_clock_contrast.py`): binary agents with log odds l_i, uniform evaluator, source sets excluding self, every channel equal to the normalized geometric mean of all agent laws (log odds mean(l)), one law for every receiver. The analytic unit-Fisher field agrees with the finite-difference natural gradient of the potential to 5.3e-10. For contrast, with the additive-moment target C = Bern(f(M)), M = sum_i tanh(l_i/2), f(M) = 0.7M + 0.2M^3, the identity d/dt(l_i - l_j) = -2(l_i - l_j) holds to 1.4e-8 over 20 random four-agent states, as the proposition says; with the geometric-mean target the deviation reaches 1.44. From l(0) = (3.2743, 4.4975, 5.8038), with the centroid-threshold selector of inh:scalar-additive, the null flow joins {1,2} first (initial gap 1.223) and the full flow joins {2,3} first (initial gap 1.306), for delta = 0.15, 0.3 and 0.5; at delta = 0.3 the full events are (0.654, {2,3}), (0.866, all) and the null events (1.406, {1,2}), (1.856, all). The crossings are transverse, so P != P^0 on a neighborhood. The target is additive in the natural parameters rather than in the expectation parameters, which is why it escapes the hypothesis. The finding changes where the outlook tells the program to look for interaction-sensitive selection.
**Fix:** Replace the four sentences by: "Under the reciprocal potential, a selector that reads pairwise log-ratios cannot be interaction-sensitive when every target is one law of the additive moment; receiver-dependent targets, or shared targets that depend non-additively on the expectation parameters, are required."
**Falsifies:** An error in the field of `shared_nonadditive_target.py` (it is checked against finite differences of the potential), or a reading of "receiver-dependent target" that includes the normalized geometric mean of all agent laws, which is the same law for every receiver.

### XC2-FRAME-05: The higher grades of (F5), the history horizon and the (F6b) counterfactual class are not well defined, and they are applied inconsistently
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1380 (inh:null-flow), 1389-1392, 1365-1371, 1399; chapters/06_fusion.tex:665-668; chapters/08_smooth_erasure.tex:263; chapters/11_conditional_formation.tex:321-327 (form:kappa-independence (iii)), 393; chapters/12_open_formation.tex:526; chapters/13_per_agent_inheritance.tex:396; Item_7_formation_feedback.tex:235, 258, 260
**Evidence:** (i) Strong form: "its final partition is not a function of any statistic of the initial data whose evolution is the same under Phi and Phi^0" (05:1392). Read as T(Phi_t x) = T(Phi^0_t x), every constant T qualifies, so the strong form fails for every construction whose final partition is constant on its admitted class. Chapter 6 says so ("fails trivially", 06:666), as does Item 7 ("a constant is a function of any statistic", it7:258). Chapters 12 and 13 also have constant final partitions on their admitted classes ({4,5},{0,1,2,3} on the open neighborhood; the full three-agent block), so they fail it too, but neither assesses it, while inh:formation-open asks for the strong form "preferably" (05:1399). The grade measures variation of the outcome over the admitted class rather than strength of interaction dependence: chapter 13's interaction fixes its outcome uniformly, and that outcome fails the grade. (ii) Amplification: "at each event, the ratio ... is smaller along Phi than along Phi^0" names neither whose events nor the metric of "diameter". Chapter 6 evaluates it at the events of Phi ("degenerate sense that within-block diameters vanish", 06:668); Item 7 declines because "Phi^0 has no events" (it7:258). Since (F5) passes exactly when the event sets differ, the grade is undefined in the cases it is meant to grade. (iii) Horizon: H(x) has no declared horizon (05:1380). The inference "Its partition sequence is therefore that of the null flow" (05:1370) holds on [0, infinity); on a finite window [0, T], with full events at t and null events at 2t, every event with t < T < 2t lies in P and not in P^0, so a pure clock change passes (F5). Chapter 12's theorem is stated on a finite horizon (12:526, "finite-window persistence"). (iv) (F6b) refers to "every counterfactual handoff in the inherited class" (05:1390) without defining the class; chapter 11 restricts it to parents "farther apart than delta" (11:393) and Item 7 to walled states (it7:235). (v) Chapter 8 writes "Clause (F5) is therefore met only through a shift of an event time" (08:263), which reads as a pass of a clause whose definition excludes time shifts, although the paragraph concludes that it fails.
**Fix:** Define H on the maximal forward existence interval, or declare its horizon and compare P with P^0 modulo monotone reparametrizations of time. Define the amplification ratio at the events of Phi, evaluated along both flows in a declared metric. Replace the strong form by "P(x) != P^0(x) at every point of the admitted class, for every selector of a class declared before the dynamics", or drop it. Define the (F6b) class as the post-handoff states in the domain of the selector. Change 08:263 to "Clause (F5) fails: the partition sequences coincide and only the switching time moves."
**Falsifies:** A definition elsewhere in chapter 5 of the horizon, the amplification convention or the counterfactual class; or a construction with a constant final partition on its admitted class that satisfies the strong form as written.

### XC2-FRAME-06: The abstract's no-go for the reciprocal potential omits the fixed-row and identity-channel hypotheses; the Gibbs-reduced potential that the constructions use is bounded, so part (a) of the proof does not reach it
**Category:** inflation
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** frontmatter.tex:5; chapters/03_dynamics.tex:4, 375-385 (prop:dyn-no-global-vfe); README.md:34; AUTHORING.md:39; chapters/06_outlook.tex:6
**Evidence:** The abstract says "we prove that it is not the free energy of any fixed normalized model whose recognition laws are the agent laws" (frontmatter.tex:5; also 03:4, README.md:34, AUTHORING.md:39). The proposition holds the rows fixed and positive and needs an identity channel from some i to some j != i; part (a) also needs a joint over finitely many finite-valued variables, part (b) fixed channels and product recognition. Part (a)'s proof uses E >= beta_ij KL(q_i||q_j), which is unbounded. Chapters 8, 9, 11 and 12 state that they use the Gibbs-reduced form (08:5, 09:4, 11:4, 12:4). For two binary agents with identity self and peer channels and priors 1/2, the supremum of E_red over a grid reaching 1e-9 from the boundary is 2.772589 = 4 log 2 (`nogo_scope.py`): E_red is bounded and part (a) gives nothing. Part (b)'s third-derivative argument does extend to E_red with fixed channels: d_{p1} d_{p2}^2 E_red = 9.7387 at (0.3, 0.7) and 13.5833 at (0.2, 2/3), while the same derivative of F(q_1 x q_2; o) vanishes identically for product recognition. The identity-channel hypothesis is load-bearing: with source-independent channels the fixed-row potential equals 2 KL(zeta_1 x zeta_2 || Pi_1 x Pi_2) + 0.21947 for a normalized product Pi (spread 3.3e-16 over six random configurations), so it is a product-model free energy. Chapter 12's channel is the twirl on every edge, including self (12:14-18), so (b) as stated does not cover it, and chapter 11's law-dependent channels with Gibbs rows are covered by neither part. Status assigned to the abstract sentence: P with rows fixed and one identity channel; true but unwritten for E_red with fixed channels; unproved for the Gibbs-reduced, law-dependent case of chapter 11. The three-objective separation that the abstract builds on this sentence is therefore stated more generally than it is proved.
**Fix:** Abstract: "we prove, with source rows fixed and one identity channel, that it is not the free energy ...". Add a part (c) to prop:dyn-no-global-vfe for E_red with fixed channels, by the same mixed third derivative, and state that the Gibbs-reduced law-dependent case is not covered.
**Falsifies:** A proof in chapter 3 that covers E_red with law-dependent channels; or a reading of "a reciprocal law potential" in the abstract that excludes the Gibbs-reduced form.

## Extensions and improvements

**A severe interaction-necessity clause.** Declare, before any flow is analyzed, a selector family S_c indexed by its constants c in a set C, and define the passing set C_pass(x) = {c in C : P_c(x) != P^0_c(x)}. A severe (F5) requires a nonempty open subset U of the admitted class and a nonempty open interval I of C, both declared in advance, with I contained in C_pass(x) for every x in U, and the report states C_pass. For pa:persistent-grouping, C_pass is (1/8, 1/3) at small epsilon (`ch13_selector_threshold.py`), so the theorem would pass with I = (0.2, 0.3) but not with an interval reaching above 1/3. A selector-free companion clause removes the threshold altogether: fix a relabeling-invariant observable O of the omega-limit set before the dynamics, for instance the number of agents whose public-odds sign differs from their record sign, and require O(omega(Phi x)) != O(omega(Phi^0 x)) on an open set. In chapter 13 this observable is 1 under the full flow and 0 under the null for every initial datum of the theorem, which is the effect the abstract wants to claim. The change costs a paragraph in chapter 5 and a sentence per construction; it gives each existence claim a test that could have failed.

**Every compression in the manuscript is transient, and convexity explains why.** The distinctions that the (F4) witnesses read all decay along the post-handoff flow: in chapter 11 the scores of joined blocks differ by delta e^{-2(t - t_k)} (form:kappa-independence (ii)); in chapter 13 db_i/dt = -b_i; in Item 7 the deviations converge to record-determined offsets (it7:193); and in chapter 12 the angle enters the force open:full-field only through 2 log q_i, so for q_i(z, theta) = mu_i(z) k_i(theta | z) the centered log conditional obeys d/dt [log k_i - E_{k_i} log k_i] = -2 [log k_i - E_{k_i} log k_i] up to theta-independent terms, the angular profiles relax to Haar like e^{-2t}, and the first moments that distinguish an H_I orbit vanish. A short proposition would make this general: if the discarded factor evolves autonomously, its potential is strictly convex in the law of that factor, its reference is invariant under a compact group K acting on it, and the gradient flow has compact sublevel sets, then the minimizer is unique, hence K-invariant, the flow converges to it, and every K-orbit distinction tends to zero. The proof is two lines (uniqueness forces invariance; Lojasiewicz or strict convexity gives convergence). It identifies the ingredient a persistent compression needs: a degenerate orbit of stationary laws with a nonzero distinguishing moment, as in an ordered phase whose stationary set is a K-orbit, which requires breaking convexity of the hidden-factor energy, for example through Gibbs-row competition or persistent records that do not fix the orbit. With a persistence requirement in (F4), no construction in the manuscript or in Item 7 passes, which makes Item 7's open problem it7:open-persistent the program-wide obligation rather than a remark about one instance. Difficulty: the proposition is an afternoon; an exact construction with a persistent orbit is open and probably hard.

**The no-go for the Gibbs-reduced potential.** Write E_red = E_0 - T sum_i log sum_j pi_ij exp(-D_ij/T). With fixed channels and an identity edge from i to j, the mixed derivative d_{w_i} d_{v_j}^2 E_red equals pi_ij integral w (v/q_j)^2 plus row-derivative terms of order 1/T, because -T log sum_j pi_ij exp(-D_ij/T) = sum_j pi_ij D_ij + O(1/T) with derivatives. For large T the first term dominates; it is nonzero by the two-point computation already in the proof of prop:dyn-no-global-vfe. The derivative is real analytic on the interior, so if it is nonzero at one point it is nonzero on an open dense set, which suffices for part (b). This gives a part (c) for E_red at every T after checking nonvanishing at one point per T, which `nogo_scope.py` does at T = 1 (value 9.7387 at (0.3, 0.7)). The law-dependent case needs a different argument, because source-independent channels make the potential a product-model free energy (checked in the same script), so any extension must use a nondegeneracy hypothesis on the channel's dependence on its source.

**Natural-parameter pooling as an escape from the common clock, and its price.** The geometric-mean target of XC2-FRAME-04 is the logarithmic opinion pool, the natural companion of the linear pool cited at 05:826. It makes membership order interaction-sensitive while every receiver uses the same target. By inh:scalar-additive, exact sum closure for every pair is then impossible in a scalar architecture, because the field contains the agent-dependent term S/(N sigma'(l_i)) and is not affine in l_i. A trade-off statement is a natural conjecture, not proved here: in a scalar architecture with a receiver-independent target, exact additive inheritance for every pair forces the target to depend on the population only through the additive moment, and then the membership order is the null order. The vector-valued escape noted at 05:1320-1327 remains the route to having both; a concrete test is a Gaussian family with population-dependent anisotropic precision targets, where one would compute whether the common propagator of natural-parameter differences is a multiple of the identity.

**One table of open problems and a pre-registration rule.** The outlook should close with all 22 open problems by label (XC2-FRAME-08), each with the construction that comes closest and the clause it lacks. For future constructions, AUTHORING can require that the selector constants, the (F4) probe class and the null convention be recorded before the flows are computed, and that each existence claim report C_pass and the selector-free discrepancy. This turns use-novelty (Worrall 2014) from a reviewer's question into a property of the text.
