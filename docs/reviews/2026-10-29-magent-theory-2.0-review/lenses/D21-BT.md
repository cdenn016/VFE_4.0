### D21-BT-01: The rectangular selector is always an orthogonal connection and cannot produce noncompact holonomy
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/16_link_base_transport.tex:206-222 (ibt:curvature, ibt:eq:metric-compatibility), :256; companion/c16_link_base_transport.tex:103-108; AUTHORING.md:89; appendices/base_connection.tex:35-43 (its explicit curved example)
**Evidence:** ibt:eq:metric-compatibility gives dH_X + aH_X + H_Xa^T = 0, equivalently dM = Ma + a^TM for M = H_X^{-1}. Along any path the transport of ibt:eq:path-ode therefore satisfies d/dt(𝒯^T M(γ) 𝒯) = 𝒯^T(-a^TM + dM - Ma)𝒯 = 0, so every transport is an isometry between the induced fiber metrics and every holonomy lies in O(H_X^{-1}(c_0)). In a frame along a loop in which E is trivial, det 𝒯 = exp(-∮Tr a) = exp(½∮ d log det H_X) = 1, so orientation-preserving loops (all loops on the trivial bundles of every example in the chapter) have holonomy in SO(H_X^{-1}(c_0)), and the restricted holonomy group lies in SO(K). Pointwise, H_X^{-1}F is skew, so F(c) has purely imaginary spectrum. The same holds with a message connection Γ that preserves the message metric: a skew Γ in ibt:eq:message-gamma gives aH + Ha^T = -dH by the same computation. Recomputed: `s01` (H_X^{-1}F + (H_X^{-1}F)^T = 0 exactly for a generic 2x4 polynomial X at three rational points); `s02` part (5) (a loop holonomy with |Hol - I| = 0.85 has |Hol^T M_0 Hol - M_0| = 1.5e-13, det Hol = 1 + 9e-14, eigenvalue moduli 1). Because the spectrum of F is gauge invariant, no full-rank message matrix of any size realizes a connection with a real nonzero curvature eigenvalue. The appendix's own explicit curved example, a_x = E_12, a_y = E_21 with F_xy = diag(1,-1), is outside the range of the rectangular rule for every X, as are all shear and dilation holonomies. The chapter states metric preservation and Tr F = 0 but not this consequence; the companion says only that the rule "does not generate every general-linear connection", and AUTHORING.md:89 calls the output "a GL connection unless a shared Lie-algebra constraint is imposed". The link-augmented alignment metric at ch16:306-311 behaves differently: `s05` shows that it obeys the GL(2) affine law (residual 4e-11) and keeps Tr a = -½ d log det H_X (links are blind to scalars) while failing H_X^{-1}-compatibility (residual 0.57), and `s05b` finds, for a smooth GL(2) link field with link weight 10, det F_xy = -11.18 (eigenvalues ±3.34) and a small-square holonomy with real eigenvalues 1.00831 and 0.99176 and determinant 1, which lies in no compact subgroup.
**Fix:** Add a corollary after ibt:curvature: "The rectangular selector, and its variant with a metric message connection, reduces the frame bundle to the H_X^{-1}-orthonormal frames. Its transports are isometries of H_X^{-1}, its holonomy lies in O(H_X^{-1}(c_0)), its restricted holonomy in SO(K), and its curvature has imaginary spectrum. Under the structure group GL(K) it never excites the noncompact directions; noncompact holonomy requires a selector that is not H_X^{-1}-metric, for instance the link-augmented alignment." Replace "it is a GL connection unless a shared Lie-algebra constraint is imposed" in AUTHORING.md:89 by "it is a connection on the GL(K) frame bundle whose holonomy is orthogonal for the induced metric".
**Falsifies:** a full-row-rank C^2 message matrix with fixed labels whose connection has a curvature value with a real nonzero eigenvalue, or a contractible loop whose transport has determinant different from 1.

### D21-BT-02: For noncompact groups the isometric-target hypothesis of link-only alignment generally fails; for GL(K) link-only alignment is never available
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/16_link_base_transport.tex:286 ("a full-rank vector collection is not necessary when the group action on links is itself identifiable"), :288-311 (ibt:alignment and the example metric), :340-348 (ibt:invisible and its remark); companion/c16_link_base_transport.tex:149-158; frontmatter.tex:17
**Evidence:** ibt:alignment assumes a Riemannian target with an isometric left G-action. If z has stabilizer G_z, each s in G_z fixes z and acts on T_z𝒵 by an 𝓗_z-isometry, so the isotropy representation of G_z must have relatively compact image. For conjugation on one link K = diag(λ_1, λ_2) in GL(2) with real λ_1 ≠ λ_2, the element s = diag(t, 1) lies in G_K and sends the tangent vector E_12 to tE_12; invariance forces 𝓗_K(E_12, E_12) = t^2 𝓗_K(E_12, E_12) for all t > 0, contradicting positivity. The same argument excludes an invariant metric at the identity tuple (stabilizer all of GL(K), isotropy representation Ad) and at any tuple whose common centralizer acts with unbounded image. For K ≥ 2, GL(K) and SL(K) carry no bi-invariant Riemannian metric, so the canonical choice used for SO(3) at ch16:315 has no analogue. At tuples whose common centralizer acts compactly the pointwise obstruction disappears, but an invariant metric must then be constructed (for example on a free proper stratum), and none is canonical. Combined with ibt:invisible, which the chapter applies to scalars in the defining GL(K) representation (ch16:346), link-only targets never give an injective infinitesimal action for GL(K). The chapter's own invariant example metric (ch16:306-310) restores isometry by dressing the link term with H_X from full-rank vector data, which is the rectangular hypothesis the alignment section was introduced to avoid. The only link-only curved example uses compact SO(3). The sentence at ch16:286 is true, but for the structure group GL(K) of the chapter's explicit construction (ch16:11), which is also the program's group, law or interface data are always required.
**Fix:** After ibt:alignment add: "For noncompact G an invariant target metric can exist at z only if the isotropy representation of the stabilizer of z is relatively compact. For G = GL(K), conjugation-only targets are never identifiable, and the link term must be dressed by law or interface data, for example Tr(Σ^{-1} δK Σ δK^T) with a law covariance transforming as Σ' = h^{-1}Σh^{-T}." Qualify ch16:286 and the abstract's "curved example built only from reciprocal rotation links" as compact-group statements.
**Falsifies:** a GL(2)-conjugation-invariant Riemannian metric on a neighborhood of a real diagonalizable link with distinct eigenvalues.

### D21-BT-03: The finite endpoint maps have an exact geometric meaning that the chapter does not state: N carries principal angles and T is transport along a Grassmannian geodesic
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/16_link_base_transport.tex:164-201 (ibt:reciprocal, ibt:path, and the remark at :201), :217-222; companion/c16_link_base_transport.tex:123-134
**Evidence:** Let W(c) = im X(c)^T in the message space. Q_X(c): (E_c, H_X(c)^{-1}) → W(c) is an isometry (Q_X^T Q_X = H_X^{-1}) with inverse X(c) restricted to W(c), and X(c') vanishes on W(c')^⊥ = ker X(c'). Hence C(c',c) is the orthogonal projection P_{W(c')} restricted to W(c), read through these isometries; C(c,c') is its adjoint; and N(c',c) corresponds to P_W P_{W'} restricted to W. Consequently: (a) the eigenvalues of N(c',c) are cos^2 θ_k for the principal angles θ_k between W(c) and W(c'); (b) N is similar to a positive operator wherever C(c',c) is invertible, so T is defined on that whole set and not only after shrinking toward the diagonal; (c) T, the orthogonal polar factor of the projection, is the direct rotation from W(c) to W(c') and is an isometry between the induced fiber metrics; (d) the direct rotation equals parallel transport of the pulled-back tautological connection along the Grassmannian geodesic t ↦ exp(tΞ)W(c), with Ξ in so(r) exchanging W(c) and W(c)^⊥, because for w in W(c) the velocity exp(tΞ)Ξw lies in W(t)^⊥, so the projected derivative vanishes.
Thus T is exact transport along a path in Gr(K, r) that need not be the image of any base path, which is the precise content of the remark that the finite endpoint rule "contains additional, nonunique higher-order data" (ch16:201). (e) Because T(c,c') = T(c',c)^{-1}, ordered products of T form a symmetric one-step method, so their second-order local error cancels; with C^3 data, products of T converge at second order while products of C converge at first order. (f) For the witness, c ↦ W(c) = (v,-1)^⊥ is inverse central (gnomonic) projection onto the unit sphere, the connection is the Levi-Civita connection of S^2 pulled back (det F_xy = s^{-3}, the square of the pulled-back area density), and straight lines map to great circles. T equals exact straight-segment transport exactly when the gnomonic images make an acute angle, 1 + v·v' > 0, and differs otherwise, because the RP^2 geodesic then runs through -n(c'), off the image of the base. Recomputed: `s02c` gives |eig N - cos^2 θ| = 2e-16, |T - direct rotation| ≤ 9e-16 and |geodesic transport - direct rotation| ≤ 5e-11 at three random pairs; `s02d` gives positive real spectrum of N and isometry defect 5.6e-14 over 200 far-apart pairs with C invertible; `s02b` gives |C - exact straight transport| ≈ 0.084 ε^2 and |T - exact| ≈ 0.0068 ε^3 for a generic 2x4 X, with observed product orders 1.00 (C) and 2.00 (T); `s01b` gives det F - area^2 = 0 exactly; `s02e` gives |T - exact straight transport| ≤ 5.3e-13 on 32 pairs with 1 + v·v' > 0 and at least 1.82 on 6 pairs with 1 + v·v' < 0.
**Fix:** Add a proposition after ibt:reciprocal stating (a)-(e) with the proof above (about ten lines), cite the direct rotation (Davis and Kahan 1970) and principal angles (Björck and Golub 1973), and use (f) as the geometric reading of the witness in the main text and the companion.
**Falsifies:** a full-rank X and a pair (c, c') with C(c',c) invertible for which T is not an isometry of the induced metrics, or N has a nonpositive or nonreal eigenvalue.

### D21-BT-04: Concrete content for the open continuation problem: a pointwise stabilizer obstruction, smooth continuation in the witness, and a generic 1/r singularity
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/16_link_base_transport.tex:338-350 (ibt:invisible and remark), :364; appendices/base_connection.tex:74 (open:conn-derived); companion/c16_link_base_transport.tex:181-183
**Evidence:** (a) Pointwise version of ibt:invisible. If at one point c_0 a nonzero ζ commutes with every link value K_e(c_0), take h = exp(fζ) with f(c_0) = 0 and df(c_0) ≠ 0. Then K_e'(c_0) = K_e(c_0) and dK_e'(c_0) = dK_e(c_0) + [K_e(c_0), ζ] df(c_0) = dK_e(c_0), while the affine law demands a'(c_0) = a(c_0) + ζ df(c_0). So no covariant selector that depends only on the one-jet of conjugation-transforming data exists at any point of nonzero common infinitesimal stabilizer; second jets do change. This covers ibt:alignment, whose normal equation uses only Z and dZ. `s06` part (a), at x = 0 of the chapter's SO(3) example (Q_1 = I): value change 0, first-derivative change 1.1e-10, second x-derivative change 1.77, required connection shift 0.76. (b) Continuation can still exist. In the chapter's example a = -w_β/(w_x + w_β) ê_3 dy is smooth across x = 0 (stabilizer span(n)) and across y = 0 and y = π (parallel axes), where the locked form is singular (`s03`: least eigenvalue 5.6e-17 at x = 0 while the a_y coefficient tends to -1), so continuity selects one element of the affine solution class there. These degenerate sets are lines only because the example's axes stay in one plane.
(c) Generic degeneracy is worse. Parallel axes is a codimension-two condition, so a generic two-link field over a two-dimensional base meets it at isolated points. For Q_1 = R_{e_1}(α) and Q_2 = R_{u(c)}(β) with u(c) proportional to (1, x, y), `s06c` gives r·max|a| = 1.0000 at r = 0.1, 0.01 and 0.001, a circulation of -2π for the e_1-component on small circles, and holonomy equal to the identity to 4e-10: a non-removable 1/r winding singularity at the point, invisible in SO(3) holonomy because it is a rotation by 2π. (This field is flat because its shape map has rank one, see D21-BT-13; the winding argument, an adapted gauge that turns once about u_1 around the point, does not use flatness.) (d) For the rectangular rule, K×r matrices of rank below K form a submanifold of codimension r - K + 1, so by transversality rank loss is generic and stable when r < K + dim 𝒞 and generically absent when r ≥ K + dim 𝒞 (the standard codimension count, for example Golubitsky and Guillemin, Stable Mappings and Their Singularities, Ch. II §5; locator from memory). The chapter's witnesses avoid rank loss only through their identity blocks.
**Fix:** Add (a) as a corollary of ibt:invisible. In open:conn-derived, state that continuation must admit singular connections whose holonomy singularity is removable, and use (b)-(d) as the first test cases. Difficulty: (a), (b) and (d) are short; classifying isolated isotropy jumps (winding numbers, and the Z_2 monodromy that a 2π rotation produces for spinor-valued data under SU(2)) is a moderate project.
**Falsifies:** a covariant selector depending only on one-jets at a point of nonzero common infinitesimal stabilizer.

### D21-BT-07: Every two-link SO(3) alignment connection is Abelian, so the curved witness exhibits no non-Abelian holonomy
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/16_link_base_transport.tex:313-336 (ibt:eq:SO3-links, ibt:eq:SO3-normal, ibt:eq:SO3-curvature); companion/c16_link_base_transport.tex:160-177
**Evidence:** In the witness, a and F lie in span(ê_3) at every point (`s03` part A; `s06` part (b): the values of a at three points span a one-dimensional subspace, and the holonomies of two loops based at (0.9, 1.1) commute to 3.8e-13). This is forced for any two links with nonparallel axes u_1, u_2 under the product bi-invariant metric with arbitrary positive weights. Choose locally a smooth gauge with u_1 = e_1 and u_2 in span(e_1, e_2). Changes of the rotation angles are self-motions, killed by C_r^T because (I - Q_r^T)u_r = 0, and the only remaining motion is rotation of u_2 about e_3, with right-trivialized tangent C_2 ê_3 dφ. The right side of ibt:eq:SO3-normal is then -w_2 ê_3 dφ, 𝒦e_3 = (w_1 + w_2)e_3, and a = -w_2/(w_1 + w_2) ê_3 dφ, so a annihilates e_3. Hence the covariant unit normal ν = u_1×u_2/|u_1×u_2|, built from the links alone, is parallel, and every holonomy lies in the SO(2) fixing ν. `s06b` confirms |D_Y ν| ≈ 9e-11 while |d_Y ν| ≈ 0.5 to 1.0 for two links whose axes both leave every fixed plane. A non-Abelian link-only witness needs at least three links with non-coplanar axes: in `s06b`, three such links give holonomies of two loops at one base point with |[H_1, H_2]| = 2.4e-4 (|H_i - I| ≈ 0.05 to 0.07, numerical floor 1e-12). The chapter does not claim non-Abelian curvature for this example, but the example is its only link-only curvature witness, and the preceding text (ch16:68) presents non-Abelian curvature as a possibility of the frame-average construction.
**Fix:** State after ibt:eq:SO3-curvature that the connection reduces to the SO(2) fixing u_1×u_2, so its holonomy is Abelian for every two-link configuration, and add a three-link example (or a remark with the numerical witness above) if non-Abelian contextual holonomy from links is wanted.
**Falsifies:** two smooth SO(3) link fields with nonparallel axes whose alignment holonomies at one base point fail to commute.

### D21-BT-08: The scalar backtrack witness differs from the reciprocal selection only by a pure gauge
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/16_link_base_transport.tex:247-251; companion/c16_link_base_transport.tex:121
**Evidence:** With X = [1, s], a = -s ds/(1 + s^2) = d(-½ log(1 + s^2)) (exact; `s01`). It is the gauge transform h^{-1}dh of a = 0 by h = (1 + s^2)^{-1/2}, so every gauge-invariant quantity of the raw-column and reciprocal-only selections (curvature, holonomy class) agrees; for K = 1 the rectangular selector is always exact, a = -½ d log H_X. The statement that "their transports differ for varying s" is correct for connections on one fixed bundle, but it exhibits no backtrack dependence of any invariant of the selected connection. A two-dimensional witness does. Let Θ_ij = Θ_ji = S(c) = diag(e^x, e^y), so that N_{ij,j} = S^2, S_{ij,j} = S and Ω_ij = I; take receiver columns e_1, e_2 and source mean (1, 1)^T. The raw linked column S(c)(1, 1)^T gives F_xy with (1,2) entry (e^{2x} + 1)e^{x+y}/(1 + e^{2x} + e^{2y})^2 > 0 everywhere and F(0,0) = [[-1/9, 2/9], [-2/9, 1/9]], while the reciprocal-only column gives F = 0 (`s01`). Curvature spectra are gauge invariant, so here the backtrack factor alone changes the gauge class of the selected connection.
**Fix:** Replace or supplement the scalar example by this two-dimensional one, and note that in rank one the rectangular selection is always pure gauge.
**Falsifies:** a gauge invariant that distinguishes the two scalar selections.

## Extensions and improvements

The most useful next result is a holonomy dictionary for the chapter's selectors under the program's group GL(K). D21-BT-01 shows that the rectangular rule always lands in SO(H_X^{-1}(c_0)) on orientation-preserving loops, and D21-BT-02 shows that link-only alignment is unavailable for GL(K); the link-augmented alignment can be hyperbolic (`s05b`) but keeps Tr a = -½ d log det H_X because conjugation is blind to scalars, so it never produces dilation holonomy on contractible loops either. A proposition of the form "for any selector whose scalar component is fixed by H_X alone, det of the holonomy is 1 on orientation-preserving loops" is a two-line consequence of the scalar normal equation. Whether a law-dressed target (Gaussian means and covariances with their Fisher metric, J_pξ = (ξμ, ξΣ + Σξ^T)) can produce det-holonomy different from one is a concrete computation: its scalar direction couples to the traceless directions through the locked form, so the trace part need not be exact. Difficulty: low for the proposition, moderate for the Gaussian computation.

D21-BT-03 gives the reciprocalized endpoint map a second use. T(c',c) is an exact, second-order consistent discrete connection in the sense of Leok, Marsden and Weinstein, defined for every pair with C invertible and isometric for the induced metrics. On the finite contextual graphs of chapter 15 it could serve as a data-derived edge transport, replacing the eliminated auxiliary minimizer by a closed form, X(v)Q_X(u)N^{-1/2}, at the cost of one K×K square root per edge; its holonomy around graph cycles then converges to the continuum holonomy at second order under refinement. The same identification gives a gauge-invariant contextual message energy: the minimized residual of ibt:group at M = H_X^{-1}, ½ Σ_μ Tr(H_X^{-1} ∂_μX (I - P_X) ∂_μX^T), equals ¼ Σ_μ ‖∂_μP_X‖_F^2, the Dirichlet energy density of the Grassmannian map c ↦ W(c) (`s08`: difference 9.5e-11). The rectangular selector is therefore the composite gauge field of a Grassmannian sigma model, the Gr(K, r) analogue of the CP^{N-1} structure cited at chapters/02_geometry.tex:262. Adding it to the reciprocal potential, as chapter 15 does for the Fisher cost, would make X dynamical; its first variation is computable in closed form, while well-posedness and rank preservation (D21-BT-04, part (d)) are the hard parts. Difficulty: moderate.

For the open continuation problem (open:conn-derived), D21-BT-04 suggests a precise program. First, prove the pointwise obstruction for one-jet selectors. Second, classify the generic isolated isotropy jumps of two-link SO(3) fields over a two-dimensional base: there the alignment connection has a 1/r winding singularity whose SO(3) holonomy is trivial; for SU(2)-valued links acting on spinor-valued agent data, the same circulation -2π should give holonomy -I, a Z_2 monodromy (expected from the computation in `s06c`, not computed here). Third, determine when continuity from the regular region selects a unique element of the affine solution class, as it does on the degenerate lines of the chapter's own example. A three-link non-Abelian witness (D21-BT-07, `s06b`) should be written in closed form; a configuration with axes along a moving orthonormal triad is the natural candidate. Difficulty: moderate.

Two expository improvements cost little. The witness is the tangent bundle of the unit sphere with its Levi-Civita connection, pulled back by central projection (`s01b`), and the Laplace example is its further pullback by a map that folds the line x = 0 to a point; saying so explains the formula for F, its zero set, and why the reciprocal map is exact transport along straight segments when 1 + v·v' > 0. And chapter 16 should state, as chapter 02 does for the Fisher target, that curvature factors through a shape map (D21-BT-13), which turns "redundant columns permit nonzero curvature" into the checkable condition that the Grassmannian map c ↦ W(c) have rank at least two.
