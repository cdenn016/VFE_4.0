### GAP07-S3-10: A corrected convex no-go for parents anchored at their own record pools
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** `Item_7_formation_feedback.tex:96`, it7:attractor; follows GAP07-S3-01.
**Evidence:** In the joint's own flow, with each parent anchored at its own cluster's record mean, the search of GAP07-S3-01 found no (F6b) instance in 18,640 trials (9,024 reachable first stages). In the mirror-symmetric no-edge family the antisymmetric sector is the two-dimensional system of GAP07-S3-01(a), with equilibrium u* = v* = obar when m_A = obar. An overshoot of the parent toward the other parent then needs alpha < 0, which needs the members to start inside v* while the parent starts outside u*. Since u* = v*, these two conditions cannot hold together when the parent starts at the pool of its members. Without mirror symmetry this argument fails: the two parents then move independently and monotonically, one inward and one outward, and the difference of two such motions can dip below its limit, so asymmetric handoffs are not covered.
**Fix:** Candidate statement: in the Items 2/3 joint's own flow with parents anchored at their clusters' record pools, the parent gap from any handoff in the class approaches its limit without entering the coherence set unless the limit lies in it, so (F6b) fails whenever (F6a) holds. The mirror-symmetric no-edge case follows from the two-dimensional argument above; the asymmetric no-edge case needs the reachable handoff states, which the pre-handoff relaxation constrains. With bridges and dictionaries, the natural route is a comparison argument: the block-Jacobi flow D^{-1}J is similar to a symmetric positive-definite matrix, and the parent-gap functional should have a nonnegative impulse response for initial deviations of the class. Difficulty: moderate for symmetric two-cluster configurations, open in general.
**Falsifies:** A self-centered-anchor instance meeting (F6b), found by a wider search or by construction.

### GAP07-S3-11: An interval lemma for the structure free energy in one dimension
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** low
**Location:** `Item_7_formation_feedback.tex:348`, it7:structure-gauss.
**Evidence:** No non-interval global minimizer in 9,000 random configurations of 4 to 6 agents (`gap07_interval_k1_output.txt`), although non-interval partitions beat interval ones below the minimum (GAP07-S3-06).
**Fix:** Conjecture: for K = 1 and the structure free energy it7:structure-gauss with homogeneous priors, every minimizer over all partitions consists of intervals of the agents sorted by mean. Proof route: an exchange argument. If blocks U and V interleave, swap their extreme members; the within-block sum of squares drops by an explicit amount proportional to the overlap, and the attention terms change by at most n_U lam |Delta ybar| times the largest weighted distance to a source. ## Extensions and improvements

The most useful next step is validated integration of the first stage alone, because GAP07-S3-03 shows that nothing after the first-stage handoff needs integration. A concrete scheme: enclose the 12-dimensional flow with individual rows on [0, 2.6] by an interval Taylor method of order 4 to 6 with QR (Lohner) wrapping control, starting from a box of radius 1e-4 about the fixed draw. At each step, evaluate the candidate predicates of it7:selector on the enclosure, locate each entry time by an interval Newton step on the bottleneck margin (whose derivative is at least 0.09 at entry), and switch the vector field at the handoff enclosures [t_c + tau_p]. The required tolerances are modest: the coherence margin 0.0045 in KL is about 0.006 in mean gap, and the post-stage gap has 0.33 to spare. In Python with mpmath this is a heavy run (minutes, not seconds); with a C++ interval library it is routine. The same enclosure on [0, 1.30] for the derived selector needs, at each step, the per-side certificate of GAP07-S3-02 evaluated on the enclosure box. Within the margins seen here (0.82 for the minimizer, 0.053 for the handoff gap), that certificate needs no subdivision.

The convex no-go should be replaced by a proved statement about the joint's own flow. GAP07-S3-10 gives the candidate and its mirror-symmetric no-edge proof. The general case reduces to a sign property of the impulse response of the parent-gap functional along mu' = -D^{-1}J(mu - x*). The relevant initial deviations are those of the handoff class: members at a reachable pre-handoff state, parents at the pools, cross-scale dictionaries at zero. Because D^{-1}J is similar to the positive-definite matrix D^{-1/2}JD^{-1/2}, the impulse response is a positive combination of decaying exponentials whenever the gap functional and the initial deviation are aligned with a common cone, and identifying that cone for self-centered anchors is the mathematical task. If the conjecture holds, Item 7's qualitative conclusion is correct in the form "a convex Gaussian hierarchy needs a supplied attraction between parents to meet (F6b)", which is exactly what (F6a) forbids. Item 7's construction would then remain the only route in the program to counterfactual recursion under inherited interactions, through nonconvex Gibbs rows.

The record-gap window of GAP07-S3-04 suggests a bifurcation picture worth stating. In the (s, tau_p) plane the two-stage regime with (F6b) is bounded below in s by the saddle-node of the gap equation and above by the curve on which the post-first-stage gap reaches d_u(s) - 0.005. For the derived selector it is also bounded in tau_p by the two switch times of the minimizer. Computing these curves (the saddle-node is an algebraic condition F = F' = 0, certified here for n = 3; the upper curve needs only first-stage integration) would replace "the parameter window is narrow" by a region with certified lower edge. It would also show directly how much of the construction's (F6b) comes from the choice s = 1.1.

The interval lemma of GAP07-S3-11 would make the derived selector's implementation exact in one dimension without the side reduction. A useful partial result is easier: the reduction of GAP07-S3-02 applies to any instance whose agents split into groups separated by a distance delta with lam delta^2/4 above the structure free energy of the candidate partition. It turns any such instance's all-partition problem into independent per-group problems, which keeps exhaustive enumeration feasible far beyond twelve agents.
