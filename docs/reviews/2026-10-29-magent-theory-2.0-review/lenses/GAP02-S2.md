### GAP02-S2-05: The C^r-neighborhood claim at 12:489 rests on an unstated C^2 flow-map lemma, and C^1 in the angle suffices for it
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/12_open_formation.tex:489 ("Smooth dependence of the fine field and its contextual variational equations preserves these bounds ... this step uses the C^r chart, since the rotation scores are angular derivatives"); :477-487 (`open:equivariant-lift`); :521 (proof of `open:formation`)
**Evidence:** Reconstruction. Take the tied common S^1 and the lift of `open:equivariant-lift`, which is a pure belief rotation in χ with context-independent model sections. Then A_χ = -a/(a+b). Here a(κ, t) is the total belief rotation inertia, the sum over i of ∫(∂_θ q_i)^2/q_i dν at context κ. b(t) is the total model inertia, and it does not depend on κ at any time, because the model sector evolves autonomously from context-independent data. A_κ does not depend on χ, since single-factor Fisher pairings are rotation invariant. Hence F = ∂_κ A_χ = -b ∂_κ a/(a+b)^2. Bit-marginal scores are Fisher-orthogonal to ∂_θ q and drop out of the connection. At the reference this reproduces `open:curvature` to 1.8e-8, 7.8e-9 and 1.3e-9 at t = 0, 0.5 and 1, for a two-agent block under the full fine flow. At a correlated law perturbed by 0.05 it stays within 1.3 percent of the reference, and so do h_χχ and the anchor moments. ∂_κ a converges at second order under δ -> δ/2 (`s5`). The curvature therefore needs the jets ∂_κ q(t), ∂_θ q(t) and ∂_κ∂_θ q(t), and no second angular derivative.
The lemma the sentence needs has four parts.
(i) The fine field is real analytic on the open set of log-densities in L^∞. It is built from exp, log, finite sums and bounded integrals, with no angular derivative. So Φ_t is C^2, with derivatives continuous in the initial point uniformly on [0, L]; this is standard Banach-space ODE theory, for example Dieudonné, Foundations of Modern Analysis, ch. 10 (locator from memory).
(ii) Equivariance transports angular derivatives: ∂_θΦ_t(q) = DΦ_t(q)∂_θ q, and ∂_κ∂_θ q(t) = D^2Φ_t[∂_κ q(0), ∂_θ q(0)] + DΦ_t[∂_κ∂_θ q(0)].
(iii) x -> (q_{i,x}, ∂_κ q_{i,x}, ∂_θ q_{i,x}, ∂_κ∂_θ q_{i,x}) is continuous from C^1 to C^0, which uses continuity of ϑ_i(x).
(iv) The context patch times [0, L] is compact.
With (i)-(iv) the claim holds, and r >= 1 suffices. This answers the question left open by 2026-10-22 L09-OPEN-11: r >= 3 is not load-bearing here.
**Fix:** State the lemma (i)-(iv) after 12:489. Say that the geometric neighborhood is open in C^1, hence in C^r, and that uniform closeness of log-densities, the topology of `open:open-laws`, is not enough for this step.
**Falsifies:** A step of the geometry that needs two angular derivatives. The χ-equivariant reduction above has none.

### GAP02-S2-06: The finite-dimensional gradient-flow clause of open:quotient-gradient does not distinguish the quotient metric from the slice metric
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/12_open_formation.tex:389 ("Its gradient structure is that of the quotient metric"), :397 (von Mises clause), :402 ("the last statement does not exercise it")
**Evidence:** On the product von Mises family, E is invariant under all of (S^1)^{2N}, and parity makes the Fisher metric block diagonal between phase and non-phase directions. So grad E has no phase component, the phases are constant and the moving term vanishes, as 12:402 says. The two metrics of `open:two-metrics` differ only on phase directions, so the restricted flow is equally the gradient flow of the restricted energy for the slice-induced metric. The clause therefore cannot single out the quotient metric. What does distinguish the two is the pointwise dissipation identity. With moving phases the slice velocity has squared norm g(V,V) + g(Jη, Jη), where η_i = omega_bar_I - omega_i. On a correlated three-agent instance, `s4` [3] gives g(Jη, Jη) = 0.239 at t = 0 and 3.4e-3 at t = 0.6, while g(V, J_i) vanishes to 1e-15 and dE/dt + g(V,V) = 2.7e-6. Only the quotient metric satisfies `open:quotient-dissipation`. The sentence at 12:389 is thus supported by the dissipation identity, not by a gradient-flow statement on any family where the phases move.
**Fix:** Prove the gradient-flow clause on the product family with two-harmonic angular factors f ∝ exp(κ_1 cos(θ - χ) + κ_2 cos(2(θ - χ) - α)), κ_1 > 0. It is invariant, because f^ρ stays in the family with χ and α fixed. The H_I action on χ is free (κ_1 > 0), proper and isometric. The anchor phase moves; D16-OPEN-05's profile cos θ + 2cos(2θ - 1) belongs to the family. The same argument via `inh:gradient-inheritance` then applies with a nonzero moving term. Alternatively, write "its dissipation is that of the quotient metric" at 12:389.
**Falsifies:** An invariant finite-dimensional family, in the text, on which the anchor phases move and the clause is proved.

## Extensions and improvements

The fastest route to an explicit witness reuses what is now certified. The early phase is analytic for A >= 33.163, and the late phase is the validated region certificate of GAP02-S2-01. The third pair is the scalar comparison ḃ_c = -b_c(1+4p_c), whose band crossing can be certified by the same 1-D interval bisection. What remains are the R-step, the h-step and the initial-data step, and all three go through `open:persistence`. In its present form the lemma multiplies every error by e^{CL} with a global Lipschitz constant. The rigorous bounds are 26.24 for the core and 57.15 for six agents, and they give the absurd scales of GAP02-S2-02. Two changes avoid this. A one-sided version, D^+|Y - X| <= e + mu_bar|Y - X|, needs only the slice supremum mu_bar of the logarithmic norm. Along the A = 34 reference mu_inf is at most 0.204 and integrates to -2.66, so the Gronwall factor should be of order one. Enclosing mu_bar is a four-dimensional and a two-dimensional interval computation of the same kind as `s2`. Then do the h-step relative to each cluster's centre, which removes the common-mode term (2/3) h^2 R^3 and leaves relative errors of order h^2 U^2. With D_R around 7 and R around 2e3 at A = 34, the temperature should come out near 1e-9. The open-law budget becomes Mε < h e_0, with e_0 a fixed fraction of the certified margins (0.37 for crossings, 0.06 for windows). This would turn `open:formation` into a theorem with stated T, horizon and neighborhood radius. That is a prerequisite for the interaction-necessity programme of `open:necessity-open`, because D16-OPEN-10's moderate-separation candidate sits at T between about 1e-6 and 1e-4.

A second improvement concerns the gradient structure of the parent. The two-harmonic product family of GAP02-S2-06 is invariant and finite-dimensional, H_I acts on it freely, properly and isometrically, and its anchor phases move. On this family the gradient-flow clause of `open:quotient-gradient` would be a statement with content. The parent flow is the quotient gradient flow, and the anchored representative moves with a nonzero vertical correction. The slice-metric gradient differs from both, by the computable amount (I_1 - I_2)^2/(4(I_1 + I_2)) in the two-child case.

A third point concerns the regular chart. Since `open:global-anchor`'s non-symmetric representative needs only |I| - 1 anchor moments, the anchored parent flow can be continued through an isolated zero of one moment by re-choosing the distinguished child. A rule that does this, chosen before selection and invariant under relabeling within a block, would make the parent dynamics of `open:all-partitions` global in time on an open dense set of initial laws. The orbit flow is already global, so only the representative needs the rule.
