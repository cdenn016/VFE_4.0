I read the findings of D01-FOUND, D04-DYN, D12-SMOOTH, D13-ERAPUB, D14-BND, D17-PA, D18-LP and XC3-SCOPE that touch this scope, the 2026-10-22 findings L01-FOUND-13, L01-FOUND-15, L03-DYN-03, L03-DYN-04, L06-ERA-12, L07-BND-04, L07-BND-12, L07-BND-15, G2-01 to G2-05 and X6-LIT-14, and the remediation log R-FOUND. Verdict: no stated result in scope fails for want of measurability. Every Banach-chart flow that the manuscript places inside a history law is Borel for the evaluation sigma-algebra, but the manuscript never proves it, and the promise at 01:117 that measurability "is a stated hypothesis" wherever such flows are used is kept nowhere it is needed (chapters 7 and 10; chapter 9 explicitly declines to use it, chapter 12 runs a separable C^r chart, chapters 13 and 14 run finite-dimensional families). I supply the missing proposition: for a chart Fisher gradient flow whose first variation is L^infinity-valued and bounded on bounded sets, the potential is Lipschitz for the L^1 norm on bounded sets, the field is of Baire class one for convergence in measure, and the flow is jointly Borel; a globally Lipschitz non-gradient chart field with a non-Borel time-t map shows that the variational structure, not Lipschitz well-posedness or nonseparability, decides the question. The one substantive defect is in chapters 13 and 14: the declared adaptive protocols admit unboundedly many transactions before a fixed time, and then the history experiment of `lp:actual-experiment` is not differentiable in quadratic mean, so the "declared regular experiment" of `lp:local-realization` needs an explicit restriction on transaction counts.

### GAP04-S4-02: The admitted adaptive protocols allow unboundedly many transactions before a fixed time, and the history experiment of lp:actual-experiment is then not differentiable in quadratic mean
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/14_collective_local_parent.tex:84 ("Policies use the complete history, fixed lab data and optional independent randomization"), :112 ("They preserve the actual collective Fisher cost on the declared regular experiment"), :137 ("The full ordered history remains unbounded in length"), :141-156 (Pi_c, lp:actual-experiment, lp:cost), :184-193 (lp:generator, "full path law"); chapters/13_per_agent_inheritance.tex:299 ("adaptive choices of ports, strengths and waiting times"), :328-334 (pa:joint-record-geometry); chapters/02_geometry.tex:12-28 (geo:dqm); chapters/03_dynamics.tex:422-424 (declared record times; "in the sense of Davis1984")
**Evidence:** The collective Fisher cost C(X, xi) = E_P (s_X + l_xi)^2 of lp:cost, and the minimizing affine class and minimized tensor built from it, presuppose that c -> P_{c,t}(dh, dY) = Pi_c(dh) Q^h_{c,t}(dY) satisfies geo:dqm with a square-integrable score. Nothing in the protocols of 13:299 and 14:84 bounds the number N_t of transactions in [0, t]. Counterexample inside the declared protocol: the policy uses its optional independent randomization to draw N with P(N = n) = 6/(pi^2 n^2) and issues N fresh public queries of one port at waiting times 2^-k, all before t = 1. A fresh public bit has law sigma(L) with public odds L, and L depends on the context whenever the prepared C^2 section of 14:96 has context-dependent l_i. Queries are diagnostics (14:84) and do not move the state, so given c the history law is Pi_c(n, z_1, ..., z_n) = P(N = n) prod_k sigma(L)^[z_k = 1] (1 - sigma(L))^[z_k = -1]; if the state moves between queries, the per-query Fisher information converges to a positive limit as the query times accumulate and the conclusion is unchanged. With rho_1 the one-query Hellinger affinity, the squared Hellinger distance is h^2 = 1 - (6/pi^2) Li_2(rho_1), and h^2/delta^2 is asymptotic to (6/pi^2)(I_1/8)(1 + log(8/(I_1 delta^2))), which diverges, whereas DQM with finite information I would give h^2/delta^2 -> I/8. `s4_history_dqm.out` (40-digit mpmath, I_1 = 0.24446) gives h^2/delta^2 = 0.168, 0.254, 0.340, 0.426, 0.511, 0.597, 0.682 for delta = 1e-1, ..., 1e-7, matching the asymptotic formula to 5e-9 at delta = 1e-7; the controls N = 5 fixed and P(N = n) proportional to n^-3 converge to 5 I_1/8 = 0.15279 and E[N] I_1/8 = 0.04182. The score has E[s^2] = E[N] I_1 = infinity, although every realized branch is finite. If the waiting times accumulate with N infinite (Zeno), the laws for distinct contexts are mutually singular by Kakutani's theorem (affinity rho_1^n = 5.4e-14 after 1e9 queries at delta = 1e-3). The isometry argument of lp:cost stays valid as an identity of costs in [0, infinity], so no displayed equality is false, but for such protocols the minimizing class, isotropy and minimized tensor are undefined, and the text never names the protocol class on which the experiment is regular. The nonflat-window witnesses (14:168-178, and pa:nonflat-window through 13:328) use one fixed finite initial episode and are unaffected. The same non-explosion question arises for def:dyn-record-interrupted, whose declared times 0 < t_1 < t_2 < ... may accumulate; Davis's framework cited at 03:424 assumes a finite-dimensional state space and a non-explosion condition on the expected number of jumps (my recollection of his standard conditions; I could not open the text).
**Fix:** Add to the protocol declarations at 13:299 and 14:84: "Policies are context-independent Borel kernels from finite histories and lab data to admitted actions and waiting times, and on every compact contextual patch sup_c E_c[N_t] < infinity, where N_t is the number of transactions in [0, t]." Then state and prove regularity: if every response density is positive and continuously differentiable in c, with scores bounded on compact tubes, the conditional Fisher information of each transaction is bounded by that of the fresh primitive samples it uses (geo:fisher-loss, 02_geometry.tex:282, for the context-independent readout kernel), the history score is a martingale sum with E[s^2] = E sum_k I_k <= C sup_c E_c[N_t], and DQM follows from continuity of the Fisher information (van der Vaart 1998, Lemma 7.6, locator from memory). The same computation gives the history term of 13:331 in closed form, g^history = E sum_k I_k(H_{<k}). At 03:422 require the record times not to accumulate, or stop the process at the accumulation time.

### GAP04-S4-03: The promised hypothesis is removable: chart Fisher gradient flows with L^infinity first variations are automatically measurable, and only non-gradient chart fields can fail
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/01_foundations.tex:117; chapters/03_dynamics.tex:223-234 (def:dyn-chart-flow, prop:dyn-chart-flow); 2026-10-22 remediation log R-FOUND, row L01-FOUND-13 ("The Picard-iterate lemma is deferred (not verified per chapter, and L^infinity non-separability makes the general lemma non-trivial)")
**Evidence:** Proposition M. Let lambda be a probability measure on a standard Borel space, B_R = {l in L^infinity_0(lambda) : ||l||_inf <= R}, chi(l) = e^l lambda / E_lambda e^l, and Psi = E o chi.
(a) On B_R the trace of the evaluation sigma-algebra (pulled back by chi) equals the Borel sigma-algebra of the L^1(lambda) norm, which equals that of the weak* topology sigma(L^infinity, L^1); (B_R, ||.||_1) is Polish.
(b) If E has first variations phi in L^infinity along C^1 chart curves in the sense of 03:223, with s_R = sup over B_R of ||phi||_inf finite for every R, then |Psi(l) - Psi(l')| <= 2 e^(2R) s_R ||l - l'||_{L^1(lambda)} on B_R, and the chart field V(l) = -(phi - E_lambda phi) is of Baire class one from (B_R, L^1) into (L^infinity, weak*), hence Borel for the evaluation sigma-algebra.
(c) If V is Lipschitz on bounded sets for the sup norm (the hypothesis of prop:dyn-chart-flow), the maximal flow (t, l_0) -> Phi_t(l_0) is jointly Borel on its domain {t < t*(l_0)}, and the escape time t* is Borel.
(d) If V is Lipschitz from (B_R, ||.||_1) into L^1 for every R, as for identity channels, fixed kernels with two-sided density bounds, evaluators and references with bounded log densities, and dynamic or Gibbs rows (the classes of prop:dyn-chart-flow, chapter 9 and chapter 10), then ||Phi_t l - Phi_t l'||_1 <= e^(K_R t) ||l - l'||_1 as long as both trajectories stay in B_R.
Proof. (a) For g in L^infinity, which lies in L^1 because lambda is finite, the pairing l -> <l, g> is weak*-continuous; for g in L^1 it is the uniform limit on B_R of pairings with L^infinity functions g_n, since |<l, g - g_n>| <= R ||g - g_n||_1. The two Borel structures therefore agree on B_R, which is closed in the separable space L^1. chi is continuous and injective from (B_R, L^1) into the laws with the total-variation norm, the law space with the evaluation sigma-algebra is standard Borel (Kechris 1995, Theorem 17.24, locator from memory), and the Lusin-Souslin theorem (Kechris 1995, Theorem 15.1) makes chi a Borel isomorphism onto a Borel image. (b) Along l_s = l' + s(l - l') the definition of the first variation gives dPsi/ds = Cov_{q_s}(phi(l_s), l - l'), and |Cov_q(phi, w)| <= 2 ||phi||_inf ||dq/dlambda||_inf ||w||_{L^1(lambda)} with ||dq/dlambda||_inf <= e^(2R) on B_R; the mean value theorem gives the Lipschitz bound. For w in L^infinity put v_w(l) = w Z(l) e^(-l) - E_lambda[w Z(l) e^(-l)], Z(l) = E_lambda e^l. Then <phi - E_q phi, w>_lambda = Cov_q(phi, v_w(l)) = lim_n n[Psi(l + v_w(l)/n) - Psi(l)], and each term is L^1-continuous on B_R, because Psi is (by the Lipschitz bound on a slightly larger ball) and l -> v_w(l) is a Nemytskii map of bounded functions. The case w = 1 gives E_lambda phi - E_q phi, so <V(l), w> is a pointwise limit of L^1-continuous functions for every w in L^infinity, and for every w in L^1 by uniform approximation; V maps B_R into a bounded set, on which these pairings generate the Borel structure. (c) Truncate V_R = theta(||l||_inf) V with theta a Lipschitz cutoff equal to one on [0, R]; ||.||_inf is lower semicontinuous for L^1 on bounded sets, so V_R is Borel, and it is globally sup-norm Lipschitz and bounded. The Picard iterates l^(n+1)_t = l_0 + int_0^t V_R(l^(n)_s) ds are jointly Borel by induction: for g in L^1 the integrand <V_R(l^(n)_s(l_0)), g> is Borel in l_0 and continuous in s, hence jointly measurable as a Caratheodory function (Aliprantis and Border 2006, Lemma 4.51, locator from memory), and Fubini applies. The iterates converge uniformly in sup norm on compact time intervals, hence pointwise in L^1, and Phi = Phi^R before the exit time of B_R, which is Borel because the supremum over rational times of the Borel function ||Phi^R_s(l_0)||_inf is Borel. (d) Gronwall's inequality for t -> ||x(t) - y(t)||_1.
Sharpness. On L^infinity_0([0,1], Lebesgue) let e_s = 1_[0,s] - s (pairwise sup distance at least 1/2), g_0 = 1_[0,1/2] - 1_(1/2,1], E a non-Borel subset of [0,1], c(f) = max(0, 1/8 - dist_inf(f, {e_s : s in E})) and V(f) = c(f) g_0. V is globally 1-Lipschitz and bounded, and it has the chart form -(phi - E_lambda phi) with phi = -c g_0, so the Picard-Lindelof argument of prop:dyn-chart-flow applies. Its unique flow satisfies Phi_t(e_s) = e_s + (1 - e^(-t)) 1_E(s) g_0 / 8: on the orbit from e_s with s in E the distance to the family is the coefficient a of g_0, so a' = 1/8 - a, and for s not in E the field vanishes at e_s. Hence s -> <Phi_t(e_s), g_0> = <e_s, g_0> + (1 - e^(-t)) 1_E(s)/8 is not Borel, while s -> e_s is L^1-continuous, and Phi_t is not Borel for the evaluation sigma-algebra. By (b), phi is not the first variation of any potential. `s3_nongradient_counterexample.out` checks the mechanism with a finite E: separation 0.5005, sampled sup-norm Lipschitz quotient 0.9997, orbit coefficient at t = 2 equal to (1 - e^(-2))/8 to 2e-12 on E and zero off E, and ||Phi_2(e_s) - Phi_2(e_s')||_1 = 0.108 at ||e_s - e_s'||_1 = 0.001. The reason given at 01:117, the strict inclusion of the evaluation sigma-algebra in the norm-Borel sigma-algebra, is correct (D01-FOUND reconstructed it), but it does not bite on any flow the manuscript uses: the dividing line is the variational form of the field.
**Fix:** Add Proposition M with this proof (about fifteen lines) after prop:dyn-chart-flow, and the sharpness example as a remark. State the per-agent version: each own-block field is the own-law first variation of F_i, and F_i is L^1-Lipschitz on bounded chart sets in all its arguments when its dependence on peer laws has L^infinity first variations (identity channels, and the fixed or marginal-reading channels of chapters 13 and 14), so (b) to (d) apply to the density-level per-agent flows if they are defined as XC3-SCOPE-02 proposes.
**Falsifies:** A potential with L^infinity first variations bounded on bounded sets that is not L^1-continuous on B_R (which would break (b)), or a chart flow in chapters 7 or 10 whose field is not of this form.

The chapters use the metric by declaration (10:94, "Thus no new mobility is introduced"), so nothing they state is wrong.
**Fix:** At 03:234 write "... singled out, among local and weakly continuous tensor fields on parametrized measure models, by invariance under sufficient statistics (Cencov 1982 for finite sample spaces; Ay, Jost, Le and Schwachhofer 2015, Theorem 2.10(2), for general ones)", and say at 03:194 that the product argument applies on general spaces with that theorem in place of Cencov's.

### GAP04-S4-06: Weak-strong uniqueness holds beyond the chart class for identity and fixed bounded-kernel channels
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/10_boundary_closure.tex:50-71 (lem:boundary-fine-wellposed); chapters/03_dynamics.tex:228-234 (prop:dyn-chart-flow); chapters/07_smooth_closure.tex:197-215 (smooth:wellposed), :253-254 (smooth:exponential-family)
**Evidence:** Statement. In the setting of lem:boundary-fine-wellposed let (q_i(t), s_i(t), beta(t), gamma(t)), t in [0, T], have densities positive almost everywhere, be continuous in total variation, have finite costs, have rows that solve the row equation, have forces with int_0^T E_{q_i}|h_i^q| dt finite (and likewise in the model sector), and satisfy int f dq_i(t) = int f dq_i(0) - int_0^t int f (h_i^q - E_{q_i} h_i^q) dq_i ds for every bounded measurable f. If the initial log densities are bounded, this family is the chart solution. Proof sketch. Disintegrating the finite signed measure (h_i - c_i) q_i ds lambda(dxi) on [0, T] x X gives, for almost every xi, absolute continuity of t -> q_i(t, xi) and the pointwise Caratheodory equation with inputs V_i^q(t, r) (|V| <= ||log rho_i||_inf for every probability s_i), c_i in L^1(0, T) and rows in [0, 1]. At an agent index maximizing w_i = log q_i(t, xi) one has w_i - w_j >= 0 and q_l/q_i <= 1, so the derivative of w_i is at most -M + K(t); at a minimizing index it is at least -m - K(t); here K(t) = max_i(||log rho_i||_inf + |c_i(t)|) + N is integrable. Hence M(t) <= M(0) + int_0^T K and m(t) >= m(0) - int_0^T K, positivity persists, and the log densities are uniformly bounded. Then costs, centerings and base potentials are continuous in t by dominated convergence, the pointwise derivative is uniformly continuous in t uniformly in xi, t -> log q_i(t) is C^1 into L^infinity, and the uniqueness of lem:boundary-fine-wellposed applies. The same argument works for fixed kernels with two-sided density bounds, with the bounds of D04-DYN-03 for log nu_ij and for the source integrals, and for the private sector of chapter 7, whose pointwise equation d_t l = H - l is linear given H; the latter supports the extension beyond (H1) that smooth:exponential-family asserts. For law-dependent public channels under (H3) the force is defined only on bounded chart sets, so the question cannot be posed without a structural hypothesis on the channel derivative, and it is open there. **2. Measurability of (initial datum, time) -> state.** Yes for every chart flow of chapters 3, 7 and 10, despite the nonseparability of L^infinity: on bounded chart sets the evaluation sigma-algebra is the Borel sigma-algebra of convergence in measure, the potentials are L^1-Lipschitz there because their first variations are L^infinity-valued and bounded, the fields are of Baire class one, and the Picard iterates are jointly Borel (Proposition M, GAP04-S4-03). For identity channels and fixed bounded kernels the flows are moreover L^1-Lipschitz on bounded invariant sets. For an arbitrary sup-norm Lipschitz chart field the answer is no (sharpness example in GAP04-S4-03).

**3. Almost-everywhere identification, jointly measurable versions and Borel representatives in chapter 10.** D14-BND-09 reported that the proof of prop:boundary-joint-spectrum is at sketch level; the following lemmas complete it, and the same lemmas are what lem:boundary-global (Dini estimates per base point) and the base-dependent automorphisms of sec:boundary-symmetry use.
Lemma C (versions). If x: [0, T) -> L^infinity(lambda) is continuous, there is a jointly measurable X on [0, T) x X with X(t, .) in the class x(t) for every t and t -> X(t, xi) continuous for every xi. Choose representatives X_s at rational s; off the null set given by the union over rational pairs of {|X_s - X_s'| > ||x(s) - x(s')||_inf}, s -> X_s(xi) is uniformly continuous on compact rational sets with the modulus of x, so it extends continuously; X is the pointwise limit of X at floor(nt)/n, hence jointly measurable. If x is C^1 with derivative y, take the version Y of y and X(t, xi) = X(0, xi) + int_0^t Y(s, xi) ds; the Bochner integral of a continuous L^infinity-valued curve is represented pointwise by the Riemann integral, since Riemann sums on rational partitions converge in L^infinity and pointwise off one null set.
Lemma D (frozen pointwise flow). Let x(t) be the fine solution on [0, T*) and take versions V_i^q(t, r), V_i^s(t, w) continuous in t (Lemma C applied to the C^1 curves t -> V(t) in L^infinity), with scalar inputs c_i(t), beta(t), gamma(t). For r outside an A-null set and every w_0 in R^N, the equation dw_i/dt = -(w_i + V_i(t, r) + sum_j beta_ij (w_i - w_j) - sum_l beta_li e^(w_l - w_i) - c_i) has a unique solution Psi_t(r, w_0) on all of [0, T*): the maximum principle of GAP04-S4-06, with the continuous K(t), bounds it on compact subintervals uniformly for w_0 in bounded sets. Psi is continuous in (t, w_0) and measurable in r, hence jointly Borel.
Lemma E (identification). For every t in [0, T*), x(t)(xi) = Psi_t(r(xi), x(0)(xi)) for lambda-almost every xi, and with the Lemma C version the exceptional set does not depend on t. The curve z(t) = Psi_t(r(.), x(0)(.)) is bounded on compacts, its t-derivative is uniformly continuous in t uniformly in xi, so z is C^1 into L^infinity and solves the frozen-input L^infinity equation, whose field is Lipschitz on bounded sets uniformly in t; x solves the same equation because the frozen inputs are its own; uniqueness gives z = x.
Completion of prop:boundary-joint-spectrum. For a second configuration y(0) with the same mu^q(0), mu^s(0) and rows, the curve z^y(t) = Psi_t(r, y(0)) has joint law (id, Psi_t)_# mu^q(0), which by Lemma E is the joint law of x(t); so z^y carries the spectra, base marginals, costs and centerings of x(t), hence its own inputs equal the frozen ones, so it is a C^1 chart solution of the fine flow from y(0), and the existence intervals coincide because z^y has the essential bounds of x. For a base-only assimilation the normalizers Z_i are agent-dependent and determined by mu^q (D14-BND-09). `s2_ch10_fine_flow.out` (b) tests the proposition beyond the symmetry group: a reference whose base fibers have atoms of masses 1/2, 1/4, 1/4, and two configurations with equal joint laws that no reference-preserving bijection relates (the value vector on the heavy atom of one is carried by the two light atoms of the other); at t = 2 the predicted value-vector exchange holds to 2.1e-17 and the rows agree to 2.2e-16.
Lemma F (canonical representatives and descended flow). The canonicalization of 10:228 is Borel, Aut(T_d)-invariant and selects an orbit point, and c o f = c o f' almost everywhere iff f' = tau . f for a measurable tau (first matching element of a fixed enumeration of the finite group). The descended flow c o Phi_t is Borel because the profile flow is L^1-Lipschitz on the ball of lem:boundary-global (Proposition M (d)); the descended record update needs L_e^q jointly measurable (GAP04-S4-01).

**4. General-space Fisher metric and weak-strong uniqueness.** The unit-mobility metric is justified on general spaces by the product computation together with Ay, Jost, Le and Schwachhofer's Theorem 2.10(2), under its locality and weak-continuity hypotheses, which 03:234 omits (GAP04-S4-04); the chapters' own symmetry groups do not select it. **6. Smoothness of the chart field on bounded L^infinity balls at 09:39.** The claim holds, in a stronger form: the reduced chart field V of eq:public-phase-chart-flow is real-analytic on all of E^N, because it is a finite composition of the analytic exponential of the Banach algebra L^infinity, bounded linear expectations, logarithms of scalars bounded away from zero, and softmax rows. ## Extensions and improvements

The most useful single addition is Proposition M in chapter 3 (GAP04-S4-03). With it and with the joint-measurability and non-explosion clauses of GAP04-S4-01 and -02, the record-interrupted dynamics of def:dyn-record-interrupted becomes a Markov process on the Borel chart image with Borel transition kernels J_k(x, dx', do) = R(Phi_{t_k - t_{k-1}}(x), do) delta_{A(Phi(x), o)}(dx'), stopped at the escape time, and every finite adaptive history statement of chapters 7 and 10 becomes an intertwining (R_ret x id)_# J_k = J_k^c o R_ret checked kernel by kernel, which is what their proofs already compute. The sharpness example belongs next to it, because it shows the reader that the variational form is the operative hypothesis and that a non-gradient modification of a chart field (for example an ad hoc moving-frame term or a fitted parent force) would need its own measurability argument.

A protocol class for history experiments should be declared once, in chapter 13, and cited in chapter 14 (GAP04-S4-02). The natural class is: context-independent Borel policies with optional independent randomization, positive response densities continuously differentiable in the context with scores bounded on compact tubes, and sup_c E_c[N_t] < infinity. On it the history experiment is DQM, and the martingale identity E[s_hist^2] = E sum_k I_k(H_{<k}) gives g^history in closed form, which would also settle the vanishing noted in D17-PA-06 by direct computation (the generative predictive kernels of the witness do not depend on c, so every I_k is zero). A deterministic bound on N_t is simpler and suffices for every witness in the manuscript.

The chapter 10 symmetry reduction does not depend on the Fisher choice of mobility (GAP04-S4-04 (iii)). For every alpha the gradient flow of E for G^alpha, f' = -f^alpha (h - c_alpha) with c_alpha = int f^alpha h / int f^alpha, is S-equivariant and well posed in the same bounded-log charts, so lem:boundary-equivariance, the fixed-point characterization and the descent of base-only records hold verbatim, while the profile equations, the inherited profile metric and the contrast rate of prop:boundary-contrast-decay change (the linearized rate acquires the base-dependent factor f_0(r)^(alpha - 1)). Stating this would separate what the hierarchy owes to symmetry from what it owes to the Fisher geometry. Difficulty: low.

Weak-strong uniqueness (GAP04-S4-06) costs one remark for identity and fixed bounded-kernel channels. For law-dependent channels the natural hypothesis is the one D04-DYN-03 proposes for global existence, a bound C(1 + |log q|) on the channel-derivative force near the essential infimum; with it the maximum-principle step of GAP04-S4-06 goes through with an exponential-in-time floor, and weak solutions with integrable forces are chart solutions. Channels whose first variation leaves L^infinity, such as the D12-SMOOTH-01 example, are outside every bounded log-density chart; an L^2 log-density chart would accommodate their L^2 first variations but loses the Banach-algebra exponential, so it is a separate construction of medium difficulty.
