## Findings

### D15-WISH-01: form:kappa-independence (iii) computes a null flow that the manuscript elsewhere declares to be a different control
**Category:** error
**Severity:** high
**Location:** chapters/11_conditional_formation.tex:316 and :324 (form:kappa-independence (iii) and its proof), :6, :393; chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1380 (inh:null-flow), :1394; chapters/13_per_agent_inheritance.tex:18-23 (pa:null-row); companion/c13_per_agent_inheritance.tex:44-51 (cp:null-row); Item_7_formation_feedback.tex:50-54 (it7:null-row); companion/c11_formation.tex:7, :115
**Evidence:** The proof of (iii) writes the null belief potential as `sum_i [KL(q_i || W_2(alpha_0, I_2)) + KL(q_i || W_2(alpha_0, Lambda_i^0))]`, which puts weight one on the self cost. inh:null-flow deletes "every peer cost D_ij with j != i" with "all other data unchanged", and three documents say how this is read. pa:null-row: "The null of inh:null-flow deletes the nonself costs, not their labels or priors ... Replacing these rows by self probability one would specify a different control." cp:null-row: "Setting either self weight to one would change this control." it7:null-row: "The null flow is that of chapter 5 ... labels and priors are kept." With the chapter 11 priors pi_ij = 1/4 and exact Gibbs rows, the declared null has self weight `beta0_ii = exp(-D_ii/T)/(exp(-D_ii/T) + 3) <= 1/4` and reduced belief potential `sum_i [KL(q_i || W_2(alpha_0, I_2)) - T log(exp(-D_ii/T)/4 + 3/4)]`. The calculation of form:full-flow then gives `d alpha_i/dt = -(1 + beta0_ii)(alpha_i - alpha_0)` and `dP_i/dt = -(1 + beta0_ii) P_i + I_2 + beta0_ii C_i^0`, with C_i^0 as in the chapter.

s2 (a) checks both nulls by a 30-digit natural gradient on four agents with unequal precisions (residuals at most 9.9e-31). Under the chapter 11 null every shape rate is 2.00000. Under the declared null the rates of agents 2 to 4 are 1.218, 1.129 and 1.067 at kappa = 0, and 1.034, 1.006 and 1.0005 at kappa = 1. s2 (b): from the chapter example the declared null gives the same partition sequence, but every event time changes (0.559, 1.025, 1.799 at kappa = 0, against 0.347, 0.549, 1.070). For gaps (0.30, 0.60, 0.29), which satisfy form:gaps, the full flow joins {3,4} at 0.532 and then {1,2}. The declared null joins {1,2} first, at 0.890 for kappa = 0 and at 1.041 for kappa = 1. s2 (c): at kappa = 1 the reversal holds for every tested d3 from 0.280 to 0.299. The event-time margins are strict, so it persists under small perturbations of all shapes and precisions, which makes it an open subset of the admitted class.

Under the declared definition, therefore, the sentence "Hence P^0(x)=P(x), and indeed H^0(x)=H(x) including event times, at every point x of the admitted class" is false, P != P^0 on a nonempty open set, and (F5) is formally satisfied. This holds at kappa = 0 as well, where no agent interacts. The control is then detecting the reweighting of the nonidentity self row, not the interaction. Its verdict also depends on T_q: as T_q grows, beta0_ii tends to 1/4 for every agent, the rates become a common 5/4, and the sequences agree again. The strong-form failure still holds, but only because the final partition is the grand block for every admitted datum. The two-stage classification survives because (F6b) fails, which itself rests on a reading (D15-WISH-07). The same ambiguity affects c11:115 ("With the peer interaction switched off, the same events occur at the same times"), which is true at kappa = 0 and false for the declared null.
**Fix:** Declare one null-row convention inside inh:null-flow and use it in chapters 11 and 13, companion c13 and Item 7.

1. If the chapter 13 convention stands, replace (iii) by the computed null law, and state that H^0 != H for every preparation and P^0 != P on an open set. Conclude that (F5) holds formally here but carries no information about interaction for constructions with nonidentity self channels. Report instead the coupling-deformation control that (i) and (ii) actually establish: an identical membership history for every kappa >= 0.
2. If the chapter 11 convention stands (self weight one, equivalent to deleting the nonself labels), state it in inh:null-flow and correct pa:null-row, cp:null-row and it7:null-row.

In both cases, justify the strong-form failure by the constancy of the final partition, as chapters/06_fusion.tex:666 does.
**Falsifies:** A reading of inh:null-flow, consistent with "all other data unchanged" and with pa:null-row, under which the null self weight is one; or an error in the s2 natural-gradient check of the stated null field.

### D15-WISH-02: "Interaction-sensitive selection ... therefore needs targets that depend on the receiver" does not follow from form:mean-field
**Category:** inflation
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/11_conditional_formation.tex:208 (paragraph after form:mean-field); the same inference at chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1371-1373, companion/c11_formation.tex:79 ("Making the interaction matter for grouping requires targets that depend on the receiver") and companion/c05_inheritance.tex:251-252
**Evidence:** form:mean-field constrains selectors that read centered log-likelihood ratios between agents. A relabeling-equivariant selector can instead read the cost of each agent, `D_i = KL(q_i || nu[M_q])`, a term of the same potential. On the chapter family, `D_i - D_j = [F(alpha_i) - F(alpha_j)] + (alpha_i - alpha_j) tau(t)`, where `F(a) = (a - alpha_0) psi_2(a) - log Gamma_2(a)` is nonlinear with its minimum at alpha_0, and `tau = Tr(Lambda_q P^{-1}) - 2` depends on kappa through P(t) and Lambda_q.

s5 (f) uses the rule "join the first pair whose |D_i - D_j| reaches delta from above", along the full flow with receiver-independent targets. The first joined pair differs between kappa = 0 and kappa = 5 in 16 of 150 random three-agent preparations. For shapes (0.772, 1.393, 1.455), for example, the rule joins agents 1 and 2 at kappa = 0 and agents 2 and 3 at kappa = 5. The coupling enters only through the common scalar tau, so this selector is coupling sensitive without any receiver-dependent target.
**Fix:** Restrict the conclusion to selectors that read only relative (centered log-ratio) data. Add that selectors reading interaction-dependent absolute quantities, such as costs or precisions, can be coupling sensitive in this architecture, so coupling sensitivity of a partition sequence is relative to the selector class. A form of interaction necessity that selector design alone cannot meet must restrict the selector class or require the amplifying form.
**Falsifies:** An error in s5 (f), or a reading of the sentence as restricted to selectors of centered log-ratios.

### D15-WISH-06: "The range nu > 1/2 ... is Gindikin set" misstates the Gindikin set
**Category:** citation
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/11_conditional_formation.tex:25
**Evidence:** For the cone of real symmetric positive-definite 2 x 2 matrices, the set of shapes for which a central Wishart (Riesz) law exists is {0, 1/2} together with (1/2, infinity). The points 0 and 1/2 give singular laws that have no density of the form form:wishart. The interval nu > 1/2 is therefore the absolutely continuous part of the Gindikin set, not the set itself. The companion phrasing (c11:22, "nu>1/2 is exactly the range of real shapes for which this density is a probability law") is correct. I relied on the standard statement of Gindikin's theorem (as in Faraut and Koranyi, Analysis on Symmetric Cones, Chapter VII, and in the cited Letac and Massam); I did not open Gindikin 1975 itself.
**Fix:** "The range nu > 1/2 is the absolutely continuous part of the Gindikin set {0, 1/2} together with (1/2, infinity)."
**Falsifies:** A version of the Gindikin theorem for the 2 x 2 real cone whose set is (1/2, infinity) alone.

### D15-WISH-09: Within its class, form:mean-field already closes the vector escape route stated after form:scalar-closure
**Category:** improvement
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/11_conditional_formation.tex:219; chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1320-1327
**Evidence:** The text says that vector parameters with a population-dependent generator that is not a multiple of the identity escape form:scalar-closure. By form:mean-field, in every exponential family under the unit-Fisher reciprocal flow with receiver-independent targets and additive moment channels, natural-parameter differences obey `d/dt (theta_i - theta_j) = -2 (theta_i - theta_j)`. The common propagator on differences is therefore -2 times the identity, whatever the dimension and the coupling. In the Wishart instance this is the pair of identities for shapes and precisions (form:precision-difference). An anisotropic common propagator cannot arise in this class at all. The escape requires receiver-dependent targets (nonconstant Gibbs rows), unequal mobilities, or a selector reading data other than natural-parameter differences (compare D15-WISH-02).
**Fix:** Add this sentence after form:scalar-closure, and in chapter 5 after inh:scalar-additive, so that the open construction is not sought inside the class where it is impossible.
**Falsifies:** A unit-Fisher reciprocal flow with receiver-independent targets whose natural-parameter differences evolve under a non-scalar common propagator; form:mean-field excludes this.

## Extensions and improvements

The first need is an interaction control that does not depend on a row convention. For a declared one-parameter family of constitutive interactions Φ_κ, whose member Φ_0 has no law-dependent peer channel, the coupling-deformation clause would require P(Φ_κ x) ≠ P(Φ_0 x) for some κ on a nonempty open set of preparations. Chapter 11 fails this clause cleanly by form:kappa-independence (i) and (ii), with identical membership histories, event times included, for every κ ≥ 0. The verdict then no longer hinges on how the null treats a nonidentity self row. If the null-flow clause is kept, its rows must be specified. With exact Gibbs rows and zero-cost peer entries kept, the effective self weight drops from one to β⁰_ii ≤ π_ii, and the null clock becomes agent dependent even without interaction (D15-WISH-01). Difficulty: low for the definition, but the verdicts of chapters 6, 12 and 13 and of Item 7 must be rechecked under whichever convention is adopted.

The second is a selector-class theorem that makes the chapter negative result sharp. Let the general linear group act on all belief laws simultaneously by congruence X ↦ BXB^T, and independently on all model laws. On the common-precision Wishart family a maximal invariant of the tuple of laws under this action is the tuple of shapes, since each common precision can be moved to I_2. A joint action with one B on both sectors would leave the relative precision P^{-1/2} Q P^{-1/2}, which depends on κ, as a further invariant. Shapes evolve independently of κ, so every selector that is a function of the laws modulo this action produces a κ-independent partition sequence. The cost-reading selector of D15-WISH-02 escapes only because it reads the references and channel targets, whose I_2 breaks the congruence. For parents, the cost KL(q_I ‖ W_2(n_I α_0, Λ_q)) is a function of the retained state (A_I, n_I, P). A cost-reading threshold selector would therefore leave form:parent and form:responses untouched, and it would pass a coupling-deformation test while the coupling enters only through the common scalar τ(t; κ). A useful standard must therefore either restrict the selector class or require the amplifying form. The congruence statement is a few lines; the amplification test for the cost-reading selector is a small computation.

The third is a partial answer, inside the Wishart family, to the question left open after form:mean-field: whether exact sum closure is compatible with receiver-selective rows. Exact parent channels for a block require the sum of independent child outputs W_2(α_0, Λ_i), i in I, to be Wishart. If the sum were W_2(ν′, Λ′), its log-Laplace transform −α_0 Σ_i log det(I + Λ_i^{-1}T) would equal −ν′ log det(I + Λ′^{-1}T) for every small symmetric T. Writing Z_i = S^{-1/2} Λ_i^{-1} S^{-1/2} with S = Σ_i Λ_i^{-1}, the first-order terms give ν′ Λ′^{-1} = α_0 S, and the second-order terms give Σ_i Tr(Z_i H Z_i H) = (α_0/ν′) Tr(H^2) for every symmetric H. Rank-one H in every direction then forces each Z_i to be diagonal in every basis, so Z_i = w_i I_2. The third-order terms give Σ_i w_i Σ_i w_i^3 = (Σ_i w_i^2)^2, which is equality in the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, so all w_i agree. Hence every Λ_i equals Λ′ and ν′ = n α_0; the same argument applies with unequal shapes. Receiver-dependent target precisions inside a block that forms therefore destroy exact closure in this family. Source-dependent targets W_2(α_0, Λ_j) remain compatible on the channel side, because all receivers of one primitive source draw share Λ_j. They make the Gibbs rows receiver selective through the receiver laws, so the open problem reduces to one requirement: the parent Gibbs row computed from KL(q_I ‖ W_2(n_I α_0, Λ_j)) must equal the aggregated child rows. That is a concrete, checkable condition, and I expect it to fail generically, which would settle the obstruction for this family. Difficulty: low for the closure statement, moderate for the row-aggregation analysis.

The fixed points solve a cubic, so implicit differentiation gives a closed form for the limit tensor as a function of κ, a and ã. Stating it would turn the remark into a proposition about how interaction rescales inherited contextual geometry. Difficulty: low.

The fifth is approximate inheritance off the common-precision family (form:approximate-open). s5 (e) confirms the second-order cancellation, with defect ratios 3.98 to 3.99 per halving. The ratio ρ = ‖P_i − P_j‖/|α_i − α_j| is invariant, so the precision mismatch at a singleton join is δρ. With a deviation of second order at the level of natural parameters, the relative-entropy defect of the moment-matched parent is of fourth order, (δρ)^4, and decays afterwards. A proof needs a quantitative bound of the Kullback-Leibler divergence of the sum law from its moment-matched Wishart law, for example a chi-square bound computed from Laplace transforms, together with a Gronwall estimate on the remaining horizon. Difficulty: moderate.
