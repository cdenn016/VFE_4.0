### D01-FOUND-01: The reciprocal dynamics does respond to a loop transport in the stabilizer of the transported law
**Category:** error
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/01_foundations.tex:246 (paragraph after prop:loop-blindness) and :87 (instantaneous definition of a gauge distinction). The sentence originates in the evidence of 2026-10-22 L01-FOUND-02 ("so the dynamics as defined cannot respond to holonomy in the stabilizer of the transported law"). The comparison statements at chapters/02_interactions.tex:217 and companion/c01_objects.tex:68 concern cost values and are correct.
**Evidence:** The text says "Every interaction cost of \cref{ch:interactions,ch:dynamics} compares laws, so the dynamics defined there cannot respond to a loop transport that lies in the stabilizer of the transported law", and then "A record that is sensitive to such a transport must read a coupling of the same sample along the routes". Part (i) of prop:loop-blindness concerns the value of a functional of $(\nu,H_\#\nu)$ at one configuration. A gradient flow depends on the cost as a function on a neighborhood, and the two functions $\nu\mapsto\KL(\cdot\Vert H_\#\nu)$ and $\nu\mapsto\KL(\cdot\Vert H'_\#\nu)$ agree only on the set where $H_\#\nu=H'_\#\nu$. The receiver first variation $\log(d\nu_i/dH_\#\nu_j)$ depends on $H$ only through $H_\#\nu_j$, but the source-role first variation of $\KL(\nu_i\Vert H_\#\nu_j)$ is $y\mapsto-(d\nu_i/dH_\#\nu_j)(Hy)$, the receiver likelihood ratio pulled back through the transport itself (the source term in the proof of prop:dyn-chart-flow). It depends on $H$ pointwise. Two exact witnesses on $\mathbb Z_4$, with the unit Fisher flow on finite positive tables, permutation channels, fixed rows and $\beta=1$ (`loop_dynamics_check.py`, RK4 with 4000 steps on $[0,1]$), follow.

(a) Self-loop. One agent with a nonidentity self channel, which def:int-channel admits ("later constructions may declare a nonidentity self channel"): $\mathcal E=\KL(\nu\Vert r)+\KL(\nu\Vert H_\#\nu)$ with $r=(0.5,0.25,0.15,0.10)$ and $\nu(0)$ uniform, which every cyclic shift preserves. For $H=\id$ and $H=$ shift the velocities at $t=0$ agree (difference 0.0, since the loop force vanishes on the stabilizer), but at $t=1$ the frame-invariant quantities differ: $\KL(\nu(1)\Vert r)=0.024003$ against $0.094777$, entropy $1.312484$ against $1.371068$, loop comparison $0$ against $0.034311$. The identity and the shift are not conjugate, so no passive change relates the two configurations.

(b) Two-cycle $i\leftarrow j\leftarrow i$ with $B=(\Theta_{ij},\Theta_{ji})=(\id,\id)$ and $A=(\text{shift},\id)$, $\nu_j(0)$ uniform, $\nu_i(0)=(0.1,0.2,0.3,0.4)$, $r_i=(0.4,0.3,0.2,0.1)$, $r_j$ uniform. At $t=0$ both edge costs coincide (0.10644014 and 0.12177727 in both), so every row and every compared law agrees, but the source log-velocity of $j$ differs at first order, $(-1.3945,-0.3014,0.5041,1.1918)$ against $(-0.9945,0.0986,0.9041,-0.0082)$, and so do the time derivatives of the gauge-invariant edge costs at $t=0$: $(\dot D_{ij},\dot D_{ji})=(-1.216687,-1.485988)$ against $(-0.979978,-1.249281)$ (Richardson-extrapolated forward differences). At $t=1$ the edge costs are $(0.004646,0.004741)$ against $(0.008422,0.006414)$, the local terms $(0.066948,0.000831)$ against $(0.062735,0.000369)$, and the loop comparison at $j$ is $0$ against $0.000738$. The loop classes at $j$ are the identity and the shift, so no passive change relates $A$ and $B$.

(c) Control. For a single edge $i\leftarrow j$ with a uniform source the shift is removable by a passive change at $j$. The run gives identical receivers and source(shift) equal to the shift-inverse pushforward of source(id) to 0.0, as covariance requires, so the responses in (a) and (b) are not frame artifacts.

Mechanism, checked at second order: for the self-loop, $\KL(\nu(t)\Vert H_\#\nu(t))=\tfrac{t^2}2\sum_y\nu_0(y)(v(y)-v(H^{-1}y))^2+O(t^3)$, with $v$ the record-free log-velocity. The ratio to $t^2$ is 0.408222, 0.422342 and 0.429606 at $t=0.02$, $0.01$, $0.005$, against the predicted 0.437011, with the gap halving with $t$. The second sentence fails with the first, since a readout of an invariant of an agent's own law at a later time (its entropy, or its divergence from its reference) separates the loop classes with no content law. Witness (b) also shows that the definition at :87 is instantaneous: no row, cost or compared law separates $A$ from $B$ at $t=0$, and the dynamics separates them afterwards.
**Fix:** Replace the first sentence at :246 by: "Each interaction cost, and each receiver force, depends on a transport only through the transported law, so at one configuration no law-level comparison separates two transports with the same image law. The source-role force of the reciprocal potential pulls the receiver likelihood ratio back through the transport, so the reciprocal dynamics separates them along trajectories; for a self-loop at a stabilized law the separation appears at second order in time." Restrict the second sentence to comparisons at one instant ("A record that is sensitive to such a transport at the instant of comparison must read a coupling of the same sample along the routes"). At :87, either define a gauge distinction relative to the time-evolved algebra (elements of the algebra composed with the flow at all times) or say that the definition is instantaneous.
**Falsifies:** A gauge-invariant function that agrees along both flows of (a) or of (b) for all $t$. The computed $t^2$ coefficient and the $t=1$ values exclude this at the tested instances.

### D01-FOUND-02: The content-law paraphrase of prop:dyn-no-global-vfe is broader than the proposition and fails on infinite content spaces
**Category:** inflation
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/01_foundations.tex:226 (last sentence of sec:content-laws) and :197 (hypotheses omitted); compare chapters/03_dynamics.tex:375-391 (prop:dyn-no-global-vfe).
**Evidence:** Line 226 reads: "The reciprocal potential of \cref{ch:dynamics} is a separate constitutive object defined on such lists, and \cref{prop:dyn-no-global-vfe} shows that it is not $\mathcal F(Q;o)$ for any fixed normalized model with these marginals as recognition laws." In context, "these marginals" are the marginals of a recognition law $Q$ on the coefficient space $\mathsf Z$ of def:content-law. Chapter 3 proves (a) on finite alphabets, for finitely many finite-valued model variables and any rule $x\mapsto Q(x)$, and (b) with fixed channels, for the product recognition $\bigotimes_k\zeta_k$ whose factors are the agent laws. Neither covers a non-product $Q$ on an infinite $\mathsf Z$, and the claim is false there. Witness (`copula_witness.py`): two agents, a trivial model sector, $\mathsf K_1=\mathsf K_2=\R$, references $\mathcal N(0,1)$, one edge $1\leftarrow2$ through the identity channel with fixed weight $\beta=0.8$, so that up to fixed row constants $\mathcal E(x)=\KL(q_1\Vert\mathcal N(0,1))+\KL(q_2\Vert\mathcal N(0,1))+\beta\KL(q_1\Vert q_2)$. Take $\mathsf Z=\R^2$, $f_i(z)=z_i$, $P_0=\mathcal N(0,1)\otimes\mathcal N(0,1)$ and no record. For a product $P_0$, $\KL(Q\Vert P_0)=I_Q(Z_1;Z_2)+\sum_i\KL(Q_i\Vert P_{0,i})$, and a Gaussian copula with correlation $\rho$ has mutual information $-\tfrac12\log(1-\rho^2)$ for atomless marginals. The rule "$Q(x)$ is the Gaussian-copula coupling of $(q_1,q_2)$ with $-\tfrac12\log(1-\rho(x)^2)=\beta\KL(q_1\Vert q_2)$" gives $\mathcal E(x)=\mathcal F(Q(x);o)$ plus a constant at every configuration with atomless laws and finite $\mathcal E$, and $Q(x)$ has the agent laws as marginals. With Gaussian marginals the maximum of $|\mathcal E-\mathcal F|$ over five random mean pairs is $8.9\times10^{-16}$. With Laplace marginals the decomposed evaluation (copula information by 120-point Gauss-Hermite, marginal divergences by adaptive quadrature) gives $|\mathcal E-\mathcal F|=4.4\times10^{-16}$ at $m=(0.3,-0.4)$, the copula information equals its target to eight digits, and the direct two-dimensional Gauss-Hermite residual falls as $3.4\times10^{-3}$, $2.0\times10^{-3}$, $1.1\times10^{-3}$ for 60, 120 and 240 nodes, a quadrature effect of the kink in the Laplace log density. The finite case agrees with chapter 3: on a finite alphabet the mutual information of a coupling is at most $\log|\mathsf K|$, which is the boundedness used in part (a). The witness is a degenerate model (the coupling only stores the cost), but the sentence is a universal negative. Line 197 ("proves that the third is not the free energy of any fixed normalized model in which the agent laws are recognition laws") is correct in the product reading and omits the hypotheses of the proposition: fixed positive rows, an identity-channel edge, finite alphabets for (a), fixed channels for (b). The abstract's sentence is the product reading and is supported by (b).
**Fix:** Replace the last clause of :226 by: "\cref{prop:dyn-no-global-vfe} shows that it is not $\mathcal F(Q;o)$ when $Q$ is the product of these marginals and the channels are fixed, nor, on finite alphabets, for any rule assigning recognition laws. On infinite content spaces the coupling of $Q$ can carry the excess $\mathcal E-\sum_i\KL(\zeta_i\Vert R_i)$ as mutual information, so a no-go there must restrict how $Q$ depends on the configuration." Add the hypotheses at :197. D01-FOUND-08 gives a restricted no-go.
**Falsifies:** An error in the decomposition $\KL(Q\Vert P_0)=I_Q+\sum_i\KL(Q_i\Vert P_{0,i})$ for a product $P_0$ or in the mutual information of a Gaussian copula, or a reading of :226 that requires $Q$ to be the product of the agent laws.

### D01-FOUND-03: The "only when" conditions for equal predictions are false; predictions identify only the composite record kernel
**Category:** error
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/01_foundations.tex:195 (after def:shared-model); companion/c01_objects.tex:32 ("two detectors with the same estimate predict the same counts only if their calibrations also agree"); AUTHORING.md, section "Beliefs, generative models, and the three objectives" ("Agreement of model laws transfers to agreement of predictions only with matched evaluators, preparation, and information").
**Evidence:** Line 195 reads "equal beliefs over presentations predict equal records only when the evaluators and record kernels agree as in \cref{eq:shared-model} and the agents condition on the same information". The sufficiency direction holds: if $(\phi,\psi)$ satisfy eq:shared-model, substituting the three relations gives $P_j=(\phi\times\psi\times\id)_\#P_i$, so $(\phi_\#s)G_j\ell_j=sG_i\ell_i$ for every model belief $s$ and the posteriors correspond. The necessity direction is false. Counterexample (`foundations_checks.py`, section J): $\mathsf K=\mathsf M=\mathsf O=\{0,1\}$, uniform $r_i=r_j$, $p=(0.2,0.7)$; agent $i$ has $G_i(m,\cdot)=\mathrm{Ber}(p_m)$ and $\ell_i(k,m;\cdot)=\delta_k$; agent $j$ has $G_j(m,\cdot)=\delta_0$ and $\ell_j(k,m;\cdot)=\mathrm{Ber}(p_m)$. The composite kernels $K^{\mathrm{rec}}(m,\cdot)=\int G(m,dk)\ell(k,m;\cdot)$ coincide (difference 0.0), so predictions and model posteriors agree for every model belief and every record history (maximum difference 0.0 over all histories of length three for two beliefs). No pair of bijections satisfies eq:shared-model (exhaustive search over the four pairs), and the content posteriors differ ($\delta_1$ against $\delta_0$ after $o=1$ at $m=1$). Quantified over all model beliefs, agreement of predictions determines only $K^{\mathrm{rec}}$ up to $\phi$, which is the condition eq:evaluator-compatibility of chapters/04_prediction.tex:16-20 and is strictly weaker than eq:shared-model. Under the recognition reading, in which the prediction is $\int\ell\,d\zeta$, equal model beliefs and an identical model do not suffice: with $s=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ and belief conditionals $((0.9,0.1),(0.2,0.8))$ against uniform conditionals, the record laws are $(0.55,0.45)$ and $(0.5,0.5)$. The companion's detector sentence fails at a single estimate whenever two calibration curves cross. Line 195 also omits the preparation that chapters/04_prediction.tex:14 holds fixed and that AUTHORING lists. "A record registered by one agent changes its posterior" should read "can change", since a record with a likelihood constant in $(k,m)$ changes nothing.
**Fix:** Replace the sentence by a proposition with four parts. (i) If $i$ and $j$ share a generative model via $(\phi,\psi)$, then $(\phi_\#s)G_j\ell_j=sG_i\ell_i$ for every $s$, and after a common record history the posteriors correspond under $\psi\times\phi$. (ii) With preparation and accessible history held fixed, $K_j^{\mathrm{rec}}(\phi(m),\cdot)=K_i^{\mathrm{rec}}(m,\cdot)$ is necessary and sufficient for equal record predictions and equal model posteriors for every model belief and every history. (iii) eq:shared-model is strictly stronger than (ii), by the example above. (iv) Recognition predictions agree when $\zeta_j=(\psi\times\phi)_\#\zeta_i$. In the companion write "are guaranteed to predict the same counts for every estimate only if their composite response curves agree", and correct the AUTHORING sentence in the same way.
**Falsifies:** An argument that eq:shared-model follows from equality of the composite kernels; the exhaustive search excludes it at this size.

### D01-FOUND-04: Records and generative models are required to be covariant only under per-agent passive changes, which dressing makes empty
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/01_foundations.tex:146 (covariance requirement), :148-152 (prop:record-covariance), :171-183 (def:generative-model, eq:agent-free-energy), :85 (dressing paragraph); compare chapters/02_interactions.tex:65-71 (hyp:int-reference-consistency) and appendices/conditional_memory.tex:204. This is the unresolved part of 2026-10-22 L01-FOUND-04.
**Evidence:** (1) Line 146 requires every readout, record kernel and response kernel to be invariant or declared equivariant under passive changes $\gamma$, which act on frames by left multiplication. By the argument at :85, any readout written through reference laws satisfies this, so the requirement constrains nothing by itself. The chapter's counterexample (first coordinate of the displayed mean of $\mathcal N(e_1,I)$) is excluded, but its dressed version passes: the first coordinate of the reference mean $U_i^{-1}\mu_i$ is 1.0 before and after a passive rotation by $\pi/2$, and changes from 1.0 to 0.0 under the common reference change $U_i\mapsto U_ih$ with $h$ the same rotation, at the same bundle point (`foundations_checks.py`, section E2). Such a record depends on the choice of $\sigma\in P_{c_*}$, which is the defect that hyp:int-reference-consistency excludes for channels ("is not a function of the bundle data"). No corresponding hypothesis is stated for readouts, record kernels or response kernels. (2) def:generative-model gives $r_i$, $G_i$ and $\ell_i$ no transformation law. If they are held fixed while $\zeta_i\mapsto g_\#\zeta_i$, then $\mathcal F_i$ changes: with $\mathsf K=\R$, $G_i=\mathcal N(0,1)$, no record and $\zeta_i=\mathcal N(0,1)$, $\mathcal F_i=0$ before and $a^2/2$ after a translation by $a$. If they are reference data, $\mathcal F_i$ must be evaluated on the reference law and the reference model must be reference consistent. The text says neither, so $\mathcal F_i$ is not shown to be a function of bundle data. (3) "a declared density of $\ell_i$" names no dominating measure on $\mathsf O_i$. Replacing the reference $\lambda_O$ by $w\lambda_O$ shifts $\mathcal F_i$ by $\log w(o)$, and for an equivariant readout with nontrivial $\tau_i$ the evidence density acquires the Jacobian of $\tau_i$ unless $\lambda_O$ is $\tau_i$-invariant or transported with it. appendices/conditional_memory.tex:204 tracks this factor ($|\det h|^{-1}$) for its own construction, and the 2026-10-21 design record stated the transport rule; chapter 1 dropped it. Joint measurability of the density in $(o,k,m)$ is not stated either. The variational identity itself is sound: with these data declared, the posterior form of eq:agent-free-energy equals the split form $\KL(\zeta_i\Vert r_iG_i)-\E_{\zeta_i}\log l_i(o\mid k,m)$ whenever the split is not of the form $\infty-\infty$ (residual 0.0 on a random finite instance; both forms infinite when $\zeta_i$ charges a zero-likelihood cell), and only $0<P_i(o)<\infty$ is needed.
**Fix:** Add after :146: "A readout, record kernel or response kernel is reference consistent if re-expressing all reference laws and supplied data under a common change of reference point leaves it unchanged, or changes its outcome by a declared action; results whose conclusions concern bundle data assume this." In def:generative-model state that the model is given in reference coordinates by $(\bar r_i,\bar G_i,\bar\ell_i)$, displayed through the frame by pushforward, and reference consistent; that $\ell_i(k,m;do)=l_i(o\mid k,m)\lambda_{O_i}(do)$ with $l_i$ jointly measurable and $\lambda_{O_i}$ declared and $\tau_i$-invariant or transported with $\tau_i$. Then add a one-line proposition: $\mathcal F_i$ is invariant under passive and common reference changes, by prop:kl-coordinate applied to $\zeta_i$ and $P_i(\cdot\mid o)$, with $P_i(o)$ invariant when $\lambda_{O_i}$ is.
**Falsifies:** A passage in chapters 1 to 4 that states reference consistency for records and generative models, or the transformation law of $(r_i,G_i,\ell_i)$.

### D01-FOUND-05: def:shared-model is not "up to frame" and contradicts the one-group convention on which downstream uses of species rely
**Category:** consistency
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/01_foundations.tex:186-193 (def:shared-model) against :83 ("Independent sector changes would require the product group $G_q\times G_s$; the theory does not use them"); chapters/17_communication_links.tex:30 ("members of a species use codes related by the frame map of their shared model") and :50 (it1:why-group, which justifies group-valued dictionaries within a species because "the relative position of two frames of one principal bundle at a context is a group element"); appendices/conditional_memory.tex:206 ("matched record kernels under the declared frame maps"); companion/glossary.tex:19 ("up to a change of frame") against companion/c01_objects.tex:34 ("up to a relabeling"); AUTHORING.md ("references and evaluators related by a frame map").
**Evidence:** The definition admits arbitrary bimeasurable bijections $\phi$ of $\mathsf M$ and $\psi$ of $\mathsf K$, chosen independently. They need not be group elements and they are not tied across the two sectors, so "up to frame" does not describe the definition, and the independent sector relabeling is the one that :83 excludes. Three consequences. (a) For nonatomic priors the condition $r_j=\phi_\#r_i$ alone restricts nothing, since two nonatomic Borel probability measures on uncountable standard Borel spaces are related by a Borel isomorphism (Kechris 1995, Theorem 17.41, locator from memory); the constraint is carried by $G$, $\ell$ and the fixed record alphabet. (b) A relabeling $\psi$ outside $G$ is not respected by $G$-covariant channels, so two members of a species can face each other through channels that are not equivariant under the relabeling that makes them a species; the argument of it1:why-group assumes a frame map and does not apply to the definition as written. (c) The companion chapter and the glossary give different definitions.
**Fix:** Define sharing up to frame with one $g\in G$ acting on reference generative models: $\bar r_j=(g^s)_\#\bar r_i$, $\bar G_j(g^sm,\cdot)=(g^q)_\#\bar G_i(m,\cdot)$, $\bar\ell_j(g^qk,g^sm;\cdot)=\bar\ell_i(k,m;\cdot)$, with $g^a=\varrho_a(g)$ in a linear realization and the declared actions in general. Keep the present definition under another name, for example "isomorphic generative models", and state that the species statements that use dictionaries or channels require the frame version. Align the companion and the glossary with the chosen definition.
**Falsifies:** A reading of eq:shared-model under which $\phi$ and $\psi$ are required to be $\varrho_s(g)$ and $\varrho_q(g)$ for one $g$.

In the run, the ratio to $t^2$ approaches the predicted 0.437011, and at $t=1$ the frame-invariant divergence $\KL(\nu(1)\Vert r)$, which does not read $H$, is 0.024003 for $\id$ and 0.094777 for the shift. The two-cycle witness of D01-FOUND-01(b) separates loop classes at first order.
**Fix:** State the proposition above, with the two-cycle version, after prop:loop-blindness, and say that law-level dynamics detects loop classes through source forces while instantaneous law comparisons do not. Separately import the shared-sample $\mathbb Z_2$ equator reduction of the design record as the content-law instance, with a record kernel reading $(Y,HY)$ through a diagonal-invariant function. Difficulty: the finite proposition is a Taylor expansion (low); a record that persists under assimilation and conditions later laws is moderate.
**Falsifies:** A frame-invariant function that agrees along both trajectories to all orders; the computed $t^2$ coefficient excludes this for the loop comparison.

### D01-FOUND-08: A restricted no-go for content laws, and the open record-bearing case
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** medium

## Extensions and improvements

The most useful repair in scope is a two-level notion of agents of one kind. Predictive equivalence asks for a bijection $\phi$ of presentations with $r_j=\phi_\#r_i$ and $K_j^{\mathrm{rec}}(\phi(m),\cdot)=K_i^{\mathrm{rec}}(m,\cdot)$ at fixed preparation and accessible history; it is exactly what records can test, and it yields equal record predictions and equal model posteriors for every model belief and history. Frame equivalence asks for one $g\in G$ relating the reference generative models in both sectors; it implies predictive equivalence and is what chapter 17 needs for group-valued dictionaries. The example of D01-FOUND-03 separates the two notions, and a short proposition with parts (i) to (iv) of that finding replaces the false "only when" sentence. The open question in op:dyn-three-pictures, whether a species admits a common joint, should then be asked for frame equivalence, where the reference models coincide and a common content variable with conditionally independent records $\prod_i\bar\ell(o_i\mid y,m_i)$ is a natural candidate.

Covariance of records and generative models should be stated at the level of bundle data. The cleanest form declares a standard Borel parameter space $\Lambda_i$ of generative models with a jointly Borel $G$-action and a jointly measurable family $(r_\lambda,G_\lambda,\ell_\lambda)$ on which $g$ acts by pushforward in both sectors (and by $\tau_i(g)$ on records, with $\lambda_{O_i}$ transported). The space of all Markov kernels on an uncountable space is not standard Borel under the evaluation structure, so a parametrized family is needed for the measurable version. The free energy is then a function on the fiber product of $\mathcal B_i$ with $P\times_G\Lambda_i$, and its invariance under passive and common reference changes is one application of prop:kl-coordinate. The same declaration gives reference consistency of readouts and record kernels, which closes D01-FOUND-04 and makes record laws functions of bundle data as hyp:int-reference-consistency does for channels.

The loop sector gains a law-level observable from D01-FOUND-01. For a self channel $H$ at a law $\nu_0$ in its stabilizer, the reciprocal flow separates the loop class of $H$ from that of $\id$ at second order, with coefficient $\tfrac12\sum_y\nu_0(y)(v(y)-v(H^{-1}y))^2$ in the loop comparison, $v$ the record-free drift; on a two-cycle the separation is first order, through the term $\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu_j}\bigl(\log(\nu_j/\Theta_{ji\#}\nu_i),\,\Delta v_j\bigr)$ in the derivative of the edge cost $D_{ji}$, where $\Delta v_j$ is the change of the log-velocity of $j$ caused by the change of its source-role field $-\beta(\nu_i/\Theta_{ij\#}\nu_j)\circ\Theta_{ij}$ when the transport is replaced. A next theorem would show that a record reading such an invariant, assimilated by eq:predict-and-assimilate, leaves a persistent loop-class dependence in the model sector, which is the holonomy-sensitive internal record that 2026-10-22 L01-FOUND-01 asked for, obtained without a content law. The shared-sample $\mathbb Z_2$ equator witness remains the content-law instance and should still be imported.

The no-go for global free energies can be sharpened along the line of D01-FOUND-08: with an $x$-independent coupling of the agent laws, a product prior and no record, $\mathcal F(Q(x))$ is separable and part (b) applies on every standard Borel space, while the Gaussian-copula witness shows that an $x$-dependent coupling realizes the potential exactly. The record-bearing case is open and is the right form of the generative question for content laws: characterize the couplings $x\mapsto Q(x)$, measurable and reference consistent, for which $\mathcal E_o=a\mathcal F(Q(x);o)+b$.

For coarse-graining, def:bundle-morphism should admit fibered equivariant kernels $\kappa:E\rightsquigarrow f^*\bar E$ between sample bundles, which compose, contract relative entropy fiberwise, and contain the constant kernels; with the generative model added to the agent type, type closure of a retention becomes a checkable statement, and the chapter 11 additive Wishart parents are the natural first test. For records, one lemma would serve chapters 10 to 14: the Ionescu-Tulcea construction with history-dependent kernels and measurable policies gives the finite and infinite adaptive history laws, and if every kernel, readout and policy is reference consistent the history law is invariant under the same changes, by induction on the history length.
