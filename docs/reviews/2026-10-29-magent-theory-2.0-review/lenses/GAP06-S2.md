### GAP06-S2-01: fus:sufficiency misidentifies the Gaussian-specific ingredient; the KL residual of the parent energy is state dependent for Bernoulli children with a coupled evaluator
**Category:** inflation
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/06_fusion.tex:606-620, `fus:sufficiency` ("The same holds for any family with a sufficient statistic ... The ingredient specific to Gaussian location families is that the square-root divergence between restored messages is the convex function |Δμ|"); the identity it generalizes, 06:517-523 (Σ n_i(C + U) = E_{0,n_I} + (n_I − 1)C) and 06:603-604 ("state-independent information loss"); origin 2026-10-22 L05-FUS-10 ("The only lognormal-specific ingredient is that sqrt(2KL) between restored messages is the convex function |Δμ|").
**Evidence:** The three properties the remark lists do hold beyond Gaussian and Gamma laws. For Bernoulli children (`g2`, exact sympy, n = 3) the conditional law of the children given T = Σ X_j is parameter free (p = 1/5, 2/3 and symbolic p agree), Fisher(Bin(n, p))/Fisher(Ber(p)) = 3, and the restored message Σ_t Bin(t) t/n equals p. With the coupled evaluator G(k|m) = e^{Jkm}/(1 + e^{Jm}) and model reference Ber(ρ), the parent joint reference of T = (S_k, S_m) factorizes as Bin(n, ρ)(S_m) G_n(S_k|S_m), with G_n the law of Bin(S_m, G(1|1)) * Bin(n − S_m, G(1|0)) (exact at n = 3, J = 7/10, ρ = 2/5), so the evaluator is inherited. What fails is the energy identity that makes the parent an agent of the children's type. The chain rule for relative entropy gives n KL(q ⊗ s ∥ R) − KL(q^T ⊗ s^T ∥ R^T) = Res(θ) := E_θ[KL(P(·|T) ∥ Q(·|T))], where P(·|T) is the parameter-free recognition conditional and Q(·|T) the conditional of the reference. For Gaussian location families with fixed covariances both conditionals are Gaussian with the same mean T̄1 and T-independent covariances, so Res = (n − 1)C; `g2` reproduces 0.450693855666 = (n − 1)C at n = 2 and 1.802775422664 at n = 5 for the means (0.3, −0.2), (1.5, 0.7) and (−2, 3). For Bernoulli children Q(·|T) ∝ exp(J Σ_j k_j m_j) on the arrangements of T depends on T, and Res varies with the state: at n = 2, J = 0.7, ρ = 0.4 it is 0.015009717 at (p, p') = (0.5, 0.5), 0.008069224 at (0.2, 0.7), 0.000541851 at (0.05, 0.05), 0.001945259 at (0.9, 0.9) and 0.005403498 at (0.5, 0.1). On a synchronized stratum the restricted fine energy is therefore KL(parent law ∥ inherited parent reference) + Res(θ), not E_{0,n} plus a constant. The restricted dynamics is still the exactly inherited dynamics, but the parent's local term is no longer the KL of its law against its inherited reference, so the handoff identity of 06:517-523 is a Gaussian feature. Under the general-law rule of AUTHORING.md this is a family feature presented as a property of the mechanism.
**Fix:** Replace the last two sentences of the remark by: "Two ingredients are specific to Gaussian location families with fixed covariances. The KL residual n E_{0,1} − E_{0,n} = E[KL(P(·|T) ∥ Q(·|T))] equals the constant (n − 1)C; when the joint reference is not in an exponential family for which T is sufficient, for example Bernoulli children with the coupled evaluator e^{Jkm}, it depends on the state, and the restricted energy is the parent's KL plus that state-dependent term. The square-root divergence between restored messages is the convex function |Δμ|; on a general regular family it equals the Fisher distance only to leading order near the diagonal." Add the sufficient condition: if the joint reference belongs to an exponential family with sufficient statistic T, then Q(·|T) = P(·|T) and Res = 0.
**Falsifies:** An exact recomputation giving a state-independent Res for Bernoulli children with the coupled evaluator; or a declared definition of the parent energy as the restricted sum rather than as its own KL, under which the remark survives but its analogy with E_{0,n} must be dropped.

### GAP06-S2-03: Heterogeneous-reference fusion: finite-time arrival at the final partition holds under strict complementarity and fails without it; membership is not monotone before arrival
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/06_fusion.tex:694-707, `fus:smooth-open` ("Both destroy the convexity on which uniqueness rests ... have not been established"); continues 2026-10-29 D11-FUS-08 (agent-specific single-well references; finite-time arrival of the continuous flow unproved, discrete runs only).
**Evidence:** Theorem (proved here). Let F(μ) = Q(μ) + Λ(μ), Q = Σ_i n_i U(μ_i − x_i), Λ = λ Σ_α Σ_{i<j} n_i n_j |μ_i^α − μ_j^α|, a > c > 0, with the flow W μ̇ ∈ −∂F(μ). F is (a − c)-strongly convex in the W metric for every reference vector x, so `fus:uniqueness` and `fus:contraction` hold verbatim and there is a unique minimizer μ*. Let L be the linear subspace on which every pair coincident at μ* coincides, sep* the smallest nonzero gap of μ*, and assume strict complementarity: 0 = ∇Q(μ*) + ξ_sep + λ Σ_coinc n_i n_j ε*_ij^α (e_i^α − e_j^α) with antisymmetric |ε*| ≤ 1 − θ, θ > 0, where ξ_sep is the gradient of the separated pair terms (equivalently, every cut inequality of every cluster's weighted base polytope is strict). Then μ(t) ∈ L, with exactly the coincidence partition of μ*, for all t ≥ T_1 + 2r/(θ c_0), where κ = a − c, c_0 ≥ √2 λ, r = min(sep*/3, θ c_0/(2(a + c))) and T_1 = max(0, κ^{−1} log(‖μ(0) − μ*‖_W / r)). Proof: for t ≥ T_1 the contraction gives ‖μ(t) − μ*‖_W ≤ r, so separated pairs keep their signs and ξ_sep is constant. Put d(t) = ‖P_⊥ μ(t)‖_W, the W-distance to L, which is absolutely continuous; at almost every t with d > 0, ḋ = −nᵀξ with n = P_⊥μ/d and ξ ∈ ∂F(μ(t)) the selection with Wμ̇ = −ξ. The coincident part ξ_c ∈ ∂Λ_coinc(μ) annihilates L, so nᵀξ_c = Λ_coinc(n) by sublinearity, while nᵀξ*_c ≤ (1 − θ)Λ_coinc(n) and |nᵀ(∇Q(μ) − ∇Q(μ*))| ≤ (a + c)‖μ − μ*‖_W. Hence ḋ ≤ (a + c) r − θ Λ_coinc(n) ≤ −θ c_0/2 on {d > 0}, and d(T_1) ≤ r. The bound c_0 ≥ √2 λ follows from Σ_{i<j∈C} n_i n_j |w_i − w_j| ≥ (n_C min n_i n_j)^{1/2} ‖w_C‖_W ≥ √2 ‖w_C‖_W for W-zero-sum w on each cluster C. This is finite identification of the active stratum under nondegeneracy, the continuous-time counterpart of identification results for proximal methods (Hare and Lewis 2004; cited from memory, not re-read in this session).
Without strict complementarity arrival fails on an open set of initial data. Two unit agents, a = 1, c = 0.5, λ = 1, x_1 = −x_2 = (1.25, 0.5): ε*_q = (aX^q − cX^s)/(2λ) = 1 exactly and ε*_s = −0.125. After the s-sector sticks (t_s = 0.256), Δ̇^q = −aΔ^q for Δ^q > 0, so Δ^q(t) = Δ^q(t_s) e^{−a(t − t_s)} > 0 for every t although the limit partition is the synchronized pair. The event-driven integrator (`g3` (B)) gives 1.292579e-1, 3.203983e-4, 1.713413e-11 at t_s + 2, t_s + 8 and t = 25, against 1.292579e-1, 3.203983e-4, 1.713417e-11 predicted; from the other side, Δ^q(0) < 0, the pair arrives at t = 0.198 (`g3` (C)). Before arrival, membership is not monotone: in that run agents 1 and 2 pass through each other in sector s at t = 0.300 and agents 0 and 2 in sector q at t = 0.417 (free-force difference above the binding 2λ), and a pair that starts synchronized splits at t = 0+ (`g3` (E)). If 0 lies in the relative interior of ∂F(μ*), every solution reaches the coincidence partition of μ* at a finite time [bound] and keeps it afterward; if a cut inequality is tight the partition can be approached only asymptotically. Difficulty: low (the proof above is complete); describing the partition sequence before arrival is open.

### GAP06-S2-04: The semiconvexity sentence of fus:sufficiency needs geodesic semiconvexity on a forward-invariant compact set; global semiconvexity fails already for Bernoulli children, and semiconvexity gives uniqueness without the contraction used by fus:sync
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/06_fusion.tex:617-620, `fus:sufficiency` ("on a general regular family it equals the Fisher distance only to leading order near the diagonal, and global uniqueness would need semiconvexity estimates in place of convexity").
**Evidence:** In the Fisher arclength coordinate φ = 2 arcsin √p of the Bernoulli family the product Fisher metric is flat, so ordinary and geodesic semiconvexity coincide. The directed square-root divergence d(φ_1, φ_2) = (2 KL(Ber(p_1) ∥ Ber(p_2)))^{1/2} satisfies 2KL/(φ_1 − φ_2)² = 0.99999 at φ_1 = 1.2, φ_2 − φ_1 = 1e-4, as the remark states (`g2` (6)). Its Hessian has minimal eigenvalue −0.858 on the box p ∈ [0.087, 0.913] away from the diagonal and −0.973 within 1e-2 of it, so it is semiconvex there with constant about one; the cone at the diagonal is convex, and the defect comes from the δ|δ| term of the expansion. At p_1 = 1/2 the minimal eigenvalue is −0.423, −0.681, −0.866, −1.087 and −1.270 for p_2 = 1e-2, 1e-4, 1e-6, 1e-9, 1e-12, consistent with the asymptotic −¼ (log 1/p_2)^{1/2}, which follows from ∂²_{p_1} d ≈ −(log 1/p_2)^{1/2}(2p_1)^{−3/2} and (dp/dφ)² = 1/4 at p = 1/2. The divergence is therefore not globally semiconvex. For multi-parameter families with curvature (free-variance Gaussians) no flat chart exists, and the notion needed is semiconvexity along Fisher geodesics (ω-convexity in the sense of gradient-flow theory). The semiconvexity defect of the pair term scales with λN², so for strong coupling F is not convex: uniqueness survives (∂F + ωI monotone gives ‖μ(t) − μ̃(t)‖_W ≤ e^{ωt}‖μ(0) − μ̃(0)‖_W), but the contraction of `fus:uniqueness`, and with it the first step of the proof of `fus:sync`, is lost.
**Fix:** "... and global uniqueness would need semiconvexity of the square-root divergence along Fisher geodesics on a forward-invariant compact set (it fails near the boundary of the Bernoulli family, and its constant grows with λ). That gives uniqueness, not the contraction used in `fus:sync`."
**Falsifies:** A finite lower bound for the Hessian of d on the whole open Bernoulli square; the computed eigenvalues and the asymptotic exclude it.

### Written-out arguments for focus questions 3 and 5

Asymptotic stability at |J| = 1 (focus 3). Work in the chart flow `eq:public-phase-chart-flow` on E^N with the adapted norm |||x||| = max(‖z̄‖, max_i ‖δ_i‖); the field V is smooth on bounded balls and permutation equivariant. (1) Difference modes. Equivariance makes the diagonal invariant, so δ̇ = M(x)δ with M(x) = ∫_0^1 D_δ(V_i − V̄)(z̄, sδ) ds continuous and M(0) equal to 𝒜 − κ_N on zero-sum vectors (`eq:public-phase-linearization`). Its logarithmic norm is at most −(1 + κ_N) + |J| = −κ_N, since the off-diagonal part of 𝒜 has norm at most |J|; by continuity μ(M(x)) ≤ −κ_N + η on a ball of radius r_1, and ‖δ(t)‖ ≤ e^{−(κ_N − η)t}‖δ(0)‖ while the trajectory stays there. (2) Mean. ż̄ = f(z̄) + g with ‖g‖ ≤ C_g‖δ‖² (`eq:public-phase-residual`, D²V bounded on the ball). (3) Splitting the mean. With a = E_A[φū], u_⊥ = ū − aφ (projections of norm at most 2) and b, v_⊥ likewise, the form of f gives u̇_⊥ = −u_⊥ + g_u^⊥ and ȧ = −a + J m_ψ(v̄) + g_u^a, where m_ψ(v) = E_{s_v}ψ satisfies m_ψ(bψ + v_⊥) = tanh b + ρ with |ρ| ≤ ‖v_⊥‖ (the covariance bound at 09:47, ψ balanced). Ergodic arc-rotation step of `prop:boundary-fixed-family` (focus 5). In the circle realization, for a node v and a base point r the + cell of σ_v is the arc (α_v(r) − π/2, α_v(r) + π/2) and the − cell its complement, up to null endpoints. Measure an arc coordinate t_v ∈ [0, π) from the start of each arc, and for constants c_v ∈ (0, 1) let φ_r act on every cell by t_v ↦ t_v + c_v π mod π for all internal nodes v. Then Φ^q(r, θ) = (r, φ_r(θ)) is fibered and bimeasurable (α_v is measurable, the inverse rotates by −c_vπ), preserves the reference (on each cell it translates arcs of length π, and the cells are preserved), and preserves every sign, so (Φ, id) ∈ H_α. On each cell, a torus (R/πZ)^{N−1} in arc coordinates with normalized Haar measure, φ_r is the rotation by (c_v); it is ergodic if and only if 1 and the c_v are linearly independent over Q (Kronecker-Weyl; Walters 1982, chapter 1, theorem number not checked). ## Extensions and improvements

A strongly convex route to persistent heterogeneous blocks with finite-time identification (GAP06-S2-03). Under agent-specific single-well references the coincidence partition of the unique minimizer is reached at a finite time whenever strict complementarity holds. Degeneracy requires a tight cut inequality, that is, the vanishing of one of finitely many affine functions of the reference data on each partition pattern of the minimizer; I expect, but did not prove, that none of these functions vanishes identically, which would make nondegeneracy generic. For the formation standard this gives a selection by attained equality whose final partition depends only on the declared references and λ: under the null flow λ = 0 two agents with distinct references coincide in both sectors at most at isolated instants (for generic data never) and never stick, so (F5) holds formally, while its strong form and a final-stage (F6b) fail for the reason D11-FUS-08 gave (a single minimizer). The inherited-parent bookkeeping, averaged reference by the parallel-axis identity and inherited evaluator offset, is exact after the arrival time; before it, splits and crossings occur. The next theorem would describe the partition sequence before arrival for data near the common-offset family, combining the event analysis of `fus:sticking` with the identification argument.

The phase parent at |J| = 1. For |J| ≤ 1 the diagonal derivative Df has logarithmic norm at most −1 + |J|(1 − m²) ≤ 0 in the maximum norm, so Dφ_t is nonexpansive for t ≥ 0, while the difference modes decay faster than e^{−ωt} for every ω < κ_N, and κ_N = 2N/(N − 1) > 2 = L. The integrand of `eq:public-phase-integral` is then O(e^{−(2ω − L)t}) for some 2 < ω < κ_N, the derivative terms need only a bound on ‖DΦ_t‖, which nonexpansiveness and the integrable coupling to the differences provide, and forward invariance can use a sublevel set of a Lyapunov function built from V = a² + b², ‖u_⊥‖, ‖v_⊥‖ and ‖δ‖ in place of a ball. I expect `thm:public-phase` to hold at |J| = 1 with these changes, which would make the companion's "exactly the condition" wrong for the construction as well as for stability. I have not proved it. Difficulty: low to moderate.

Minimality beyond the first case (GAP06-S2-02). The product-family invisibility points to the minimal root descriptor: the base marginal together with the base variation of the shape functionals that enter ∂_t f_0. At d = 1 the root history determines f_0(t) and hence the driving term κ(a_0(t)) − κ(a_1(t)) along the autonomous contrast flow ȧ = A(a). Near 0, a(t) ≈ a e^{−5t} and κ(a) ≈ κ_2 a², so the coefficients of e^{−10t} and e^{−20t} in that term recover a_0² − a_1² and, when it is nonzero, a_0² + a_1²; this suggests that the root history separates unordered descriptors exactly off the diagonal a_0 = a_1, which would make the minimal d = 1 root retention the unordered tree with the diagonal collapsed. Proving that, and computing the rank at d = 2 with a two-point base (7 coordinates per sector, jets to order 6 or two base-only interventions), are the next steps. A depth-l node version should satisfy the same invisibility for subtree shapes that do not depend on base and ancestor coordinates; that needs a check of the incoming terms across the classes k < l.

A precise general-law version of the fusion inheritance (GAP06-S2-01, -04). The parent of a synchronized block in a general family is an agent of the children's type, with local energy equal to its own KL against its inherited reference up to a constant, exactly when Res(θ) = E_θ[KL(P(·|T) ∥ Q(·|T))] does not depend on θ. Sufficient conditions are that the joint reference lies in an exponential family for which T is sufficient (then Res = 0), or that a family of T-dependent measurable bijections carries both conditionals at one value of T onto both conditionals at another (translations do this for Gaussian location families, which gives the constant (n − 1)C). Combined with semiconvexity along Fisher geodesics on a forward-invariant compact set, this would give a general-law version of `fus:uniqueness`, without contraction, and of the handoff identity. Difficulty: moderate.
