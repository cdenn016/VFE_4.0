# D02-LINKS: Directed interactions, group-valued links and channels

Verdict: no proposition, lemma or displayed identity in this scope is false as stated. I reconstructed every proof and recomputed every identity and worked example, with float64 residuals of at most 2e-11 (finite-difference comparisons excepted) and exact agreement wherever I used sympy. The defects sit beside the statements. Two remarks draw false consequences: a rotation is said to be visible against any anisotropic Gaussian, and a nonidentity sector mismatch is said to mean that the belief and model transports disagree. The reciprocal/backtrack split is a per-sector construction that does not respect the group-valued and tied links defined two sections earlier. `prop:int-loop-defect` uses reference consistency without stating it. The s02 regularity sentence names a condition (differentiability in quadratic mean) that is neither necessary nor sufficient for what it is attached to.

### D02-LINKS-01: The reciprocal/backtrack split is computed per sector and does not respect group-valued or tied links
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/02_interactions.tex:192-210 (prop:int-reciprocal, eq:int-reciprocal, and "The reciprocal parts Ω form a representation in which reversal is inversion, as for the transport of app:connection"); chapters/02_interactions.tex:94-103 (def:int-group-links); appendices/base_connection.tex:8; future_TODO.md:13, :82, :100
**Evidence:** prop:int-reciprocal takes "invertible links of one sector" and uses the principal square root in GL(V_a). The proof is correct. I reconstructed it from the f(AB)/f(BA) identity with f(z) = z^{-1/2} and the similarity invariance of primary functions; over 60 random GL(3) pairs, |Ω_jiΩ_ij − I| ≤ 4.8e-13 and the covariance residual is ≤ 1.9e-11. The principal root does not commute with representations, however: ϱ(n)^{1/2} = ϱ(n^{1/2}) fails whenever ϱ moves an eigenvalue of n across the branch cut. Take G = SO(2) and tied links θ_ij = R(a), θ_ji = R(b) with a + b = 0.8π, so that n_j = θ_jiθ_ij = R(0.8π). In the fundamental sector the split gives Ω^q_ij = R(a − 0.4π) = ϱ_q(Ω^G), residual 0.000. future_TODO.md:100 declares the representation fundamental ⊕ Sym² for Gaussian model parameters. In that representation Sym²(n_j) has eigenvalues e^{±1.6πi} = e^{∓0.4πi} and 1, so its principal root has angles ∓0.2π, whereas Sym²(n_j^{1/2}) has angles ±0.8π. The script finds |Ω^s − ϱ_s(Ω^G)| = 3.126, and |Ω^s − ϱ_s(its own fundamental block)| = 3.126. Since the fundamental block of ϱ_s(k) is k, this shows Ω^s ∉ ϱ_s(GL(2)). With the charge-2 representation alone, Ω^s = ϱ_s(ψ) but ψ − (a − 0.4π) ≡ π/2 (mod π), so the reciprocal parts of a tied pair are not tied. app:connection (line 8) requires principal G-connections to take values in the represented Lie algebra. The program plans to use Ω as the input for G-valued transport (future_TODO.md:82), in the tied case it calls the focus (:100). As written, "as for the transport of app:connection" holds only for the extended general linear frame bundle.
**Fix:** State prop:int-reciprocal for GL(V_a)-valued links, and add a group-level version. If n_j = θ_jiθ_ij = exp(X) with X in the Lie algebra of G, and every eigenvalue of dϱ_a(X) has |Im| < π in each sector used, put Ω^G_ij = θ_ij exp(−X/2). Then ϱ_a(n_j)^{1/2} = ϱ_a(exp(X/2)) by uniqueness of the square root with spectrum in the open right half-plane. So ϱ_a(Ω^G_ij) is the sector split of eq:int-reciprocal, it lies in ϱ_a(G), Ω^G_ji = (Ω^G_ij)^{-1} (because n_i = exp(Ad_{θ_ij}X)), and tied links have tied reciprocal parts. For fundamental ⊕ Sym², it suffices that every eigenvalue of n_j has |arg λ| < π/2.
**Falsifies:** A representation of G in which the per-sector principal-root split of a tied pair is always ϱ_a of a single G-valued split. The SO(2) fundamental ⊕ Sym² instance above excludes this.

### D02-LINKS-02: Every centered Gaussian, anisotropic or not, has nonidentity rotations in its stabilizer
**Category:** error
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/02_interactions.tex:217 (remark after prop:int-loop-blindness: "a rotation loop, for example, is invisible against a centered isotropic Gaussian and visible against an anisotropic one"); chapters/01_foundations.tex:246 ("A rotation by a fixed angle, for instance, leaves an isotropic Gaussian law unchanged")
**Evidence:** For a centered Gaussian, R_#N(0,Σ) = N(0,RΣR^T), so KL(ν‖R_#ν) = 0 exactly when R commutes with Σ. The stabilizer of N(0,Σ) in O(d) is the commutant of Σ, a product of the groups O(m_k) over the eigenvalue multiplicities m_k. Its intersection with SO(d) is never trivial for d ≥ 2: the half-turn R = I − 2(v_1v_1^T + v_2v_2^T) in the plane of two orthonormal eigenvectors has determinant +1 and commutes with Σ. Numerically, for Σ = diag(1,3), KL(ν‖R_#ν) is 0.276678 at angle 0.7, 0.666667 at π/2 and 0.000000 at π. For Σ = diag(1,1,3), a rotation by 0.7 in the (1,2)-plane gives KL = 0.0. For random SPD Σ in d = 2, 3, 5, the eigenplane half-turn gives |RΣR^T − Σ| ≤ 7.8e-15. In even dimension, −I is a rotation that fixes every centered law invariant under y ↦ −y. The shared-sample pair still separates these cases: the mean of |HY − Y| is 3.48 for H = −I and 0 for H = I. The chapter 1 version also omits "centered": a rotation moves N(m, I) unless Rm = m. The proposition itself is correct. The illustrating sentences, however, misstate the point the proposition exists to make: visibility is controlled by the stabilizer, not by isotropy.
**Fix:** Replace the sentence by: "a rotation R is invisible against a centered Gaussian N(0,Σ) exactly when RΣR^T = Σ; for every Σ in dimension at least two this stabilizer contains nonidentity rotations (half-turns in eigenplanes), and for isotropic Σ it is all of SO(d)". Add "centered" in chapter 1.
**Falsifies:** An anisotropic covariance in dimension at least two whose SO(d)-stabilizer is trivial. The half-turn construction excludes it.

### D02-LINKS-03: A nonidentity sector mismatch need not mean that the belief and model transports disagree
**Category:** error
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/02_interactions.tex:113 and :118 (prop:int-mismatch and the paragraph after it: "a nonzero mismatch is a frame-independent property of the interaction: the belief and model transports along the edge disagree"); chapters/01_foundations.tex:8 ("The actions may be different representations of the same group"), :87 (a distinction is gauge relative to a declared observable algebra); chapters/11_conditional_formation.tex:452-453
**Evidence:** The transformation law Ξ ↦ g_iΞg_i^{-1} is correct: the passive GL(3) residual is 4.4e-12, and under a reference change with equivariant generators Ξ is unchanged (1.6e-13). The channels, however, see only Θ^q = ϱ_q(θ) and Θ^s = ϱ_s(θ̃). A single G-valued link θ* realizes both iff ϱ_q(θ*) = ϱ_q(θ) and ϱ_s(θ*) = ϱ_s(θ̃), that is, iff Ξ ∈ ker ϱ_s · ker ϱ_q, which is a normal subgroup. Proof: θ* = k_qθ = k_sθ̃ with k_a ∈ ker ϱ_a. The main text never assumes faithfulness (grep finds no "faithful"). Two counterexamples follow. (i) G = SO(2), with the congruence action Y ↦ gYg^T that chapter 11 (lines 452-453) uses, in the model sector, and the fundamental representation in the belief sector. The congruence action has kernel {±I} (Sym²(R(π)) = I to 3.9e-16). Then θ̃ = −θ gives the central mismatch Ξ = −I ≠ e, while ϱ_s(θ̃) = ϱ_s(θ), so the tied link θ realizes both displayed links. (ii) G = SO(2) with charges 2 and 3. Their sum is faithful: no nonidentity kernel angle appears on a grid of 200001 points. With θ = 0 and θ̃ = π/3, Ξ ≠ e, yet θ* = π satisfies ϱ_q(θ*) = ϱ_q(θ) and ϱ_s(θ*) = ϱ_s(θ̃), with residuals of 3.5e-16. By the definition in chapter 1 (:87), the distinction between Ξ = e and Ξ ∈ ker ϱ_s ker ϱ_q is gauge relative to the channel algebra, so the sentence contradicts the manuscript's convention. Also, "nonzero" should read "nonidentity".
**Fix:** Either assume ϱ_q and ϱ_s faithful where the sentence is made, or state two things. First, the frame-independent invariant of the displayed interaction is the conjugacy class of Ξ_ij in G/(ker ϱ_s ker ϱ_q). Second, the tied hypothesis that later results need is Ξ_ij ∈ ker ϱ_s ker ϱ_q, for which Ξ_ij = e is sufficient.
**Falsifies:** A proof, without a faithfulness hypothesis, that Ξ ≠ e forces ϱ_s(θ̃) ≠ ϱ_s(θ*) for every θ* with ϱ_q(θ*) = ϱ_q(θ). Example (i) excludes it.

### D02-LINKS-04: Law-adjoint pairs give an unconditional covariant reciprocal split that matches second moments, and the moment family is never reciprocal on independent means
**Category:** extension
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/02_interactions.tex:192-210 (prop:int-reciprocal and the remark "When Θ_ji = Θ_ij^T, eq:int-reciprocal is the polar decomposition"), :153-176 (def:int-moment-family, prop:int-moment-equivariance); future_TODO.md:13, :82; the target theorem in the extensions section of 2026-10-22 X1-GAUGE
**Evidence:** The polar remark uses a frame-dependent condition, since the transpose is not covariant under non-orthogonal g. A covariant replacement uses the law metrics M_i = Σ_i^{-1}: call a pair law-adjoint when Θ_ji = M_j^{-1}Θ_ij^TM_i. The condition is preserved by passive changes, because M_i ↦ g_i^{-T}M_ig_i^{-1}. Proposition (proved here): for a law-adjoint pair, N_j = M_j^{-1}(Θ_ij^TM_iΘ_ij) is a product of two SPD matrices. Its spectrum is therefore positive, so the hypothesis of prop:int-reciprocal holds automatically. Since M_jN_j is symmetric, every real power N_j^s is M_j-self-adjoint. This gives Ω_ij^TM_iΩ_ij = M_j, that is, Ω_ijΣ_jΩ_ij^T = Σ_i: the reciprocal part is a congruence isometry that carries the sender covariance onto the receiver covariance. In the script, over 200 random GL(3) pairs, the minimum eigenvalue of N_j is 8.4e-4 and the worst |Ω^TM_iΩ − M_j| is 2.3e-11. The moment family is law-adjoint when λ_ij = λ_ji and Σ_i = Σ_j: the residual is 2.3e-15, and |Ω_12ΣΩ_12^T − Σ| = 3.2e-14. With Σ_i ≠ Σ_j adjointness fails (residual 9.2), because the identity part of e^L is not an isometry between different metrics; the Laplace example of the chapter is the case Σ = I. Second proposition (proved here): in the moment family, if μ_i and μ_j are linearly independent and λ_ij, λ_ji ≠ 0, then Θ_ijΘ_ji ≠ I, so Q_ij > 0 and N_j ≠ I. Proof: Θ_ij = I + φ_1(t_ij)L_ij with φ_1 > 0 on R. Θ_ijΘ_ji = I would force φ_1(t_ji)L_ji = −φ_1(−t_ij)L_ij, an equality between two nonzero rank-one matrices with images span(μ_j) and span(μ_i). In the script, the minimum Q_12 over 2000 random draws is 4.4e-5 > 0; with parallel means, equal covariances and λ_ji = −λ_ij the pair is reciprocal (residual 7.6e-18). This settles the 2026-10-22 X1-GAUGE target theorem. Its general form, that Q_ij = 0 iff the pair is reciprocal, is immediate from prop:int-loop-defect.
**Fix:** Add the law-adjoint proposition after prop:int-reciprocal as the covariant form of the polar remark, and the nonreciprocity proposition after prop:int-moment-equivariance. Difficulty: low, since each proof is a few lines.
**Falsifies:** A law-adjoint pair whose N_j has a nonpositive eigenvalue, or a moment-family pair with independent means and nonzero coefficients whose two-cycle is the identity. The proofs above exclude both.

### D02-LINKS-05: Reference consistency is used without being stated at prop:int-loop-defect, and its exponential-link form is stronger than the hypothesis
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/02_interactions.tex:219-228 (prop:int-loop-defect: "unchanged ... by every common invertible reference change"; proof: "A common reference change acts on the reference loop and reference covariance in the same way"); :72 ("Every result below whose conclusion concerns bundle data ... assumes reference consistency and says so"; "for the exponential generators below it is eq:int-reference-equivariance"); :24 and :66 (per-sector h_a); :84-103; companion/c02_links.tex:39 ("the main text imposes it as an explicit standing hypothesis wherever a conclusion should not depend on the choice of reference frame")
**Evidence:** (a) The quoted proof sentence is exactly reference consistency of the loop. For a rule that is not reference consistent, the displayed links change under U ↦ Uh, and Q changes with them. The Euclidean rule L_ij = 0.7 μ_iμ_j^T gives Q = 7.526699 before a non-orthogonal common change and 145.931445 after it; the moment rule gives 2.481973 both times. No file other than chapter 2 cites hyp:int-reference-consistency (grep), and prop:int-loop-defect does not invoke it, so neither the chapter's own rule nor the companion's description is met here. (b) For exponential links, the hypothesis (the displayed channel is unchanged, eq:int-kernel-reference) is equivalent to e^{L'} = h^{-1}e^Lh, not to eq:int-reference-equivariance. The constant rule L ≡ 2πJ in two dimensions makes every displayed link equal U_iU_j^{-1} in every presentation (|e^{L'} − h^{-1}e^Lh| = 1.0e-15), while |L' − h^{-1}Lh| = 20.97. The generator-level form is the one that D𝓛 in eq:int-total-variation and priors on generators need, so imposing it is reasonable; but line 72 presents it as the hypothesis itself. (c) With one structure group, the common reference change is one h ∈ G with h_a = ϱ_a(h). Yet lines 24 and 66 and eq:int-reference-equivariance quantify over independent per-sector h_a. **Fix:** Add "for reference-consistent links" to prop:int-loop-defect, or restate it for fixed linear maps re-expressed in new coordinates. At line 72, call eq:int-reference-equivariance the generator-level strengthening that the theory imposes. Write h_a = ϱ_a(h) at lines 24 and 66. In def:int-group-links, say that tied law-dependent generators are functions of both sectors' reference laws, equivariant under one h.
**Falsifies:** A reading of prop:int-loop-defect in which W is a fixed pair of linear maps rather than links produced by a rule. Under that reading (a) is not a gap, but the invariance under reference changes is then trivial, and the proof sentence about reference changes should be dropped.

### D02-LINKS-06: The reciprocal split fails on an open set of the chapter's own family, is unique only under a balance condition, and its notation hides the partner
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/02_interactions.tex:192-210 (prop:int-reciprocal); chapters/02_interactions.tex:37 (self channels are constitutive); chapters/16_link_base_transport.tex:72 (N_{ij,j}); companion/c02_links.tex:63
**Evidence:** (a) Domain. On the moment family with identity frames, Σ = I, μ_1 = e_1 and μ_2 = e_2, the backtrack is N_2 = [[1, λ_12], [λ_21, 1 + λ_12λ_21]], with determinant 1 and trace 2 + λ_12λ_21. For λ_12λ_21 = −4 it is similar to J_2(−1) (sympy Jordan form [[−1, 1], [0, −1]]). For λ_12λ_21 < −4 it has two distinct negative eigenvalues (−0.382 and −2.618 at −5). Neither matrix has a real square root (by the real-square-root theorem in Higham 2008, Chapter 1; theorem number unverified; sympy also finds no real solution of X² = N_2 in either case). So no balanced real split exists there, and the failure is not an artifact of the branch choice. Reversing the sign of one edge coefficient in the chapter's Laplace example and scaling it suffices to leave the domain. (b) Uniqueness is not stated. Any Ω̃ gives a factorization Θ_ij = Ω̃B_j, Θ_ji = Ω̃^{-1}B_i (residuals 1.4e-15 and 1.8e-15, with |B_j² − N_j| = 16.9). The split of eq:int-reciprocal is the unique one with Ω_ji = Ω_ij^{-1}, the balance condition B_i = Ω_ijB_jΩ_ij^{-1} (checked, 8.0e-15), and the spectrum of B_j in the open right half-plane, because balance forces B_j² = N_j. (c) For a nonidentity self channel the formula gives Ω_ii = Θ_ii(Θ_ii²)^{-1/2} = sign(Θ_ii), an involution that differs from I when Θ_ii has eigenvalues in the left half-plane (residual 1.2e-15 against sign(Θ_ii); |Ω_ii − I| = 2.31). The statement does not exclude i = j, although self channels are constitutive data (:37). (d) N_j depends on the partner i. Chapter 16 had to rename it N_{ij,j} ("The ordered-pair subscripts distinguish loops from different neighbors").
**Fix:** State the uniqueness characterization. Exclude i = j, or record Ω_ii = sign(Θ_ii). Write N_{ij,j} in chapter 2 and in the companion. Add the boundary λ_12λ_21 = −4 of the moment-family example to the remark.
**Falsifies:** A real square root of J_2(−1), or of a 2×2 matrix with two distinct negative eigenvalues. The real-square-root criterion, the analogue of Culver's criterion for logarithms, excludes both.

### D02-LINKS-07: The s02 regularity sentence names a condition that is neither necessary nor sufficient
**Category:** error
**Severity:** low
**Location:** supplements/s02_geometry.tex:69-71 ("Differentiating a cost such as KL(ν_i‖Θ_#ν_j) in Θ requires in addition that Θ ↦ Θ_#ν_j be differentiable in quadratic mean, which is a shape assumption, namely finite location-scale Fisher information"; "Under that additional hypothesis, the resulting terms enter a variational derivative"); persists from 2026-10-22 L01-FOUND-14, whose proposed wording the remediation adopted
**Evidence:** The condition is not necessary. Let ν have density e^{−|x−1|^{1/2}}/4 on R, and take the scale family σ_#ν. Near x = 1 the scale score −x∂_x log p − 1 behaves like (1/2)|x − 1|^{−1/2}, so the scale Fisher information diverges logarithmically and the family is not DQM. The truncated integrals are 0.951, 1.509 and 2.083 over |x − 1| > 10^{−2}, 10^{−4} and 10^{−6}, with increments matching (1/8)·ln 100 = 0.576. Yet KL(ν‖(1+ε)_#ν)/ε is 5.5e-3, 6.9e-4, 8.4e-5 and 9.8e-6 for ε = 10^{−2} to 10^{−5} (mpmath at 30 digits; the same order for 1 − ε). So the cost is differentiable at the diagonal with derivative 0. KL/ε² grows like log(1/ε) (from 0.55 to 0.98), as infinite Fisher information predicts. The condition is not sufficient either. The Gaussian scale family is DQM, but ν_i = (δ_{−1} + δ_1)/2, which has finite moments and variance 1, is singular to every Θ_#ν_j, so the cost is identically +∞. Also, for linear Θ the family Θ_#ν_j is the linear-transformation (scale and shear) orbit of ν_j, not a location-scale family. The uniform counterexample in the same paragraph is correct: for ε = 10^{−4} the support witnesses have preimage coordinates x_1 = 0.99995 < 1 and 2.00015 > 2.
**Fix:** Replace the sentence by sufficient conditions for differentiability at Θ_0: ν_i ≪ Θ_#ν_j with finite KL for Θ near Θ_0; ν_j with a C¹ positive density p_j on the relevant region; and E_{ν_i} sup_{Θ near Θ_0} |∂_Θ[log p_j(Θ^{-1}Y) − log|det Θ|]| < ∞. Mention DQM only as the condition for the second-order expansion at the diagonal.
**Falsifies:** A proof that differentiability of Θ ↦ KL(ν_i‖Θ_#ν_j) at a point implies DQM of the family there. The first example contradicts this numerically; an analytic estimate KL(ν‖σ_#ν) = O(ε² log(1/ε)) would make the counterexample rigorous, and I did not derive it.

### D02-LINKS-10: Citation locators are missing or chapter-level, and Culver's criterion is quoted in a compressed form that invites a wrong reading
**Category:** citation
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/02_interactions.tex:53 (KullbackLeibler1951), :92 and supplements/s02_geometry.tex:40 (Culver1966), chapters/02_interactions.tex:202 (Higham2008, "Chapter 1"), supplements/s02_geometry.tex:13 and :55 (Higham2008 without a locator); persists from 2026-10-22 L01-FOUND-20 and X6-LIT-13

## Extensions and improvements

The reciprocal split should be lifted to the group before the program uses it as transport input. In the tied case, let n_j = θ_jiθ_ij ∈ G and suppose n_j = exp(X) with X in the Lie algebra, where every eigenvalue of dϱ_a(X) has absolute imaginary part below π for each sector. Then Ω^G_ij = θ_ij exp(−X/2) satisfies Ω^G_ji = (Ω^G_ij)^{-1}, because n_i = exp(Ad_{θ_ij}X). It transforms by g_iΩ^G_ijg_j^{-1}, and its image ϱ_a(Ω^G_ij) is the sector split of eq:int-reciprocal, by uniqueness of the square root with spectrum in the open right half-plane. The tie is then preserved, and the reciprocal parts are G-valued, as app:connection line 8 requires for principal G-connections. For the declared Gaussian model representation, fundamental ⊕ Sym², the strip condition holds whenever every eigenvalue of n_j has |arg| < π/2. Outside it the per-sector splits exist but disagree (D02-LINKS-01). Difficulty: low for the statement; continuation across the boundary of the domain is the same open problem as for the per-sector split.

The law-adjoint condition of D02-LINKS-04 has a second consequence that bears directly on the unipotent blind spot recorded at prop:int-loop-defect. If every mutual pair in a cycle is law-adjoint, each reciprocal part carries Σ_j onto Σ_i by congruence. The reciprocal holonomy of any cycle based at i0 therefore preserves Σ_{i0}: Ω_{i0i1}⋯Ω_{iki0} lies in O(Σ_{i0}) = Σ_{i0}^{1/2}O(K)Σ_{i0}^{-1/2}, a compact group. On this compact group, conjugacy classes are closed, so the unipotent blind spot cannot occur. A holonomy H in O(Σ_{i0}) is similar to the orthogonal, hence normal, matrix Σ_{i0}^{-1/2}HΣ_{i0}^{1/2}, so the traces Tr(H^k) determine its eigenvalues and its conjugacy class in O(Σ_{i0}); in particular Tr H = K iff H = I. For law-adjoint fields, then, the reciprocal holonomy is detected by traces, and all of the nonreciprocity sits in the positive backtracks. Combined with a displacement family in the sense of chapter 16, the same identity at neighboring contexts gives a connection compatible with the covariance field: in the convention D = d + B of app:connection, with transport dT/dt = −B(γ̇)T, the condition is dΣ + BΣ + ΣB^T = 0, the law-dressed analogue of metric compatibility. Difficulty: low for the one-context statement; moderate for the contextual version, which needs the chapter 16 selector to be law-adjoint.

Chapter 2 never states the pure-gauge case; grep finds neither "pure gauge" nor "coboundary" in chapter 2, s02 or c02. A one-paragraph proposition would close this. Take a directed link field whose underlying undirected graph is connected. It is pure gauge, Θ_ij = V_iV_j^{-1} for some V_i, iff two conditions hold: every mutual pair is reciprocal (Q_ij = 0, equivalently N ≡ I), and the holonomy of every cycle of the underlying graph is the identity when edges traversed against their orientation are read through Θ^{-1}. The proof is a spanning-tree construction. Two consequences deserve a sentence. L = 0 is sufficient but not necessary: the fusion links built from χ(μ_i − μ_j) are nonzero coboundaries, as the 2026-10-22 X1-GAUGE-01 check showed. Curvature diagnostics must therefore read cycle products, never generators. Difficulty: low.

prop:int-loop-defect and prop:int-loop-blindness are two faces of one object, and saying so would strengthen both. For Y ~ ν_i with mean m and covariance Σ, Q_ij = ½E‖(I − W)(Y − m)‖²_{Σ^{-1}}. A Monte Carlo estimate with 4·10^5 samples gives 26.64 against the closed form 26.59. Q is therefore the expected squared Mahalanobis displacement along the shared-sample coupling of the centered variable Y − m and its image. Since that coupling is admissible for the transport problem between the centered law ν̄ and W_#ν̄, Q ≥ ½W_2²(ν̄, W_#ν̄) in the Σ^{-1} metric, and the inequality is strict on the stabilizer, where W_2 = 0 and Q > 0. Q is thus the second-moment instance of the shared-sample record. This suggests record-level loop statistics for non-Gaussian laws of the form E_ν c(Y, WY), for a law-dressed cost c, with the same invariance proof whenever c is built from the law. Difficulty: low.

The larger invariant ring under the common reference group has a direct graphical reading. In displayed coordinates, Tr(e^{L_12}e^{L_34}) = Tr(U_1^{-1}Θ_12U_2U_3^{-1}Θ_34U_4) is the trace of a closed path that uses the flat frame links U_2U_3^{-1} and U_4U_1^{-1}. The dressing field supplies a complete graph of flat links, and words in reference links are oriented-cycle traces in the quiver augmented by that graph. This explains, in one sentence, why the common-reference invariants exceed the per-vertex quiver invariants of Le Bruyn and Procesi, and it marks exactly which invariants depend on treating the frames as agent state. Difficulty: low.
