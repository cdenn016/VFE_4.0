### D16-OPEN-01: The classification "two-stage selection, not a recursive formation" is not established, because no clause of the standard is shown to fail
**Category:** gap
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/12_open_formation.tex:4 ("The construction is therefore a two-stage selection with exact inheritance, not a recursive formation"), :248-249 (open:null-control), :519 and :526 (text after open:formation); chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1392 (definition of the two-stage class) and :1394 ("it is a two-stage selection with exact inheritance"); chapters/06_outlook.tex:10 ("Every construction is therefore a single-stage or two-stage selection with exact inheritance, and each chapter states which clauses of the standard fail")

Chapter 12 proves (F1) to (F4) and (F6a) (12:526) and says of the remaining two only that the theorem "does not establish (F5)" and that the counterfactual part (F6b) "is not verified". Intersecting with the routing neighborhood gives an admitted class on which the null forms the same block hierarchy; with (F5) read as in D16-OPEN-02, (F5) fails there and the classification follows. Until that is written, replace "is therefore a two-stage selection with exact inheritance, not a recursive formation" by "is not shown to be a recursive formation: (F5) and (F6b) are not established".
**Falsifies:** A proof in the manuscript that (F4), (F5) or (F6b) fails on the admitted class, or a reading of chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1392 under which "not established" suffices for the two-stage class.

### D16-OPEN-02: Under the literal (F5), reordering of the nearly simultaneous first-stage events gives $P\neq P^0$ on open wedges of the admitted class
**Category:** weakness
**Severity:** medium
**Location:** chapters/05_inheritance.tex:1379 (inh:null-flow: $P$ is the history with event times removed) and :1389 ((F5)); chapters/12_open_formation.tex:164-166 and 230 (reference: every pair starts with half-gap 2, the core is reflection symmetric), :245 (perturbation of all twelve coordinates), :248-249 (open:null-control)
**Evidence:** $P(x)$ is the ordered sequence of selected partitions. When two disjoint pairs merge at distinct times the intermediate partition records which came first, so reversing their order changes $P$ although the formed blocks, the second stage and the final partition agree. The reference of open:routing places the three first-stage events at nearly the same time and the two core pairs at exactly the same time. The exact scaled field at fixed $h$ makes event times position dependent. For the null, a pair with scaled center $U$ and half-gap $b$ crosses $\beta=1/10$ at $t\approx t^0+c_Nh^2U^2$ with $t^0=\log(b/\sqrt{\log9/2})$ and $c_N=(2-e^{-2t^0})/2=0.8627$ (from $\operatorname{atanh}x(t)=e^{-t}\operatorname{atanh}x(0)$ and $d\approx\tfrac12(1-\bar x^2)(\Delta\vartheta)^2$); s9 measures $(t_N(\text{far})-t_N(\text{core}))/(hR)^2=0.8629$ at $h=10^{-9}$, and the corresponding full-field coefficient $c_F=0.826$. The full-minus-null offsets of the core pair and the far pair therefore differ by $1.14\times10^{-5}$, and a relative perturbation $\eta$ of the far pair's initial gap reverses the far-core order under one flow and not the other for $\eta\in(-2.656\times10^{-4},-2.543\times10^{-4})$: the scan finds different orders at $\eta=-2.65,-2.625,-2.60,-2.575,-2.55\times10^{-4}$ and equal orders at the other 23 values (s9). That wedge lies at relative distance $2.6\times10^{-4}$ from the reference, so it is in the admitted class only if the unquantified neighborhood is that large. For the two core pairs the null and full tie hypersurfaces $\{t(01)=t(23)\}$ both pass through the reflection-symmetric reference. In the plane of (gap, center shift) of pair 01 their slopes are $8c_Nh^2A$ and $8c_Fh^2A/0.99866$ (gap sensitivity $1/g$ for the null and $1/(g(1+4p(g/2)))$ for the full field at $g=4$); since $c_N\neq c_F/0.99866$ they are transverse, and every neighborhood of the reference contains an open wedge on which the two flows order the core pairs differently. This second wedge (opening of order $10^{-15}$ at $A=4096$, $h=10^{-9}$) is argued from the first-order expansion and not resolved numerically. On such wedges the literal (F5) holds for a reason unrelated to interaction; with (F4) proved, the literal classification would then rest on (F6b) alone.
**Fix:** In inh:null-flow compare the formed block hierarchy (the set of blocks with their nesting) or the partition sequence modulo reordering of disjoint merges, which is what "A difference only in event times does not count" intends. In chapter 12 state the null control for the block hierarchy, or take the admitted class as a small neighborhood of a reference with separated first-stage event times (for example initial half-gaps 2, 2.05, 2.1, which separate the pair events by about 0.025 under both flows); there the ordered sequence is locally constant for both flows and $P=P^0$ holds literally.
**Falsifies:** A proof that the full and null event-time offsets of disjoint pairs coincide identically near the reference (then the tie hypersurfaces coincide and no wedge exists), or a definition of $P$ that already ignores the order of disjoint merges.

### D16-OPEN-05: "On the product reference family the phases are constant" is false off the von Mises subfamily
**Category:** error
**Severity:** low
**Location:** chapters/12_open_formation.tex:421 (open:loss-information)
**Evidence:** open:product-reference admits arbitrary positive angular profiles, which evolve as $f_0^{e^{-2t}}$ (open:angular-flow). The phase of the first moment is fixed only for von Mises and other axially symmetric profiles. For $\log f_0=\cos\theta+2\cos(2\theta-1)$ the phase is 0.3929 at $t=0$, 0.2913 at $t=0.347$, 0.0793 at $t=1.151$ and 0.0084 at $t=2.303$; a von Mises phase stays at 0.9 to twelve digits (s4). The conclusion of the remark survives with the right reason: by equivariance the discarded offsets are constant group elements $b\in\mathsf H_I$ along the flow ($\tau_bx(t)$ is the trajectory of $\tau_bx(0)$), and a record of agent $i$ at time $t$ carries Fisher information $\mathbb E_{q_i(t)}[(\partial_\theta\log q_i)^2]$ about $b_i$. That inertia decays (6.29 at $t=0$, 0.085 at $t=1.15$ for the profile above), so $n_i$ records carry the sum of the inertias at their record times, not $n_i$ times one inertia, and the circular mean attains the $(n\mathcal I)^{-1/2}$ rate only for von Mises profiles; for a general profile the efficient estimator is the maximum-likelihood location estimate.
**Fix:** Replace "On the product reference family the phases are constant, so" by "By equivariance the discarded offsets are constant group elements along the flow, so", replace "$n_i$ times its inertia" by the sum of the inertias at the record times, and replace "their circular mean estimates" by "the maximum-likelihood location estimate from them estimates", or restrict the sentence to the von Mises family.
**Falsifies:** A proof that anchor phases are constant for every positive angular profile under open:angular-flow; s4 is a counterexample.
