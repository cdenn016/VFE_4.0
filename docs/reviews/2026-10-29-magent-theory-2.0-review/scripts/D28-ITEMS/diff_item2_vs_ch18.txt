+ \theoremheading{Global flow in the Gaussian tangent family}{it2:coupled-global} Fix a finite graph, fixed codes and operating maps $\kappa_i$, proper Gaussian content references with $P_i\succ0$, a fixed normalized dictionary prior with $A_\omega\succ0$, message noises $R_e\succ0$, and positive fixed normalized source priors and temperatures.
+ From every finite initial mean, positive-definite content and dictionary covariance, and interior initial row, \cref{it2:coupled-velocities,it2:coupled-row-flow} has a unique solution for every $t\geq0$.
+ The means remain bounded, all covariance eigenvalues remain bounded above and away from zero, and all row entries have positive uniform lower bounds depending on the initial state and fixed data.
+ The Gibbs-reduced convention has the same conclusions.
+ Both potentials have an interior stationary point; along either flow the squared Fisher-gradient norm is integrable and tends to zero, and every accumulation point is stationary.
+ \begin{proof} The vector fields are smooth on the product of mean spaces, positive-definite covariance cones, and simplex interiors, so they have unique maximal interior solutions.
+ All terms of $\mathcal E_T$ are nonnegative.
+ Since $C_e\geq0$ and the priors are normalized, $0<Z_i\leq1$, so $E_T\geq F_0\geq0$ as well.
+ Energy dissipation therefore bounds each content self KL and the dictionary KL by the initial energy.
+ If $\lambda_k$ are the eigenvalues of $P_i^{1/2}\Sigma_iP_i^{1/2}$, the content KL is \begin{equation} \tfrac12\|\mu_i-m_i\|_{P_i}^2+ \tfrac12\sum_k(\lambda_k-\log\lambda_k-1).
+ \label{it2:coupled-coercivity} \end{equation} The scalar summand is nonnegative and diverges at zero and infinity.
+ Thus the means are bounded and the covariances are spectrally bounded in the interior.
+ The same argument applies to the eigenvalues of $A_\omega^{1/2}VA_\omega^{1/2}$ and the mean $a-a_0$ in \cref{it2:coupled-costs}.
+ Fixed positive-definite $A_\omega$ converts these to bounds on $V$, and hence on every $V_e$.
+ The product-covariance restriction is closed within these bounds.
+ The matter variables therefore stay in a compact interior set $\mathcal K$, on which every continuous cost lies in $[0,M]$ for some finite $M$.
+ For Gibbs rows, $\beta_e^*\geq\pi_e e^{-M/T_i}$.
+ For a full row put $\eta_{ef}=\log(\beta_e/\pi_e)-\log(\beta_f/\pi_f)$.
+ Subtracting \cref{it2:coupled-row-flow} in logarithmic form gives \begin{equation} \dot\eta_{ef}=-T_i\eta_{ef}-(C_e-C_f),\qquad |\eta_{ef}(t)|\leq \max\{|\eta_{ef}(0)|,M/T_i\}.
+ \label{it2:coupled-row-floor} \end{equation} The bound follows by variation of constants.
+ Let $L_i$ be the maximum of these bounds over the row.
+ With $u_e=\beta_e/\pi_e$, one has $u_f\leq e^{L_i}u_e$ and $\sum_f\pi_fu_f=1$, so $\beta_e\geq\pi_e e^{-L_i}>0$.
+ Singleton rows are identically one.
+ The full trajectory stays in a compact subset of its smooth domain; ordinary finite-dimensional continuation excludes a finite maximal time.
+ The reduced field is smooth on $\mathcal K$ and has the same conclusion.
+ The reduced potential attains a global minimum on a nonempty compact sublevel by \cref{it2:coupled-coercivity}; the minimum lies in the interior.
+ Its Gibbs rows give a full stationary point by \cref{it2:row-gap}.
+ Finally let $\mathcal D$ be the right side of \cref{it2:coupled-dissipation}, with the row term omitted in the reduced convention.
+ Integrating dissipation gives $\int_0^\infty\mathcal D(t)dt\leq E_{\mathrm{init}}$.
+ On the compact trajectory closure $\mathcal D$ and its derivative along the smooth vector field are bounded, so $\mathcal D$ is uniformly continuous.
+ A nonnegative uniformly continuous integrable function tends to zero: otherwise disjoint intervals of fixed positive width would each contribute a fixed positive amount to its integral.
+ Continuity and the positive-definite Fisher metric then make every accumulation point stationary.
+ \end{proof} These bounds need not be uniform as temperature tends to zero or reference precisions become singular.
+ The theorem concerns a fixed finite Gaussian family; it neither proves uniqueness of the stationary point nor asserts convergence to one particular point.
+ Its hypotheses exclude moving operating contents and changes of the reference laws.
+ \propositionheading{Source adaptation and the complete Hessian}{it2:coupled-critical-structure} On any regular finite-dimensional matter family where $F_0$ and all $C_e$ are twice differentiable, let $c_e=\nabla C_e$ and $\bar c_i=\sum_{e\in\mathcal I_i}\beta_e^*c_e$.
+ The reduced Hessian is \begin{equation} H_{\mathrm{red}}=\nabla^2F_0+\sum_e\beta_e^*\nabla^2C_e -\sum_iT_i^{-1}\left[ \sum_{e\in\mathcal I_i}\beta_e^*c_ec_e^T-\bar c_i\bar c_i^T\right].
+ \label{it2:coupled-hessian} \end{equation} At a critical point the full and reduced Hessians have the same negative and zero indices.
+ With either positive-definite Fisher metric, a positive-definite Hessian gives local exponential stability, while a negative direction gives instability.
+ The linearized eigenvalues are real; a zero Hessian mode requires a separate nonlinear analysis.
+ \begin{proof} Differentiating the normalized Gibbs row gives $d\beta_e^*[\xi]=-\beta_e^*(dC_e[\xi]-\sum_f\beta_f^*dC_f[\xi])/T_i$.
+ Differentiate \cref{it2:row-gap} once more to obtain \cref{it2:coupled-hessian}.
+ For a row tangent $v_i$ with $\sum_ev_e=0$, write $z_e=dC_e[\xi]$ and $\bar z_i=\sum_e\beta_e^*z_e$.
+ At a critical point, direct completion of squares gives \begin{equation} \delta^2\mathcal E_T[(\xi,v),(\xi,v)] =\xi^TH_{\mathrm{red}}\xi +\sum_iT_i\sum_{e\in\mathcal I_i} \frac{[v_e+\beta_e^*(z_e-\bar z_i)/T_i]^2}{\beta_e^*}.
+ \label{it2:coupled-inertia} \end{equation} The shift of $v_i$ has zero sum, so this is an invertible tangent change of variables plus a positive row form.
+ It is the Schur-complement identity behind \cref{prop:dyn-morse} and Haynsworth's inertia relation \citep{Haynsworth1968}.
+ At a stationary point the flow Jacobian is $-g^{-1}H$, because the metric derivative multiplies a vanished gradient.
+ It is similar to the symmetric matrix $-g^{-1/2}Hg^{-1/2}$.
+ Positive definiteness gives a negative spectrum and local exponential stability; a negative Hessian direction gives a positive eigenvalue and instability.
+ A zero eigenvalue is not classified by this argument.
+ \end{proof} The last term of \cref{it2:coupled-hessian} lowers the quadratic form relative to rows frozen at the same state and weights, by $-\sum_iT_i^{-1}\operatorname{Var}_{\beta_i^*}(dC_e[\xi])$ in direction $\xi$.
+ This is not a general ordering in temperature: changing temperature usually changes the weights and the stationary point.
+ Nor does finite row relaxation create a Hopf bifurcation at an interior stationary point of this exact gradient system.
+ \section{An exact stability threshold with changing uncertainty} \label{it2:ch-threshold} The following example keeps every content and dictionary mean and variance dynamic.
+ It shows why the fixed-covariance mean theorem does not decide stability after source selection and uncertainty are allowed to respond.
+ \theoremheading{A three-agent coupled threshold}{it2:coupled-threshold} Take three scalar content laws $q_i=\mathcal N(\mu_i,v_i)$, $i=0,1,2$, and two scalar dictionary laws $r_j=\mathcal N(a_j,V_j)$ on edges $j\to0$, $j=1,2$.
+ Set $\bar T_e=1$, $R_e=1$, $\kappa_0=1$, and $\lambda=0$.
+ Use content reference precisions $(P_0,P_1,P_2)=(1,5/4,5/4)$ and means $(m_0,m_1,m_2)=(0,-23/15,23/15)$, and independent dictionary references $\mathcal N(0,4/3)$.
+ Receiver $0$ has exactly the two peer candidates, with prior $(1/2,1/2)$ and temperature $T>0$; no identity self candidate is added to this row.
+ The source agents may have singleton identity self rows, with zero contribution.
+ In the coordinate order \begin{equation} x=(\mu_0,\mu_1,\mu_2,a_1,a_2,v_0,v_1,v_2,V_1,V_2), \label{it2:threshold-coordinates} \end{equation} the point \begin{equation} x_*=(0,-4/3,4/3,1/3,-1/3,1,1,1,1,1),\qquad \beta_*=(1/2,1/2) \label{it2:threshold-point} \end{equation} is stationary for every $T>0$.
+ For both the Gibbs-reduced flow and full unit-mobility row relaxation it is locally exponentially stable when $T>T_c=3269/10118$, and a hyperbolic saddle with one unstable direction when $0<T<T_c$.
+ At $T=T_c$ the Hessian has exactly one zero mode.
+ If all five variances are instead held at one, the mean-only stability threshold is $T_{\mathrm{mean}}=31/122<T_c$.
+ \begin{proof} Put $S_i=v_i+1$ and $d_j=\mu_0-\mu_j-a_j$.
+ The costs and full objective are \begin{equation} \begin{aligned} C_j&=\tfrac12[(S_0+d_j^2+V_j)/S_j-1+\log S_j-\log S_0],\\ \mathcal E_T(x,b)&=F_0(x)+bC_1+(1-b)C_2 +T[b\log(2b)+(1-b)\log(2(1-b))], \end{aligned} \label{it2:threshold-objective} \end{equation} where $F_0$ is the sum of the five Gaussian self KL terms with the references just specified.
+ At $x_*$, $S_i=2$, $d_1=1$, $d_2=-1$, and $C_1=C_2=1/2$, so $b_*=1/2$.
+ The two source self mean derivatives are $(1/4,-1/4)$ and their weighted edge derivatives are $(-1/4,1/4)$; the dictionary mean derivatives have the same cancellation, and the two receiver mean forces cancel each other.
+ Each source variance has self and weighted edge derivatives $1/8$ and $-1/8$, each dictionary variance has $-1/8$ and $1/8$, and the receiver variance derivatives vanish.
+ Thus all ten matter derivatives vanish.
+ Let $H=\nabla^2(F_0+(C_1+C_2)/2)(x_*)$ and $g=\nabla(C_1-C_2)(x_*)$.
+ Separate the first and last five coordinates by a semicolon; then \begin{equation} g=(1,-1/2,-1/2,-1/2,-1/2;0,-1/4,1/4,1/4,-1/4)^T .
+ \label{it2:threshold-g} \end{equation} For an arbitrary perturbation $\xi=(u_0,u_1,u_2,b_1,b_2,h_0,h_1,h_2,k_1,k_2)$, set $z_j=u_0-u_j-b_j$ and $\epsilon_1=1$, $\epsilon_2=-1$.
+ Direct differentiation of \cref{it2:threshold-objective} gives \begin{equation} \begin{aligned} \xi^TH\xi={}&u_0^2+\tfrac54(u_1^2+u_2^2) +\tfrac34(b_1^2+b_2^2)+\tfrac12h_0^2\\ &+\sum_{j=1}^2\left[ \tfrac14(z_j-\epsilon_jh_j/2)^2+\tfrac1{16}(h_0-h_j)^2 +\tfrac{71}{128}h_j^2+\tfrac12(k_j-h_j/8)^2\right].
+ \end{aligned} \label{it2:threshold-positive} \end{equation} For completeness, one weighted edge contributes $z_j^2/4-\epsilon_jz_jh_j/4+h_0^2/16-h_0h_j/8+3h_j^2/16-h_jk_j/8$.
+ Adding the self mean terms and the five self variance terms $\sum_i h_i^2/2+\sum_j k_j^2/2$ and completing squares gives \cref{it2:threshold-positive}.
+ Its vanishing forces all ten components of $\xi$ to vanish, proving $H\succ0$ without restricting the tangent space.
+ Solving the linear equations specified by this quadratic form gives, in the same two blocks, \begin{equation} \begin{aligned} (H^{-1}g)_{\mu,a}&=(2400,-960,-960,-1600,-1600)^T/5059,\\ (H^{-1}g)_{v,V}&=(0,-716,716,2440,-2440)^T/5059,\\ g^TH^{-1}g&=6538/5059.
+ \end{aligned} \label{it2:threshold-inverse} \end{equation} These rational values are checked by multiplying with the symmetric matrix of \cref{it2:threshold-positive}.
+ At the equal-cost point, \cref{it2:coupled-hessian} becomes $H_T=H-gg^T/(4T)$.
+ Congruence by $H^{-1/2}$ gives the identity minus one rank-one matrix, with nine positive eigenvalues and the remaining sign determined by $1-g^TH^{-1}g/(4T)$.
+ Hence $T_c=g^TH^{-1}g/4=3269/10118$, with the stated positive, negative, and zero indices.
+ The inverse matter Fisher metric is \begin{equation} M(x)=\operatorname{diag}(v_0,v_1,v_2,V_1,V_2, 2v_0^2,2v_1^2,2v_2^2,2V_1^2,2V_2^2).
+ \label{it2:threshold-mobility} \end{equation} For full row relaxation the inverse metric is $\operatorname{diag}(M(x),b(1-b))$ and the stationary Hessian is \begin{equation} \mathbb H_T=\begin{pmatrix}H&g\\g^T&4T\end{pmatrix},\qquad (\xi,\delta b)^T\mathbb H_T(\xi,\delta b) =\xi^TH_T\xi+4T(\delta b+g^T\xi/(4T))^2 .
+ \label{it2:threshold-full} \end{equation} It has the same negative and zero indices, with an additional positive direction.
+ \Cref{it2:coupled-critical-structure} therefore gives the asserted stability classifications in all ten or eleven coordinates.
+ To freeze the variances, set $h_i=k_j=0$ in \cref{it2:threshold-positive}.
+ Put $t=u_0-(u_1+u_2+b_1+b_2)/2=g_{\mathrm{mean}}^T\xi_{\mathrm{mean}}$.
+ Convexity and exchange of the two sources put the minimum of the mean quadratic at fixed $t$ in $u_1=u_2=y$, $b_1=b_2=z$.
+ Minimizing there gives $(u_0,y,z)=(15,-6,-10)t/31$ and value $61t^2/62$.
+ The minimum of $\xi^TH_{\mathrm{mean}}\xi$ under $g_{\mathrm{mean}}^T\xi=t$ is also $t^2/(g_{\mathrm{mean}}^TH_{\mathrm{mean}}^{-1}g_{\mathrm{mean}})$ by completing a positive quadratic square.
+ Thus the inverse quadratic form is $62/61$ and $T_{\mathrm{mean}}=31/122$.
+ \end{proof} The exact inequality $31/122<3/10<3269/10118$ means that at $T=3/10$ the frozen-variance mean system is stable while both complete coupled systems have one unstable direction.
+ At $T_c$ the reduced null vector is $H^{-1}g$, and the full null vector is $(H^{-1}g,-1)$.
+ Source and dictionary uncertainty participate in the zero linear mode.
+ The following nonlinear calculation classifies that mode within this Gaussian tangent model.
+ \section{The local subcritical pitchfork} \label{it2:ch-critical-mode} Retain all hypotheses, references, coordinates, and Fisher mobilities of \cref{it2:coupled-threshold}.
+ Write $s=b-1/2$, $\delta=T-T_c$, and \begin{equation} \begin{aligned} A(x)&=F_0(x)+(C_1(x)+C_2(x))/2,\qquad \Delta(x)=C_1(x)-C_2(x),\\ G(x,s)&=A(x)+s\Delta(x),\\ h(s)&=(1/2+s)\log(1+2s)+(1/2-s)\log(1-2s).
+ \end{aligned} \label{it2:critical-functions} \end{equation} Thus $\mathcal E_T=G+Th$.
+ The scalar $s$ is the actual row imbalance for full relaxation, and the Gibbs row imbalance $s=b^*(x,T)-1/2$ for the reduced flow.
+ Both reductions below use the original time variable.
+ \theoremheading{Critical dynamics and nearby branches}{it2:critical-pitchfork} There are local parameter-dependent invariant center manifolds on which \begin{equation} \dot s=-\frac{4\delta s+c s^3}{d} +O(\delta^2|s|+|\delta||s|^3+|s|^5),\qquad d=\begin{cases}D,&\text{Gibbs reduction},\\D+4,&\text{full row relaxation},\end{cases} \label{it2:critical-normal-form} \end{equation} where the remainder is uniform near $(s,\delta)=(0,0)$ and \begin{equation} c=-\frac{657534393590529472}{6223404588394626861}<0,\qquad D=\frac{19189456}{25593481}>0.
+ \label{it2:critical-constants} \end{equation} In sufficiently small state and temperature neighborhoods, the symmetric equilibrium is the only equilibrium for $\delta\leq0$.
+ For each sufficiently small $\delta>0$ there are exactly two additional equilibria, common to the full and reduced systems, with \begin{equation} s_\pm(\delta)=\pm\sqrt{-4\delta/c}+O(\delta^{3/2}).
+ \label{it2:critical-branches} \end{equation} Each asymmetric equilibrium has one unstable direction, with eigenvalue $8\delta/d+O(\delta^2)$, and nine reduced or ten full stable directions.
+ At $T=T_c$ the symmetric equilibrium is nonlinearly unstable.
+ With destabilizing parameter $T_c-T$, this is a subcritical pitchfork; it creates no locally stable asymmetric branch.
+ \begin{proof} The reflection and source exchange \begin{equation} R x=(-\mu_0,-\mu_2,-\mu_1,-a_2,-a_1,v_0,v_2,v_1,V_2,V_1) \label{it2:critical-reflection} \end{equation} fixes $x_*$, preserves $A$, and sends $\Delta$ to $-\Delta$.
+ The mobility satisfies $M(Rx)=RM(x)R^T$.
+ Since $H=A_{xx}(x_*)\succ0$, the implicit-function theorem gives a unique analytic stationary matter curve $X(s)$ near $x_*$ with \begin{equation} G_x(X(s),s)=0,\qquad X(0)=x_*,\qquad p=X'(0)=-H^{-1}g.
+ \label{it2:critical-stationary-curve} \end{equation} Uniqueness gives $X(-s)=RX(s)$.
+ The stationary scalar potential $\Phi(s,T)=G(X(s),s)+Th(s)$ is therefore even.
+ This curve solves the matter stationary equations at each fixed row; invariance under either flow has not been asserted.
+ All tensor derivatives in the next calculation are at $x_*$.
+ Put $k=A_{xxx}[p,p]+2\Delta_{xx}p$.
+ Differentiating \cref{it2:critical-stationary-curve} twice gives $X''(0)=-H^{-1}k$.
+ Four derivatives of $\Phi$ then give \begin{equation} \Phi_{ssss}(0,T_c) =A_{xxxx}[p,p,p,p]+4\Delta_{xxx}[p,p,p]-3k^TH^{-1}k+32T_c=6c.
+ \label{it2:critical-quartic} \end{equation} For the explicit costs in \cref{it2:threshold-objective} and the tangent $p$ in \cref{it2:threshold-inverse}, the three matter contractions are \begin{equation} \begin{aligned} A_{xxxx}[p,p,p,p]&=\frac{236914859731152}{655026269697361},\\ \Delta_{xxx}[p,p,p]&=-\frac{31633266640}{129477420379},\\ 3k^TH^{-1}k&=\frac{21485600170644766720}{2074468196131542287}.
+ \end{aligned} \label{it2:critical-contractions} \end{equation} These values follow by differentiating the rational costs and Gaussian self terms; together with $32T_c$ they give \cref{it2:critical-constants}.
+ The negative stable-response term in \cref{it2:critical-quartic} would be absent if one restricted the potential to the straight line $x_*+ps$.
+ Since $\Phi_{ss}(0,T)=4(T-T_c)$ and $h$ is even, \begin{equation} \Phi_s(s,T_c+\delta)=4\delta s+c s^3+O(|\delta||s|^3+|s|^5).
+ \label{it2:critical-stationary-force} \end{equation} Analyticity and evenness give $\Phi_s=sQ(s^2,\delta)$ with $Q(0,\delta)=4\delta$ and $Q_z(0,0)=c<0$.
+ A second implicit-function argument gives the unique small root $z(\delta)=-4\delta/c+O(\delta^2)$.
+ It is positive precisely for small positive $\delta$, proving the complete local equilibrium count and \cref{it2:critical-branches}.
+ At these points $G_{xx}$ remains positive definite, whereas $\Phi_{ss}=-8\delta+O(\delta^2)<0$.
+ The latter is its Schur complement in the full stationary Hessian.
+ Thus that Hessian has one negative and ten positive directions.
+ Eliminating the positive row block gives one negative and nine positive directions in the reduced Hessian.
+ Positive Fisher mobilities preserve these stability indices.
+ It remains to compute the actual center velocity.
+ The fields are analytic on the interior and equivariant under the orthogonal reflection above.
+ At $T_c$ each has a simple zero eigenvalue and all remaining eigenvalues are negative.
+ The parameter-dependent equivariant center-manifold theorem \citep[Theorem~1.2]{Ruelle1973Symmetry} therefore supplies a local $C^7$ invariant reduction containing the nearby equilibria; no analytic center manifold is required.
+ The full coordinate $s=b-1/2$ is regular on its center tangent $(p,1)$.
+ For the reduced flow, \begin{equation} D_xs^*(x_*,T_c)p=-g^Tp/(4T_c)=1, \label{it2:critical-reduced-coordinate} \end{equation} so its Gibbs imbalance also supplies a regular coordinate.
+ Reflection makes the scalar field $f(s,\delta)=\dot s$ odd in $s$.
+ Write $Y(s,\delta)$ for the matter coordinates on either invariant manifold.
+ Its matter equation is \begin{equation} Y_s f=-M(Y)G_x(Y,s).
+ \label{it2:critical-invariance} \end{equation} At $\delta=0$, $Y_s(0,0)=p$ and $f=O(s^3)$, so comparison through quadratic order gives $Y_{ss}(0,0)=X''(0)$.
+ Set $B=H^{-1}M_*^{-1}p$, with $M_*=M(x_*)$, and let $w=Y-X$.
+ Then \begin{equation} D=p^TM_*^{-1}p=\frac{19189456}{25593481},\qquad g^TB=-D.
+ \label{it2:critical-metric-length} \end{equation} If $f=\alpha\delta s+\eta s^3+O(\delta^2|s|+|\delta||s|^3+|s|^5)$, the coefficients of \cref{it2:critical-invariance} give $w_{\delta s}=-\alpha B$ and $w_{s^3}=-\eta B$.
+ Variation of $M$ contributes only at higher orders.
+ Consequently the row force $\Delta(Y)+(T_c+\delta)h'(s)$ has coefficients $4+D\alpha$ and $c+D\eta$ at $\delta s$ and $s^3$.
+ For the reduced flow this force is identically zero, so $\alpha=-4/D$ and $\eta=-c/D$.
+ Full relaxation instead obeys \begin{equation} f=-(1/4-s^2)[\Delta(Y)+(T_c+\delta)h'(s)], \label{it2:critical-full-row} \end{equation} giving $\alpha=-(4+D\alpha)/4$ and $\eta=-(c+D\eta)/4$.
+ These yield the denominator $D+4$ in \cref{it2:critical-normal-form}.
+ Odd $C^7$ regularity gives its stated uniform remainder.
+ In particular, \begin{equation} Y(s,0)-X(s)=\frac{c}{d}B s^3+O(s^4), \label{it2:critical-graph-difference} \end{equation} which exhibits the nonzero difference between stationary elimination and dynamical invariance.
+ At either asymmetric branch, differentiation of the center field gives $8\delta/d+O(\delta^2)>0$.
+ At criticality it gives $\dot s=(-c/d)s^3+O(s^5)$, pointing away from zero on both sides.
+ Arbitrarily small nonzero center data therefore leave a fixed small interval, proving nonlinear instability at the threshold.
+ The stable transverse spectrum persists from the Hessian calculation.
+ \end{proof} The additional $4$ is the Fisher squared length of the row tangent at $b=1/2$.
+ Thus full relaxation has $D/(D+4)=4797364/30390845$ times the leading center speed of matter-metric Gibbs reduction, or a leading time scale about $6.34$ times longer.
+ This comparison identifies the leading scalar polynomials after a constant time rescaling; it does not identify higher-order trajectories or stable transients.
+ Distant stable equilibria, folds, and the destinations of trajectories leaving this neighborhood require separate arguments.
+ The local result establishes neither monotone uncertainty growth, hysteresis, grouping, nor formation of an inherited parent.
+ \section{Scope and open problems} \label{it2:ch-next} \openproblemheading{Coupled dynamics beyond the finite example}{it2:open-reciprocal} \Cref{it2:coupled-flow,it2:coupled-global} give the complete Gaussian tangent flow and global forward existence, and \cref{it2:critical-pitchfork} resolves the local critical mode of the three-agent example as a subcritical pitchfork with nearby asymmetric saddles.
+ Determine its distant stationary branches and long-time destinations, the corresponding behavior on graphs with cycles, and well-posedness beyond regular finite-dimensional law families.
+ Neither the scalar certainty result nor this local instability establishes grouping or formation.
