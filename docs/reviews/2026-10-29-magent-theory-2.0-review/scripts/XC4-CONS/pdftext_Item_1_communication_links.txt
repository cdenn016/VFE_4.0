Status and summary

The supported results of this working document, with the rank, compatibility, integrability, and
observational-scope corrections below, are integrated into the main manuscript chapter Commu-
nication Codes and Learned Alignment and its explanatory companion. This standalone source
retains the derivations and open obligations. It uses the main manuscript's conventions: one
structure group G acts on the belief and model sectors through two representations, an agent's
frame is Ui  G, and statements are made at one context in reference coordinates unless they say
otherwise. To keep the formulas short, the belief sector is written with V = RK and G  GL(K, R)
acting by matrix multiplication; the model sector is analogous with its own representation.

The motivating picture is the following. When agent j communicates a belief to agent i, most of
what the belief means to j is lost: j projects it into a low-dimensional public package, a sentence, a
drawing or a signal, and i rebuilds it inside its own, larger internal space using its own conceptual
structure. In the manuscript's index convention, with receiver i and sender j, the chain is

qj - (Tj)#qj - noise - decoding by i - qi.  (1)

Two different objects are built from the same maps. The composite of a sending and a receiving
map has rank at most the width of the message space and is lossy. An exponentiated link with a
generator of the same form is invertible and loses nothing. This document keeps them apart: each
directed edge carries an invertible dictionary, which is the gauge field, and each modality carries a
lossy code.

The main results are these. The receiver's decoder is not a free map: for linear Gaussian codes
it is the gain computed from the receiver's own prior covariance, and the reconstructed belief
adopts the sender's belief along the communicated directions while keeping the receiver's own
belief along the discarded ones (proposition 2.4). For general laws and a noiseless code, the
meaning lost in transmission equals the total disagreement minus the disagreement visible through
the code (proposition 2.1). Coded messages identify the dictionary only up to the subgroup that
leaves the code unchanged, a subgroup of dimension K(K - r) in GL(K, R) for a code of rank
r (theorem 3.1); several modalities sharing one dictionary identify it up to the intersection of their
stabilizers, so overlapping modalities help less than independent ones (corollary 4.1). Exact
consensus of transported beliefs is possible exactly on the common fixed space of the holonomies
of the communication graph, so nontrivial holonomy on a loop is an obstruction to agreement, a
dissonance (theorem 5.1). Finally, with n coded messages per exchange the posterior over who was
heard behaves as a Gibbs row at temperature 1/n whose costs are divergences between message
laws, not between full beliefs (proposition 6.1). Learning the dictionaries from grounded messages
under a prior that penalizes holonomy concentrates the recognition law on the identified coset, and
within it the loop term fills exactly those invisible directions that create holonomy (theorem 7.2).
These are the same directions in which a true dissonance would go undetected and be absorbed
into the dictionaries (proposition 7.4). A pair detects its mutual misunderstanding only in what both

Codes and dictionaries

Definition 1.1 (Code of a modality). A modality c, such as speech, drawing or gesture, has a
public message space Hc = Rrc and a noise covariance Rc, either positive definite or zero for

a noiseless code. The message space carries no frame of its own: its coordinates are public
conventions. Sender j's code in modality c is a linear map T¯jc : V  Hc of rank rc  K on reference
coordinates. Its displayed form is Tjc = T¯jcUj-1. A message produced from displayed content yj is

h = Tjcyj + ,   N (0, Rc).                                            (1.1)

Under a passive change Uj  gjUj the displayed content becomes gjyj and the displayed code
becomes Tjcgj-1, so the message is unchanged. Public messages therefore do not depend on
the sender's frame, and the reference code T¯jc is unchanged by passive display changes at fixed
reference coordinates. Codes are agent specific. A shared language is part of a shared generative

model in the sense of the main manuscript: members of a species use codes related by the frame

map of their shared model.

Definition 1.2 (Dictionary). Receiver i's dictionary for sender j is

ij = Ui¯ij Uj-1  G,     ¯ij  G.                                       (1.2)

The general dictionary is unrestricted in G. The local parameterization ¯ij = exp(ij) with ij  g is
used for tangent learning. It can cover less than G: a single real matrix exponential need not cover
even GL+(K, R) [7]. The global coset and exact-identification results below concern the stated
group, and a restriction to the exponential image retains only the solutions lying in that image.

It is owned by the receiver: it is i's hypothesis about how j's internal reference coordinates sit
relative to its own. Content with reference coordinates y¯ for i corresponds, according to i, to ¯i-j1y¯
for j. The effective code that i attributes to j is therefore

A^icj = T¯jc¯i-j1.                                                    (1.3)

The dictionary ji is j's hypothesis about i. It is an independent datum, learned by a different
agent, so ji need not equal i-j1. The product ¯ij¯ji is the holonomy of the two-cycle i  j  i:
the composite of what i thinks j means with what j thinks i means. It is the identity exactly when

the two agents' translations of each other are mutually inverse; otherwise it measures mutual

misunderstanding. The main manuscript's decision not to impose the inverse convention is, in this

reading, the statement that two agents learn their dictionaries of each other separately.

Remark 1.3 (Why the dictionary is group valued). A model that i holds of j need not in
general be a group element, and the group-valued form is a hypothesis with a stated scope. Four
considerations support it within a species. First, the two agents' internal spaces are copies of
one G-space, and the relative position of two frames of one principal bundle at a context is a
group element; partial access is carried by the code, not by the dictionary. Second, one dictionary
serves every modality, which is what lets several codes pool their evidence in corollary 4.1. Third,
invertibility is used: the consensus theorem theorem 5.1 needs inverses to show that transported
beliefs are well defined along different paths, and group-valued holonomy composes invertible links;
singular path products can still have trace invariants, but do not define transport by group elements.
Fourth, i's knowledge of j is a probability law over G, or over ij in a declared exponential chart;
uncertainty about the dictionary is carried by that law. For agents of different kinds, whose internal
spaces differ in dimension or representation, an invertible dictionary does not exist. The natural
replacement is a linear or Markov map between different spaces, with consistency measured by
global sections of a sheaf rather than by holonomy [10].

Learning the dictionaries

The receiver owns its dictionaries and has to learn them. Theorem 3.1 says what messages can
teach: a coset of the stabilizer of the code. This chapter asks what a prior that rewards agreement
around loops adds to that, and answers the question exactly in the tangent model, the linearization
at the identity dictionaries. The identity is the natural prior mean. Before any exchange the receiver
assumes that the sender means what it would mean itself.

The tangent model is a computational simplification in two respects: it linearizes at the identity
dictionaries, and it takes Gaussian noise and a Gaussian anchor. Its conclusions split accordingly.
The invisible directions, the unreachable directions and their sheaf count, the split of the loop
space into filled and detectable directions as a dimension count, and the exact identification results
propositions 7.8 and 7.9 depend only on the identifiability kernel and on the loops; by remark 3.2
they hold for every location family of noise and every prior. The limiting law as a conditioned
Gaussian, the shrinkage and bias formulas, and the variances of remark 7.10 are features of the
Gaussian family.

7.6 Receiver-owned recognition laws

Remark 7.10 (Ownership and the loop term). In the per-agent picture of the main manuscript
each receiver infers only its own dictionaries, so its recognition law is one factor qi(i·) of a law
factorized over receivers. A two-cycle couples two receivers and a directed triangle three, so the
loop term couples the factors. In the tangent model the factorized law closest to the posterior in
DKL(qposterior) has the exact posterior means and, as covariances, the inverses of the diagonal
blocks of the posterior precision [5, Section 10.1.2]. These are smaller in the Loewner order than
the exact marginal covariances. In the Gaussian tangent model, ownership therefore leaves the
estimates of the dictionaries unchanged and makes each receiver overconfident about them. Exact
means are a feature of Gaussian targets: for a non-Gaussian posterior a factorized law generally
has means different from the exact ones, so for general laws ownership can change the estimates
as well. To update its factor, receiver i needs the current means of the dictionaries that other agents
hold along its loops: a loop prior can be used only if agents communicate about how they read each
other, in addition to communicating about the world. In the numerical check, coordinate ascent over
the three receivers of the complete exchange reproduces the exact means to 10-14, the differences
between exact and factorized block covariances are positive semidefinite, and the total variance is
4.59 against the exact 7.75.

Related work

The ingredients appear separately in the literature; their combination was not found in a standard
search, which is not exhaustive. Opinion dynamics on discourse sheaves [10] gives agents private
opinion spaces and linear expression maps into shared discourse spaces, with dynamics that can
also evolve the expression maps; opinions are vectors, and there is no receiver reconstruction,
gauge group or holonomy. A cognitive-geometric model of interpretation [1] uses linear interpretation
maps between agents' value spaces, with beliefs lost in their null spaces and composite maps
along chains. Communication without a shared reference frame treats unknown frame changes as
gauge data [3, 19]. The meeting of minds in conceptual spaces models meaning as maps between
individual spaces with agreement as a fixed point [21]. The relativistic theory of consciousness
[14] holds that an observer reading another system's representation measures only its physical
coordinates and constructs a new representation of its own; the code-then-reconstruction chain
eq. (1) is a formal version of that measurement, and the identity dictionary between equivalent
systems is the transformation it uses for that case. Communication as active inference with a
shared generative model is developed by Friston and Frith [8] and Friston et al. [9], and language
as efficient compression by Zaslavsky et al. [23], in the tradition of Shannon [16], the information
bottleneck [18] and coding with side information [22].
