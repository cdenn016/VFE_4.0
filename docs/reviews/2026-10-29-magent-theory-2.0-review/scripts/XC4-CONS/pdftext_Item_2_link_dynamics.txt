Status and summary

This working document preserves the standalone derivation of receiver-owned link dynamics. Its
supported results are integrated into the main manuscript's chapter on learning directed links and
its explanatory companion. The calculations distinguish a normalized global model, normalized
per-agent models with peers held as data, and a reciprocal potential on laws.
The dictionary derivatives agree with the global derivatives only in the plug-in per-agent model.
Integrating the peers' laws instead widens both record noise and loop factors. Equality of objective
derivatives does not imply equality of Fisher velocities unless the recognition metrics also agree.
The linear mean-flow results hold with the stated Gaussian covariances and source rows fixed. The
grounded-limit theorem covers the global and plug-in models; it does not cover coupled law-level
covariance updates.
Comparison records confound changes in content with changes in the dictionary. Gaussian condi-
tioning resolves a discrepancy among receiver adjustment, sender adjustment, and reinterpretation
according to their prior covariances. A directed ring gives a non-gradient per-agent flow; its weak-
anchor coupling vanishes only at nonzero operating content. A single loop gives an exact example
of overconfidence from factorization and recycled peer information. The reciprocal potential has a
source-role covariance derivative, but increasing stationary variance with disagreement is proved
only in the stated scalar regime. General-law extensions and the joint mean, covariance, and row
dynamics remain open.

Scope and open problems

Open problem 7.1 (Rows and covariances in the reciprocal potential). With Gibbs rows the
costs select sources and also affect sender covariances. Determine the stationary points and
stability of the coupled restricted-family dynamics of means, covariances, and rows. The constant-
coefficient mean-flow results do not settle this problem, and the scalar certainty result does not
establish grouping or formation.
Open problem 7.2 (Convergence of per-agent flows). Find conditions on the self, record, and
loop precisions under which the per-agent fixed point is unique and its mean flow converges.
Determine whether the law-level covariance updates have a unique fixed point. The directed ring
supplies one stable non-gradient example, not a general convergence theorem.
Open problem 7.3 (Beyond the tangent model). Keep the product e(yi-ci) and let the operating
contents follow the beliefs. Factorized recognition then acquires second-moment terms, and the
interpretation gauge of theorem 2.2 becomes a nonlinear orbit. Determine whether grounding still
separates belief from meaning at the nonlinear level.
Open problem 7.4 (General laws). The Gaussian tangent model is a computational simplification.
Extend its conclusions to general laws, with record kernels that read the coded discrepancy and
location-family noise. Four parts are open: the attribution of a coded disagreement among receiver
persuasion, sender persuasion and reinterpretation when contents and dictionary priors are not
Gaussian; the grounded limit, in which the reinterpretation share should vanish as grounding
identifies the dictionaries; whether disagreement changes certainty in the two free energies, and
how that Bayesian effect compares with the source-role moment matching of the reciprocal potential;
and the echo and cavity statements for non-Gaussian loop factors, where cavity messages are
belief propagation without Gaussian closure and the factorized means are no longer exact.
Open problem 7.5 (What agents must exchange). A receiver using a loop prior needs information
about dictionaries owned elsewhere. An echo-free protocol must specify which prior contributions
are removed from each transmitted law. Formulate that exchange through declared codes and
record kernels, and determine the loop information it makes observable.
