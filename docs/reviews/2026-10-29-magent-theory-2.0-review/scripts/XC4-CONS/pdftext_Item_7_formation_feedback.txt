Status and summary

This is a working document for the seventh item of the future-work list, consequences for formation.
It takes up the open problem on formation with top-down feedback left by the Item 3 working
document. It is not part of the main manuscript and is not cited by it. The question is whether
parents created by a state-determined handoff when a wall forms, and then acting back on their
members, give an inherited recursive formation in the sense of the standard in chapter 5 of the
main manuscript, clauses (F1) to (F6), with the null flow as the control for interaction necessity.
The constructions are stated for general probability laws. The Gaussian location family is the
computational instance, and every statement that depends on it says so.

Feedback of the obvious kinds does not change what forms. A parent added as one more source
in its members' rows multiplies the row part of each member's force by a common factor when
the member agrees with it, leaves record forces unchanged, and alters no exponent of the rows
(proposition 2.1); in an example without records the same blocks form and only the second stage
is delayed. In an objective with a gradient flow, such as the global joint of Items 2 and 3 or the
reciprocal potential with fixed rows, a top-down cost invariant under the collective motion of a
block exerts no force on the block's momentum whenever that motion has a momentum function,
as it does in the Gaussian location family (proposition 2.2). An exactly inherited pooled parent
therefore does not see it; the sharpening of walls reported in Item 3 passes through the distribution
of that momentum among the members, which sparse bridges read. A post-handoff flow with a
globally attracting equilibrium cannot meet the counterfactual clause (F6b) (proposition 2.3), and
the Gaussian tangent global joint of Items 2 and 3 is strictly convex, so no declared Gaussian
hierarchy in it can.

What changes outcomes is the pooling of evidence. When a block forms, its members share one
source label: they infer jointly which source to trust, with the prior over sources applied once and
their costs added. For general laws the shared row depends on the block only through the linear
pool of the members' laws, and it equals the Gibbs row of a single receiver holding that pool at
temperature T /n (proposition 3.2); for sources in an exponential family it depends only on the pooled
mean parameter. The parent presents the pool to its members and to every receiver outside the
block. A tempered label, which averages the members' costs instead of adding them, reproduces
the dynamics without feedback on internally synchronized configurations (proposition 3.4), so the
factor n in the exponent is the operative part of the feedback.

In the Gaussian location family the shared-label parent is exactly inherited and compresses. The
pooled mean of every block evolves autonomously, by the per-agent law of a single agent at
temperature T /n with one additional source at its own law, while the members' deviations from the
pool relax at a fixed rate to offsets determined by their records, whatever the spread within the block
(theorem 4.1). External equitable partitions give exact block-mean dynamics for linear consensus
on a fixed graph [15]; here the rows are state-dependent Gibbs rows. The result does not extend
beyond this family: if the pooled parent of a two-member block closes for every configuration and

In a twelve-agent instance with four groups of three conflicting records, the null flow never selects
a block (proposition 5.1). The shared-label flow selects the four groups and then, on each side,
the union of two first-stage parents. The second stage reduces to a one-dimensional equation for
the gap between two parents, with a stable merged state, an unstable middle root, and a stable
separated state held by the records. The union forms exactly when the first stage leaves the parents
inside the middle root (proposition 5.3 and corollary 5.4). Counterfactual handoffs outside it never
unite, while the control without feedback unites from every tested handoff. At slightly larger record
gaps the hierarchy stops at depth one in the separated state, and the distance from which two
blocks still unite shrinks with block size roughly as n-1/2.

Measured against the standard, the instance meets (F1) to (F6) (chapter 6). The qualifications are
these. The selector thresholds and the label-sharing handoff are declared, and the compression
interface is derived from that rule, not from dynamics that are unchanged at the handoff. The
compressed coordinates are transient and converge to record-determined offsets. Exactness holds
only in the Gaussian location family. The first-stage events and the unions from the actual handoffs
come from fixed-step numerical integration with explicit margins, not from validated arithmetic. The
strong form of (F5) fails, and the parameter window is narrow. For general laws the pooled parent is
not exactly inherited, and the inheritance clause of the general-law construction is open. A bounded
literature search found exact quotients of linear consensus, Bayesian inference of latent social
groups, representative agents for coherent groups, and Bayesian structure learning of hierarchies,
but no construction combining shared-label pooling, exact block-mean closure away from synchrony,
and merging that depends on the state at formation (chapter 8). This is not a priority claim.

A derived selector replaces the declared thresholds by a comparison of free energies (chapter 7).
A block is selected when the shared-label models that the handoff would create, together with the
models of all other units, have a lower free energy than the current ones, and the comparison has
held for the declared window. For general laws this structure free energy reads only the spread of
each block, n times a generalized Jensen­Shannon divergence, and the free energy of its pool
at temperature T /n, which includes the block's own parent at cost zero (proposition 7.2). Two
agents whose mutual attentions have a product above the peer prior gain from a shared label,
through a saving of labels and a double use of their laws in the pool (proposition 7.3). The derived
selector therefore groups whatever attention the initial state contains, and in the instance the null
flow selects the four groups at once. Interaction necessity moves to the second stage. The criterion
for uniting two parents is a radius, d = 0.7685 for blocks of three, below the middle root of the
gap equation (proposition 7.6), so the counterfactual dependence survives (corollary 7.7). With
p = 1.25 both flows select the four groups and only the shared-label flow unites them. The window
now decides the regime: with an earlier handoff the hierarchy stops at depth one, as the null does,
and with the window p = 2 of the declared instance the flow selects the two unions directly as a
single stage. No window gives interaction necessity at both stages in this instance.

is checks_output.txt; the checks support the instances tested and are not proofs. Statements
that rest on numerical integration are labeled as such.

Proposition 2.3 (A globally attracting equilibrium excludes counterfactual recursion). Fix

the first-stage blocks of a construction and let C be a set of post-handoff states, the counterfactual

given by strict inequalities between continuous functions of the state, defining an open set S, and
let persistence mean that the relation holds at all later times. If z  S, the block B is selected from
every state in C and (F6b) fails for B. If z / S¯, no state in C leads to a persistent selection of B.

Proof. If z  S, some neighborhood of z lies in S, and every trajectory from C remains in it after a

finite time, so the candidate persists beyond any window p. If the relation holds at all times after
some t0 along a trajectory converging to z, then z is a limit of points of S.

The Gaussian tangent global joint of Items 2 and 3 has a positive definite quadratic energy in the
contents and the dictionaries whenever the content anchors and the dictionary priors are positive
definite. Declared Gaussian parents add positive semidefinite cross-scale terms and a parent
anchor. The gradient flow of this energy in any constant metric therefore converges to its unique
minimizer from every state, every counterfactual handoff with the same first-stage blocks leads to
the same limit, and (F6b) fails for every persistent second stage that is selected at all. Counterfactual
recursion needs several stable states after a handoff, hence a nonconvex objective: Gibbs rows,
dictionaries acting through the exponential map, or non-Gaussian laws. The construction below
uses Gibbs rows.

Remark 3.3 (When the shared row is a posterior). In the registered-agreement model of
chapter 3 of the main manuscript, an agent's Gibbs row is the posterior of the source it heard,
given that it registered agreement with that source, at temperature equal to the reciprocal message
count. If the members of I heard one common source JI and their registrations are conditionally
independent given JI , the posterior of JI is I times the product of the members' registration
probabilities, and the source-independent factors cancel; it is the row of definition 3.1(a). The
shared row is then the exact posterior of a common cause. Members that have influenced one
another do not in general supply conditionally independent evidence, and the row is then a Gibbs
row of summed losses, a generalized-Bayes update in the sense of Bissiri et al. [1], not a posterior.
It adds the members' costs, unlike a logarithmic opinion pool with weights summing to one [5]; the
tempered label is the pool that averages them.

Two times matter: the time at which the interaction has brought the groups within the middle root
of the gap equation, and the later time at which the minimizer itself moves to the unions. A first
handoff before the first time stops the hierarchy at depth one, a handoff between them gives two

interaction necessity at both stages in this instance: until t = 1.75 the minimizer along both flows is
the four groups, and after that the minimizer of the shared-label flow is already the pair of unions.
A prior over coarsenings that offsets the saving of labels, or a cavity parent at the cost of exact
inheritance, would change the radius and the regimes (open problem 8.3).

Prior work and open problems

The ingredients have precedents. Inferred group membership that feeds back on individual updating
appears in the meta-contrast model of Salzarulo [17], where categories are recomputed from the
opinion context and agents move toward prototypes. It also appears in the latent-group model of
Gershman et al. [6], where an observer infers group structure with shared choice parameters and
the posterior determines social influence. Replacing locally coherent groups by representatives that
act at the next stage is the hierarchical consensus scheme of Perony et al. [16], with prescribed
stages and no feedback to the members. Martins [12] derived bounded confidence from Bayesian
updating, with stubbornness from shrinking individual uncertainty. Normalized state-dependent
weights are the setting of Motsch and Tadmor [14], and anchoring of opinions to fixed data is
the model of Friedkin and Johnsen [4]. Exact block-mean dynamics of linear consensus over
an external equitable partition is due to O'Clery et al. [15], and Schaub et al. [18] used these
partitions for cluster synchronization in nonlinear networks. Multilevel selection has been written as
Bayesian inference, with the formation of new levels as structure learning [3]. A literature search
on 2026-10-23, a research run of the Exa agent followed by checking each cited entry against its
DOI record or publisher page, found no work that combines a shared source label for dynamically
formed groups, exact block-mean closure for state-dependent rows away from synchrony, and
merging that depends on the state at formation. The search was bounded and does not establish
priority.

Open problem 8.1 (General laws). Find an exactly inherited compressive parent for shared-label
blocks outside the Gaussian location family. Candidates are a retention other than the pool, such as
the canonical parent of chapter 5 of the main manuscript for the routed interface, or an approximate
inheritance whose defect is controlled by the spread inside the block. Alternatively, show that no
exact compressive parent exists for Bernoulli blocks with Gibbs rows.

Open problem 8.2 (A derived persistence criterion). The derived selector of chapter 7 keeps
the window p, and the window decides whether the hierarchy stops at depth one, forms two stages,
or forms one coarser stage (remark 7.8). Replace it by a criterion read from the agents' models,
for example accumulated evidence for the shared label in the sense of a sequential test [19], or
the message counts of the registered-agreement model of chapter 3 of the main manuscript, and
decide whether the two-stage regime survives.

Open problem 8.3 (Interaction necessity at both stages). Under the derived selector the first
stage reads the attention present in the initial state (proposition 7.3 and corollary 7.4). Decide
whether a prior over coarsenings that offsets the saving of labels, or a cavity parent together with
an exact replacement for theorem 4.1, gives an admitted class on which the null flow does not
select the first-stage blocks while the second stage still depends on the first, and whether some
instance gives interaction necessity at both stages without either change.

Open problem 8.4 (Persistent compression). The coordinates discarded here are transient.
Combine the shared label with the code-invisible sectors of Items 1 to 3, or with the flatness
condition of remark 3.5, so that the parent forgets coordinates that persist and that an admitted
child-level probe still reads.

Open problem 8.5 (The reciprocal potential). In the per-agent model a source feels no force
from the receivers that attend to it. Under the reciprocal potential a parent that presents a pool
receives source-role forces from every receiver, and Item 2 found that disagreement makes sources
less certain there. Determine whether this loss of certainty offsets the n-fold sharpening of the
shared row, and whether exact inheritance survives the source-role terms. This is the question
about certainty raised in Item 3.

Open problem 8.6 (Depth). By eq. (5.3) the capture distance falls roughly as n-1/2. Determine
the depth reached from random records, and whether partial pooling, with the members' costs
added with weight np-1 for some 0 < p < 1, allows any finite depth while keeping counterfactual
dependence at every stage.

Open problem 8.7 (Validated numerics). Certify the first-stage events and the unions from the
actual handoffs on an explicit open subset of A by interval integration, and with it the open-set form
of (F5) and (F6a).
