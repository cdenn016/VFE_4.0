## 2. Cluster verdict

No lens in S1 found a stated theorem, proposition or lemma false, and no finding is critical: the lenses reconstructed or recomputed nearly every result of chapters 1 to 4, of the supplements s01 to s04 and of the two memory appendices, at exact or rounding-level residuals. The defects sit in what is said around the results, and two of them reach the abstract. The no-go for a global free energy is advertised in the abstract, chapters 1 and 3, the outlook, README, AUTHORING and the companion as excluding every fixed normalized model whose recognition laws are the agent laws, while `prop:dyn-no-global-vfe` proves a finite-model statement and an exact-product, fixed-channel statement; D05-OBJ and D01-FOUND give counterexamples to the advertised version (S1-H01). The interacting-memory appendix and companion describe the exact changing state of the protocol as the joint private-record law, and D07-IMEM1 and GAP09-S1 report that the reachable laws are product tilts of the fixed coupling, so that the remarks about what must be retained are false on the reachable family; D08-IMEM2 takes the opposing position on minimality (S1-H02). At medium severity the cluster holds false remarks beside correct propositions (loop transports in the stabilizer, equal predictions, a constant evaluator, Gaussian stabilizers, the meaning of a sector mismatch, the lower strata of the determinant example, Lyapunov functions, records and basin selection), definitions that do not yet carry what later chapters ask of them (record and model covariance, species, the per-agent picture, the record-interrupted process, the reciprocal split for group-valued links), and finiteness hypotheses on free-energy identities that can exclude their own optimizer. For the long-term program the lenses report foundations that are sound where stated; what they ask for is narrower claims at the abstract level, a measurable and reference-consistent declaration of generative models, records and policies, and precise definitions of the composite record process and of the per-agent picture.

### S1-H01: The no-go for a global free energy is advertised beyond `prop:dyn-no-global-vfe`, and the advertised version has counterexamples

**Sources:** D05-OBJ-01 (inflation, high); D01-FOUND-02 (inflation, medium); D04-DYN-02 (inflation, medium). Related items: S1-L27 (D05-OBJ-10, hypotheses missing from the proposition itself), S1-L23 (D05-OBJ-11, the companion restatement at c03:12), S1-M19 (D01-FOUND-08, a restricted no-go).

**Location:** `frontmatter.tex:5`; `chapters/01_foundations.tex:197`, `:226`; `chapters/03_dynamics.tex:4` and `:375-387` (`prop:dyn-no-global-vfe`); `chapters/06_outlook.tex:6`; `README.md:34`; `AUTHORING.md:39`; `companion/c03_dynamics.tex:12`.

**Finding and evidence.** The three lenses agree on what the proposition proves. Part (a): for finite K_i = K_j with at least two points, no normalized joint over finitely many finite-valued variables, record value, rule x -> Q(x) and constants a > 0, b give E(x) = a F(Q(x); o) + b on the whole interior; the proof uses only that a finite-model free energy is bounded above while E is unbounded on the interior (D05-OBJ s3(a): E = 2.51, 4.83, 7.78, 13.31 along a path toward opposite point masses). Part (b): with every channel fixed and recognition exactly the product of the agent laws, no affine identity holds on an open set; the proof compares the third mixed derivative d_{q_i} d^2_{q_j}, which vanishes for every mean-field free energy (sympy, arbitrary positive table) and equals -2 x_j/(1 - x_j^2)^2 for E (value -0.72455 at (0.1, 0.3)). D05-OBJ and D04-DYN both judge both proofs correct. The surrounding sentences are broader: chapter 3:4 and chapter 1:197 say the reciprocal potential "is not the free energy of any fixed normalized model in which the agent laws are recognition laws", chapter 1:226 says it is not F(Q; o) "for any fixed normalized model with these marginals as recognition laws", and the outlook, AUTHORING and c03:12 repeat this; the abstract and README:34 say "any fixed normalized model whose recognition laws are the agent laws", while the abstract also admits interactions that depend on the current laws.

D05-OBJ-01 refutes the broad statement on finite content alphabets with countable auxiliary latents. Absorption (script s7): with P = Unif(K_i) x Unif(K_j) x pi_theta, pi_theta(n) = 2^-(n+1), and recognition Q(x) = q_i x q_j x Q''(x), where Q''(x) = (1 - t) pi_theta + t delta_{n*} is tuned so that KL(Q''||pi_theta) = E(x) - KL(q_i||Unif) - KL(q_j||Unif) + 2 log 2, direct summation gives F(Q(x)) = E(x) + 2 log 2 on the whole interior (residual 6.9e-15 over logits in [-25, 25]). A structural version (s3(c)) keeps the receiver dependence inside the model: two binary agents, identity channel j -> i, fixed row beta = 0.6, an integer latent delta with prior c/(1 + delta^2) and a natural-number latent theta, auxiliary recognition reading q_j only; max |(1 + beta) F(Q(x)) - E(x) - U'| = 7.1e-15 on a 9 x 241 grid (U' = 9.213), and a finite truncation is exact on the open set |logit q_j| <= 6 (1.8e-15), so the finiteness and whole-interior hypotheses of (a) are both essential. D01-FOUND-02 refutes the chapter 1:226 sentence on infinite content spaces: two agents on R with references N(0, 1), one identity-channel edge with beta = 0.8, coefficient space R^2 with product prior, and a Gaussian-copula rule with -(1/2) log(1 - rho(x)^2) = beta KL(q_1||q_2) give E = F(Q(x); o) + const at every configuration with atomless laws and finite E (max |E - F| = 8.9e-16 over five Gaussian mean pairs, 4.4e-16 with Laplace marginals by a decomposed evaluation; `copula_witness.py`); on finite alphabets the coupling information is at most log |K|, consistent with (a). D04-DYN-02 adds that the proof of (a) fails on infinite spaces, that law-dependent channels on non-finite spaces and non-product recognition on non-finite spaces are not covered, and that on the slice of unit-variance Gaussian beliefs with identity channels and fixed rows E - F(Q; o) is constant for the normalized model y_i ~ G_i, o_ij given y ~ N(y_i - y_j, 1/beta_ij) registered at 0 (sympy, s9; a recomputation of 2026-10-22 X3-PIFB-01(c)), so the obstruction has no content in the mean directions. D04-DYN also states that by its reconstruction the third-derivative argument of (b) extends to general spaces for fixed channels. D05-OBJ records two statements as unproved: the abstract's version for law-dependent channels on non-finite models, and a profile version in which every non-agent recognition factor is free-energy optimal (its constructions use non-optimal auxiliary factors and do not refute that version). D05-OBJ finds no conflict with `thm:dyn-registered` (2026-10-22 C1), whose point recognition on a finite lattice falls under neither part.

The lenses differ on the abstract: D01-FOUND-02 reads its sentence as the product reading, supported by (b); D04-DYN-02 and D05-OBJ-01 read it as stated without qualifier and as covering law-dependent channels, for which (b) is not proved. They also differ in severity (high for D05-OBJ, medium for D01-FOUND and D04-DYN).

**Proposed fix.** D05-OBJ-01: replace every cited sentence by the two proved statements, "no finite normalized model, with any recognition rule, reproduces E on the interior; no normalized model whose recognition is exactly the product of the agent laws reproduces E on any open set when the channels are fixed"; add a remark with the absorption and structural constructions; prove (b) for law-dependent channels or restrict the abstract and README to fixed channels; pose the profile version as an open problem. D01-FOUND-02: end chapter 1:226 with "it is not F(Q; o) when Q is the product of these marginals and the channels are fixed, nor, on finite alphabets, for any rule assigning recognition laws; on infinite content spaces the coupling of Q can carry the excess as mutual information, so a no-go there must restrict how Q depends on the configuration", and add the hypotheses of the proposition at :197 (fixed positive rows, an identity-channel edge, finite alphabets for (a), fixed channels for (b)). D04-DYN-02: in chapter 3:4, c03:12 and the abstract, write "on finite alphabets the reciprocal potential is not the free energy of any fixed normalized model in which the agent laws are recognition laws, and for fixed channels it is not a mean-field free energy with the agent laws as factors on any open set", and add after the proposition that the obstruction lives in the variance and shape directions, citing the unit-variance slice.

**What would falsify it.** D05-OBJ: an error in s3 (Q(x) not a probability law, or the directly summed divergence not constant in x), or a rewording of every cited sentence to exact-product recognition with fixed channels, which would leave only the abstract's law-dependent case. D01-FOUND: an error in KL(Q||P_0) = I_Q(Z_1; Z_2) + sum_i KL(Q_i||P_0,i) for a product P_0 or in the mutual information of a Gaussian copula, or a reading of :226 that requires Q to be the product of the agent laws. D04-DYN: a proof of (a) on general standard Borel spaces with law-dependent channels and arbitrary recognition rules.

### S1-H02: The reachable private-record posteriors of the interacting protocol are product tilts of the fixed coupling, and the retention remarks that require the joint law are false on the reachable family

**Sources:** D07-IMEM1-01 (improvement, high); D07-IMEM1-02 (error, medium); GAP09-S1-02 (improvement, medium). Opposing position: D08-IMEM2-03 (extension, medium), kept as its own item S1-M32 and set against this one in section 9.

**Location:** `appendices/interacting_memory.tex:153-215` (`imem:reduction`, `imem:retention`), `:213`, `:580` (remark after `imem:gaussian-closure`), `:663-664` (`imem:open`); `companion/c04_interacting_memory.tex:55-89` (`comp:interaction-retention`), `:87`, `:89`; `AUTHORING.md:9`; `README.md:20`; `chapters/06_outlook.tex:8`.

**Finding and evidence.** D07-IMEM1-01 derives from `imem:history-likelihood` that at a fixed history every message factor depends on the private records d = (d_0, d_1) only through the speaker coordinate, because a speaker policy reads its own record and the common transcript. Hence, for every preparation, encoder and fixed schedule, L_t(d; h) = F_0^t(d_0) F_1^t(d_1) and lambda_t = F_0^t F_1^t lambda_0 / Z_t, with three consequences: (i) agent i reads the transcript only through the peer factor F_{1-i}^t (the own factor cancels, which is sender invariance); (ii) a speaker kernel depends only on the peer factor, and a message from j multiplies F_j and leaves F_{1-j} unchanged; (iii) the cross-agent dependence of lambda_t is frozen at its prior value: on finite tables every cross-ratio of lambda_t equals that of lambda_0, in the nondegenerate Gaussian case the off-diagonal block of S_t^{-1} equals that of S_0^{-1}, and for private-record spaces with n_0 and n_1 values the reachable lambda_t lies in a family of dimension (n_0 - 1) + (n_1 - 1), not n_0 n_1 - 1. Checks (`binary_exact.py`, `gaussian_check.py`, `asymptotic_sim.py`): the exact tilt and the cross-ratio 81/49 on every history of the schedules (1,0,1), (1,1,0), (0,1,0) and (1,0,1,0); invariance of all 36 cross-ratios for a random rational model with three-valued records; the off-diagonal precision block unchanged to 1.7e-16 over five Gaussian messages and three seeds while the diagonal blocks move by up to 3.6; and a filter that stores only the two log-likelihood ratios reproduces the four-weight filter to 2.4e-15 over 300 runs of 1000 messages. D07-IMEM1 identifies the factorization as Lemma 6.7 of Bar-Yossef, Jayram, Kumar and Sivakumar (J. Comput. Syst. Sci. 68, 2004), read in the publisher PDF.

D07-IMEM1-02 applies this to four sentences: appendix :213 ("Keeping only the two private-record marginals would also discard the conditional pair law in imem:reduced-readouts"), :580 ("Replacing it by separate-agent blocks changes the conditional averages"), companion :87 ("The joint law of the two private records is needed") and :89 ("Binary private records require four masses, or three independent probabilities"). Two product tilts of lambda_0 with the same marginals, mutually absolutely continuous and with integrable log-ratios, coincide, because KL(P||P') and KL(P'||P) are negatives of one number. In the binary example the marginals of lambda_2 are 7/11 and 4795/7502, and iterative proportional fitting of lambda_0 = (9, 7, 7, 9)/32 to them returns lambda_2 = (105/242, 49/242, 70/341, 54/341) with error 5.6e-17, while the product of the marginals differs; the odds ratio is 81/49 on every history, so binary records need two changing numbers. D07-IMEM1 reads the Gaussian sentence at :580 as true for covariance blocks and misleading in information coordinates, and states that the theorems themselves are unaffected. GAP09-S1-02 removes the two hypotheses of that uniqueness argument where the appendix needs it: mutual absolute continuity of two product tilts with equal marginals is automatic; uniqueness holds on finite alphabets for any lambda_0, zeros allowed; it holds without integrability whenever lambda_0 is equivalent to the product of its marginals, by Lemma 5.1(iii) of Ghosal, Nutz and Bernton (arXiv:2106.03670v2, read in the arXiv text), which covers the Gaussian closure family, the Gaussian receiver examples and the binary example; and it holds for every lambda_0 when the log-ratios are integrable. Its script `s4_marginal_uniqueness.py` finds all 58 scalings on 15 sparse 3 x 3 preparations ending at P (9.9e-14), Sinkhorn from 100 random scalings returning P (9.6e-16), and one positive root in the Gaussian case. GAP09-S1 leaves open the case of a lambda_0 with a part singular to the product of its marginals on infinite spaces with non-integrable log-ratios; it found no counterexample.

D08-IMEM2-03 takes a different position on minimality. For finite positive preparations whose encoded tables are affinely independent, it argues that any changing state from which the protocol computes the next two speakers' message kernels at every private value determines lambda_t (the next speaker's kernels give one family of conditionals, the reweighting after the message gives the other, and Brook's lemma recovers the positive joint law); `binary_exchange.py` recovers lambda_1 = (45, 21, 35, 27)/128 exactly from agent 0's kernels at t = 1 and agent 1's kernels at t = 2. It concludes that "in that example no changing state smaller than the three independent weights of lambda_t serves this interface" and that the companion's "four masses, or three independent probabilities" becomes a minimality statement for the example, and it lists positivity and the exact definition of the admitted interface as proof obligations. D07-IMEM1 counts two changing numbers, with lambda_0 held as fixed model data; D08-IMEM2 counts three independent weights.

**Proposed fix.** D07-IMEM1-01: add after `imem:reduction` a product-tilt proposition (dominated hypotheses, 0 < Z_t < infinity) with consequences (i) to (iii); restate the exact changing state as the classes [F_0^t], [F_1^t] up to reciprocal positive scaling, with lambda_0, one fixed disintegration of lambda_0, W, the encoders, d_i and the schedule as fixed data, and say that the two marginals of lambda_t are an equivalent changing state when lambda_0 has a positive density against a product measure; add to `imem:gaussian-closure` that only the diagonal precision blocks and the information vector change; restate `imem:open` as compression of the one-agent evidence functions; note that an autonomous update on (F_0, F_1) removes the jointly measurable disintegration on a law-valued family required at :52; revise AUTHORING.md:9. D07-IMEM1-02: at :213 write "Replacing lambda_t by the product of its marginals would discard the conditional pair law; the marginals together with the fixed lambda_0 do not, because lambda_t is a product tilt of lambda_0"; at companion :89 write that binary private records need two changing numbers, the log-likelihood ratios accumulated by each agent's messages, the four masses being a redundant form of the same state; at :580 add "in covariance coordinates; in information coordinates the cross-precision is fixed model data and only the diagonal blocks and the information vector change". GAP09-S1-02: add a proposition "Product tilts are determined by their marginals" with its four cases, qualify the :213 replacement by "whenever the private-record alphabets are finite or lambda_0 is equivalent to the product of its marginals", and use the equivalence case in place of the integrability hypothesis.

**What would falsify it.** D07-IMEM1-01: a reachable dominated history at which lambda_t is not a normalized f_0(d_0) f_1(d_1) lambda_0, or a finite instance in which a cross-ratio of lambda_t differs from that of lambda_0. D07-IMEM1-02: two distinct reachable lambda_t in the binary example with equal marginals. GAP09-S1-02: two distinct product tilts with equal marginals of a finite lambda_0 or of a lambda_0 equivalent to the product of its marginals, or a misreading of Lemma 5.1(iii). For the opposing position, D08-IMEM2-03 names two distinct reachable positive lambda_t, with affinely independent tables, that give identical next-two-step kernels at all private values.

## 8. Improvements and extensions, merged and prioritized

Priority is the severity the source lenses assigned. Within a priority level, items that a lens names as its most valuable next step come first, then items that would close a defect recorded above, then the rest in file order. Difficulty estimates are the lenses' own.

### Priority high

**S1-H02 (D07-IMEM1-01, with GAP09-S1-02; opposed by D08-IMEM2-03).** D07-IMEM1 calls this the highest-value step in its scope. Sketch: at a fixed dominated history L_t(d; h) = F_0^t(d_0) F_1^t(d_1), so lambda_t = F_0^t F_1^t lambda_0 / Z_t; agent i reads the transcript only through F_{1-i}^t, a speaker's policy depends on the history only through its peer's factor, and a message updates only its speaker's factor. The approximation question recorded on 2026-10-28 becomes a question about two functions of one variable each: for the Gaussian banks, log F_1^t is a sum of logarithms of finite Gaussian mixtures in h with logistic weights in d_1, so a finite basis for log F_i with the fixed lambda_0 is a candidate family, assessed by its error in future message laws and receiver updates. GAP09-S1 (its Proposition B) supplies uniqueness of product tilts with given marginals on finite alphabets, for lambda_0 equivalent to the product of its marginals, and under integrable log-ratios, leaving the singular infinite case open, and states that its Proposition D then lets `imem:open` be posed on (F_0^t, F_1^t) modulo reciprocal scaling with no measurable-selection hypothesis. Difficulty: one page for the proposition; medium for the singular case.

### Priority medium

**S1-M23 (D04-DYN-03): global chart theorem for fixed channels.** D04-DYN calls it the most useful next result; it would close remediation author decision 2 for every downstream chapter with fixed channels. Sketch: let m(t) and M(t) be the essential infimum and supremum over agents of log(dq_i/d lambda); near a minimizing point the force is bounded so that the lower Dini derivative of m is at least -m - A with A = 2 C_g + N + max(0, -log c), and near a maximizing point dM/dt <= B - M with B built from 2E(0), C_r, C_g and the kernel bounds, using E(t) <= E(0); the continuation alternative of `prop:dyn-chart-flow` then gives global existence with time-uniform two-sided density bounds. The only step beyond the finite proof is a lemma that a C^1 curve into L^infinity whose derivative is bounded below on the sublevel set {f <= ess inf f + eps} satisfies the Dini inequality for its essential infimum. For law-dependent channels, a bound C(1 + abs(log q)) on the channel-derivative force near the essential infimum gives existence with an exponential floor; the Bhattacharyya example shows some such hypothesis is needed. Convergence would need a Lojasiewicz-Simon inequality. Difficulty: low.

**S1-M26 (D06-PRED-01): the joint-law Fisher flow of the local free energy.** D06-PRED calls it the most valuable step. Sketch: for phi = log(zeta/zeta^o) the flow reads d phi/dt = -(phi - E_zeta phi), so zeta_t is proportional to zeta_0^{e^{-t}} (zeta^o)^{1 - e^{-t}}; on the bridge family zeta_beta the natural-gradient flow is d beta/dt = 1 - beta. This gives a continuous-time counterpart, with an explicit rate, of the assimilation maps of `def:dyn-record-interrupted` for the evidence-augmented local term, separates the flow that targets the posterior from the structured flow that targets a restricted optimum, and has a per-agent version with frozen peers (nonautonomous with moving peers, limit open). Difficulty: low on tables; moderate for densities, which need a declared chart admitting quadratic log-density perturbations.

**S1-M30 (D08-IMEM2-01): retention in the affine Gaussian family.** Sketch: S_t is a deterministic function of the step, the changing state is m_t in R^{d_0 + d_1}, and each complete local law is M_t m_t + c_t; local-only retention with the fixed model, private values and step index is exact exactly when the fiber test holds, sufficiently when rank M_t = d_0 + d_1 at every step (for d_0 = d_1 this reduces to invertibility of Gamma_0 and Gamma_1 through the factorization M_t = diag(Gamma_0, Gamma_1) [[-S_10 S_00^{-1}, I], [I, -S_01 S_11^{-1}]]). The open converse is whether a nontrivial kernel forces failure for generic parameters, a determinant condition that M_{t+1} Phi_t not vanish on ker M_t. Difficulty: moderate; it would settle the Gaussian part of `imem:open`.

**S1-M33 (GAP08-S1-01): the filter on standard Borel spaces.** Sketch of the stated theorem: regular conditional laws give the filter kernel pi_n; disintegrating the kernel p -> nu_p, nu_p(D) = integral p(dx) T_n(x, dy) 1_D(y, Ry), gives the predictive kernel mu_n and a jointly measurable update U_n; a pi-lambda argument gives Pi_{n+1} = U_n(Pi_n, Z_{n+1}, .) almost surely, linearity of the future-path law in p gives prediction from Pi_n, and the filter is a Markov chain on Prob(S). **S1-M25 (D05-OBJ-12): global contraction of per-agent belief dynamics.** Sketch: in log coordinates d theta_i/dt = -theta_i + beta_ii log G-hat_i + sum_{j != i} beta_ij log(q_j K_ij) + record term; q -> qK does not increase osc(log q - log q'), so M(t) = max_i osc(theta_i - theta_i') satisfies D+M <= -M + (1 - b)M = -bM.
