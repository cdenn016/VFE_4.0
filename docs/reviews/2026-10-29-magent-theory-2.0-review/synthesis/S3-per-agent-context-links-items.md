## 2. Cluster verdict

The lenses of this cluster report no false theorem, proposition or corollary in the integrated chapters 13 to 19, their companion chapters or appendix base_connection, and none failing at a load-bearing proof step; the five high-severity defects are in the unintegrated working document Item 7, whose claim to meet (F1) to (F6) is reported as not established and whose convex no-go is reported false, and in chapter 19, whose cohomological curvature cost is defined by a weak-anchor limit that D26-CURV places outside the validity of the tangent model. The medium defects fall into five groups. First, what the per-agent and collective constructions certify: the erased coordinates of chapters 13 and 14 are read by no admitted probe and fade, and the nonflat geometry is prepared at t = 0, present under the null and, by GAP01-S3, transient at every positive epsilon (2026-10-22 H7, T4 and T5 recurring). Second, undefined objects and unstated hypotheses: the Gibbs-reduced per-agent flow and its chart, the "rich smooth open class", the "regular domain" of the finite contextual flow, the known sender code, the common code of the data-processing remark, the regularity of the own-block contextual field, and the code kernel of the moment-matching remark. Third, limits under the program's noncompact frame groups: the branch hypothesis fails at GL(K) consensus, the rectangular selector has orthogonal holonomy, and link-only isometric targets do not exist for GL(K) or on the non-proper free SL(K) locus. Fourth, Item 1 still carries corrected claims, one of them false for the law-level per-agent model, contrary to the integration record. Fifth, Item 7's parent type, merge rule, scope statements, selector and parameter window. The improvements and extensions are many and several are cheap: explicit persistence constants, a proof of chart-flow well-posedness, uniqueness of the law-level covariance fixed point, Łojasiewicz single-limit convergence for the coupled Gaussian flow, the pullback-metric reading of D versus D+4, a Kempf-Ness comparison metric, and certified replacements for Item 7's sampled numbers. Much of the evidence behind the findings themselves is floating point on single families (section 10).
